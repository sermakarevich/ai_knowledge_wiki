# AI Is Everywhere. The Agentic Organization Isn't -- Yet

**Paper:** AI Is Everywhere. The Agentic Organization Isn't -- Yet (Alexis Krivkovich, Lucia Rahilly, Roberta Fusaro -- McKinsey & Company, April 2026)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Almost every big company is trying out AI tools, but most aren't seeing real payoff yet. It's like buying a state-of-the-art kitchen but still cooking the same old recipes -- the technology alone doesn't help unless you rethink how you work, who does what, and how teams are organized. The real challenge isn't plugging in AI; it's redesigning the entire way a company operates so that humans move from doing the work to overseeing and judging the work that AI agents do.

## TL;DR

McKinsey research identifies five pillars of the "agentic organization" -- business model, operating model, workflows, leadership, and talent/culture -- arguing that over 80% of companies investing in agentic AI are failing to realize bottom-line returns because they treat AI as a point solution rather than a catalyst for end-to-end organizational redesign. The paper advocates shifting humans from "in the loop" to "above the loop," where judgment, oversight, and systems thinking become the primary human contributions while AI agents handle execution.

---

## Problem & Motivation

Companies are investing heavily in agentic AI but failing to translate experimentation into enterprise value. More than 80% report no bottom-line impact from their AI investments. The gap isn't technological -- it's organizational. Leaders are stuck asking tactical questions ("which tasks can AI do?") instead of strategic ones ("how must my entire operating model change?"). As agentic AI matures toward near-zero marginal cost of delivery, companies that don't redesign their workflows, team structures, and leadership models risk being disrupted by those that do.

---

## Main Original Ideas

1. **The Five Pillars of the Agentic Organization** -- The transformation requires simultaneous redesign across business model (near-zero marginal cost enabling hypersegmentation), operating model (flatter orgs, broader spans), workflows (end-to-end reimagination, not point solutions), leadership (leaders must personally transform first), and talent/culture (continuous learning as a perpetual state).

2. **"Above the Loop" vs. "In the Loop"** -- A critical conceptual shift: "human in the loop" means agents do parts and hand off to humans for other parts; "human above the loop" means agents handle most or all of the core process while humans provide judgment and oversight on top. The future is moving toward the latter.

3. **Workflow Reimagination Over Point Solutions** -- The highest-impact AI deployments reimagine entire end-to-end workflows (e.g., insurance underwriting, HR hire-to-onboard) across multiple teams and touchpoints, not just individual tasks. This cross-functional redesign is where "the magic starts to happen."

4. **The 75% Reshaping Mandate** -- 75% of roles need fundamental reshaping right now, including both leaders and their reports. This isn't about role elimination but redefinition -- strategic thinking, systems orientation, and people management soft skills become more valuable, not less.

5. **The Junior Talent Pipeline Paradox** -- If organizations eliminate entry-level roles because AI handles "grunt work," they create an expensive senior-only workforce and lose the pipeline for developing the next generation of talent. Leaders must rethink how juniors build judgment without the traditional apprenticeship model.

6. **Change Management as a Perpetual State** -- Unlike previous transformations with a defined endpoint, agentic AI requires organizations to become comfortable in constant change. Change management is no longer episodic -- it's the new normal.

## Key Findings

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Companies seeing ROI** | <20% of companies report bottom-line impact from AI investments |
| **Roles needing reshaping** | 75% need fundamental redefinition in the next 2-3 years |
| **Org layer bloat** | Most companies added 1-3 management layers in the past decade |
| **Leader skill gap awareness** | ~50% of leaders recognize skill gaps in their organizations |
| **Job description changes** | Nearly every employee will need a new job description within 2-3 years |

- Pharma/life sciences companies are envisioning "huge squads of agents" in R&D to supercharge innovation speed, not replace researchers
- Corporate functions (HR, finance, legal) seen as highest-potential areas for agentic transformation of cost centers
- Two tech organization models emerging: centralized "SWAT teams" vs. embedded expertise in each business area
- The American Arbitration Association case study showed agents could review case files, compile timelines, analyze both sides, and produce summary decisions -- sometimes better than the traditional process
- Fluid "pods" of work (forming, reforming, reusable, moving nimbly across the org) are exciting in theory but hard in practice -- job hierarchies still needed for evaluation and managerial support

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. **Leaders must transform themselves first** -- Executives should ask whether 50% of their own time is spent differently due to AI, not just dabble with email tightening and ad-hoc queries.

2. **Invest in end-to-end workflow redesign** -- Move beyond point solutions; identify cross-functional workflows and reimagine them entirely with human-above-the-loop architecture.

3. **Rethink talent development from day one** -- Make learning and development central to the employee journey, not a periodic "sidecar." New hires should have access to AI tools immediately.

4. **Build a culture of curiosity, not fear** -- Foster organizations where experimentation is encouraged, two-way doors (reversible decisions) are the norm, and people explore change with optimism.

5. **Involve all levels in process redesign** -- Don't limit workflow reimagination to senior leaders; employees at every level should participate in rethinking how work gets done.

6. **Develop systems thinkers** -- Invest in people who can identify reusable, deployable agent teams and spot lighthouse use cases across the organization.

7. **Embrace fluid talent flows** -- Build HR functions that support nimbler movement of talent across roles and teams as a competitive advantage.
