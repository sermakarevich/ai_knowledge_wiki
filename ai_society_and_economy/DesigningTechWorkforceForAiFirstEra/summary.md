# Designing an End-to-End Technology Workforce for the AI-First Era

**Paper:** Designing an End-to-End Technology Workforce for the AI-First Era (Henning Soller, Mike Stefanelli, Rajat Goel, Umar Husain -- McKinsey & Company, April 2026)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine your company's tech team is like a sports roster. AI agents are now star players that can handle a lot of the routine game play on their own. So instead of hiring a huge bench of generalists, you need fewer but smarter coaches (senior engineers, architects) who know how to direct these AI players, plus a training program so your existing team members learn to work alongside them. On top of that, the deals you cut with outside contractors need to shift from "pay per hour of work" to "pay for actual results" -- because AI makes the old billing model obsolete.

## TL;DR

As agentic AI absorbs routine development and maintenance, CIOs must simultaneously restructure hiring (fewer but more senior technologists), build internal AI capabilities through skills-based workforce development, and renegotiate vendor contracts from labor-intensive managed services to outcome-linked managed value. Getting this three-part formula right can triple technology ROI.

---

## Problem & Motivation

Technology leaders face four simultaneous pressures: cutting run costs while funding AI innovation, managing geopolitical risks in global delivery, ensuring vendor partnerships deliver real ROI instead of empty promises, and hiring/reskilling talent in a market where agentic AI is eliminating junior roles while intensifying competition for senior specialists. CIOs who fail to act deliberately risk being locked into structures that limit flexibility and returns.

---

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Three-lever framework for CIOs** -- The article argues that hiring, internal capability building, and vendor strategy are no longer independent decisions. They must be treated as an interconnected system where each lever reinforces the others.

2. **Shift from volume hiring to strategic placement** -- Leading organizations are hiring fewer technologists overall but targeting senior engineers, architects, product managers, and designers who can orchestrate human-agent teams. The business case for large junior developer cohorts is weakening as agentic AI absorbs that work.

3. **Skills-based workforce view over static role definitions** -- The most effective CIOs map skill proficiency across their workforce, identify untapped skills, define career paths, and eliminate overlapping roles -- rather than managing by headcount. A healthcare case study showed this could yield tens of millions in new value.

4. **Managed value over managed services** -- Vendor contracts should shift from rewarding activity, headcount, or volume to linking compensation to delivery speed, quality, modernization progress, or business impact. SaaS models are moving from seat-based to API-first, outcome-linked consumption.

5. **Hybrid human-agent workforce as the end state** -- Organizations must design governance structures and collaboration models for teams where humans and AI agents operate as coworkers, not just tool users.

## Key Findings

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| CIO involvement in strategy | Two-thirds of top-performing companies have tech leaders "very involved" in enterprise strategy vs. 52% at other orgs |
| Expert productivity multiplier | With agents as tools, expert technologists can be several times more productive than less experienced peers, with modest pay differential |
| Budget trends | 25% of top-performing companies plan to increase tech budgets by >10% this year vs. 3% of others |
| Vendor renegotiation ROI | A bank that aligned vendor decisions to product road maps and set AI automation targets saw 10% productivity uplift across internal and external teams |
| Skills mapping impact | A healthcare company mapped skill proficiency for hundreds of roles, found half of people were proficient in core skills, enabling targeted development and skill-based hiring that could yield tens of millions in value |

- The shift is not purely technological -- it represents a profound change management challenge
- Waiting for clarity carries its own risk; organizations that delay will be locked into suboptimal structures
- Companies that hire for volume rather than expertise risk inflating costs without increasing impact

---

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. **Reassess hiring plans** with a 3-5 year view of how technology roles will evolve, not just today's skill gaps
2. **Invest in internal capabilities** to anchor long-term advantage -- companies cannot outsource their understanding of how AI impacts their development road maps, platforms, data, or architecture
3. **Reset vendor relationships** according to outcomes rather than effort, with explicit AI-driven automation targets
4. **Design hybrid human-agent operating models** including new collaboration models and governance structures
5. **Drive change management** to help employees navigate fast-evolving roles and reimagine positions rather than simply upskilling existing ones
6. **Draw an explicit build-vs-buy line** for AI capabilities and revisit it as AI matures
