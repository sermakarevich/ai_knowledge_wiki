# Reinventing Marketing Workflows with Agentic AI

**Paper:** [Reinventing Marketing Workflows with Agentic AI (Dianne Esber, Eli Stein, Julien Boudet, Kelsey Robinson, Nilay Shah -- McKinsey & Company, April 2026)](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine a marketing team where, instead of dozens of people doing repetitive tasks like writing copy, testing ads, and checking legal compliance, a handful of humans supervise teams of AI assistants that handle all that automatically -- 24/7, at massive scale. Today most companies have dabbled with individual AI tools (like using ChatGPT to draft emails) but those tools work in isolation and don't connect. This article is a recipe for how to wire everything together so AI agents handle the bulk of the work while human marketers focus on creativity and strategy -- like a conductor leading an orchestra rather than playing every instrument.

## TL;DR

McKinsey argues that agentic AI (multi-step, autonomous AI systems) can power ~60% of marketing tasks and deliver 10--30% revenue growth from hyperpersonalization, with campaign execution accelerated 10--15x. The bottleneck is not model capability but workflow reimagination: most organizations have disconnected gen AI pilots that boost individual productivity without enterprise-wide impact. The article prescribes a five-step framework -- task taxonomy, agent archetypes, modular agent design, human-role redefinition, and phased rollout -- illustrated with a consumer brand case study.

---

## Problem & Motivation

Marketing organizations adopted gen AI early but fragmented: isolated tools for copy generation, image creation, analytics -- none integrated. This "gen AI paradox" produces more activity (images, drafts) but negligible bottom-line impact. The root cause is legacy martech architecture (siloed CMS, DAM, CRM, analytics) never designed for real-time agentic orchestration or shared data models. Meanwhile, consumer expectations for personalization and immediacy are accelerating, and AI-mediated discovery (consumers finding products via AI systems) is reshaping the funnel itself.

---

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Agentic Workforce Model** -- Replace the model of individual humans using isolated tools with hybrid human--agent teams where one marketer supervises a squad of agents executing most production work. Humans shift from execution to orchestration, strategy, and creative judgment.

2. **Five-Step Workflow Design Process** -- A structured methodology: (1) taxonomy of current marketing microtasks, (2) classification into reusable agent archetypes, (3) identification of specific modular agents per archetype, (4) future-state workflow design with explicit human-in-the-loop roles, (5) phased rollout prioritized by business value and technical readiness.

3. **Agent Archetypes as Reusable Blueprints** -- Six archetypes cover all marketing work: Content Generation, Knowledge Management, Localization, Analysis, Planning, and Operations. Each archetype contains dozens of modular agents that can be composed across different workflows rather than rebuilt per use case.

4. **Modular Agent Design** -- Agents are intentionally small and single-purpose (e.g., a short-form text generator) so they can be inserted into multiple workflows (creative development, sales collateral, e-commerce copy). System interoperability -- not model capability -- is identified as the primary limiting factor.

5. **Clean-Sheet Workflow Redesign** -- Rather than automating existing processes, organizations should redesign workflows from scratch around what agents do best, then carve out the tasks that still require human judgment (brand strategy, qualitative "taste," stakeholder relationships).

## Key Findings

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Share of marketing tasks AI can power | ~60% overall (70% creation, 70% execution, 60% e-commerce, 50% strategy) |
| Revenue growth from hyperpersonalized marketing | 10--30% |
| Campaign creation/execution speed-up | 10--15x |
| End-to-end process speed gain (content creation pilot) | 4x vs. traditional |
| CMOs experimenting with AI | ~90% |
| CMOs capturing value across end-to-end workflows | <10% |

- The gap between experimentation (90%) and value capture (10%) is the core problem the framework addresses.
- Brand and legal governance, human capability gaps, technology underinvestment, and data bottlenecks are the top CMO concerns (survey of 35 Fortune 250 CMOs).
- Agentic media execution (autonomous bid/budget optimization) is already showing measurable ROAS improvement in early pilots.
- Platforms like Adobe and HubSpot are embedding agents directly into creative workflows for real-time asset adaptation.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. **Set top-down vision** -- Board and CEO must lead; strong governance is required to ensure adoption scales while limiting brand and legal exposure.

2. **Don't over-index on agents** -- Scripting, RPA, and traditional ML remain necessary complements; focusing only on agentic AI leaves efficiency gains on the table.

3. **Invest in new human skills** -- Organizations must upskill marketers in: prompt engineering, agent collaboration and handoffs, quality/compliance monitoring, data and AI fluency, and ML workflow orchestration.

4. **Build governance for AI-generated insights** -- Insights teams need validation frameworks with confidence thresholds before AI-generated findings inform brand or investment decisions.

5. **Modernize the tech foundation** -- Unified identity/data layers, flexible model-serving infrastructure, and API-exposed content/activation systems are prerequisites; agents cannot be orchestrated on top of fragmented legacy martech.

6. **Design for reuse and upgradability** -- Agents built for one workflow should be modular enough to reuse across functions, and architectures must accommodate model upgrades as the technology evolves.
