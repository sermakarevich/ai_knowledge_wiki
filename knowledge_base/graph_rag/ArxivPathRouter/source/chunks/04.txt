4.3
Ablation Study
Table 2 assesses how the reward design, teacher KL
objective, and routing mechanism each contribute
to path faithfulness. In the reward group, removing
the path reward rp primarily lowers SF-F1 while
retaining much of the answer F1, indicating that
this signal improves evidence quality rather than
merely supporting answer prediction. By contrast,
removing either the exploration bonus re or the
lazy penalty rl substantially reduces retrieval turns
to approximately one and causes broad degradation
across all metrics, showing that both terms are es-
sential for sustaining multi-step evidence seeking.
The timeout ro and redundancy rd penalties yield
more modest but consistent regularization benefits.
In the teacher KL group, removing KL pre-
serves multi-turn behavior but reduces answer F1,
whereas applying KL uniformly underperforms se-
lective KL, suggesting that teacher guidance is
most effective when focused on evidence-poor tra-
jectories. In the routing group, removing route
scaling or reducing the 2D taxonomy to a single
axis degrades both F1 and SF-F1, demonstrating
that answer correctness and evidence-path overlap
provide complementary supervision signals.

4.4
Routing and Trajectory Quality
This analysis examines whether route-conditioned
training promotes evidence-path exploration be-
yond improvements in answer F1.
Figure 3
compares the converged 2×2 trajectory distribu-
tions across all six datasets for Base, GRPO, and
PATHROUTER. PATHROUTER consistently reduces
evidence-deficient trajectories, including shortcut
success (C ↑P ↓) and joint failure (C ↓P ↓),
while increasing faithful success (C ↑P ↑) on all
datasets, with the largest gain on MuSiQue. In addi-
tion, evidence-present reasoning failure (C ↓P ↑)
increases on five of six datasets, indicating that
trajectories previously failing at both retrieval and
answering are converted into cases where relevant
evidence is successfully retrieved, even when an-
swer synthesis remains unresolved. The overall
shift from the P ↓column to the P ↑column demon-
strates that PATHROUTER improves evidence ac-
quisition independently of answer correctness, sup-
porting the conclusion that route-conditioned train-
ing encourages genuine evidence-path exploration
rather than exploiting parametric answer priors.

4.6
Cross-Dataset Transfer
To assess whether path-faithful retrieval behav-
ior generalizes beyond individual training distri-
butions, we train PATHROUTER on each dataset
separately and evaluate on all others. Figure 4
reports the OOD generalization ratio for each train–
eval pair. PATHROUTER achieves a highly uniform
transfer profile, with all off-diagonal ratios exceed-
ing 89% and an average ratio of 95.7%, suggesting
that path-aware routing and selective query guid-
ance encourage retrieval strategies that are not tied
to the distributional characteristics of any single
training set. In comparison, Search-R1 and Graph-
R1 achieve average ratios of 70.6% and 85.8% re-
spectively (Appendix D.3).
