**What it shows.** Figure 2 is a set of eight pairwise‑comparison matrices (4 metrics × 2 datasets: *Podcast transcripts* and *News articles*, each scored on *Comprehensiveness, Diversity, Empowerment, Directness*). Each matrix lists the six retrieval conditions — **SS** (naïve vector RAG), **TS** (text‑summarization RAG without a graph), and **C0–C3** (GraphRAG community summaries at four hierarchy levels) — along both rows and columns. The cell value is the **head‑to‑head win rate (%) of the row condition over the column condition**, averaged over 125 questions per comparison (each run 5×). The diagonal is the uncomputed self‑comparison, shown as the 50 % reference; the **green‑shaded / bold** cell in each row marks the condition that beat all others for that row (i.e., the row winner), and the overall bold entry is the best condition for that dataset/metric.

**Axes / structure.** There are no continuous axes: the rows are "row condition" and the columns are "column condition," so each panel is a 6×6 tournament grid where >50 % in a cell means the row condition won that matchup.

**Trends.**
- *Comprehensiveness & Diversity:* the naïve condition **SS** is the consistent loser (its row stays at/below ~50 %), while **TS** and all four **C**‑conditions win over SS, and the **C**‑conditions edge out TS slightly. The advantage is strongest and most consistent in these two panels.
- *Empowerment:* results are mixed and close to the 50 % line; **TS** is competitive (often a row‑winner) and the **C**‑conditions do not show a clear win over TS or SS.
- *Directness:* the pattern reverses — **SS** is frequently competitive or best (rows ≈ 50–65 %), while the **C**‑conditions are weaker, indicating a small trade‑off.

**Takeaway.** GraphRAG community‑summary conditions (C0–C3) and text‑summarization (TS) reliably beat naïve RAG (SS) on answer *comprehensiveness* and *diversity*, with the community summaries at least matching the no‑graph summarization baseline. The gains are not uniform: there is no consistent advantage on *empowerment*, and a modest cost on *directness*, where naïve RAG can hold up. In short, the figure argues that the graph‑indexed summaries win mainly on the "coverage/variety" dimensions of answers rather than on every quality metric.
