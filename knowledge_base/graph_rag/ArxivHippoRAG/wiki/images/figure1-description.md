**Figure 1 — "Knowledge Integration & RAG" (schematic, not a quantitative plot).** The figure has no numeric axes or data trends; it is a conceptual diagram organized as a 2‑column × 3‑row grid. Columns are the two pipeline stages, *Offline Indexing* (left) and *Online Retrieval* (right); rows are three systems — **Current RAG**, **Human Memory**, and **HippoRAG** — each applied to the same example task: *"Which Stanford professor works on the neuroscience of Alzheimer's?"* (answer: Prof. Thomas).

- **Current RAG (top row).** Offline, passages are encoded in isolation as embedded vectors (rows of small dot‑tokens tagged with person icons). Online, top‑k retrieval surfaces a handful of passage cards, but because no passage co‑mentions both *Stanford* and *Alzheimer's*, the system cannot bridge the two concepts and fails (marked with a "?" / no answer).
- **Human Memory (middle row).** Offline, this is depicted as a brain with neocortical storage regions (red) and a C‑shaped hippocampus (blue). Online, recall is shown as associative activation spreading across the brain to link the two concepts, yielding the correct answer (green check + Prof. Thomas).
- **HippoRAG (bottom row).** Offline, the corpus is transformed into a schemaless graph of associations — a blue C‑shaped structure whose nodes are concepts/people and whose edges are stored associations (accompanied by a hippocampus mascot icon). Online, a highlighted path (yellow edges) connects the query seed nodes to the target node, retrieving the correct answer in one step (green check).

**Iconography:** red marks the "Stanford/professor" concept, the purple ribbon marks "Alzheimer's," person‑cards denote passages, the blue C‑shape denotes the hippocampus/association index, and the green check vs. question mark denotes success vs. failure.

**Takeaway.** Knowledge‑integration / "path‑finding" multi‑hop QA is difficult for standard RAG because it encodes passages in isolation and thus cannot connect disparate concepts; the human brain solves such queries via hippocampus‑mediated associative memory. HippoRAG mimics this by building a graph of associations (an artificial hippocampal index) and traversing it (via Personalized PageRank) to perform single‑step multi‑hop retrieval, thereby answering integration queries that current RAG misses.
