**Figure 1 — Overall architecture of the proposed LightRAG framework (schematic diagram, not a quantitative plot).**

*What it shows:* A left‑to‑right pipeline diagram of the LightRAG system, illustrating how raw text is turned into a knowledge graph and how a query is retrieved against it. It is a component/flow diagram, so there are **no axes, curves, or numerical trends** to report; the "takeaway" is structural rather than quantitative (no exact numbers are given in the figure).

*Left half — Graph‑based Text Indexing:* A source document (a beekeeping passage) is processed by three operations labeled **D(·)** (Deduplication), **P(·)** (LLM Profiling), and **R(·)** (Entity & Relation Extraction), producing entity/relation records (e.g., entity *Beekeeper* of type *PERSON*; relation *Honey Bee → Industrial agriculture* with keywords and a description, each tied to original chunk IDs). These are merged into an **Index Graph** (a small node‑and‑edge network) that serves as the retrieval structure.

*Right half — Dual‑level Retrieval Paradigm:* A query is combined with an LLM and routed through **two complementary key levels**: **low‑level keys** (concrete entities such as *Beekeeper, Honey Bee, Farmers, Hive*) and **high‑level keys** (abstract themes such as *Agriculture, Production, Environmental impact*). Together with **Relations** and the **Entities** graph, these yield the **Retrieved Content**. The far‑right panel defines the three retrieved components: **Entities** (entities relevant to the query), **Relationships** (edges among them), and **Contexts** (the original text snippets used to describe them).

*Takeaway:* LightRAG indexes documents as an LLM‑built knowledge graph (entities + relations + chunk references) and retrieves at two granularities—specific entities and higher‑level themes—so that the generative model receives a compact, contextually rich set of entities, relationships, and source texts rather than raw chunks. The figure is a conceptual blueprint of that index‑then‑retrieve pipeline.
