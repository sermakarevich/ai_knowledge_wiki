**Figure 3** presents an overall QA performance comparison across four retrieval methods—RAG (blue), GraphRAG (orange), Selection (green), and Integration (red)—evaluated on four datasets (x‑axis): NQ, Hotpot, MultiHop‑RAG, and NovelQA. The y‑axis is "Performance" (score), spanning roughly 50–75 with gridlines every 5 units. The figure has two panels: (a) using Llama3.1‑8B and (b) using Llama3.1‑70B.

**Trends.** In both panels, Integration (red) is consistently the top or near‑top performer across nearly all datasets, and Selection (green) typically ranks second, often edging out the standalone RAG and GraphRAG bars. The standalone methods are more uneven: on NQ and Hotpot, RAG and GraphRAG land in the mid‑60s, slightly below the two combined strategies. On MultiHop‑RAG all four methods rise sharply, with Integration clearly leading (high 60s for the 8B model, low 70s for the 70B). NovelQA is the notable weak point for the single paradigms—both RAG and especially GraphRAG drop to the low 50s, while Selection and Integration remain markedly higher. Comparing the two panels, moving from the 8B to the 70B backbone lifts performance across the board (all bars shift upward by a few points), while preserving the same relative ordering of methods.

**Takeaway.** Combining the RAG and GraphRAG paradigms (Integration) yields the strongest QA accuracy, and the lighter Selection strategy captures most of that gain while using only one method per query. GraphRAG alone is competitive on multi‑hop/reasoning tasks but degrades on datasets like NovelQA, whereas the combined and selection strategies are more robust; overall, accuracy improves with a larger underlying LLM.
