# Connections

- [[graph_rag/ArxivGraphReasoningAgentGRA/summary|GRA: Graph Reasoning Agent]] — same problem space (an LLM agent autonomously traversing a knowledge graph), but GRA focuses on giving the agent better navigation tools (unix-style graph commands) to reach an answer efficiently, while this paper instead audits *what the agent's citations actually account for* after that kind of traversal — shares-technique (agentic graph traversal) with a different research question (efficiency vs. provenance).
- [[graph_rag/ArxivGraphScout/summary|GraphScout]] — another agentic graph-traversal system (a small trained LLM exploring a KG with code-query/fuzzy-search tools); relevant as a concrete example of exactly the kind of system this paper's ablation methodology could be applied to, to check whether GraphScout's own citations are necessary-but-not-sufficient in the same way.
- [[graph_rag/ArxivRAGvsGraphRAG/summary|RAG vs. GraphRAG]] — a controlled benchmark comparing RAG and GraphRAG families on single-hop vs. multi-hop QA; same-problem-different-method relative to this paper in that both use controlled benchmarks to make careful comparative claims about GraphRAG behavior, though RAG vs. GraphRAG measures retrieval accuracy trade-offs rather than citation faithfulness.
- [[graph_rag/ArxivGraphRAGUnderFire/summary|GraphRAG Under Fire]] — studies how poisoning a single shared relation can corrupt every query depending on it; connects to this paper's finding that graph *structure* (not just individual cited facts) carries outsized influence over agentic GraphRAG answers — both papers show that treating a GraphRAG system's behavior as reducible to a small set of named facts (a citation, or a single poisoned relation) understates how much the surrounding graph structure actually drives outcomes.
