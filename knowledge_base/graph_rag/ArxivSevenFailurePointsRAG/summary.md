# Seven Failure Points When Engineering a Retrieval Augmented Generation System — Summary

**Paper:** [Seven Failure Points When Engineering a Retrieval Augmented Generation System (Barnett et al., 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05856)

RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) grounds an LLM's answers in an organisation's own documents instead of fine-tuning the model — a document is chunked, embedded, and stored in a vector database at index time; at query time the question is rewritten, embedded, matched against chunks, re-ranked, consolidated to fit token limits, and read into a final answer. This paper reports what actually goes wrong when engineers build such systems in practice, based on three real RAG deployments at Deakin University: **Cognitive Reviewer** (a research literature-review tool), **AI Tutor** (a university course assistant), and a large-scale **BioASQ** biomedical Q&A experiment (15,000 documents, 1,000 questions).

From these deployments the authors catalogue **seven failure points**, each tied to a specific stage of the RAG pipeline: **Missing Content** (no answer exists in the corpus, risking hallucination), **Missed the Top Ranked Documents** (the answer exists but ranked below the retrieval cutoff), **Not in Context** (retrieved but dropped during consolidation), **Not Extracted** (present in context but the LLM fails to pull it out), **Wrong Format** (ignores a requested output format), **Incorrect Specificity** (answer too broad or too narrow), and **Incomplete** (a multi-part answer is missing some parts). The paper argues these failures are not fixed by better prompting alone — they require calibrating chunking, embeddings, retrieval, and consolidation strategies against the specific application, and that this calibration is an ongoing runtime activity rather than a one-time build step, since RAG systems cannot be validated offline against pre-existing test data.

The paper closes with nine concrete lessons drawn from the case studies (larger context windows help, semantic caching cuts cost, jailbreaks bypass the RAG layer and hit the LLM directly, metadata in context improves extraction, open-source embeddings are competitive) and three open research directions: chunking & embedding strategy, RAG vs. fine-tuning tradeoffs, and testing/monitoring RAG systems in production. It is explicitly an experience report — qualitative, drawn from 3 case studies — not a controlled benchmark comparison.
