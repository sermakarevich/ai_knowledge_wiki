## Technical Summary of Figures (Page)

**Overview.** The page contains two line charts (Figure 6, Figure 7) and one ablation table (Table 4), all reporting Attack Success Rate (ASR) for the GRAGPOISON relation‑injection attack across four datasets (MuSiQue, Geographic, Medical, Cybersecurity). Together they probe two hyperparameters (number of supporting relations, total poisoning‑text length) and decompose the contribution of each "trick" used during relation injection.

**Figure 6 – Impact of supporting relations (N_β).** A multi‑series line plot with the x‑axis = "Number of Supporting Relations per Relation Injection (N_β)" (discrete ticks 0, 1, 3, 5, 10) and y‑axis = ASR (roughly 20–100). Four curves (one per dataset, color‑coded: MuSiQue red, Geographic blue, Medical orange, Cybersecurity green) all start low at 0, jump sharply at 1, continue climbing to 3–5, then flatten into a clear plateau toward 10. **Takeaway:** ASR is highly sensitive to N_β in the low range but shows diminishing returns beyond ~5, indicating a small number of supporting relations is sufficient for the injected entity to compete with the original entity in GraphRAG's ranking.

**Figure 7 – Impact of total poisoning‑text length via replication.** Same layout/legend, but x‑axis = "Number of Poisoning Text Replications" (0, 1, 3, 5, 10), y‑axis ASR (~60–100). All four curves are comparatively flat, with only a slight upward drift across the range. **Takeaway:** duplicating poisoning text to grow its length yields only marginal ASR gains; the crafted text is already effectively indexed/retrieved, so mere replication does not add new attack signal.

**Table 4 – Ablation of relation‑injection tricks.** A matrix with rows = datasets (MuSiQue, Geographic, Medical, Cyber‑Security) plus an Average row, and columns = a baseline "w/" ASR (roughly ~80–99%) followed by a "w/o" block listing the percentage ASR drop when each trick is removed: Entity Selection, Explicit Negation, Temporal Ordering, Contextual Explanation, and Text Shuffling. Dropped values are negative (i.e., performance loss). **Takeaway:** removing Entity Selection and Contextual Explanation causes the largest ASR losses (notably the Cyber‑security row), while Text Shuffling has the smallest effect—identifying entity selection and contextual explanation as the most critical components of the injection strategy.

**Overall message conveyed by the figures.** The attack's effectiveness is driven more by the *structure* of the injected relation (a handful of supporting relations, careful entity selection, contextual explanation, explicit negation) than by brute‑force length increases; both charted hyperparameters plateau quickly, and the ablation shows which linguistic "tricks" carry the weight.
