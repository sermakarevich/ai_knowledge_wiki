# Retrieval Practice: The Master and His Emissary

Answer from memory before opening any answer. Run sessions with `ai show summary/quiz`.

### Q1. McGilchrist explicitly rejects the popular "left brain = logical, right brain = creative" cliché. What does he argue the hemispheres actually divide, if not subject matter?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Not content but *mode of attention* — the "how," not the "what." Every faculty (language, reason, emotion, music) is served by both hemispheres; the right hemisphere (RH) attends broadly, contextually, and to the whole, while the left hemisphere (LH) attends narrowly, abstracting fixed parts out of context. See [[wiki/03-what-the-hemispheres-do|Ch.2 What Do the Two Hemispheres 'Do'?]].

### Q2. What does McGilchrist argue is the primary function of the corpus callosum — and how does that complicate the naive idea that it "unifies" the two hemispheres?

> [!tip]- Answer
> He argues its main role is largely *inhibitory*: it lets each hemisphere suppress the other's competing take on the world, rather than blending two signals into one unified stream. Brain unity is therefore achieved despite this connection, through a more complex process of division and reintegration, not because the callosum merges the two sides. See [[wiki/02-asymmetry-and-the-brain|Ch.1 Asymmetry and the Brain]].

### Q3. What is the Master-and-emissary parable, and which hemisphere does each figure represent?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Adapted from Nietzsche (echoing an older Sufi/Indian story): a wise Master rules a domain he knows intimately and delegates practical business to a clever emissary/vizier. The emissary is meant to serve, but comes to believe his narrow expertise is the whole truth and usurps the Master. The right hemisphere is the Master; the left hemisphere is the emissary. See [[wiki/01-introduction|Introduction]].

### Q4. The left hemisphere usually wins the day-to-day contest for control of attention and behaviour. So why does McGilchrist still argue the right hemisphere has "primacy"?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Primacy here means ontological, not competitive: the RH delivers the world new, whole, and embodied *before* the LH can abstract and "unpack" any part of it, and the LH's output must be handed back to the RH for reintegration into a livable whole. Evidence drawn on includes Libet's readiness-potential experiments, gesture research, and split-brain syllogism studies. See [[wiki/06-primacy-of-the-right-hemisphere|Ch.5 The Primacy of the Right Hemisphere]] and [[wiki/07-triumph-of-the-left-hemisphere|Ch.6 The Triumph of the Left Hemisphere]].

### Q5. How, according to Chapter 7, can one hemisphere's "way of being" spread across an entire civilisation and down the generations, given that brains don't evolve on a century-by-century timescale?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Through imitation — a uniquely human, empathic capacity (plus epigenetic mechanisms) that transmits cultural dispositions far faster than genetic inheritance, letting a civilisation be "re-modelled" toward one hemisphere's values within historical rather than evolutionary time. See [[wiki/08-imitation-and-evolution-of-culture|Ch.7 Imitation and the Evolution of Culture]].

### Q6. Why does McGilchrist call the Reformation a "pivotal hinge" rather than simply describing it as a swing toward one hemisphere?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Because it didn't reverse direction so much as change meaning mid-course: it began, in Luther, as an authentically right-hemisphere protest against empty ritual and a plea for inner/outer unity, but "slipped" into iconoclasm, literalism and bureaucratisation — ending up serving the left hemisphere's agenda despite its right-hemisphere origin. See [[wiki/10-renaissance-and-reformation|Ch.9 The Renaissance and the Reformation]].

### Q7. McGilchrist draws a systematic parallel between Modernist/Post-Modernist culture and the phenomenology of schizophrenia. If that parallel turned out to be superficial rather than causal, what would it do to his larger argument?

> [!tip]- Answer
> It would weaken, though not fully collapse, his case: the clinical parallel is one of several independent lines of evidence (alongside sociological, epidemiological and art-historical readings) he offers for Modernism/Post-Modernism as left-hemisphere-dominated culture, but it is the most vivid and most load-bearing one rhetorically. Without it, the epoch-by-epoch history becomes more a suggestive analogy than a mechanistically grounded claim. See [[wiki/13-the-modern-and-postmodern-worlds|Ch.12 The Modern and Post-Modern Worlds]] and [[critical_thinking|Critical Analysis]].

### Q8. Using the Roseto, Pennsylvania case, explain why McGilchrist argues the left hemisphere's project fails even by its own professed standard.

> [!tip]- Answer
> Roseto was a tight-knit Italian-immigrant community with less than half the national heart-attack rate in the 1940s despite higher risk factors; once the younger generation left and abandoned its traditional communal "folkways," the heart-attack rate rose as predicted. McGilchrist reads this as evidence that happiness and health track broad, empathic (right-hemisphere) attention and belonging, not the left hemisphere's programme of control, information, and material getting-and-using — so even judged by its own goal of maximised happiness, the left hemisphere's project is "bound to fail." See [[wiki/14-conclusion-the-master-betrayed|Conclusion: The Master Betrayed]].

### Q9. Pick a domain outside the book's own history — for instance, modern AI/agent system design. Where would McGilchrist's "left-hemisphere pattern" (context and judgment replaced by fixed, explicit, measurable procedure) show up, and what would a "right-hemisphere corrective" look like there?

> [!tip]- Answer
> There's no single correct answer — this is a transfer question. A strong answer identifies a concrete instance of context/judgment being replaced by a metric or rule (e.g. an eval score standing in for real task success, a rigid guardrail replacing situational judgment) and describes a corrective that restores contextual, embodied, or relational grounding rather than simply adding more rules. See the Conclusion's own attempt at this move in [[wiki/14-conclusion-the-master-betrayed|Conclusion: The Master Betrayed]] and [[critical_thinking|Critical Analysis]] for how directly the book's framework transfers outside its own domain.
