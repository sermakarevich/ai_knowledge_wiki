# Enterprise Case Studies

**In one sentence:** The article names nine enterprise deployments across nine industries -- Best Buy, PayPal, Tata Steel, Highmark Health, Elanco, Amdocs, Thomson Reuters, Citi Wealth, and Coca-Cola/Footballco -- as illustrations of "world-class organizations driving the ROI of AI," but only four carry any quantified outcome, all nine run on a Google Cloud product, and the section's own summary sentence contains a grammatical error in the source as published.

## Key points

- The section names nine organizations, one per industry, each paired with a specific Google Cloud product: Best Buy (retail, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform), PayPal (financial services, Gemini Enterprise), Tata Steel (manufacturing, Gemini Enterprise), Highmark Health (healthcare, Sidekick on Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform), Elanco (life sciences, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform + Gemini models), Amdocs (telecommunications, Gemini Enterprise for Customer Experience), Thomson Reuters (media & entertainment, Gemini Pro models), Citi Wealth (wealth management, underlying platform unnamed), and Coca-Cola/Footballco (consumer goods, generative image/video/voice models).
- Only four of the nine cases carry any quantified outcome: Tata Steel's "more than 300 specialized AI agents" deployed "in just nine months," Highmark Health's "$27.9 million in value in 2025 alone," Elanco's "estimated ROI of $1.9 million since launching last year," and Thomson Reuters' claim that Gemini makes some tasks "as much as 10-times faster to process."
- The two dollar figures (Highmark's $27.9M, Elanco's $1.9M) are self-reported customer claims described respectively as "value" and "estimated ROI," with no stated cost base, baseline, or measurement methodology given in the article -- neither figure is presented as a net return, and the underlying calculation is not disclosed.
- The remaining five cases -- Best Buy, PayPal, Amdocs, Citi Wealth, and Coca-Cola/Footballco -- are described only qualitatively (what was built and its intended purpose), with no number of any kind attached.
- Citi Wealth's case is distinct in tense from the others: the article describes Citi Sky as "launching," not as delivering measured results, so it is an announced capability rather than an observed outcome at time of publication.
- Every one of the nine cases runs on a Google Cloud product (Gemini Enterprise, the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, or Gemini models), which makes this section a vendor-curated set of customer references rather than an independently sampled or representative subset of the 2,403-executive survey discussed elsewhere in the article.
- The section's own framing sentence -- "By moving past isolated pilots and embedding AI directly into core operations, they are experience real change in revenue and operations" -- contains a grammatical error ("they are experience") that is reproduced verbatim from the published article and marked `(sic -- as published)` in the source transcription.
- The nine cases span markedly different task types (review summarization, decision-layer infrastructure, agent fleets, research-protocol automation, pharmacovigilance/order workflows, customer-experience agents, document-processing model selection, client-facing always-on assistants, and generative content production), so the set illustrates breadth of applicability rather than a comparable, apples-to-apples outcome metric.

## Per-case detail

### Retail -- Best Buy

Best Buy uses the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform "to condense thousands of product reviews into concise insights that help customers make informed buying decisions." The use case is customer-facing content synthesis rather than internal operations. No quantified result is given.

### Financial services -- PayPal

PayPal is "using Gemini Enterprise to build the intelligent, data-driven decision layer that powers smarter, more personalized customer experiences at scale." The article describes this as infrastructure -- a decision layer -- rather than a single application, and gives no quantified result.

### Manufacturing -- Tata Steel

Tata Steel is described as "rapidly scaling autonomous capabilities across its vast global organization," having deployed "a fleet of more than 300 specialized AI agents built on Gemini Enterprise in just nine months to drive efficiency and precision across its global operations." This is the only case where the quantified figure is a count and a timeline (agents deployed, time elapsed) rather than a dollar value or speed multiplier -- it does not state a cost saving, revenue effect, or ROI figure.

### Healthcare -- Highmark Health

Highmark Health "scaled employee impact with Sidekick, an AI assistant built on the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, that has delivered $27.9 million in value in 2025 alone by automating research protocols and providing 'AI search proxies' for internal teams." The $27.9 million figure is labeled "value," not profit, savings, or ROI, and the article does not state what costs (platform, integration, staff time) are netted against it or how "value" was calculated.

### Life sciences -- Elanco

Elanco "is using Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform and Gemini models to implement a framework supporting critical business processes, such as pharmacovigilance and customer orders, resulting in an estimated ROI of $1.9 million since launching last year." Unlike Highmark's figure, this one is explicitly labeled "estimated ROI," but the article gives no cost base, investment figure, or calculation method against which that estimate was derived.

### Telecommunications -- Amdocs

Amdocs "used Gemini Enterprise for Customer Experience to create its Telco Customer Experience Agent, which can reason, orchestrate, and execute end-to-end telco processes that improve resolution speed, cost efficiency, and customer satisfaction." Three improvement dimensions are named (resolution speed, cost efficiency, customer satisfaction) but none is quantified.

### Media & entertainment -- Thomson Reuters

Thomson Reuters "added Gemini Pro models to its suite of approved models specifically because Gemini makes some tasks as much as 10-times faster to process, and it can process entire documents in context." This is the only non-dollar quantified claim among the nine cases, and it is phrased as a ceiling ("as much as") rather than a typical or average speedup, applying to "some tasks," not a stated share of Thomson Reuters' workload.

### Wealth management -- Citi Wealth

Citi Wealth "is launching Citi Sky, an always-on AI-Powered member for the Citi Wealth team that brings Citi's global intelligence to clients' fingertips through the Citi app." The verb tense ("is launching") distinguishes this case from the other eight: it is presented as a rollout in progress at time of publication, not as a deployment with observed results. No quantified result, and none implied, is given.

### Consumer goods -- Coca-Cola and Footballco

Coca-Cola and Footballco "use advanced image generation, cinematic video synthesis and high-fidelity voice modeling -- combined with human creativity -- to deliver real-time, social-first entertainment at scale for their global content series to reach football fans during the world's biggest tournament." This is the only case naming two organizations jointly and the only one centered on generative content production rather than internal operations, customer service, or decision infrastructure. No quantified result is given.

## Observations about the set as a whole

- **Sparse quantification.** Of nine named cases, five (Best Buy, PayPal, Amdocs, Citi Wealth, Coca-Cola/Footballco) carry no number at all. The remaining four each carry exactly one kind of figure: a deployment count and timeline (Tata Steel), two dollar "value"/"ROI" figures with no cost base (Highmark Health, Elanco), and a speed multiplier (Thomson Reuters).
- **Self-reported dollar figures without methodology.** Highmark Health's $27.9 million is called "value"; Elanco's $1.9 million is called "estimated ROI." Neither figure is accompanied by a stated investment, baseline, or calculation method in the article, so neither can be read as a net financial return -- they are customer-reported headline numbers, not audited or independently verified results.
- **Single-vendor sample.** Every one of the nine cases is built on a Google Cloud product -- Gemini Enterprise, the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, or Gemini models. This makes the section a set of Google Cloud customer references cited by a Google Cloud VP on the Google Cloud blog, not an independently drawn sample of enterprise AI deployments across vendors.
- **Announced vs. measured.** Eight of the nine cases describe something already built or delivering results ("delivered," "resulting in," "used to create," "added"). Citi Wealth is the exception: Citi Sky "is launching," placing it in a different evidentiary category -- an announcement of capability rather than a report of a completed deployment or measured outcome.
- **Published wording error.** The section's introductory sentence reads, as published: "By moving past isolated pilots and embedding AI directly into core operations, they are experience real change in revenue and operations." The phrase "they are experience real change" is grammatically incorrect in the source article and is reproduced here verbatim (marked `sic` in the underlying source transcription) rather than silently corrected.
