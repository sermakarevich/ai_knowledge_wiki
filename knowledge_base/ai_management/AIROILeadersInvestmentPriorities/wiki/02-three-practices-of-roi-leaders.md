# The Three Practices of AI ROI Leaders

**In one sentence:** Among the AI ROI Leader subgroup (the 26% of surveyed organizations with accelerating AI returns), the survey found three recurring practices -- clear ownership, embedding AI into workflows, and mandated AI fluency -- that co-occur with faster returns and, alongside them, 86% of all surveyed executives report AI is driving cost-efficient growth rather than proportional cost increases.

## Key points

- The three practices are measured within the AI ROI Leader subgroup only (the 26% with accelerating returns), not across the full sample of 2,403 executives -- the percentages below describe how common each practice is inside that subgroup, not how many of all respondents have it.
- Clear ownership: 48% of AI ROI Leaders institute "extremely clear" ownership and decision-making authority for AI and agentic initiatives.
- Embed AI into workflows: almost half of AI ROI Leaders said they have "embedded AI into core business processes and revenue streams" or that "AI is enabling new business models or revenue opportunities" -- the article gives this as a qualitative "almost half," with no exact percentage published.
- Mandate AI fluency: 38% of AI ROI Leaders have "comprehensive, ongoing AI capability development embedded into roles with required training," e.g. ongoing skilling programs and constant investment in the current workforce.
- The article presents these three practices as things AI ROI Leaders "consistently use" and as correlates of accelerating returns -- it does not report a causal test isolating each practice's individual effect on ROI.
- The article's interpretation ("What does this all mean?") frames the pattern as: leaders set a clear point of view on how AI should change work, then follow with resources and training so employees actually use it, paired with accountability.
- Two worked examples illustrate this: a finance leader sharing the best AI agents for streamlining purchase-order approvals, and an HR leader synthesizing dense manuals and benefit plans into immediate answers for employees.
- Separately from the AI ROI Leader subgroup figures, 86% of the full 2,403-executive sample said AI is driving cost-efficient growth -- scaling revenue or output without a proportional increase in operating costs.

## The three practices

The article frames this section as findings specific to the AI ROI Leaders -- "those 26% of organizations who are seeing accelerating financial returns from AI." All three percentages below are shares of that subgroup, not of the full 2,403-executive sample.

| Practice | Figure (within AI ROI Leaders) | Exact survey wording (as quoted in the article) |
|---|---|---|
| Clear ownership | 48% | "extremely clear" ownership and decision-making authority for AI and agentic initiatives |
| Embed AI into workflows | Almost half (no exact number given) | have "embedded AI into core business processes and revenue streams" or "AI is enabling new business models or revenue opportunities" |
| Mandate AI fluency | 38% | "comprehensive, ongoing AI capability development embedded into roles with required training" |

### 1. Clear ownership

48% of AI ROI Leaders institute "extremely clear" ownership and decision-making authority for AI and agentic initiatives. This is the strongest form of the survey's ownership-clarity option -- the article does not report what share picked weaker gradations (e.g. "somewhat clear"), nor does it report this figure for the full sample or for organizations outside the AI ROI Leader subgroup.

### 2. Embed AI into workflows

Almost half of AI ROI Leaders said they have "embedded AI into core business processes and revenue streams" or that "AI is enabling new business models or revenue opportunities." The article reports this as a qualitative "almost half," not as a specific percentage -- no exact number is given in the source for this practice, and none should be inferred.

### 3. Mandate AI fluency

38% of AI ROI Leaders have "comprehensive, ongoing AI capability development embedded into roles with required training." The article's example of what this looks like in practice: ongoing skilling programs and constant investment in the current workforce.

## What does this all mean? (the article's interpretation)

The article's own synthesis: "The successful AI leader establishes very clear POVs on how AI can be used to transform the way work is done, followed by the necessary resources and training so employees can put it to use." Two examples are given to make this concrete:

- A finance leader may share the best AI agents for streamlining purchase-order approvals.
- An HR leader presents effective ways to synthesize massive volumes of dense manuals and benefit plans to provide immediate answers to employees.

The article adds that these leaders "then provide ongoing training and enablement and a focus on improving both technology and processes alongside accountability" -- i.e., the pattern is POV-setting followed by sustained enablement and accountability, not a one-time rollout.

Note on evidentiary framing: the article states that AI ROI Leaders "consistently use the same set of practices to achieve this success" and lists the three practices as what these leaders are "doing to drive cost-effective growth." This is a correlational finding presented within a self-selected high-performing subgroup (those already reporting accelerating returns); the article does not describe a controlled comparison or causal mechanism linking any one practice to the returns.

## The cost-efficient-growth result

Distinct from the AI ROI Leader subgroup figures above, the article reports a full-sample result: 86% of the 2,400 executives surveyed said that AI is driving cost-efficient growth, helping scale revenue or output without proportional increases in operating costs. This figure applies to the whole surveyed population (n approx. 2,400 / 2,403), not just to AI ROI Leaders.
