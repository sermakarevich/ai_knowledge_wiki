# Workers Delos — Autonomous AI Workers, 24/7

**Article:** [Delos site](https://delos.so/) — Delos company + product + worker-profile pages (35 pages, fetched 2026-09-16)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine staffing your company with teammates who never sleep, already know tools like Slack, Gmail, Figma, and GitHub, and remember exactly how you like things done — that is the Delos pitch. Instead of a chatbot that only answers questions, you hire ready-made digital workers for marketing, coding, design, finance, HR, support, and data, connect them to your apps in a few clicks, and hand them whole jobs to finish on their own, like onboarding a tireless colleague in under five minutes. There is also a full AI office suite with the best-known AI models plus a browser companion that follows you across the web and your inbox, all wrapped in European-style privacy promises so your data stays locked up and never trains outside models.

## TL;DR

Delos (delos.so) is a European company selling specialized autonomous AI workers that act like real teammates with their own personality, skills, and persistent memory of preferences, templates, and interaction history, running end to end 24/7 across Slack, Microsoft Teams, and 3,000+ tools. The catalog is advertised as 15 specialized profiles (the wiki captures 17 named worker pages) across marketing, development, design, data, HR, finance, and customer support, with over 10,000 workers claimed deployed and 200+ documented use cases with recommended workers, time savings, and ROI (return on investment) estimates. The platform combines an AI office suite (Student 10, Explore 25, Advanced 45, Premium 80 euros per month, plus a per-Worker hire fee and custom Enterprise plan, all commitment-free with a free trial), native no-code integrations (Gmail, Outlook, Notion, Google Drive, Confluence, HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Jira, Linear, GitHub, GitLab, Asana, Trello, Figma, Canva, and more via OAuth — a standard secure login method — plus webhooks/API), secure RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation, where the AI looks up your documents before acting) over company data, multi-agent orchestration over open standards like MCP (Model Context Protocol), and automatic model selection across the GPT, Claude, and Gemini families. Trust packaging includes end-to-end encryption, isolated per-tenant environments, granular revocable permissions with optional human approval on sensitive actions, full audit logging, no training on customer data, GDPR (EU privacy law) compliance, and SOC 2 (independent security audit) certification. Around the core sit team-building (1–15 workers that share context instead of working in silos), a clone-yourself digital twin trained on your emails, messages, and docs, a Chrome/Edge companion extension for writing help and page-aware chat, a solutions layer per function, and a blog of field reports and compliance guides.

## Problem & Motivation

The core motivation presented across the 35 pages is that generic chatbots do not fit how businesses actually operate, because they produce text while humans still carry the work into other systems and finish it. Teams already coordinate in Slack or Teams and execute across mail, docs, CRM (customer relationship management), development, and design tools, so an assistant that cannot touch those systems adds friction rather than removing it. Delos therefore frames its offering as workers embedded directly in existing processes: they reach company data securely at the moment of acting, remember context across interactions instead of starting over each chat, coordinate with each other (for example marketing briefing design, then SEO — search engine optimization — polishing the result), and complete missions end to end while reporting through familiar channels. The surrounding pages extend the same logic to scaling (add workers in a launch spike, remove them when volume drops, with no hiring admin), to personal leverage (a clone that answers and drafts in your tone), and to ubiquity (an office suite and browser companion so the AI is present wherever work happens), always paired with the claim that every automation stays traceable, controllable, and human-validated on important decisions.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Teammate-like AI workers with personality, skills, and memory.** Each worker is defined not as a prompt box but as an autonomous agent with a role-calibrated tone and style, specialized skills, and persistent per-tenant memory of preferences, history, templates, and processes, so it can run tasks end to end 24/7 and behave like a genuine team member — for instance James talking ROI (return on investment) before creativity, Laura citing PR (pull request) numbers and proposing diffs, or Sophie reasoning in visual systems and accessibility.

2. **Breadth-first integration as the hands of the worker.** The product's reach comes from living inside Slack, Teams, Gmail, Outlook, Notion, Google Drive, Confluence, HubSpot, Salesforce, Jira, GitHub, Figma, and 3,000+ tools via no-code OAuth setup in under five minutes with granular read/write scope, so the same worker can monitor channels, enrich CRM contacts, open tickets, comment on code, update knowledge bases, and escalate to humans without forcing the team onto a new tool.

3. **Office suite plus best-model routing and grounded execution.** Delos packages everyday AI applications (analyze documents, prepare meetings, build presentations, research the web, build digital products) with continuous internal benchmarking that auto-switches between the GPT, Claude, and Gemini model families per task type, grounded by RAG retrieval over Notion, Drive, Confluence, and Slack at the moment of acting to cut hallucinations, and executed in isolated environments with logged tool calls and optional human approval gates.

4. **Team assembly, cloning, and companion ubiquity.** Beyond single hires, customers can build 1-to-15-worker teams whose shared memory breaks silos (marketing sees support tickets, finance receives sales signals), train a clone-yourself twin from emails, Slack messages, docs, and brand guidelines for replies, drafts, and social presence in their own style, and carry Delos everywhere via a Chrome/Edge companion extension for inbox rewriting, instant translation, and page-aware chat with favorite AI models.

5. **Enterprise-style trust packaging for a self-serve product.** The pitch pairs a commitment-free free trial with add-or-remove-anytime scaling and full user control over permissions against strong compliance assurances — end-to-end encryption, isolated hosting, never using customer data for model training, GDPR compliance, and SOC 2 certification — designed to lower both adoption friction and compliance objections from startups to large enterprises.

## Key Findings

The use-cases catalog claims 200+ entries filterable by role, tool, and autonomy level (supervised versus autonomous), each naming a recommended worker, automated tasks, time savings, measured ROI, and integration prerequisites, with an explicit land-and-expand rollout of starting from one high-impact low-effort case and widening coverage over weeks.

Pricing and packaging center on the Delos Office Suite at four monthly tiers — Student 10 euros, Explore 25 euros, Advanced 45 euros, Premium 80 euros — plus a per-Worker hire fee whose figure scraped as "0" and must be confirmed on the live page, plus a custom Enterprise plan, all with no minimum commitment and scale-up, scale-down, or cancel anytime. ## Suggestions & Future Directions

The natural next step implied by the source is the prospect funnel itself: pick a role-matching profile from the catalog or a tender/bid specialist like Camille, connect the tools that role needs, hand off one high-impact low-effort mission, measure against the promised savings, and then expand toward a multi-worker team or a personal clone under continued permission control and human validation. Open questions left unanswered by the wiki material include the exact per-Worker price beyond the suite tiers, the full list and depth of all 3,000-plus connectors, how sandboxing, review queues, and approval gates behave when workers act autonomously in sensitive billing, legal, or production systems, which model and agent architecture actually serve each task behind the auto-switching claim, what measured customer evidence sits behind the 10,000-worker and 200-use-case figures, and how the catalog reconciles its 15-profile headline with 17 named worker pages and occasionally mismatched role descriptions.
