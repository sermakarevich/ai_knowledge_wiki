# Study Characteristics, Tools and Strategies (RQ0 Summary and RQ1 Start)

**In one sentence:** The field is almost entirely post-ChatGPT (35/39 studies), concentrated in SE/CS venues, and methodologically dominated by laboratory experiments (38%) triangulated through mixed methods with heavy reliance on self-reported data (90%).

## Key points

- 35 out of 39 primary studies (90%) were published after the release of ChatGPT in November 2022; only 4 studies (2014–2022) precede it and rely on pre-LLM paradigms.
- Authorship is fragmented: 147 out of 154 authors contributed a single publication, while only 7 authors published two or more papers.
- By venue, 18 studies appeared in Software Engineering and Computer Science venues, followed by 7 in Human-Computer Interaction venues.
- Laboratory experiments are the most common research strategy at 38% (15/39), used to isolate LLM-assistant effects on specific tasks in controlled environments.
- Field studies are second at 23% (9/39), prioritizing ecological validity by observing developers in real-world settings without researcher intervention; sample studies (large-scale surveys) account for 15% (6/39).
- Less frequent strategies: experimental simulations 13% (5/39), field experiments 5% (2/39), and judgment studies 5% (2/39, structured expert opinion via interviews/questionnaires).
- 90% of studies (35/39) leverage self-reported data (surveys, interviews); 41% (16/39) use experimental settings; 69% (27/39) adopt a mixed-method approach, most commonly user experiment paired with survey (10 studies).
- Research objectives are balanced rather than conclusive: 59% (23/39) formative (exploring/refining tools) vs 41% (16/39) summative (evaluating effectiveness/impact); analysis is 67% mixed quantitative+qualitative (26/39), 21% qualitative-only (8/39), 13% quantitative-only (5/39).

## RQ1 — Distribution of the research strategies

Studies classified using the Stol and Fitzgerald taxonomy, which distinguishes strategies by obtrusiveness (control) and generalizability (realism):

| Strategy | Primary studies | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| Field Study | [54, 56, 63, 64, 66, 76, 82, 85, 86] | 23% |
| Field Experiment | [79, 88] | 5% |
| Experimental Simulation | [68, 70, 71, 77, 78] | 13% |
| Laboratory Experiment | [50, 51, 52, 55, 57, 59, 61, 62, 67, 69, 72, 73, 74, 84, 87] | 38% |
| Sample Study | [53, 58, 60, 65, 75, 81] | 15% |
| Judgment Study | [80, 83] | 5% |

- Laboratory experiments (38%, 15/39): "rely on controlled environments to isolate the effects of LLM-assistants on specific development tasks."
- Field studies (23%, 9/39): "prioritize ecological validity by observing developer behavior in real-world settings without the need for researcher intervention."
- Sample studies (15%, 6/39): "typically large-scale surveys, they aim to capture broad trends and perceptions across diverse developer populations."
- Field experiments (5%, 2/39): occur in real-world settings but "researchers actively manipulate specific variables such as changes to a tool or process to evaluate their effects within the natural context."
- Experimental simulations (13%, 5/39): "combine elements from both laboratory and field studies by replicating real-world scenarios within controlled environments to study certain phenomena under realistic but simulated conditions."
- Judgment studies (5%, 2/39): "involve collecting expert opinions in a structured manner in a series of interviews and questionnaires."

## RQ1 — Distribution of the research procedures

Taxonomy adapted from Glass, Vessey, and Ramesh; one or more procedures per study:

| Procedure | Primary studies | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| Survey | [50, 51, 52, 54, 56, 57, 58, 60, 61, 62, 63, 64, 65, 66, 67, 68, 69, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74, 75, 76, 79, 80, 81, 84, 85, 86, 87, 88] | 82% |
| User Experiment | [50, 51, 52, 55, 57, 61, 62, 67, 69, 71, 72, 73, 74, 78, 84, 87] | 41% |
| Concept Implementation (Proof of Concept) | [50, 59, 77, 88] | 10% |
| Interview | [52, 64, 70, 74, 75, 78, 80, 82, 83, 86] | 26% |
| Case Study | [51, 52, 53, 63, 65, 70, 75, 76, 77, 83, 85, 86] | 31% |

- 90% (35/39) leverage self-reported data such as surveys and interviews; 41% (16/39) are conducted in experimental settings (user studies, controlled or quasi-experiments).
- User experiments are almost exclusively associated with the laboratory experiment strategy (Fig. 3).
- 69% (27/39) adopt a mixed-method approach (Fig. 4); the most common pairing is user experiment + survey in 10 studies, "frequently used to triangulate self-reported perceptions (e.g., user experience or satisfaction) with measured performance metrics."
- Study objectives (Hartson et al. taxonomy): 59% (23/39) formative — "exploring, refining, or improving a process, tool, or methodology" — vs 41% (16/39) summative — "drawing conclusions about the effectiveness, outcomes, or impact of a completed process, tool, or methodology."
- Data analysis: 67% (26/39) mix quantitative and qualitative, 21% (8/39) qualitative-only, 13% (5/39) quantitative-only.
