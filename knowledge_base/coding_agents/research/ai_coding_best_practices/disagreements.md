# Disagreements — ai_coding_best_practices (coding_agents)

Focus: What are the evidence-backed best practices for using AI coding assistants for writing, debugging, and refactoring code?

Scope: this file is drawn ONLY from `topics/*/digest.md` in this research folder and the source summaries those digests link. Two real (directional, same-question) contradictions were found. The six recovered sources added in the 2026-09-23 update were screened against the same bar and produced no new directional contradiction; notes on where they land are appended under "Explicitly not counted". Within-study moderators (e.g. a prompt technique helping one model but harming another) and differences of emphasis (e.g. multi-agent overhead framed as justified cost vs validation time framed as a tax) are not counted.

## AI assistance improves code quality (fewer defects) vs AI assistance degrades code quality (more subtle errors and harder-to-maintain code)

- Side A — quality improves: the ANZ Bank controlled crossover experiment on GitHub Copilot reports statistically significantly fewer bugs (p = 0.033) and fewer code smells (p = 0.007) with Copilot on twelve short Python algorithmic challenges, plus a non-significant 12.86% uplift in unit-test success ratio — [[research_topics/coding_agents/ImpactOfAiToolOnEngineeringAtAnzBank/summary|Copilot at ANZ Bank]].
- Side B — quality degrades: the PRISMA-guided review of 90 in-IDE studies reports an efficiency–correctness trade-off in which acceleration comes with subtle errors, insecure patterns, and harder-to-maintain code (overly concise structures, unconventional naming, missing comments) — [[research_topics/coding_agents/HumanAiExperienceInIntegratedDevelopmentEnvironments/summary|In-IDE HAX review]]. Production-grounded mining corroborates the risk side: 27.3% of committed Copilot/CodeWhisperer/Codeium snippets contain confirmed weaknesses (~3 each across 43 CWE types) — [[research_topics/coding_agents/SecurityWeaknessesOfCopilotGeneratedCodeInGitHubProjects/summary|Security weaknesses of Copilot code]] — and the Copilot survey cites 44% insecure outputs across 89 lab scenarios — [[research_topics/coding_agents/GithubCopilotProductivitySecurity/summary|Copilot productivity and security survey]].
- Likely reason: different setting and different metric. Side A measures SonarQube bug/smell counts on short, atomic, well-specified Python puzzles where scaffolding helps; Side B synthesizes correctness, readability, maintainability, and security judgments across diverse professional tasks, including complex and security-sensitive work where assistants struggle with context-specific nuances.
- Which side the evidence favours: **unresolved / context-dependent**. The 39-study productivity review explicitly leaves quality unresolved and context-dependent, while itself citing isolated wins (e.g. defect reductions in a Copilot case study) alongside persistent correctness risks — [[research_topics/coding_agents/ImpactOfLLMAssistantsOnSoftwareDeveloperProductivity/summary|LLM assistants and productivity review]]. Best-supported reading: assistants reduce surface defects on small well-scoped tasks but increase subtle-error and maintainability risk on complex work; net quality claims must be conditioned on task, not stated universally.

## Experienced developers gain the most vs beginners/novices gain the most

- Side A — experts gain most: the 90-study in-IDE review finds productivity gains strongest among experienced users, with marginal gains on proprietary or highly complex logic, and notes professionals verify output better while novices over-trust it — [[research_topics/coding_agents/HumanAiExperienceInIntegratedDevelopmentEnvironments/summary|In-IDE HAX review]].
- Side B — beginners gain most: the ANZ crossover finds the largest time savings for beginners (52.27%, 20.07 → 9.58 min/problem) versus ~41% for intermediates and ~40% for advanced engineers — [[research_topics/coding_agents/ImpactOfAiToolOnEngineeringAtAnzBank/summary|Copilot at ANZ Bank]] — and the Copilot survey synthesis likewise reports beginners showing the largest relative gains — [[research_topics/coding_agents/GithubCopilotProductivitySecurity/summary|Copilot productivity and security survey]].
- Likely reason: different metric and different setting. Side B measures relative time reduction on short algorithmic puzzles (a metric that favours low baselines); Side A synthesizes effective throughput across professional work where verification, proprietary context, and complex logic dominate. Confounders include self-reported proficiency, volunteer enthusiasm, and small samples (median 17 in HAX research).
- Which side the evidence favours: **unresolved as a universal claim; reconciled form favoured**. The evidence jointly supports: novices show the largest *relative* speedups on routine, well-specified tasks but carry the highest over-reliance and skill-erosion risk, while experts realize larger *net* gains on complex work because they verify better. Both the code-generation digest and the underlying reviews describe this as double-edged rather than one-sided.

## Explicitly not counted

- Security-aware prompting helping newer GPT models while harming GPT-3.5-turbo (prefix pe-03-a −47–56% on GPT-4o-mini/GPT-4o, backfiring on GPT-3.5-turbo) is a documented within-study model moderator — [[research_topics/coding_agents/BenchmarkingPromptEngineeringTechniquesForSecureCodeGeneration/summary|Secure-code prompting]] — not a cross-source contradiction.
- Prompt-framing effects appearing on algorithmic benchmarks (LiveCodeBench) but vanishing on real-world maintenance tasks (SWE-bench Verified), with model choice dominating throughout, is a task-contingent effect reported inside one study — [[research_topics/coding_agents/InfluenceTacticsPromptFraming/summary|Prompt framing]] — not a contradiction with the secure-code prompting findings.
- Multi-agent context-engineering overhead presented as justified versus validation time presented as a productivity tax is a difference of cost framing across settings (5-task repo pilot vs broad review synthesis), not opposing factual claims — [[research_topics/coding_agents/ContextEngineeringForMultiAgentLLMCodeAssistantsUsingElicitNotebookLMChatGPTAndClaudeCode/summary|Context engineering]].
- Zoominfo's ~20% enterprise time savings versus 40–55% controlled-study speedups is a setting moderator (production measurement net of review overhead vs task-timed experiments), not a contradiction — [[research_topics/coding_agents/ExperienceWithGithubCopilotForDeveloper/summary|Zoominfo Copilot]].
- StarCoder2 out-cleaning developers on smells (44.36% vs 24.27%) while passing far fewer tests (57% Pass@5 vs 100%) looks two-directional but answers two different questions (transformation depth vs correctness preservation), and its task-contingent split (surface vs structural) reinforces the quality-is-context-dependent resolution rather than contradicting either side — [[research_topics/coding_agents/LlmCodeRefactoringCapability/summary|RefactoringCapability]].
- DebugRepair, PracRepair, and the PracAPR-vision paper all agreeing that runtime evidence plus iteration beats one-shot static patching is a three-way replication, not a disagreement — [[research_topics/coding_agents/DebugRepair/summary|DebugRepair]].
