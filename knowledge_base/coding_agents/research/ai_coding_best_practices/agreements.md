# Agreements — AI coding best practices

Focus: What are the evidence-backed best practices for using AI coding assistants for writing, debugging, and refactoring code?

Each section below is a claim supported by two or more independent sources (different teams, methods, or samples — not one study repeated through a survey that cites it). Sections are ordered from strongest to weakest joint support. Contested net judgments (overall quality direction, novice net-benefit, prompting as first-order lever, validation-cost framing, proactive-vs-passive review) are excluded; see disagreements.md.

## AI generation speeds routine drafting but imposes a large verification tax

Backed by: [[research_topics/coding_agents/ImpactOfAiToolOnEngineeringAtAnzBank/summary|Copilot at ANZ Bank]] (six-week crossover experiment, 100+ bank engineers, Python algorithmic tasks, ~42% faster with Wilcoxon p = 0.001); [[research_topics/coding_agents/ExperienceWithGithubCopilotForDeveloper/summary|Copilot at Zoominfo]] (400+ developers, 26 production days, 33% suggestion / 20% line acceptance, ~20% time savings, 72% satisfaction); [[research_topics/coding_agents/HumanAiExperienceInIntegratedDevelopmentEnvironments/summary|Human-AI experience in IDEs]] (PRISMA review of 90 in-IDE studies: gains plus verification/rework consuming up to 50% of assisted time); [[research_topics/coding_agents/ImpactOfLLMAssistantsOnSoftwareDeveloperProductivity/summary|LLM assistants and productivity review]] (39-study systematic review: 21–45% controlled efficiency gains, majority of assisted time spent verifying/refining); [[research_topics/coding_agents/GithubCopilotProductivitySecurity/summary|Copilot productivity and security survey]] (selective synthesis: ~55% faster completion, gains concentrated in simple routine work); [[research_topics/coding_agents/DemystifyingPracticesChallengesGithubCopilot/summary|Demystifying Copilot practices]] and [[research_topics/coding_agents/PracticesAndChallengesOfUsingGitHubCopilot/summary|Practices and challenges of using GitHub Copilot]] (community mining: faster development / useful generation as top-cited benefit).

Strength: strongest in the set — two systematic reviews plus a controlled crossover experiment with significance testing plus a large enterprise deployment plus two independent community-mining studies, all measuring the same pattern (faster drafting paired with review/rework cost) with different instruments (timers, telemetry, surveys, post mining). Holds in controlled tasks and in production; gains shrink on complex, domain-specific, or security-sensitive logic, where validation overhead offsets much of the saving.

## Committed AI-generated code carries material inherited security risk

Backed by: [[research_topics/coding_agents/SecurityWeaknessesOfCopilotGeneratedCodeInGitHubProjects/summary|Security weaknesses of Copilot code]] (733 committed Python/JS snippets, dual analyzers plus manual CWE adjudication at Kappa ≥ 0.82: 27.3% vulnerable, ~3 flaws each across 43 CWE types led by CWE-330/94/79/78, ~37% of instances in the CWE Top-25); [[research_topics/coding_agents/GithubCopilotProductivitySecurity/summary|Copilot productivity and security survey]] (cites Pearce et al.: 44% insecure across 89 scenarios spanning 25 CWEs; Fu et al.: 24.5% of JS snippets across 38 CWE categories); [[research_topics/coding_agents/HumanAiExperienceInIntegratedDevelopmentEnvironments/summary|Human-AI experience in IDEs]] (up to 36% of vulnerabilities in AI-assisted code traced to replicated insecure training-data patterns).

Strength: strong — production-grounded mining with dual-scanner plus manual adjudication converges with independent lab scenario studies and a 90-study review on the same mechanism (training-data weaknesses resurfacing in suggestions) and the same order of magnitude (roughly a quarter to two-fifths affected). Holds for Copilot-style assistants in Python/JavaScript; absolute rates are scanner- and sample-dependent, so treat them as relative signals that make human oversight plus testing and static analysis non-optional.

## Debugging correctness comes from runtime evidence beyond static code and error messages

Backed by: [[research_topics/coding_agents/AiDrivenAdvancementsAutomatedProgramRepairCodeGeneration/summary|AComprehensiveSurveyOfAIDrivenAdvancementsAndTechniquesInAutomatedProgramRepairAndCodeGeneration]] (survey of 27 papers crediting neural fault localization, generated tests, and multi-modal context); [[research_topics/coding_agents/PracRepair/summary|PracRepairLLMEmpoweredAutomatedProgramRepairInspiredByHumanLikeDebuggingPractices]] (Defects4J experiments: dynamic traces alone worth +19 correct fixes by ablation, statement-level traces with values and branch outcomes); [[research_topics/coding_agents/DebugRepair/summary|DebugRepairEnhancingLLMBasedAutomatedProgramRepairViaSelfDirectedDebugging]] (Defects4J/QuixBugs: print-trace runtime states fixing Chart-24/Lang-6 style misdiagnoses; removing debugging drops 224 to 165 correct); [[research_topics/coding_agents/TowardsPracticalAndUsefulAutomatedProgramRepairForDebugging/summary|TowardsPracticalAndUsefulAutomatedProgramRepairForDebugging]] (vision paper: augmented prompts with inputs, callees, and traces fixing a Chart_3 misdiagnosis that shallow prompts get wrong).

Strength: strong within debugging — one secondary synthesis plus three independent primary frameworks (question-driven diagnosis, self-directed print debugging, IDE-integrated vision) demonstrating the same lesson with ablations and case studies. Holds in controlled Defects4J-family tasks, mostly Java; the survey ranges over bug classes but stays at taxonomy level.

## Iterative feedback-driven repair loops beat single-shot patching

Backed by: [[research_topics/coding_agents/PracRepair/summary|PracRepairLLMEmpoweredAutomatedProgramRepairInspiredByHumanLikeDebuggingPractices]] (89 correct with no refinement rising to 107/119/139 over three compile-plus-test rounds); [[research_topics/coding_agents/DebugRepair/summary|DebugRepairEnhancingLLMBasedAutomatedProgramRepairViaSelfDirectedDebugging]] (hierarchical debugging sessions with patch augmentation: 224 Defects4J on GPT-3.5, 295 on DeepSeek-V3); [[research_topics/coding_agents/TowardsPracticalAndUsefulAutomatedProgramRepairForDebugging/summary|TowardsPracticalAndUsefulAutomatedProgramRepairForDebugging]] (ROSE user study: +44% task success, −16.5% debugging time); [[research_topics/coding_agents/AiDrivenAdvancementsAutomatedProgramRepairCodeGeneration/summary|AComprehensiveSurveyOfAIDrivenAdvancementsAndTechniquesInAutomatedProgramRepairAndCodeGeneration]] (feedback loops and interactive debugging with human help as the field trend).

Strength: strong — quantified round-by-round gains and ablations in two head-to-head primary experiments plus a user study plus survey-level trend agreement, all on shared suites (Defects4J, QuixBugs; HumanEval/DebugBench as peer harnesses). Holds under perfect or test-free fault localization in benchmark settings; budgets used were up to 3 refinement rounds / 32 patches.

## Specific diagnostic prompts outperform bare, vague, or coercive ones — conditionally

Backed by: [[research_topics/coding_agents/SecurityWeaknessesOfCopilotGeneratedCodeInGitHubProjects/summary|Security weaknesses of Copilot code]] (warning-fed enhanced prompt fixes 55.5% vs 31.8% basic vs 19.3% bare `/fix`); [[research_topics/coding_agents/BenchmarkingPromptEngineeringTechniquesForSecureCodeGeneration/summary|Benchmarking prompt engineering for secure code]] (security-aware prefix cuts scanner-agreed vulnerabilities ~47–56% on GPT-4o-mini/GPT-4o; one self-critique-and-fix iteration removes ~25–65% depending on model); [[research_topics/coding_agents/InfluenceTacticsPromptFraming/summary|Influence tactics in prompt framing]] (~180k generations: Neutral beats Pressure framings on correctness; pressure adds security warnings; model choice and task difficulty dominate); [[research_topics/coding_agents/OnTheRobustnessOfCodeGenerationTechniques/summary|On the robustness of code generation techniques]] (46% output change on semantically equivalent paraphrases; ~28% of passing methods reachable only one way).

Strength: moderate-strong — four independent controlled experiments converging on direction (diagnostic/contextual helps, vague or pressuring hurts) but disagreeing on magnitude, which is model- and task-dependent (prefix backfires on GPT-3.5-turbo; framing effects vanish on SWE-bench maintenance tasks). Holds as a relative nudge for security posture, tone, and documentation — not a reliability guarantee; clear description wording is itself a first-order correctness practice.

## Real-world use concentrates on JavaScript/Python in mainstream IDEs, where integration friction dominates complaints

Backed by: [[research_topics/coding_agents/DemystifyingPracticesChallengesGithubCopilot/summary|Demystifying Copilot practices]] (303 SO posts + 927 discussions: JS/Python ~one-fifth each, VS Code 48.0%, integration difficulty 28.1% and access difficulty 17.0% as top limitations); [[research_topics/coding_agents/PracticesAndChallengesOfUsingGitHubCopilot/summary|Practices and challenges of using GitHub Copilot]] (earlier independent mining of 169 SO + 655 discussions: same JS/Python concentration, 49.0% useful-generation benefit, too-few-suggestions and file-growth/privacy complaints); [[research_topics/coding_agents/ExperienceWithGithubCopilotForDeveloper/summary|Copilot at Zoominfo]] (top four languages ~80% of suggestions at ~30% acceptance; weaker rates on HTML/CSS/JSON/SQL; JetBrains vs VS Code volume split).

Strength: moderate — two independent community-mining studies by overlapping author teams but non-overlapping samples and time windows converging on the same usage map, corroborated by enterprise telemetry on language concentration. Holds for Copilot-style assistants circa 2022–2024; observational and self-report-based, so it describes where value concentrates, not where value must concentrate.

## LLMs complement rather than replace humans on refactoring and review

Backed by: [[research_topics/coding_agents/LlmCodeRefactoringCapability/summary|AnEmpiricalStudyOnTheCodeRefactoringCapabilityOfLargeLanguageModels]] (leakage-controlled paired comparison, 30 Java projects: StarCoder2 cuts smells 44.36% vs 24.27% for developers on test-passing outputs but passes only 57.15% at Pass@5 vs 100%; wins 10/16 smell types, dominating syntactic within-class work while losing architecture-sensitive smells and dependency-propagating moves); [[research_topics/coding_agents/FromRestructuringToStabilization/summary|FromRestructuringToStabilizationALargeScaleExperimentOnIterativeCodeReadabilityRefactoringWithLargeLanguageModels]] (10,350 snippets: repeated refactoring converges diverse inputs toward one stable style, cross-variant similarity ~0.87, with structural drift and comment loss); [[research_topics/coding_agents/RethinkingCodeReviewWorkflowsWithLLM/summary|RethinkingCodeReviewWorkflowsWithLLMAssistanceAnEmpiricalStudy]] (field experiment, 10 developers × 2 real PRs: assistance confirms judgment or surfaces missed issues such as race conditions, but noisy suggestions trigger the false-positive trap; preference is situational by familiarity, size, and risk).

Strength: moderate — three different objects of study (transformation quality vs developers, transformation dynamics over iterations, human–AI collaboration) with different methods (paired comparison with significance tests, large-scale experiment, qualitative field study) converging on the same division of labor: model on high-volume surface clean-ups and briefings, humans on modularization, coupling, and final judgment. Holds for Java refactoring and moderate-size pull requests; review evidence is small-sample single-company.

## Guardrails are non-optional: tests, stopping criteria, sampling, and over-trust vigilance

Backed by: [[research_topics/coding_agents/LlmCodeRefactoringCapability/summary|AnEmpiricalStudyOnTheCodeRefactoringCapabilityOfLargeLanguageModels]] (one-shot plus best-of-five sampling recipe; comprehensive testing mandatory at ~57% Pass@5); [[research_topics/coding_agents/FromRestructuringToStabilization/summary|FromRestructuringToStabilizationALargeScaleExperimentOnIterativeCodeReadabilityRefactoringWithLargeLanguageModels]] (explicit stopping criteria plus comment preservation; functionality breaks rare but non-zero); [[research_topics/coding_agents/RethinkingCodeReviewWorkflowsWithLLM/summary|RethinkingCodeReviewWorkflowsWithLLMAssistanceAnEmpiricalStudy]] (optional adoption, concise file-and-line-precise embedded output, vigilance against over-reliance highest when the AI leads); [[research_topics/coding_agents/HumanAiExperienceInIntegratedDevelopmentEnvironments/summary|Human-AI experience in IDEs]] and [[research_topics/coding_agents/ImpactOfLLMAssistantsOnSoftwareDeveloperProductivity/summary|LLM assistants and productivity review]] (treat output as preliminary drafts requiring review; budget validation time; preserve review and collaboration practices).

Strength: moderate — broadest source spread but the most generic claim: each guardrail is grounded in a measured failure mode (test failures, functionality breaks, false-positive flooding, over-trust by novices) rather than in a single shared metric. Holds across writing, debugging, and refactoring as a workflow prescription, not as a measured effect size.
