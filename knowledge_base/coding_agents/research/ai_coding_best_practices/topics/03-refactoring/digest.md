# Refactoring

**In one sentence:** Iterative LLM readability refactoring converges toward a stable, normalized style across starting points and LLM review assistance helps most as a situational human-led aid — but both need guardrails, concise context-aware behavior, and human verification because over-refactoring, false positives, and trust limits persist.

## Key points
- Iterative readability refactoring follows a restructuring-then-stabilization trajectory: on already-clean Java code with a general prompt, unchanged lines climb 45% (v0→v1) through 76%, 86%, 89% to 92% (v4→v5), with early renames (9%) and code insertions (11%) fading to all change types under 1% while adjacent-version similarity rises 0.86 to 0.90 without ever reaching 1.00 [[research_topics/coding_agents/FromRestructuringToStabilization/summary|RestructuringStabilization]].
- Even clean code drifts structurally under repeated refactoring: code lines grow from 58 to over 73, empty lines and method counts rise and stabilize from the third version onward, comment lines fall slowly, and inline comments are almost completely removed [[research_topics/coding_agents/FromRestructuringToStabilization/summary|RestructuringStabilization]].
- Degraded inputs are normalized toward the same end state: Meaningless-identifier variants start at 31% unchanged (24% renames) and NoComment variants at 44% unchanged (14% renames), then follow the Original trajectory to 90% and 89% unchanged, with code lines converging 56 to about 73, methods 3.1 to about 6, and cross-variant similarity settling near 0.87 within 3% [[research_topics/coding_agents/FromRestructuringToStabilization/summary|RestructuringStabilization]].
- Targeted prompts steer the change mix without breaking convergence, but need care: naming-focused prompts keep rename rates high late (around 20% on Original, 25–30% on degraded variants, with oscillatory renaming on NoComment) while comment-focused prompts front-load comment edits (around 36% of changes on Meaningless in v0→v1) then stabilize without inflating documentation; functionality breaks are rare but non-zero per iteration, so explicit stopping criteria and comment-preservation mechanisms are recommended [[research_topics/coding_agents/FromRestructuringToStabilization/summary|RestructuringStabilization]].
- The review assistant contrasted two RAG-based interaction modes at WirelessCar: Mode A (Co-Reviewer) proactively generates an up-front summary of major changes and concerns via a `start_review` sub-agent with full PR data injected, while Mode B (Interactive Assistant) stays passive and answers only targeted on-demand queries, each tested by ten developers reviewing two matched real pull requests with rotated assignment, think-aloud observation, and post-session interviews [[research_topics/coding_agents/RethinkingCodeReviewWorkflowsWithLLM/summary|CodeReviewWorkflows]].
- Review assistance often confirms reviewer judgment or surfaces missed issues such as race conditions and deadlocks on large pull requests while cutting search effort across code, docs, and Jira tickets, yet it also produces strange or noisy suggestions (e.g. wrongly flagged imports, overlong summaries), triggering the false-positive trap where flooded reviewers stop reading and miss real issues, with over-reliance risk highest when the AI leads [[research_topics/coding_agents/RethinkingCodeReviewWorkflowsWithLLM/summary|CodeReviewWorkflows]].
- Preference is situational rather than absolute: AI-led summaries win for unfamiliar or complex code, large pull requests, low-risk changes, newcomers, and weak-review-discipline teams, while on-demand assistance wins where reviewers know the codebase or want full control, with proposed extensions to author-side pre-review and human-first-then-AI-validate patterns; developers insist deployment live inside GitHub, GitLab, IDEs, or Slack with concise file-and-line-precise output, fast responses, and deeper context (diffs, source files, requirements, docs, connected repos) [[research_topics/coding_agents/RethinkingCodeReviewWorkflowsWithLLM/summary|CodeReviewWorkflows]].
- A leakage-controlled StarCoder2-vs-developer study adds the missing transformation-quality evidence: on test-passing outputs StarCoder2 cuts smells 44.36% versus 24.27% for developers (p = 0.003) with slightly better metric improvement (19.32% vs 17.46%), but passes only 57.15% of unit tests at Pass@5 (28.36% at Pass@1) against developers' 100% — so the model is a powerful but unreliable cleaner that must be paired with strong tests [[research_topics/coding_agents/LlmCodeRefactoringCapability/summary|RefactoringCapability]].
- The advantage splits cleanly by kind: StarCoder2 wins 10 of 16 smell types, dominating systematic within-class syntactic work (Long Statement, Magic Number, Empty Catch Clause, renames, annotation changes), while developers win architecture-sensitive smells (Broken Modularization, Deficient Encapsulation, Multifaceted Abstraction, Missing Default) and dependency-propagating operations (Move Attribute, Change Attribute Type, Encapsulate Attribute) — framing the pair as complementary, with the model on high-volume surface clean-ups and humans on modularization, encapsulation, and coupling [[research_topics/coding_agents/LlmCodeRefactoringCapability/summary|RefactoringCapability]].
- Prompting plus sampling is the recommended recipe: one-shot prompts with a real before/after example (34.51% pass, 42.97% smell reduction) and chain-of-thought with refactoring-type hints (32.22%, 42.34%) both outrank zero-shot (28.36%, 39.45%), and generating five candidates per input beats one by roughly 29 points of pass rate [[research_topics/coding_agents/LlmCodeRefactoringCapability/summary|RefactoringCapability]].

## What the sources agree on
- LLMs work as normalizing/augmenting partners, not replacements: the refactoring experiment shows convergence toward a consistent readable style across diverse inputs, the StarCoder2 study shows the model out-cleans developers on surface smells while losing on structural work and test survival, and the review study concludes LLMs complement rather than replace reviewers.
- Human oversight and guardrails are required: stopping criteria plus comment preservation on the iterative-refactoring side, comprehensive unit tests plus one-shot multi-generation sampling on the StarCoder2 side (only ~57% Pass@5), and optional adoption plus vigilance against over-reliance and false positives on the review side.
- Context and phrasing shape outcomes: targeted prompts steer refactoring change types, one-shot examples plus type-hint chain-of-thought materially raise both correctness and smell removal, and reviewer prompting skill, PR size, familiarity, and available project context shape review usefulness.
- More context helps but current context is thin: the refactoring authors call for broader code bases and prompt/context variation, while reviewers blame missing architecture docs, conventions, READMEs, and connected repositories for superficial feedback.

## Where they differ
- Object of study: one measures automated code transformation over five iterations (what the model changes in the artifact), one measures transformation quality against developers with test gating (who wins on which smell and refactoring type), and the other measures human–AI collaboration during review (how developers want to interact with the assistant).
- Method and scale: one is a large-scale quantitative experiment (10,350 generated snippets, line-level DiffParser with horizontal/vertical/combined similarity metrics on GPT-5.1), one is a leakage-controlled paired comparison (30 Java projects, 5,194 pure-refactoring commits, three-axis scoring with Mann-Whitney U and Cliff's delta on StarCoder2-15B), and the other is a small-sample qualitative field program (7 exploratory plus 10 experimental interviews with thematic analysis at one company using o4-mini).
- Guidance specificity: the refactoring studies prescribe transformation guardrails (stop early, phrase naming prompts carefully, preserve valuable comments, gate on tests, default to one-shot plus best-of-five sampling), whereas the review study prescribes workflow design (dual proactive/reactive modes, embedded concise fast context-aware output, upstream pre-review use).
- Stance on convergence versus contingency: the refactoring paper stresses a robust cross-variant convergence dynamic, while the review paper stresses situational contingency where no single interaction mode wins everywhere.

## Evidence quality
- The iterative-refactoring study is strong primary experimental evidence (controlled variants, three prompts, five iterations, fine-grained change taxonomy, cross-variant similarity) but limited to Java snippets from one educational repo, a single main model (GPT-5.1, temperature 0 which reduces but does not remove non-determinism), aggregate metrics that can hide individual back-and-forth sequences, incomplete diff-pair coverage, per-occurrence rename counting, and readability judged only anecdotally rather than measured directly.
- The review study is credible qualitative field evidence (real developers, real pull requests, rotated modes, think-aloud plus observation plus post-session interviews, open-source RAG artifact) but limited by small convenience samples at one company (7 then 10 participants, partial overlap, 4 familiar versus 6 unfamiliar), moderate-size pull requests only, manual RAG index preparation, and an emphasis on perceived interaction value over detection performance or longitudinal trust.
- The StarCoder2-vs-developer study is strong paired-comparison evidence (leakage-controlled project selection, pure-refactoring commits, three-axis quality gating with significance and effect-size tests) but limited to Java, one open model, and accepted-PR test suites as the correctness oracle, with tool choice (RMiner, DesigniteJava, Understand) able to shift results.
- All three assigned sources now contribute evidence; nothing is drawn from skipped sources because none remain.
