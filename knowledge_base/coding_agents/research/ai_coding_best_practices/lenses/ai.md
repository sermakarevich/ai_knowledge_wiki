# coding_agents -- for AI engineers building and evaluating coding assistants

## What this means for you

- Net value of your assistant is decided by verification and workflow governance, not raw generation. Copilot-style generation speeds up routine, well-specified work (boilerplate, CRUD, scaffolding, tests) by roughly 40–55%, with the largest gains on simple tasks and for less experienced developers — but reviewing suggestions, refining prompts, and reworking output can consume up to half of assisted development time, shifting the user from writer to reviewer.
- Treat acceptance and merge rates as misleading proxies for productivity. Measure end-to-end task time including review, rework, and debugging/validation overhead instead.
- Security defects are a first-class failure mode, not an edge case. About a quarter of Copilot-generated snippets committed to real projects contain confirmed weaknesses (29.5% Python, 24.2% JavaScript, ~3 flaws each across 43 CWE types led by CWE-330, CWE-94, CWE-79, CWE-78; over a third of instances in the CWE Top-25), tracing to insecure training-data patterns resurfacing in suggestions.
- Prompting is a real but bounded lever, and its effect is model-dependent. A security-aware persona prefix cut scanner-agreed vulnerabilities ~47–56% on GPT-4o-mini/GPT-4o while backfiring on GPT-3.5-turbo; one self-critique-and-fix iteration removed ~25–65% depending on model; feeding analyzer warnings into Copilot Chat fixed ~55% versus ~19% for a bare `/fix`; coercive urgency framing lowered correctness and raised security warnings. Sensitivity to wording grows with model capability, but architecture and task difficulty dominate correctness, maintainability, and style — so frame prompting as a nudge, and invest in model choice and context depth.
- For repair, diagnosis beats one-shot patching. The strongest pipeline in the digests combines on-demand static-dynamic context (Joern Code Property Graph plus JavaAgent/ASM statement-level traces with variable values and branch outcomes, served through a retrieval interface rather than dumped into the prompt), budgeted question-driven diagnosis (one typed what/why/how question per round via a ReAct-style loop, distilled into a four-field hypothesis: faulty behavior, evidence, root cause, modification suggestion), and validation as behavioral evidence (up to 3 refinement rounds of hypothesis-guided generation with compile-plus-test validation and feedback as diagnostics plus code diff plus aligned trace diff) — reaching 139/136 correct Defects4J V1.2/V2.0 fixes on GPT-3.5 and 162/171 on GPT-4o, with dynamic traces alone worth +19 fixes at lower per-bug cost than ReInFix, RepairAgent, and ChatRepair.
- A second repair architecture corroborates the thesis independently: DebugRepair's self-directed debugging (test semantic purification by iterative backward slicing, hybrid LLM-plus-AST simulated instrumentation, hierarchical debugging sessions with patch augmentation) fixes 224 Defects4J bugs on GPT-3.5 and 295 on DeepSeek-V3 plus all QuixBugs Java/Python at $0.036 per bug with +51.3% average gains across five backbones — consider purification plus print-trace instrumentation as an alternative or complement to CPG-plus-trace-diff pipelines, and note both assume explicit test cases.
- Design for the debugger, not just the benchmark: the PracAPR vision (grounded in the ROSE prototype: 89% correct-location localization, 36/40 QuixBugs and 37/60 Defects4J in seconds, +44% task success) argues for test-free flow-analysis localization from a suspended program, developer-supplied problem specifications, and re-execution-free simulated-trace validation — the direction that makes repair usable inside the IDE while debugging.
- For repo-level generation, engineer context and enforce process instead of growing the single prompt. A multi-agent pipeline (intent clarification, external retrieval plus distilled synthesis, role-specialist agents with a reviewer) reached 80% single-shot success versus 40% for a single agent with zero hallucinated APIs at ~3–5× token cost; a TDD-governance protocol (test-first gating, proposal/engine separation, bounded N=3 repair) stabilized multi-agent synthesis. Both are still preliminary or small-scale.
- Design review assistance as situational and human-led. At WirelessCar, a proactive Co-Reviewer (up-front summary of major changes and concerns) versus a passive Interactive Assistant (on-demand answers only) showed no absolute winner: AI-led summaries fit unfamiliar or complex code, large pull requests, low-risk changes, newcomers, and weak-review-discipline teams, while on-demand fits reviewers who know the codebase or want control. Assistance confirms judgment or catches missed issues (e.g. race conditions, deadlocks on large PRs) and cuts search across code, docs, and tickets — but noisy suggestions (wrongly flagged imports, overlong summaries) trigger a false-positive trap where flooded reviewers stop reading, with over-reliance highest when the AI leads. Ship inside GitHub, GitLab, IDEs, or Slack with concise file-and-line-precise output, fast responses, and deeper context (diffs, source files, requirements, docs, connected repos).
- Expect iterative refactoring to normalize rather than reinvent. On already-clean Java code, unchanged lines climb 45% (v0→v1) to 92% (v4→v5) while adjacent-version similarity rises 0.86 to 0.90 without ever reaching 1.00; degraded inputs (meaningless identifiers, no comments) converge toward the same end state (cross-variant similarity near 0.87). Structurally, code lines grow (58 to over 73), methods and empty lines rise and stabilize, and inline comments are almost completely removed — so build in stopping criteria and comment preservation.
- Route refactoring work by kind, not by habit: StarCoder2 out-cleans developers on surface syntactic smells (44.36% vs 24.27% reduction; renames, annotation edits, long statements, magic numbers) but passes only ~57% of tests at best-of-five versus 100% for developers and loses on modularization, encapsulation, and coupling — so default to model-plus-test-gating with one-shot examples and best-of-five sampling for surface clean-ups, and keep structural design with humans.
- Know where real usage concentrates: JavaScript/Python in VS Code (48.0% of IDE mentions) for data processing, test, and front-end work, where the top complaint is IDE-integration and access difficulty (28.1%/17.0%) rather than generation quality. An earlier community-mined study (169 SO posts, 655 GitHub Discussions) converges independently: JS for front-end, Python for ML, mainstream IDEs jointly 85.9%, useful generation (49.0%) and speed (16.3%) leading benefits. Novices gain the most speed with the worst over-trust; experts gain less but verify better.
- Calibrate productivity claims against enterprise measurement: Zoominfo's phased deployment to 400+ developers measured 33% suggestion / 20% line acceptance with ~20% time savings and 72% satisfaction — lower than 40–55% controlled-study speedups because production figures are net of review overhead. Treat acceptance rates as productivity proxies with that discount applied, and expect weaker results on HTML/CSS/JSON/SQL and domain-specific logic.
- Treat description wording as a correctness lever: 46% of semantically equivalent paraphrases changed Copilot's output, with ~28% of passing methods reachable only one way — so invest in clear, well-phrased task descriptions, and evaluate with CodeBLEU plus test outcomes jointly since each misleads alone.

## Decisions this informs

- Evaluation design: run shared suites (HumanEval/MBPP for generation, DebugBench/VulnLoc for debugging and localization, Defects4J for real bugs, ProFuzzBench/SCTBench for protocols and concurrency, TransCoder for translation), report head-to-head fix counts with scenario splits and ablations per stage, and never let acceptance or merge rates stand in for net productivity.
- Retrieval architecture: serve static-dynamic context (code property graphs, statement-level traces with values and branch outcomes) through a retrieval interface instead of dumping it into the prompt.
- Repair loop budgets: prefer typed iterative diagnosis plus bounded refinement (e.g. ~10 diagnosis rounds, up to 3 validation rounds with compile-plus-test and trace-diff feedback) over one-shot patching; match technique family to bug class (template/dynamic/search/specification-guided and memory-safety for security bugs; pattern/fuzzing/evolutionary/specification-guided for semantic bugs; mined-pattern/grammar-based/mutation-search for syntactic bugs).
- Prompt and model defaults: ship security-aware, specific, diagnostic, well-contextualized prompts (persona prefix, self-critique-and-fix, analyzer-warning-fed repair) while avoiding coercive urgency framing — and re-validate every prompt default per model, since effects flip across models; expect model choice to dominate framing.
- Cost posture: budget explicitly for the speed-versus-depth trade-off (Codex for real-time completion and common bugs; GPT-4-class for complex large-context bugs at higher cost; CodeT5 for localized tasks and summaries; GraphCodeBERT for structural/data-flow settings at higher resource cost) and for multi-agent overhead (~3–5× tokens).
- Review UX: offer both proactive summaries and on-demand querying, default to human-first-then-AI-validate, keep output concise and file-and-line-precise, and extend to author-side pre-review.
- Refactoring guardrails: set explicit stopping criteria, phrase naming-focused prompts carefully (they keep rename rates high late, ~20% on Original and 25–30% on degraded variants, with oscillatory renaming), and preserve valuable comments, since functionality breaks are rare but non-zero per iteration.

## Risks and unknowns

- Quality direction is unresolved and context-dependent: some studies report fewer defects and better maintainability with assistants while others report subtle errors, insecure patterns, and harder-to-maintain code; gains are task-contingent (simple and well-specified beats complex and security-sensitive).
- Verification overhead can erase generation savings on complex work, and human verification stays non-optional because repair still mislocalizes faults, overfits to tests, and produces locally correct but globally wrong or insecure patches.
- Prompt effects do not transfer cleanly: proactive security prefixes help newer GPT models but harm GPT-3.5-turbo, and influence framing moves correctness on algorithmic benchmarks but vanishes on real-world maintenance tasks.
- Evidence limits you inherit if you build on these findings: Python/JavaScript and Copilot over-representation; short or atomic tasks; small samples (median 17 in human-AI interaction research; 7 then 10 participants at one company for the review study); self-reported time and satisfaction; scanner and metric proxies as relative rather than absolute signals; benchmark overfitting and uneven language coverage in the repair survey.
- The strongest-sounding pipeline claims are the narrowest: the multi-agent context result rests on 5 tasks in one codebase, the TDD-governance proposal is explicitly preliminary with no full evaluation, PracRepair's ablations are confined to Defects4J V1.2 with GPT-3.5 with baselines reusing published numbers and partly manual correct-patch judgment (Java-only, fixed temperature and time budgets), and the refactoring convergence result is Java snippets from one educational repo on a single main model with readability judged anecdotally.
