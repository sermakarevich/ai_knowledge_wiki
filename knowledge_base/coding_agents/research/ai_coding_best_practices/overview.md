# coding_agents

**Research:** 20 sources, date range unavailable (sources.md absent at time of writing), focus: What are the evidence-backed best practices for using AI coding assistants for writing, debugging, and refactoring code?

## Human Readable TL;DR

Think of an AI coding assistant as a very fast junior teammate who types boilerplate at superhuman speed but occasionally installs the wrong wiring behind the wall, so you go faster only if you act like the senior electrician who checks every circuit with tests and scanners. Debugging with AI works less like asking for the answer and more like a doctor who needs blood tests and X-rays, meaning runtime traces and repeated test feedback, not just a glance at the symptoms, to get the diagnosis right. Refactoring and review help work like a tidy-up robot plus a second pair of eyes: the robot steadily irons everything toward the same neat style if you tell it when to stop, and the second pair of eyes is most useful when you choose between a proactive summary for unfamiliar code and a quiet on-demand helper for code you already know.

## TL;DR

Across code generation, debugging, and refactoring, the evidence converges on a single pattern: AI assistants reliably accelerate routine drafting and surface useful fixes and cleanups, but their net value is decided by the surrounding workflow rather than raw generation quality. The most replicated generation finding is 40–55% faster routine work paired with a large verification tax — reviewing, re-prompting, and reworking output that can consume up to half of assisted time — plus a material security tail, with roughly a quarter of committed Copilot-style snippets carrying confirmed weaknesses across dozens of CWE types. Prompting and context engineering move outcomes substantially but conditionally: specific, diagnostic, warning-fed prompts and engineered multi-stage context (intent clarification, retrieval plus synthesis, reviewer roles, test-first gating) improve correctness and security, while vague or coercive phrasing hurts or does nothing and effects vary by model and task. Debugging follows the same workflow lesson in stronger form, where budgeted question-driven diagnosis over on-demand static-dynamic context with compile-plus-test refinement beats one-shot patching by a wide margin, and refactoring plus review assistance converge toward a stable readable style and situational review gains only when bounded by stopping criteria, comment preservation, concise embedded output, and vigilant human verification against over-refactoring, false positives, and over-trust.

## What is established

The evidence ledger for the claims below is [agreements.md](agreements.md); only the narrative synthesis lives here. See [agreements.md](agreements.md).

For code generation, speed on routine well-specified work (boilerplate, CRUD, scaffolding, tests) alongside a verification burden is the most replicated finding in the set, reported by controlled experiments, both systematic reviews, community-mining studies, and an enterprise deployment (Zoominfo: 33% suggestion / 20% line acceptance, ~20% time savings, 72% satisfaction across 400+ developers), with gains that shrink on complex, domain-specific, or security-sensitive logic and accrue disproportionately to less experienced developers who also verify least well. Generated code carries real inherited risk, demonstrated by production-grounded mining of committed snippets with dual-analyzer plus manual adjudication, which makes human oversight plus testing and static analysis non-optional rather than precautionary. Prompting is an effective but bounded lever: security-aware persona prefixes, self-critique-and-fix iterations, and feeding analyzer warnings into repair prompts cut vulnerabilities substantially on newer models, while model choice and task difficulty dominate correctness, maintainability, and style, so framing should be treated as a nudge for tone, documentation, and security posture rather than a reliability guarantee — with the paraphrase-robustness result (46% output change on equivalent descriptions) showing that clear description wording is itself a first-order correctness practice.

For debugging and repair, three independent strands agree that runtime and contextual evidence beyond static code and error messages is what separates shallow from correct fixes, with neural fault localization, AI-generated tests, and multi-modal context in the survey literature, statement-level traces with variable values, branch outcomes, and trace diffs in the strongest primary experiment, and self-directed print-trace debugging plus failure-aware augmented prompts in two further primary sources. Iterative feedback-driven loops decisively beat single-shot patching, quantified as rising from roughly 89 to 139 correct Defects4J fixes over three refinement rounds (and 224 total for the self-directed approach on the same benchmark family), and shared suites (Defects4J, HumanEval, DebugBench and peers) remain the required evaluation harness even as human verification stays mandatory because repair still mislocalizes, overfits to tests, and produces locally plausible but globally wrong or insecure patches.

For refactoring and review, the evidence establishes a restructuring-then-stabilization dynamic in which repeated readability refactoring converges across clean, identifier-degraded, and comment-stripped starting points toward a common normalized style without ever fully freezing, alongside a paired-comparison finding that StarCoder2 out-cleans developers on surface syntactic smells (44.36% vs 24.27% reduction) while losing on structural work and test survival (57% Pass@5 vs 100%) — prescribing a division of labor with the model on high-volume surface clean-ups under test gating and humans on modularization and coupling — and a consistent collaboration finding that LLM review assistance complements rather than replaces reviewers. Both need explicit guardrails: stopping criteria and comment preservation on the refactoring side because clean code still drifts structurally and functionality breaks are rare but non-zero, and optional adoption with vigilance against the false-positive trap on the review side because noisy suggestions flood reviewers who then miss real issues, with over-reliance highest when the AI leads. Context and phrasing shape both outcomes — targeted prompts steer the refactoring change mix and reviewer skill, pull-request size, familiarity, and available project context shape review usefulness — while current context remains thin, with calls on both sides for broader codebases, architecture docs, conventions, and connected-repository signals.

## What is contested

- Whether assistant use improves or degrades overall code quality and maintainability across contexts — see [disagreements.md](disagreements.md).
- Whether novices are net beneficiaries (largest speedups) or net at-risk (worst over-trust and skill erosion) — see [disagreements.md](disagreements.md).

## What is open

Genuine gaps remain around generalization beyond the studied envelope — Python and JavaScript, Java-only repair benchmarks, Copilot-heavy samples, short atomic tasks, small participant pools, and scanner or metric proxies — as well as longitudinal effects on skill, trust calibration, and defect rates in production, cost-justified deployment of token-heavy multi-agent pipelines, and standardized benchmarks that capture real-world maintenance rather than isolated algorithmic tasks.
