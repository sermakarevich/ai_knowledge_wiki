# SwarmAgentic Overview: Fully Automated Generation via Swarm Intelligence
**In one sentence:** SwarmAgentic is a fully automated framework that generates agentic systems from scratch and jointly optimizes agent functionality and collaboration through language-driven Particle Swarm Optimization, achieving a 261.8% relative gain over Automated Design of Agentic Systems on TravelPlanner with only a task description and objective function as input.
## Key points
- SwarmAgentic is the only framework in the Table 1 comparison satisfying all three autonomy criteria, namely from-scratch agent generation, self-optimizing agent functionality, and self-optimizing agent collaboration, while Solo Performance Prompting satisfies none and Evolutionary Agent, AgentSquare, AutoAgents, AFlow, Agent Symbolic Learning, and Automated Design of Agentic Systems satisfy only subsets.
- Each agentic system is formalized as a particle encoding an agent set and a collaborative structure, where each agent is a triple of identifier, responsibility, and execution policy, and fitness is scored by a task objective function.
- Optimization reinterprets Particle Swarm Optimization in language space with Large Language Model-driven flaw identification, failure-aware velocity updates combining failure-driven adjustments with personal-best and global-best guidance, and position updates applying Add, Delete, Modify, and Reorder operators.
- On TravelPlanner with GPT-3.5 / GPT-4o executor reporting, SwarmAgentic reaches 100.0 / 100.0 delivery rate, 70.9 / 92.9 commonsense micro/macro, 12.8 / 56.1 hard-constraint micro/macro, 21.0 / 66.7 final-pass micro/macro, and 3.3 / 32.2 final rate, beating Automated Design of Agentic Systems on every constrained metric.
- Across Natural Plan trip planning, meeting planning, and calendar scheduling plus Creative Writing and Multilingual Grade School Math, SwarmAgentic leads all baselines, for example 13.1 / 13.1 on trip planning, 23.0 / 56.0 on meeting planning, 28.0 / 82.0 on calendar scheduling, 8.2 / 8.5 on Creative Writing, and 65.6 / 88.4 on Multilingual Grade School Math.
- Ablation on 20 Creative Writing instances shows the full 5-particle 10-iteration configuration scoring 8.8 for a 41.9% gain over Direct, with removal of collaborative-structure reconfiguration dropping to 6.7, removal of agent-level adaptation dropping to 7.3, and removal of failure-driven adjustments dropping to 8.4, while cross-model transfer from GPT-4o-mini to GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-sonnet, DeepSeek-V3, and Gemini-1.5-Pro preserves the lead in every case.

## Motivation and autonomy gap
The paper starts from the observation that Large Language Models have advanced decision-making, coordination, and complex task execution, yet existing generation frameworks lack full autonomy because they miss from-scratch agent generation, self-optimizing functionality, and self-optimizing collaboration. These rigidities suppress self-optimizing system behavior, impose engineering overhead, and force substantial manual intervention whenever task specifications change, which becomes prohibitive in open-ended exploratory tasks that demand high-level planning and system-level coordination. Table 1 makes the gap explicit by scoring seven prior methods on the three criteria, showing Solo Performance Prompting as no on all three, Evolutionary Agent and AgentSquare as yes only on functionality, and AutoAgents, AFlow, Agent Symbolic Learning, and Automated Design of Agentic Systems as yes on functionality and collaboration but no on from-scratch generation, leaving SwarmAgentic as the sole yes on all three. The authors argue that no fixed templates or handcrafted agents can be reused in structurally unconstrained settings, so a practical framework must construct agents, behaviors, and collaboration strategies from scratch and jointly optimize them without human intervention.
## Particle Swarm Optimization background
The work builds on Particle Swarm Optimization, a gradient-free population-based method inspired by bird flocking and fish schooling that balances exploration and exploitation through decentralized self-organization. Each particle holds a position representing a candidate solution and a velocity governing movement, updated as v(t+1)_i = omega v(t)_i + c1 r1 (p*_i - x(t)_i) + c2 r2 (g - x(t)_i) and x(t+1)_i = x(t)_i + v(t+1)_i, where p*_i is the personal best, g is the global best, omega is the inertia weight, c1 and c2 are learning coefficients, and r1 and r2 are stochastic factors. Fitness is evaluated by J after each iteration until a stopping criterion such as a fixed iteration count is met. The key move is to reinterpret these numerical updates as semantic transformations over structured language representations, enabling swarm search over discrete combinatorial configurations of agent functionality and collaboration rather than continuous vectors.
## System representation
An agentic system at generation t is denoted S(t)_i for the i-th population member, comprising an agent set A(t)_i = {A(t)_i,1 through A(t)_i,m} and a collaborative structure W(t)_i = {W(t)_i,1 through W(t)_i,n}, where each step W(t)_i,l assigns a specific agent to its task. Each agent is represented as A(t)_i,k = (I(t)_i,k, R(t)_i,k, P(t)_i,k), where I is the identifier defining the role, R is the responsibility specifying performable tasks, and P is the execution policy governing decision-making and execution. Optimization iteratively refines A and W to maximize fitness J(S), with the basic structure detailed in Appendix D.1 and pseudocode in Appendix D.2. Figure 1 summarizes the three stages as initialization of diverse language-encoded systems, particle position update through failure-aware velocity and position steps with Add, Delete, Modify, and Reorder operators, and return of the best-performing global system refined by self-adaptation and swarm guidance.
## Particle initialization
SwarmAgentic initializes candidate systems S(0)_i as particles with position x(0)_i encoding the configuration and velocity v(0)_i governing textual modification of agents and collaboration across iterations. Diversity comes from temperature-controlled sampling where low-temperature particles stay close to established patterns, medium-temperature particles balance stability with innovation, and high-temperature particles maximize exploration toward unconventional architectures. Initial velocities are assigned from estimated fitness to push toward promising regions while avoiding stagnation, personal bests start at initial positions scored by task metrics, and the global best is set after all particles are assessed as the top configuration for subsequent guidance.
## Flaw identification
Unlike classic Particle Swarm Optimization, which moves on scalar fitness alone, SwarmAgentic inserts an explicit Large Language Model-driven diagnosis before each move so refinements target real bottlenecks rather than arbitrary edits. Agent flaws cover missing agents that leave critical tasks unassigned, redundant agents that add inefficiency, and ambiguous policies that hinder coordination, while collaborative-structure flaws cover missing steps, redundant steps, incomplete context that blocks informed decisions, and misaligned outcomes that cascade into later failures. Execution is scored by the objective function to yield an error set E(t)_i, and a model analyzes E(t)_i together with S(t)_i to produce flaw set f(t+1)_i split into agent and structure flaws. This structured diagnosis feeds every downstream velocity term and is presented as the reason updates stay interpretable and effective.
## Failure-aware velocity update
Velocity is computed as v(t+1)_i = LLM_vel(c_f r_f F(v(t)_i), c_p r_p (p*_i - x(t)_i), c_g r_g (g - x(t)_i)), where c_f, c_p, and c_g are repulsion, cognitive, and social coefficients for failure-driven adjustments, personal-best guidance, and global-best guidance, r_f, r_p, and r_g add controlled randomness, and F captures the failed component of the prior update. Failure-driven adjustments use LLM_fail on the prior plan v(t)_i plus prior flaws f(t)_i and current flaws f(t+1)_i to detect persistent defects and prune ineffective corrections, formalized as c_f r_f F(v(t)_i) = LLM_fail(v(t)_i, f(t)_i, f(t+1)_i). Personal-best guidance compares the current configuration against p*_i conditioned on f(t+1)_i as c_p r_p (p*_i - x(t)_i) = LLM_pers(x(t)_i, p*_i, f(t+1)_i), and global-best guidance compares against g as c_g r_g (g - x(t)_i) = LLM_glob(x(t)_i, g, f(t+1)_i), in both cases extracting transferable improvements instead of copying past configurations. Together the three terms eliminate recurring flaws while balancing self-learning against swarm knowledge and guarding against premature convergence.
## Position update
Positions advance as x(t+1)_i = LLM_pos(x(t)_i, v(t+1)_i), translating the velocity plan into concrete structural edits through two mechanisms that run every iteration until the iteration limit. Agent-level adaptation edits individual agents by changing roles, responsibilities, and policies, adding new agents and deleting redundant ones on feedback, while collaborative-structure reconfiguration reorders steps, removes overhead, adds needed steps, and repairs sequencing and dependencies to improve coordination. The paper stresses that functionality and collaboration are treated as interdependent and refined together, which is the central contrast with methods that optimize them separately or freeze collaboration as static templates.
## Experimental setup
Evaluation uses six real-world tasks spanning planning, collaboration, generation, and reasoning, most of them open-ended and structurally unconstrained. TravelPlanner tests long-horizon planning under user constraints, the three Natural Plan tasks of trip planning, meeting planning, and calendar scheduling test multi-agent scheduling with conflict minimization, Creative Writing tests coherent multi-paragraph generation from unordered key points, and Multilingual Grade School Math adds a structured math reasoning case where predefined logic might suffice. Prompting baselines are Direct with a fixed prompt, Chain-of-Thought with step-by-step generation, and Self-Refine with self-feedback iteration, while automated baselines are Solo Performance Prompting with multi-persona self-collaboration, Evolutionary Agent with evolutionary role and policy search, and Automated Design of Agentic Systems with a meta agent discovering coded systems iteratively. Following Automated Design of Agentic Systems, GPT-4o-mini-0718 serves as optimizer and GPT-3.5-turbo-0125, GPT-4o-0806, Claude-3.5-sonnet-0620, DeepSeek-V3, and Gemini-1.5-Pro serve as executors, with SwarmAgentic set to 5 particles and 10 iterations against a 30-iteration budget for Automated Design of Agentic Systems.
## Results on TravelPlanner and beyond
Table 2 reports TravelPlanner cells as GPT-3.5 / GPT-4o and shows SwarmAgentic at 100.0 / 100.0 delivery, 70.9 / 92.9 commonsense, 12.8 / 56.1 hard constraint, 21.0 / 66.7 final pass, and 3.3 / 32.2 final rate, ahead of Direct, Chain-of-Thought, Self-Refine, Solo Performance Prompting, Evolutionary Agent, and Automated Design of Agentic Systems on the constrained metrics. The headline claim is a 261.8% relative gain over Automated Design of Agentic Systems on TravelPlanner, supported by large gaps such as hard-constraint macro 56.1 versus 34.4 and final-rate macro 32.2 versus 8.9. Table 3 extends the lead to Natural Plan, Creative Writing, and Multilingual Grade School Math with SwarmAgentic at 13.1 / 13.1 trip planning, 23.0 / 56.0 meeting planning, 28.0 / 82.0 calendar scheduling, 8.2 / 8.5 Creative Writing, and 65.6 / 88.4 Multilingual Grade School Math, the best in every column. The authors conclude that full autonomy wins precisely where static templates fail, yet still leads on structured math, so autonomy does not trade off template-compatible performance, and they attribute the edge to from-scratch construction plus interpretable feedback-driven joint optimization rather than rigid personas, fixed scaffolds, or seed agents.
## Transferability, ablation, and trajectory
Cross-model transfer optimizes with GPT-4o-mini and executes the same discovered Creative Writing system on stronger models, yielding 8.5 on GPT-4o, 8.3 on Claude-3.5-sonnet, 9.0 on DeepSeek-V3, and 7.5 on Gemini-1.5-Pro, each the best in its column, while direct optimization and evaluation on Gemini with Gemini-1.5-flash training and Gemini-1.5-Pro testing reaches 7.8. Ablation on 20 Creative Writing instances varies iterations as (3,1) at 5.9, (3,5) at 6.4, and (3,10) at 7.0 against a 6.2 Direct baseline, and varies particles as (1,5) at 6.3, (3,5) at 6.7, and (5,5) at 6.9, confirming that more refinement and broader exploration help up to an 11.3% gain. Component removal at (5,10) gives 6.7 without collaborative-structure reconfiguration, 7.3 without agent-level adaptation, 8.4 without failure-driven adjustments, and 8.8 full, confirming that each mechanism matters and that persistent-error memory is load-bearing. Figure 2 traces TravelPlanner Success Rate climbing as a Quality Assurance Specialist plus accommodation verification first lifts Success Rate to 11%, a budget and preference verifier then lifts it to 22%, and an explicit budget-compliance update reaches 33% Success Rate, with task-dependency reordering proceeding in parallel even though the figure foregrounds agent evolution.
## Conclusion and limitations
The paper concludes that language-driven Particle Swarm Optimization enables fully automated self-optimizing systems by dynamically refining agent sets and collaborative structures, delivering superior adaptability, constraint satisfaction, and coordination across complex real-world tasks. Limitations are stated as lack of inductive priors or domain templates that could accelerate convergence in structured settings, susceptibility to hallucinations that can propagate through optimization cycles and may require external knowledge, and purely text-based operation without perception or physical action, which motivates future constraint-guided initialization plus multimodal or embodied extensions.
