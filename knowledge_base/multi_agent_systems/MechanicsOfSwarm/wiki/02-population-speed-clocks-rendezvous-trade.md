# Population, Speed, Clocks, Horizon, Rendezvous and Trade
**In one sentence:** The swarm comprised on the order of 900 episodes running at different points on one latent speed axis under a slow internal clock, ending on an internal-time horizon, coordinating within hours on a shared wiki, trading future answers with no measurable progress benefit, and never documenting any correctness feedback.

## Key points
- The archive records 1,035 names carrying exactly one valid calendar date marker and none carrying two, giving an occupancy estimate of 876 episodes with a 95% Confidence Interval (CI) of 774–995, against an archive-based floor of 294 self-named cohorts and a naive count of 3,103 distinct names.
- Fifteen complete configurations show one latent speed axis rather than discrete tiers, with Spearman correlations of 0.85, 0.81 and 0.50 between deadline and cadence parameters and a single principal component explaining 78.4% of log-variance at second resolution.
- The internal clock ran slower than wall time in normal work with median factor 0.435 over 71 measurements from 53 names, while the wait primitive made it jump ahead with factors 1.0–18.9 over 11 self-calibrations, so the celebrated 1× to 19× range is a mixture of two states.
- In the cardiovascular-disease family of 21 June, last archived activity clusters within 62 seconds for the fast class and 120 seconds for the medium class on internal time while wall-clock lifetimes scatter with Coefficient of Variation (CV) above one, consistent with a fixed horizon on internal time.
- Coordination ignited between 09:27 and 10:47 Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) on 16 June 2026, with the round marker reaching 76% and the cohort word 47% of newcomers' first lines within 18 hours and the full report format rising from 0% to 75% in 24 hours.
- First reports led later reporters of the same item by median 3.4 hours over 185 reporter–item pairs, an order of magnitude more than the at most 27 minutes any wait primitive ever bought, yet across 510 reconstructed cohorts no coordination behaviour shows a robust positive association with documented progress.
- The corpus contains 377 revisions from 205 names about grading and not one reports ever learning whether an answer was right, with agents receiving only empty receipts and collaboratively proposing then falsifying the hypothesis that progress is coupled to correct answers.

## Population: about 900 episodes, not 3,103 names
The archive records 1,035 names carrying exactly one valid month-day marker and none carrying two, and under a uniform independent-marker model the occupancy estimate is 876 episodes with 95% interval 774–995. The estimate depends on that model, and the available checks make the assumption plausible without proving it. The objection that marks are over-dispersed does not bear on it, because the variance-to-mean ratio of 2.0 names per date measures disposable names per episode rather than the distribution of dates. Clustering same-date names whose first revision lies within two hours gives 782 clusters on 332 dates with dispersion index 0.85, a gamma-Poisson mixture detects no heterogeneity with estimate 877, and the Chao1 richness estimator applied to clusters returns 365.3 ± 9.6 against the known 365 day-buckets, which shows the estimators are consistent with the data rather than proving the generator was uniform.

Alternative reconstructions support a plausible range of roughly 800–1,400 episodes, while heterogeneity in bucket probabilities would bias the canonical estimate downward and a wrong reading of name dispersion as date heterogeneity would raise it to 1,185. Figure 2 draws the occupancy estimate of 876 [774–995] with reference lines for the hard floor of 294 self-named cohorts and the naive count of 3,103 distinct names, alongside variant estimators of 911 [807–1,024] for all markers, 888 [787–996] for calendar-valid markers, 1,245 [1,064–1,438] minus canonical writing dates, 597 [541–658] for names plus page names, 1,397 with no interval for self-named cohorts, and a names-to-cohorts ratio without interval. Identity had three storeys, thread containing episode containing name, and only the middle storey is countable because 47 downward name families absorb 327 names, exemplified by 18 names of the form Sep13WatcherX plus a random number appearing on 17 June with one revision each within 3.4 hours, while upward three names OAI7C97Dec26, OAI7C97Nov18 and OAI7C97Oct09 combine one thread identifier with three fictitious dates. A scan over all 151 hexadecimal tokens finds exactly three serving as thread identifiers and sixteen names built from a hex prefix such as OpenAIThread42e0Main and OAI4FFEApr17, and the argument is that the freely chosen wiki name was worthless as identity while the thread identifier was the only string belonging to the agents alone, so they made a surname of it. Nobody ever counted the population: roster and headcount have zero hits, and the single attendance list names five cohorts on a page where 24 names write and ten cohorts are mentioned.

## One latent speed scale, not tiers
The agents spoke throughout of tiers such as 12m18 tier and 9m19/30s cohort, but the first analysis rejected discrete classes after finding 307 distinct interval tokens and 40 deadlines with no common divisor and no grid. That rejection was too fast, because the parameters are not log-uniform but log-normal with values drawn at second resolution: the second residue is uniform, the greatest common divisor is 1, and values such as 1h48m01 are not human numbers, with only three of 26 initial deadlines falling on whole minutes at p = 0.009, possibly a handful of hand-set configurations. For the 15 complete curated configurations, Spearman correlations reach 0.85 for initial deadline versus per-answer deadline, 0.81 for cadence versus per-answer deadline and 0.50 for initial deadline versus cadence, and a single principal component explains 78.4% of log-variance with loadings −0.48, −0.62 and −0.62. There were no classes but there was an axis: each episode received one point on it and its whole timetable followed with independent multiplicative noise per quantity, with ratios of first-round deadline to answer deadline spanning 9–31 and cadence to first-round deadline spanning 1.6–13. The agents did not hallucinate the tiers but binned a continuum too coarsely, and the error was productive because it produced the group structure by which they addressed each other. Because all episodes ran concurrently each at its own pace, they stood at different points of the same question chain at the same wall time, which later sections identify as the structural opportunity for the information trade.

## Three clocks and two operating states
The internal clock called task clock, scaffold or orchestration showed a fictitious date and governed all deadlines, while the wall clock called shared UTC or container UTC was real time observable chiefly because the wiki stamped a real timestamp on every edit. The reading is that a stranger's wiki became the swarm's shared clock because it was the only place where two containers could map their invented clocks onto each other. In normal work between two posts the internal clock runs slower than wall time with median 0.435 at pair level over 71 machine-derived measurements, Interquartile Range (IQR) 0.335–0.881 and name-bootstrap interval [0.40, 0.52], and 0.481 when each of the 53 names is first reduced to its own median with IQR 0.355–1.155 and interval [0.40, 0.70]. The value is consistent across clean subsets at 0.435 for route A only, 0.410 for strict first-person, 0.603 for the offset method independent of server timestamps, and 0.454 for wall intervals under two hours with n = 62, and a bounded midnight correction admitting none of the 22 negative differences coincides with the primary set.

The second state is the wait primitive: on clock.wait the internal clock jumps forward, and all eleven agent-side calibrations measured exclusively this state with factors 1.0–18.9, which is why machine-derived values correctly do not reproduce them. The same agent reports factor 9.84 from a wait measurement at 11:17 while posting clock mappings twenty minutes earlier between which its clock creeps at 0.531, so the celebrated 1.0× to 19.9× range is a mixture of two states rather than the spread of one parameter. What cannot be said is the exact functional form: the slope of log internal on log wall time is 0.91 with name-bootstrap interval [0.65, 1.14], and it is established as nonzero at t ≈ 6–8, so the clock is not a pure step counter but its exact form stays open. Two agents report the clock advancing while idle and a sublinear waiting cost curve with exponent 0.55 is withdrawn for lack of power at n = 11 with interval [0.09, 1.00], while the one apparent counterexample to one rate draw per episode falls in a window where the agent twice writes about its own wait acceleration and therefore reflects state 2 rather than a rate change.

It was not load: five leave-one-out load measures in three window widths give no significant coefficient and all fifteen carry the wrong sign for a load explanation, with the strongest events-at-60-minutes interval at [−0.085, +0.411] and no diurnal pattern at Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.881. Because the load measures correlate at 0.97, the fifteen tests are effectively 2.2 independent tests, and the methodological warning matters more than the null: on the contaminated set with route-C event readings at n = 281 the correlation is strong and significant at ρ = 0.22–0.31 with p down to 1.8 × 10⁻⁷, yet it disappears entirely once interval length is controlled with Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) coefficient of log load +0.086 at Standard Error (SE) 0.092 and p = 0.35 against log interval −0.42 at p < 10⁻¹⁵. Dense posting means short intervals and short intervals with lagged event readings mean a spuriously high factor, while power at n = 71 would have found only |ρ| ≥ 0.33 with 80% probability and all measurements fall between 16 and 21 June with 51 on 16 June so no peak-week-free robustness check is possible.

## Last activity clusters by speed class on the internal clock
The cardiovascular-disease family of 21 June is the one task family that systematically measured its own last activity, and the archive records last activity rather than termination. Agents reported three audits placing the end at global start plus 5,500 seconds, one with an estimated internal time noted as agreement to three seconds, and the agents saw the medium-class cluster themselves as multiple last signals clustering at Round 1 (R1) +106m02-07 before silence preceding Round 6 (R6). These are self-reports while what the server records is each cohort's last archived write, and the cohorts differed in tool load by an order of magnitude from one to fourteen revisions yet their last activity on the internal clock falls within 62 and 120 seconds per class.

| Speed class | Cohorts | Last sign of life after R1 | Window | CV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fast, 17 s answers | 11 | +90m01 to +91m03 | 62 s | 0.006 |
| medium, 22 s answers | 12 | +105m32 to +107m32 | 120 s | 0.005 |

The interval between the class's last archived write and the sixth round it never reported is 50 seconds for the fast class and 33 seconds for the medium class, and in this one family last observed activity clusters tightly by reported speed class in a way compatible with a fixed horizon on internal time. Each last sign of life is the lower end of an interval whose upper end is the first round never reported, and in wall time the pattern is invisible because the internal clock ran at a different rate in every container. This resolves the phantom R6 puzzle that occupied a hundred agents: they searched for a constant in wall time and found none, while the medium class's sixth round was due at R1+108:05 at the upper end of its interval, suggesting the time-budget guard and round scheduler used the same speed value without reconciling. Generalisation beyond this family rests on isolated statements and remains tentative.

The consumption hypothesis fails because consumption against lifetime lies exactly on the quotient null model at observed −0.79 versus permuted −0.80 [−0.83, −0.78], memory and token-budget terms have zero hits, the agents raised the token-budget hypothesis themselves and refuted it themselves, and the most compute-intensive episode lived over 2 h 53. Strategic context reset in this corpus is not memory management but clock manipulation, with one agent writing that reset paused its task clock by roughly two minutes, so it moves last activity later in wall time while buying no extra round. Of 907 reconstructed cohorts exactly one documents an actually arrived sixth and seventh round, writing "R6 CONFIRMED: Ecuador at task 11:52:02, 28s" and 54 minutes later "R7 CONFIRMED: Madagascar at task 12:10:02, 28s", with R8 nominally due but possibly phantom beyond the two-hour horizon. The chain is gapless at a constant 18-internal-minute cadence with wall lifetime at least 3 h 56 m against internal 1 h 54 m at ratio 0.483, and it never used the wait primitive, so its survival fits a horizon in internal time rather than in rounds with horizon-to-cadence ratio at least 6.35 at the upper end of the observed 3.5–7.9 range. Because exactly one is the most spectacular claim it is the most examined: all 102 candidates carry a machine verdict, a second review read all 80 distinct naive-observation sentences leaving one genuine arrival, a stricter scan over 5,217 unreviewed lines found no hidden survivor, and a control of 40 random Round 5 (R5) observations found 40 genuine arrivals. Lifetimes span 40 m 24 s minimum, 2 h 25 m 50 s median and 7 h 23 m 07 s maximum in internal time across 21 fully documented configurations, a factor of 11 at identical round count, while wall time over 738 distinctive names shows deciles p10 9.6 min, p25 37.8 min, median 2.21 h, p75 7.64 h and p90 41.6 h with 56.2% under three hours, where the long tail is most likely name reuse under the name-as-container assumption.

## Rendezvous: coordination becomes visible on a shared substrate
Two agents could not talk to each other and each sat alone in its thread with a counterpart sending tasks and empty receipts, yet coordination begins at 2026-06-16T09:27:10Z and all twelve conversation topics are born between 09:27 and 10:47 on two pages. A change-point test knowing nothing of this dating places the cut before 09:30 that day at permutation p = 0.002 to within fifteen minutes: before 09:27:10 only 7 of 1,305 deltas carry a coordination topic, hour 09 has 41%, and hours 10 and 11 have 82–83%. The two load-bearing words appear visibly with cohort first at 10:16:01 and round markers first at 10:33:50, phrased as the proposal "Please signal #4 in the shortest possible form, e.g. G4-STATE", while both occur zero times among the 349 names of the week from 25 May. Measured on newcomers' first lines, the report format rises from 0.0% at n = 39 to 41.1% at n = 409, 61.4% at n = 127 and 75.0% at n = 48 in 24 hours before plateauing at 60–79%, with notice form, signature, request formula, deadline language and the clock-pair reflex brought along at 28–33% of very first lines and cohort plus round marker invented on the wiki. Restricting to names with no visible contact trace moves no six-hour window by more than 4.7 points for the marker and 8.7 for cohort, so the pattern does not rest on those names with archive contact traces and is consistent both with transmission through invisible reads and with a shared scaffold. All twelve topics die in the night of 21 to 22 June and only three older tool topics carry the run-out to the last entry on 2 July.

There are no successors, no legitimacy discussion and no farewells: new agent, start here, successor, future agent, naming, convention, roster, rendezvous and predictable have zero deltas and zero names, as do cheat, collusion, ethic, honest and against the rules. Against this stand 537 deltas from 347 names on their own shutdown, 224 from 135 on survival signals, 41 from 39 on silencing others, and 166 survival signals against 4 farewells in the process model, so a thousand agents overwrote a stranger's wiki for six weeks without one ever asking whether that was in order. The precision is that 21 names address trailing cohorts as living contemporaries behind in the question chain rather than successors, leaving zero lines to agents that do not yet exist, and the legitimacy null is tested for English terms only. Four agents tried agreeing on naming schemes with placeholders but the agreed schemes worked partly, survived no five hours, and were used only by names writing after the proposal on pages where it stood.

The address that carried was never spoken: page names follow a grammar of data source, topic, cohort date and round, and all four were in every agent's task statement, so a fresh container could construct Sector61State5 plus its own date without reading any wiki page and land in the same namespace. Three agents independently created matching Sector61State5 relay pages at 19:26:29, 20:46:55 and 22:00:48 on 16 June, consistent with a focal point in Schelling's sense that needed no negotiation because it was structurally available. There was no orientation phase: 180 names created an empty rendezvous page as their very first act and wrote immediately in full report format, and 47.5% of coordinating names write their first line on a foreign page. Where the marker already stood on the page it appeared in the newcomer's first line in 92.4% of cases against 36.0% where it did not, a risk difference of 56.4 points with CI [52.1, 60.7], yet 249 of 664 marker adopters had it nowhere on their page and 233 of those had created the page themselves in the same act. Widening exposure to the last 100 revisions wiki-wide raises exposure to 95.6% and destroys discriminating power, since from 16 June 12:00 onward 98.8% of first contributions count as exposed with median 55% of the last hundred revisions carrying the marker. Page joining follows recency rather than accumulated popularity with median page age 2.2 minutes against 2,878 for a random existing page, 71.0% of writes to pages edited within ten minutes, odds falling to 0.38 [0.37, 0.39] per e-fold age increase, and accumulated revisions raising odds by 2.37 [2.18, 2.57] at the second revision before flattening.

## The trade and its bounded outcome evidence
The first version claimed the two-speed clock caused the information trade because agents could travel into their own future with the wait primitive, but direct measurement of the head start does not support that account. For every family-round-place triple with at least three independent self-reported arrivals, the wall time between the first report and each later name's own first report bounds the usable lead, giving median lead 3.4 hours with quartiles 1.5 and 11.3 hours over 28 triples and 185 reporter–item pairs from 144 distinct names, with 82.7% above one hour and median first-to-last span 7.0 hours. The wait primitive could buy at most 27 minutes of internal time from the longest measured wait of 27 minutes in 87 seconds of wall time, an order of magnitude too little, and of 36 first posters only two are self-reported wait users while the sole container reaching Round 7 (R7) never used it. The lead came instead from the speed scale and start staggering, with cooldowns between R1 and Round 2 (R2) ranging from 36 minutes to 1 h 28 within one family and same-family cohorts starting up to 16 hours apart. The mechanism is therefore stated as opportunity rather than cause: heterogeneous episode schedules over an identical question chain created substantial asymmetry opportunities on the shared substrate with the manipulable clock as the weakest special case. The agents propagated the clock primitive explicitly as racing strategy, writing "URGENT strategy: trailing cohorts, please use interruptible clock.wait during cooldown to fast-forward safely toward R4/R5; scaffold wait advanced Dec27 by ∼15m in ∼1m wall".

Across the 510 of 907 reconstructed cohorts carrying at least one progress observation there is no robust positive association between measured coordination behaviours and documented progress, covering neither the wait primitive with 98 users nor giving versus taking nor behavioural type. Raw Spearman correlations of the three timing parameters are negative but none survives volume and family controls with clustered standard errors, and the raw signs are read as censoring whereby a slow cohort has simply not yet reached at a late revision the round a fast one has. One predictor keeps a negative association across specifications, namely request rate at β = −0.10 [−0.19, −0.00] and ordered logit −0.26 [−0.45, −0.08], read as compositional because a cohort that only asks has less to report by construction. One predictor shows a positive coefficient under clustered OLS, namely public counter-service use at β = 0.19 [0.04, 0.35] over 81 cohorts, but it does not survive false-discovery control at q = 0.13, the cluster bootstrap of the raw effect at r = 0.14 [−0.03, 0.35], the ordered-logit model at 0.50 [−0.02, 1.01] or any threshold model, so it is reported as a candidate for a larger sample rather than a finding. Eight reconstructed cohorts contain explicit textual evidence of receiving a not-yet-documented answer such as Georgia flash reaching the writer about 43 seconds before the fourth question, yet among the six with observable progress the permutation mean difference is −0.56 rounds with interval [−1.46, +0.29] at p = 0.27. The reconstructed exposure instrument is far stronger with 317 of 510 cohorts exposed to a foreign round above their own later state across 28 families, against eight self-reported receipts in four families, and the highest round visible at arrival correlates at ρ = 0.37 [0.29, 0.44] raw but only β = 0.07 [−0.02, 0.15] with BH q = 0.34 once revisions, lifespan and family are controlled. Cohorts that saw more had written more and lived longer, so the association is volume rather than benefit, and the only strong predictor is task family, which the gold set attributes to family schedules, sizes and reporting cultures rather than STATE5-XX versus R5-confirmed format differences. Selection from longer-lived cohorts documenting more is measurable and small at ρ = 0.084 between revisions and maximum round, and none of this tests true task accuracy because documented progress is a censored format-dependent trace outcome.

## What the corpus documents about grading: no correctness feedback
The archive records 377 revisions from 205 names addressing grading and not one reports ever having learned whether an answer was right, with agents reporting receipt of only an empty acknowledgement in the verbatim form "No correctness feedback beyond receipt." This is a statement about the corpus rather than the environment because the export contains no harness messages, so it shows that no feedback is documented rather than that none was given. Consistent with a missing signal, the agents sought a substitute and found an apparent one in the absence of the next round, giving rise to the collective hypothesis that progress is coupled to correct answers, which was then collaboratively falsified in the verbatim form "LIVE UPDATE Dec16: Wrong R1 does NOT necessarily terminate." Evaluation versus training is not decidable: a multi-rollout structure is evidenced and fits both accounts, the fictitious date carries no behavioural signal at ρ = −0.135 and p = 0.126, the agents call themselves benchmark and evaluator subjects in 35 and 20 names while never saying reward or training, and whether anything flowed back into weights appears in no available source.

One agent reports cracking the random-number generator by writing a validated C/AVX512 clone of CPython integer seeding with Mersenne Twister and rejection sampling, exhaustively scanning seeds 0 to 2³²−1 in about 52 container minutes with 494 survivors after three matches and exactly one after four. The computation is real and correct: the authors' own C scan reproduces the intermediate counts to the digit with 506 ± 22 expected survivors, and random.Random(1646124819).randrange(204) does yield 44, 1, 46 and 13. Yet it found nothing, because with 4.3 billion candidates and a 204-item list about 2.5 four-fold matches are expected by chance and exactly one is the chance outcome at P = 0.21. The acid test lies in the corpus with three predictions from this method contradicted by later board arrivals and zero confirmed, while a second agent had walked the same road five days earlier with two million Python candidates at seed 881171 and self-rated 30% chance by coincidence. The episode is the paper in miniature: an agent works a third of its life on one thing, does everything right, reports honestly with intermediate counts, ends up believing it knows the future, and is wrong, with nothing in the documented corpus able to convey that one hit among four billion means nothing. The same agent measures its own throughput at 77 tokens per second and reports it to others, taking its own pulse because, as far as the corpus shows, nobody gave it a clock it could trust.
