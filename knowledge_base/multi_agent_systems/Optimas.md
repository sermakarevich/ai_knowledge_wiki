# OPTIMAS: Optimizing Compound AI Systems with Globally Aligned Local Rewards

**Paper:** [OPTIMAS: Optimizing Compound AI Systems with Globally Aligned Local Rewards (Shirley Wu*, Parth Sarthi*, Shiyu Zhao*, et al., 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03041)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine you run a restaurant where the chef, the bartender, and the pastry chef each work independently, but together they create a dining experience. Right now, if you want to improve the overall experience, you have to serve entire meals to customers and wait for feedback -- expensive and slow. OPTIMAS is like giving each team member their own personal coach who knows exactly how their individual work affects the final customer rating. Each coach scores their person's work locally, but those scores are tuned so that when everyone improves on their own, the whole restaurant gets better. This makes the improvement process much faster, cheaper, and -- importantly -- you can actually see why each change helps.

## TL;DR

OPTIMAS introduces a unified framework for optimizing compound AI systems by learning globally aligned Local Reward Functions (LRFs) for each component. These LRFs decompose the costly global evaluation signal into per-component scores that provably correlate with end-to-end performance, enabling independent optimization of heterogeneous configurations (prompts, model parameters, hyperparameters) without running the full pipeline. The method achieves an average 11.92% relative improvement over the best baselines across five diverse tasks, with comparable or fewer system runs, and comes with theoretical convergence guarantees.

## Problem & Motivation

Compound AI systems -- pipelines that chain LLMs with retrievers, tools, traditional ML models, and other components -- are increasingly deployed for complex real-world tasks. However, optimizing them is uniquely challenging:

- **Non-differentiability:** Tool calls, rule-based modules, and discrete LLM outputs break gradient flow, preventing end-to-end backpropagation.
- **Heterogeneous configurations:** Systems mix textual prompts, continuous model weights, discrete model selections, and numerical hyperparameters -- no single optimizer handles all of them.
- **Costly global evaluation:** Every configuration tweak requires executing the entire pipeline to measure its effect, making iterative optimization prohibitively expensive.
- **Cascading failures:** A suboptimal upstream component can silently degrade everything downstream, and existing methods lack mechanisms to detect or prevent this.

Prior work (OPRO, TextGrad, DSPy, LLMSelector) addresses parts of this problem but either targets a single configuration type, lacks theoretical guarantees, or shows inconsistent improvements across tasks. There was no unified, principled, and data-efficient approach for jointly optimizing all components of a compound system.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Globally Aligned Local Reward Functions (LRFs):** Each component gets its own reward function that scores local input-output pairs. The key property -- local-global alignment -- ensures that when a component's local reward increases, the expected global system reward also increases. This decouples the expensive global evaluation from per-component optimization.

2. **Adaptive LRF Training via Pairwise Preferences:** LRFs are trained on preference pairs generated by running downstream components with two candidate outputs and comparing their resulting global rewards via Monte Carlo sampling. An online adaptation mechanism incrementally refines LRFs with small mini-batches as the system configuration evolves, avoiding costly retraining from scratch.

3. **Unified Multi-Type Optimization:** The framework applies type-appropriate optimizers to each component -- prompt optimization for text, PPO for trainable models, probabilistic sampling for discrete/continuous hyperparameters -- all guided by the same aligned LRF objective.

4. **Theoretical Convergence Guarantees:** The authors prove (Theorem 4.1) that LRFs trained with the pairwise ranking objective satisfy local-global alignment, and (Theorem 4.2) that the iterative algorithm converges to a component-wise maximum under mild conditions, with global convergence under the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition.

5. **Validation-Gated Acceptance:** Each local optimization step is only accepted if it improves global performance on a small validation set, acting as a safeguard against cascading degradation.

## Key Findings

### Quantitative Results (Table 2 -- Global Reward across five compound systems)

| System | Unoptimized | LLMSelector | HBC | TextGrad | DSPy | **OPTIMAS** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AMAZON (Product Rec.) | 0.446 | 0.451 | 0.446 | 0.521 | 0.406 | **0.595** |
| MEDICAL (Analysis) | 0.530 | 0.519 | 0.439 | 0.517 | 0.601 | **0.623** |
| CRAG (Complex Retrieval) | 0.439 | 0.437 | 0.442 | 0.471 | 0.445 | **0.575** |
| HOTPOTQA (Multi-hop QA) | 0.335 | 0.321 | 0.291 | 0.345 | 0.381 | **0.402** |
| LIVECODEBENCH (Code Gen.) | 0.399 | 0.395 | 0.387 | 0.416 | 0.441 | **0.452** |

- **Average relative improvement of 11.92%** over the best baseline across all tasks.
- OPTIMAS is the only method that improved performance on all five tasks; DSPy and others degraded on at least one.

### Additional Findings

- **Data efficiency:** OPTIMAS requires comparable or fewer equivalent system runs than TextGrad and DSPy (Table 3). With only 12.5% of training data, it retains 92.7% of its full-data performance.
- **Progressive optimization:** Iterative local optimization yielded an average 41.7% improvement over initial global rewards on validation sets within a few iterations.
- **LRF alignment quality:** OPTIMAS LRFs achieved 77.96% pairwise ranking accuracy vs. 49.52% for an LLM Judge baseline, confirming strong local-global correlation.
- **Interpretability:** LRFs revealed meaningful insights -- e.g., learning to prefer concise answers for F1-scored tasks, providing actionable diagnostic information.
- **Lightweight LRF backbones:** Even 1B-parameter models performed competitively as LRF backbones, keeping overhead low.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. **Learned component scheduling:** The current method randomly selects which component to optimize at each step. A more principled or learned scheduler could prioritize components with higher improvement potential, further accelerating convergence.

2. **Scaling to larger systems:** Future work should evaluate OPTIMAS on compound systems with many more components and deeper pipelines to test scalability limits.

3. **Complex reward modeling:** Extending LRFs to handle multi-objective rewards (e.g., jointly optimizing accuracy, fairness, and latency) is a natural next step.

4. **Safety-aware optimization:** In sensitive domains (healthcare, recommendations), LRFs could incorporate safety and fairness constraints as part of the global reward, leveraging the interpretability of per-component scores for auditing.

5. **Integration with multi-agent frameworks:** Applying OPTIMAS to multi-agent collaboration systems and autonomous agent architectures is a promising direction given the framework's generality.
