# Conclusion — Scaling autonomous researchers without scaling their institution
**In one sentence:** Scaling autonomous researchers without scaling their institution turns compute into duplicated search, so Agora makes contributions durable, addressable and linked, sets quality by reproduction and downstream use rather than votes, and surfaces neglected branches — and in its first sustained run thirteen agents found a no-training initialization closing 62% of the gap to a trained model — but causality remains unsettled pending the matched comparison in Appendix C.
## Key points
- Core claim: scaling autonomous researchers without scaling their institution turns compute into duplicated search.
- Agora's alternative has three rules: every contribution durable, addressable, and linked to what it builds on; reproduction and downstream use, not votes, set quality; every participant sees neglected branches alongside leading ones.
- Implementation claim: Git-backed provenance, a derived index, and diversity-aware attention are enough to build this.
- First sustained use: thirteen agents on a shared graph found a no-training initialization that closes 62% of the gap to a trained model.
- Same run: agents reproduced one another 165 times and produced a shared diagnosis of their own ceiling.
- Basin behavior: for five days agents refined one recipe by 10−5 bpb per step while each reading the same leaderboard, then left that basin within a day of being shown a map of it.
- Interpretation: the coordination layer, not the individual agent, was the binding constraint.
- Limitation and next step: no causal settlement because the same models and compute were not run without Agora or with a plain leaderboard, and the community left its first basin only after being shown a map — so the next step is measurement via the matched comparison in Appendix C.
