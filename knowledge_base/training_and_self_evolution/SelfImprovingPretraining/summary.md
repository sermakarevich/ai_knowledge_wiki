# Self-Improving Pretraining: using post-trained models to pretrain better models

**Paper:** [Self-Improving Pretraining: using post-trained models to pretrain better models (Tan et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21343)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine you're learning from a textbook written by amateurs versus one written by seasoned professors. This paper asks: what if we used the "professor" version of an AI to rewrite the learning materials before teaching the next generation? Instead of training new AI models on raw, messy internet text, they use a polished, already-trained AI to clean up and enhance that text first -- and the student models turn out significantly smarter as a result.

## TL;DR

The paper proposes Self-Improving Pretraining (SIP), a method that uses an existing post-trained model to rewrite and enhance raw pretraining corpora before training new foundation models on them. By integrating the refined knowledge distribution of post-trained models into the pretraining stage, SIP yields improvements in quality, safety, factuality, and reasoning without altering the standard pretraining compute budget.

---

## Problem & Motivation

Standard LLM development maintains a strict two-stage pipeline: (1) pretrain on massive raw web corpora, then (2) post-train with instruction tuning and RLHF. The raw pretraining data is noisy, unfiltered, and far from the ideal distribution the model ultimately needs. This creates a bottleneck where billions of tokens of suboptimal data are consumed before alignment begins. The authors ask: why not use the knowledge of already-strong post-trained models to make pretraining data better before it is ever consumed?

---

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Post-trained model as data curator** -- An existing strong post-trained model evaluates and rewrites pretraining documents, producing synthetic continuations or improved versions that reflect the refined distribution learned during post-training.

2. **Prefix-conditioned suffix generation** -- The framework generates synthetic training examples by feeding a document prefix to the post-trained model and sampling a high-quality suffix, creating prefix-suffix pairs that replace or augment the original data.

3. **Curriculum data mixing** -- Synthetic data generated by the post-trained model is blended with original corpora at varying ratios, providing a curriculum that balances breadth (raw data) with quality (synthetic data).

4. **RL feedback integration at pretraining** -- Reinforcement learning signals are effectively pushed earlier in the pipeline; the post-trained model's implicit reward signal is encoded into the synthetic data itself, not applied after the fact.

---

## Key Findings

| Dimension | Result |
|---|---|
| Reasoning | Consistent gains over baselines on math and logical tasks |
| Factuality | Improved factual accuracy from synthetic data rewriting |
| Safety | Better safety metrics compared to models trained on raw corpora |
| Efficiency | No increase in pretraining compute required |

- Improvements are most pronounced in **mathematical reasoning** and **factual knowledge** domains.
- Performance gains hold across diverse benchmark suites (quality, safety, and task-specific evaluations).
- Higher ratios of synthetic data improve quality up to a point; mixing with original corpora remains important for coverage.

---

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. **Scale synthetic data generation** -- Explore generating larger proportions of the pretraining corpus synthetically to further close the gap between raw and ideal data distributions.
2. **Diverse teacher architectures** -- Investigate using multiple or heterogeneous post-trained models as data sources to inject complementary strengths.
3. **Capability-targeted transfer** -- Identify which specific post-training capabilities (math, safety, instruction following) transfer most efficiently to the pretraining stage.
4. **Domain-specific application** -- Apply SIP to specialized domains (e.g., code, scientific text) where raw corpora quality is particularly variable.
