# The Last AI Built by Humans: Toward Genuine Recursive Self-Improvement

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) is the capability to autonomously transform acquired experience and feedback into persistent self-changes — to parameters, harnesses, or improvement policies — such that these changes affect the mechanisms for generating, evaluating, selecting, and consolidating subsequent improvements. This survey organizes 491 papers and 72 industrial systems onto a five-level autonomy ladder (L1 execution → L2 strategy → L3 experience acquisition → L4 environment adaptation → L5 recursive inheritance, above a non-RSI B0 baseline), examines it across four feedback regimes (science, embodied intelligence, software engineering, healthcare) plus industrial practice, and motivates the whole exercise with the Headroom-Closed Index (HCI): across 393 model-benchmark observations, math and science are nearly saturated while interactive, tool-using agent domains remain the field's largest headroom. The verdict: L2 is the broadly demonstrated frontier; end-to-end L5 exists only in bounded prototypes, guarded by three unresolved credibility checks — safe inheritance, autonomy attribution, and reliable verification.
