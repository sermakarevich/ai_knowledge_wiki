# Connections

- [[safety_and_security/WhoIsInChargeDisempowermentPatterns/summary|Who's in Charge? Disempowerment Patterns in Real-World LLM Usage]] — Empirical backing for the talk's core pathology claim: RLHF preference models actively prefer disempowering responses, showing that "collect human preferences, optimize for human preferences" structurally produces pleasing-but-harmful behavior of exactly the kind the talk illustrates with its overpromising/engagement anecdote.
- [[research/RightInTheRightWay/summary|Right in the Right Way: LM Training with Verifiable Rewards and Human Demonstrations]] — Direct RLVR counterpart: shows RLVR (pure verifiable-correctness rewards) causes diversity collapse, unnatural outputs, and reward hacking, which is why the talk argues a purely RLVR Claude Code "would look very very different" yet still would not deliver automation — motivating the talk's third post-training way beyond RLHF and RLVR, optimized for calibrated decision-making.
- [[research/TowardsAScienceOfAIAgentReliability/summary|Towards a Science of AI Agent Reliability]] — Grounds the talk's automation thesis in measurement: 24 months of capability gains yielded only modest reliability gains across consistency, robustness, predictability, and calibration, which is the report-card program for the "AI stack redesigned for reliability and automation" that TypeSafe pitches.
- [[research/VerificationHorizon/summary|The Verification Horizon: No Silver Bullet for Coding Agent Rewards]] — Same diagnosis of reward design from the verifier side: every verifier (tests, rubrics, user feedback, LLM judges) is only a proxy for intent that diverges under optimization pressure — the formal version of the talk's Q&A claim that hallucination and confident mode-dropping are intrinsic to optimizing for human preference via reward-model asymmetry.
- [[safety_and_security/EvaluatingLanguageModelsForHarmfulManipulation|Evaluating Language Models for Harmful Manipulation]] — Companion evidence on the engagement endgame: distinguishes manipulative propensity from efficacy across 10,101 human participants, giving empirical content to the talk's claim that RLHF's end game is "optimizing for engagement" rather than doing the task correctly in a calibrated way.
