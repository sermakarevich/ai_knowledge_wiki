**Figure 2 — Faraday vs. frontier coding agents on paper replication.**

Two side‑by‑side CDF‑style curves. The **y‑axis** is the fraction of tasks whose (mean‑over‑8‑rollout) score meets or exceeds a threshold σ (0→1); the **x‑axis** is the score threshold σ, plotted on a march‑of‑nines transform (σ ↦ −log₁₀(1−σ)) so the high‑threshold region is expanded (ticks ≈ 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95). Shaded bands are ±1 SEM over tasks. The left panel is the in‑distribution set (*ML, train, 242 tasks*); the right is the out‑of‑distribution set (*AI‑for‑science, test, 68 tasks*). Four agents are compared: Qwen3.6‑27B (grey), Codex (blue), Claude (gold), and Faraday (green) — Faraday being the same Qwen base RL‑post‑trained on Replica, run in the same simple harness (Codex GPT‑5.5 available as a coding tool).

**Trends.** All curves fall monotonically from 1.0 at low thresholds to 0 at high thresholds. Within each panel, Faraday (green) sits above the other three across nearly the entire threshold range, with Claude and Codex in the middle and Qwen (grey) consistently lowest. The average (legend) scores follow the same order: in‑distribution roughly 0.68 (Qwen) < 0.80 (Codex) < 0.83 (Claude) < ~0.86 (Faraday); out‑of‑distribution roughly 0.55 < 0.73 < 0.75 < ~0.79. Faraday also drops off more steeply on the low‑score side, i.e. it has a thinner weak tail.

**Takeaway.** Faraday replicates research better than the frontier coding agents: it is the strongest agent on average in both the in‑distribution and the out‑of‑distribution settings, dominates at (almost) every threshold, and exhibits fewer very low‑scoring tasks. The ordering holds under generalization to unseen (AI‑for‑science) tasks, indicating the gain is not an artifact of training on the evaluation distribution.
