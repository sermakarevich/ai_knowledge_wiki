# RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses

**Paper:** [RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24972) — arXiv, 2026-09-22

## Human Readable TL;DR

Think of an AI agent as a chef working in a kitchen: the model is the chef, but the harness is everything around them — the recipes, the layout of the tools, the checklists, and the cleanup routine. Recent work lets the chef rearrange their own kitchen by trial and error, but this tends to overfit, like organizing the shelves perfectly for last week's menu while becoming slower and clumsier at any new dish. RRSI acts like a strict kitchen consultant that only allows a few changes at a time, throws out gimmicks that only work for one recipe, and rejects expensive gadgets unless they clearly earn their keep. The result is a tidier, more general kitchen that cooks better everywhere, not just on the practice menu, while using fewer ingredients and steps.

## TL;DR

RRSI frames iterative harness evolution as adaptive empirical optimization over a fully open edit space and shows that prior methods gain largely from increased test-time computation rather than reusable mechanisms, with large evolve-set improvements that shrink or vanish out of distribution. It regularizes the search trajectory rather than the hypothesis space, splitting constraints into proposal-side capacity control (annealed update sparsity, evidence-aware credit assignment over full history, and structured exploration during stalls) and selection-side survival criteria (leakage screening, a noise-adjusted stability floor, Ridge-style complexity-aware acceptance, and Lasso-style structural pruning). Across eight benchmarks in coding, agentic workspace, and engineering design, RRSI improves up to 14.1 points on the evolve split and every held-out split by up to 4.7 points, outperforming the average prior baseline by up to 22.9% while running on 30% fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution.

## Problem & Motivation

Modern LLM agents are systems rather than standalone models: a frozen backbone policy wrapped in a harness of prompts, control flow, tool interfaces, memory, skills, and context management that largely determines whether the same model reads the right file, recovers from failures, and produces valid deliverables. Because manual harness engineering is bottlenecked by how many trajectories a human can inspect, recent methods automate it by repeatedly proposing and selecting harness edits using feedback from a finite evolve set, creating a practical form of recursive self-improvement at the agent-system level. The problem is that this loop reuses the same evolve set adaptively across rounds, so evolve-set scores can climb through benchmark-specific fitting, noise chasing, and complexity accumulation without improving the underlying mechanism. The paper documents this gap empirically: prior methods retain little of their evolve-set gain on held-out benchmarks, with several finishing below the unevolved base harness, and their gains trace largely to spending more test-time tokens and steps rather than to transferable improvements.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Open reachable set with regularized trajectory.** RRSI defines Ω(H) as all harnesses reachable by arbitrary source edits and deliberately leaves it unconstrained, instead regularizing how finite evolve-set feedback guides movement through it. Proposal-side rules control how much adaptive capacity is exercised per round and where it is spent, while selection-side rules control which measured improvements are strong, efficient, and leakage-free enough to become permanent state.

2. **Annealed L0-style update sparsity.** Each round's candidate is limited to at most b(t) independently attributable edits under a cosine schedule decaying from b_max to b_min, so early rounds can make coordinated changes while late rounds are sparse and attributable. This bounds update cardinality like an L0 constraint without optimizing any L0-penalized objective, preventing bundled unrelated edits that fit feedback idiosyncrasies and obscure credit assignment.

3. **Evidence-aware credit assignment and structured exploration.** Every evaluated candidate logs its modified component, hypothesis, diff, score and cost deltas, and acceptance outcome, and the proposer conditions on this full history so rejected mechanisms remain negative evidence and successful ones retain explicit credit. When progress over a window w stays within the noise band δ, a small reserved portion of the proposal budget is redirected toward unexplored components, acting as a diversity or entropy-like redirect of limited capacity without changing which mechanisms the harness may contain.

4. **Leakage screening and stability-aware acceptance.** Before full evaluation, a critic rejects diffs encoding task names, entity names, task-specific values, answers, or benchmark-specific logic, so leaking candidates never earn the inflated scores that would attract further search. Survivors must then clear a noise-adjusted floor estimated from repeated evaluations of the unchanged base harness, Ŝ(H′) ≥ S★ − δ, which permits within-tolerance fluctuation while blocking downhill walks through accumulated noise-sized regressions.

5. **Ridge-style complexity-aware acceptance with Lasso-style structural pruning.** For gains above noise (ΔS > δ), RRSI requires ΔC ≤ β₀ + β₁ΔS on relative policy-token cost, allowing more inference cost only with proportionally larger improvement and otherwise shrinking aggregate footprint growth without forcing any component's removal. Within the noise band, a shaped rule weighing score change, cost change, and structural novelty decides admissibility, while a separate pruning rule flags components with no strictly positive measured gain over a fixed window as deletion targets, sparsifying the retained harness so every mechanism must earn its place.

## Key Findings

RRSI improves every split outside the evolve set across all three domains, with evolve-set gains of 6.0 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 4.9 on EngDesign, and 1.1 on Harvey LAB, alongside a 1.8-point gain on never-scored SWE-bench Verified, 2.3 points on the pristine in-distribution Harvey LAB held-out, 3.5 to 4.7 points (7.2%–13.1%) on the three out-of-distribution agentic benchmarks, and 4.3 Medal points (24.3% relative) on Frontier-Eng. The baseline comparison inverts out of distribution: RRSI posts the smallest evolve-set gain of any evolved harness yet the only out-of-distribution average clearing the base by more than a point (43.6 versus 39.7), while Meta-Harness adds only 0.9, HarnessX lands on base, and AHE and TTHE finish below base. Ablations confirm the trade is caused by the regularizers, since removing acceptance constraints lifts evolve score from 90.5 to 91.5 while dropping OOD average from 43.6 to 41.0 and raising token cost by half, and removing both regularizers yields the highest evolve score of all (92.8) but leaves OOD near base at 40.3 on 3.80M versus 2.42M tokens per trial. Transfer is not a judge-styling artifact and not tied to one backbone: it survives deterministic simulation grading on EngDesign and Frontier-Eng, replicates under Gemini 3.5 Flash (+14.1 evolve, +2.2 OOD), and even transfers to an unseen weaker backbone, with the Gemini-evolved coding harness lifting Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite from 11.2 to 14.6. RRSI is also the lightest evolved harness at 26.3 steps per trial against 27.3–34.6 for prior methods, showing that evolution still buys part of its gain with compute but the budget decides how much.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper's stated limitations point directly to future work: extending regularized self-improvement beyond frozen backbones to joint weight-and-harness updates, reducing reliance on a finite evolve set and on several regularization hyperparameters sensitive to feedback quality and search budget, and validating transfer across more architectures, tool ecosystems, and longer self-improvement horizons. Natural extensions include adaptive or learned schedules for the edit budget, noise band, and cost-tradeoff parameters rather than evolve-set-fixed values, richer leakage and novelty critics, and tighter integration with concurrent generalization-as-objective and diversity-archive approaches since the contribution is orthogonal to what is edited. Broader validation on longer runs matters because pruning, credit assignment, and stall-driven exploration may behave differently once the harness has accumulated many mechanisms and the easy reusable gains are exhausted.
