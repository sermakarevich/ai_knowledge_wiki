# An Ontology-Guided, Deduplication-Aware Extraction Layer for Knowledge Graph Construction from Heterogeneous Documents

**Paper:** [An Ontology-Guided, Deduplication-Aware Extraction Layer for Knowledge Graph Construction from Heterogeneous Documents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28662v1) — arXiv, 22 Jul 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine asking several librarians to catalogue the same pile of messy newspaper clippings, and each one invents their own subject headings, spells names differently, and files duplicate cards for the same person — that is what a raw large language model does when it builds a knowledge graph. This paper describes a strict head librarian that hands each cataloguer only the relevant page of the official subject catalogue (fetched live from a graph database), then runs a careful clean-up crew that expands abbreviations, hunts the original text for misspellings, and checks roles and employers before merging two similar-looking names. It also sorts mixed paperwork page by page, sending clean typed pages down one chute and scanned or photographed pages to vision-based reading, instead of forcing the whole folder down one path. The result is a system that finds far more of the right people and connections while refusing to merge two different people who happen to share a name, much like a good librarian who would rather flag an uncertain card for review than file it in the wrong drawer.

## TL;DR

The paper presents a production Kafka-based extraction layer that converts heterogeneous documents (text, PDF, spreadsheets, Word, PowerPoint, images) into a validated, ontology-aligned JSON knowledge graph using a locally hosted ontology-tuned Qwen3.5-9B model with live Neo4j ontology-slice retrieval injected into the prompt. Retrieval replaces static domain catalog slices (~11,200 tokens) with content-conditioned class-level retrieval (~700 tokens, ~94% reduction) under a PageRank penalty, 0.72 cosine floor, and token budget, refined by term vectors, subclass expansion, predicate full-text search, and density-ranked windowing plus prompt and finalization guards for predicate fan-out, orientation, and vocabulary canonicalization. A five-stage pipeline (chunked LLM extraction, per-chunk cleaning, cross-chunk merging, relationship second pass, post-extraction enhancement) pairs six deterministic zero-inference rule-based deduplication algorithms with an optional embedding-based resolution subsystem guarded by hard-conflict rules that no similarity score can override. Evaluation on intelligence-domain corpora reports search recall rising from ~70% to ~95% with zero false merges, correction of seven silent quality-defect classes, and on an adversarial image-only naval corpus a cut in hallucinations from 174 to zero with tripled relationship coverage, at the cost of heuristic thresholds tuned on the development corpus and small-sample public benchmarks.

## Problem & Motivation

Large language models extract entities and relationships fluently but inconsistently: type vocabularies fracture across documents and chunks (the same class surfacing as GovBody, Government Organization, or Ministry), the same person appears under several spelling and abbreviation variants, relationships duplicate with varying qualifier detail, and distinct same-named individuals risk silent conflation. The paper frames this as a production problem in a real-time intelligence-document stream, where graph searches missed roughly a quarter of valid matches from name variation while naive merging threatened to attribute one person's actions to another. Prior LLM knowledge-graph work (zero-shot, NER-style, generative and revisited relation extraction) reports the same failure modes of fragmented vocabularies, hallucinated relations, and description-over-relation bias, and the authors position their contribution not as a new extraction model but as an architecture that constrains and repairs a stock model's output at every stage.

The practical constraints sharpen the problem further. Static ontology catalog slices injected whole domains at ~17.7K tokens worst case, crowding a 32K context window, misrouting on opaque filenames and synonyms, and drifting from the live schema between rebuilds. Binary whole-document PDF routing either discarded scanned pages or wastefully OCRed born-digital ones, while multi-phase chunked extraction introduced its own defects: title-prefixed duplicates, case-variant relationship types, self-loop hallucinations, type misassignment in later chunks, heterogeneous IDs, and abbreviation false positives. The motivation is therefore defense in depth: steer the model with only the ontology it needs, route each page optimally, then clean, merge, and deduplicate deterministically with full provenance and human review rather than silent merges.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Live content-conditioned ontology retrieval.** The static per-domain catalog files and keyword router are replaced with live Neo4j vector retrieval over class and predicate definition embeddings using multi-span windowing (~1,500 chars, 300-char overlap, up to four windows), PageRank-penalised scoring `score(c) = cos(q,c) × (1 − λ·pr(c))` with λ = 0.4, a 0.72 floor, and a ~5,000-token budget split 70/30 between classes and predicates. Version-keyed caching and a 60-second circuit breaker degrade gracefully to un-guided extraction, and post-extraction `flag_novel_types()` surfaces novel emissions for human-reviewed schema growth. Grounding is steering rather than a hard constraint, and the measured effect is ~11,200 → ~700 catalog tokens with the retained tokens being the content-nearest classes rather than a truncation.

2. **Retrieval refinements: term vectors and subclass expansion.** Because a long prose window embeds to a blurred centroid distant from terse ontology labels, each window is additionally queried as a compact proper-noun and abbreviation term vector with best-match-per-class scoring, matching the register of ontology definitions more tightly. Because vector retrieval favours well-described generic parents, every class scoring ≥ 0.80 triggers one subClassOf hop downward with children injected at 0.78, so the model sees SecurityForce or TerroristGroup alongside Organization and can choose the tighter fit. Predicate retrieval is correspondingly made subclass-aware, lifting recovery from 3 tangential predicates at baseline to 68 with complete hierarchy coverage.

3. **Predicate prompt guards and finalization conformance.** Retrieval alone does not bind a 9B local model, which still over-emits, mislabels, and mis-orients relationships, so three deterministic ontology-grounded guards compose as retrieval steers, the prompt restrains, and finalization enforces. A prompt fan-out guard (explicitly-stated-only wording plus a no-grid clause) collapsed a 2×58 CLAIMED_BY Cartesian grid from 116 edges to 1 and total relations from 258 to 112. Rank-guarded orientation canonicalizes inverse and downward phrasings into upward subordinate-to with endpoint swapping by coarse formation tier on organisation↔organisation edges, and vocabulary canonicalization snaps predicates to 954 ontology object-property keys via exact, de-pluralised, then ≥ 0.80 embedding tiers, flagging the rest as novel for admin review before the dedup key sees them.

4. **Six-signal per-page PDF classifier with mixed-path merging.** Each PDF page is routed by a priority-ordered cascade over six PyMuPDF structural signals (page area, image-block coverage, xref-image coverage, character count, drawing primitives, xref count), sending image-dominant pages to OCR/vision, character-rich pages to text extraction, vector-drawn pages to OCR, and degenerate pages to skip. Document-level routing by text-page ratio (≥ 80% text, ≤ 20% OCR, otherwise mixed) partitions mixed documents so text and OCR pages flow through their respective pipelines and merge via cross-chunk infrastructure with a relationship second pass and ID reassignment, eliminating the information loss of binary routing.

5. **Six zero-inference rule-based deduplication algorithms.** On a provenance-carrying schema (five-plus aliases per entity with AliasOccurrence records, context attributes, scored PossibleDuplicate flags, and exported dedup_candidates), the layer runs deterministic Person-only alias expansion, Ratcliff/Obershelp source-text mining at τ = 0.85 over consecutive word pairs, a five-signal weighted name-similarity composite (Jaro–Winkler, Double Metaphone, token overlap, abbreviation confidence, cultural variants) with strong-signal boost, context-aware duplicate detection requiring Sim ≥ 0.75 plus full agreement on non-null role/org/location with auto-merge only above 0.95, qualifier-preserving relationship folding on (source, target, type), and sequence-ordered parallel output via mutex plus sequence-keyed buffering. The corrected abbreviation test requires a genuine single-letter part-pair, fixing 79 false positives.

6. **Embedding-based resolution with hard-conflict guard and five-stage hardened pipeline.** A complementary optional resolution subsystem follows the canonical blocking–matching pipeline with FAISS cosine top-k plus Double Metaphone blocking, seven-signal weighted scoring, and auto-merge/manual-review/codename-candidate thresholds under a discriminator-key hard-conflict guard (date of birth, nationality, gender, passport/employee number) that forbids merging regardless of score. Around it sits the five-stage pipeline (chunked two-call LLM extraction, deterministic per-chunk cleaning, title-aware cross-chunk merging with uniform ID reassignment, batched relationship second pass with a four-check quality gate, post-extraction enhancement), hardened after empirical evaluation exposed an `_env_int()` one-character truncation bug and six upstream data-quality issues now fixed at Stages 2–3 before Stage 5 enrichment.

## Key Findings

Deduplication and grounding substantially improve retrieval quality at negligible cost: aliases per entity rise from 1.0 to 5.0 (+400%) with ~6 variants discovered per entity, search recall rises from ~70% to ~95% with under 250 ms overhead and zero false merges, and 4–8 false merges per document are prevented by context validation and the hard-conflict guard. Data-cleaning fixes on the 45-page IR-001 report cut total entities from 579 to ~400 (−31%), removed all 114 self-loops (21% of edges) and 9 dangling references, normalised ~30 relations, reclassified 42 mistyped entities, unified 7 ID schemes into 1, and eliminated 79 abbreviation false positives while restoring per-chunk relationships from 0 to 500+ total. On the 10-page image-only Joint Fleet Summary naval corpus, the fixed pipeline cut wall-clock time by 30% (294.6 s → 204.9 s), reduced entities from 215 to 83 by removing fakes, cut the hallucination rate from 80.9% (174 Dong-You-style Person entities from mis-read hull columns) to 1.2%, corrected typing to Ship/ShipClass, and raised ground-truth vessel-class recall from 0/17 to 5/17, with a chunk-size ablation to 2 pages further doubling recall to 10/17 and tripling relationships from 64 to 206 at a 2:30 latency cost.

Calibration benchmarks show the local ontology-tuned model is production-viable: it trails Gemini 2.5 Flash by ~6 points on entity spans (59.6% vs 65.3% CoNLL04, 60.8% vs 66.6% Re-DocRED) but matches on relations, matches or beats the cloud model on OCR (token-F1 0.86–0.95, stronger on multilingual XFUND scans, zero runaway failures), and reaches high schema conformance (68–70% Text2KGBench, ~89% OSKGC) with near-zero hallucination but low triple F1 and 42–50% fine-grained type-mapping accuracy, motivating retrieval grounding. End-to-end accuracy on a synthetic intelligence report is ~93% across six dimensions with residual gaps described as cosmetic artifacts addressable by Stage 2–3 cleaning. The authors stress limits throughout: single representative documents per condition, small public samples (n = 8–50, directional only), a 58.8% OCR recall ceiling, heuristics tuned on the development corpus, and hardware-specific throughput numbers.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper's own roadmap prioritises five costed OCR interventions ordered by implementation complexity: multi-pass OCR with transcript union for non-determinism, higher DPI and contrast enhancement for below-floor image quality, directive transcription prompts preserving headings and numeric prefixes, a dedicated OCR model such as Tesseract with a custom dictionary unioned with VLM output for long-tail transliterated vocabulary, and dense-region crop-and-re-OCR at 400 dpi for multi-column tables, with the multi-pass union and directive prompts requiring no infrastructure changes as the natural next step. Natural extensions named elsewhere include learning the name-similarity signal weights and retrieval thresholds once labelled match data exist, promoting novel-type and novel-predicate flags into the ontology through the admin panel, and adopting each component independently (ontology grounding, deduplication, graceful degradation) into existing pipelines for compliance monitoring, investigative journalism, and enterprise knowledge management. Operational guidance includes tuning Kafka session timeouts and worker concurrency to the inference-latency profile (WORKER_COUNT 10→4, in-flight 40→16 after OOM and vLLM saturation), retaining the legacy binary PDF heuristic only as a zero-usable-page fallback, and keeping the embedding resolution layer behind a single configuration flag when semantic matches beyond surface strings are needed at scale.
