# OntoExtend: A Framework for Requirement-driven and Scalable Ontology Extension with LLMs

**Paper:** [OntoExtend: A Framework for Requirement-driven and Scalable Ontology Extension with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17963v1) — arXiv, 20 Jul 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine you have a huge, carefully organised library catalogue and you need to add a new section without messing up the existing shelves or rewriting every card by hand. OntoExtend works like a smart librarian's assistant: given a plain-English question about what the catalogue should newly answer, it pulls only the most relevant shelf cards, drafts the new catalogue entries in the library's own style, and then files them back in. In tests on a public circular-economy catalogue and a private Bosch factory catalogue, it answered every test question correctly with almost no junk entries, like a junior drafter whose work needs only a quick proofread. It works best when the request is precise, much as a tailor sews better from exact measurements than from a vague description.

## TL;DR

OntoExtend is a retrieval-augmented, requirement-driven framework that extends an existing ontology one competency question (CQ) at a time: an Ontology Retriever finds the top-k relevant ontology elements, an Ontology Extender prompts an LLM with the CQ plus retrieved Turtle context to generate a grounded TBox fragment, and an Ontology Integrator deduplicates and concatenates it. Evaluated on 39 CQs from the Onto-DESIDE ontology network and an industrial Bosch ontology, with text-embedding-ada-002 retrieval and o1-preview and GPT-5 generation, the fragments showed almost no syntax errors, no new critical or important OOPS! pitfalls, 100% CQ-verification success, and under 2% superfluous elements, with engineers rating industry fragments as needing only minor edits and EU-project fragments as needing moderate revision. The authors position it as a drafting assistant for real-world ontology extension that is sensitive to CQ specificity and modelling profile, and that scales by retrieving compact context instead of feeding ~75k-token ontologies to the LLM.

## Problem & Motivation

Ontology extension — systematically enriching an existing ontology to satisfy new functional requirements — is described as resource-intensive and error-prone, yet prior LLM work has focused on generating ontologies from scratch, taxonomy enrichment, or interactive tooling rather than on reusing elements of a mature input ontology while modelling additional CQs. The practical blockers are scale and focus: input ontologies often contain hundreds of classes and properties that exceed LLM context limits, and even when they fit, irrelevant details can misguide the model into off-target or inconsistent outputs. At the same time, surveyed prior methods are mostly semi-automatic enrichment biased toward taxonomic growth, with limited support for complex axioms, reproducibility concerns, reliance on interactive tools, continued need for human judgment, and minimal to no formal evaluation — and none of them focuses on retrieval of existing elements from the baseline ontology. OntoExtend is motivated by this gap: to ground LLM generation in the input ontology's own terminology and modelling patterns, keep prompts compact and cheap, and evaluate extensions rigorously on structural, functional, and human dimensions across real-world ontologies.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Retrieval-grounded extension pipeline:** a three-stage architecture in which the Ontology Retriever semantically indexes the same input ontologies targeted for extension, the Ontology Extender generates one self-contained RDF/Turtle fragment per CQ from retrieved context, and the Ontology Integrator merges fragments back, so that new axioms reuse rather than redeclare existing elements.
2. **OntologyElement FAISS index:** preprocessing that parses every declared OWL entity (classes, object/data/annotation properties, SHACL shapes where available) into records of IRI, labels/comments, domain/range, super-/sub-relations, and verbatim Turtle snippets, serialised as pipe-delimited strings, embedded, normalised, and stored in a FAISS inner-product index queried per CQ with top-k default 20.
3. **Constrained Extender prompting with two-stage validation:** prompts that inject retrieved Turtle snippets as read-only context plus a merged namespace prefix block, enforce reuse-without-redeclaration, and support swappable per-use-case templates (a SHACL NodeShape/PropertyShape template for industry versus a generic OWL-restriction plus mandatory rdfs:label/rdfs:comment template for the EU project), gated by a Turtle-parser syntax check and a configurable domain/range constraint checker with retry-or-flag on failure.
4. **Multidimensional extension evaluation methodology:** a combined protocol of RDFLib syntax checks, before/after OOPS! pitfall comparison with Pellet consistency checking, writable-SPARQL CQ verification by two cross-checking engineers, a refined superfluous-element count that exempts elements mentioned in the verification query or linked by subClassOf/subPropertyOf, and a six-engineer Likert survey separating Correctness (fragment alone) from Completeness (fragment plus input ontology, i.e. effort to make usable).

## Key Findings

Across 39 CQs created by removing classes and their referencing properties and then writing one CQ per removed unit (20 from four Onto-DESIDE modules, 19 from the Bosch manufacturing ontology), retrieval tuning on five held-out CQs selected text-embedding-ada-002 with pipe-separated axioms plus comments (Mw 0.63, Mprod 0.23) for the main experiment with o1-preview and GPT-5. Structural results showed almost no Turtle syntax errors (0% EU-project, 2.5% industry overall) and no new critical or important OOPS! pitfalls, only minor ones: P02 synonyms-as-classes and P04 unconnected elements in the EU setting, and P08 missing annotations averaging about 3.7 per extension in the industry setting, attributed to replicating the input's annotation-free style. Functional results were strong in both settings with both models: 100% CQ verification with no minor issues of the previously reported kind, and superfluous elements below 2% (2% EU-project, 0% industry), compared with roughly 30% reported in prior generation work. The engineer survey diverged by setting: industry fragments scored about 4.9 correctness and 4.5 completeness with high agreement, needing only minor or no changes (occasional SHACL-only additions, missing sh:datatype, subClassOf, or comments), while EU-project fragments scored about 3.7 correctness and 3.0 completeness, needing moderate revision for unconnected elements, over-specific names, missing or wrong domain/range and restrictions, and redefined classes. The authors attribute the EU gap to open-ended, under-specified CQs that left modelling decisions to the tool rather than to framework failure, noting GPT-5 and o1-preview performed comparably and that compact retrieval substantially cut latency and API cost versus sending ~75k-token ontology parts directly.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper's stated limitations point to evaluating on only two domain-specific use cases with few LLM configurations, a data-leakage threat since LLMs may have seen similar public ontologies in pre-training (mitigated in the Bosch case by its non-public status), and strong sensitivity of outcomes to CQ formulation. Proposed future work includes broader domains and modelling scenarios, systematic comparison across models and providers, leakage safeguards such as on-the-fly benchmark generation, open-weight models trained on controlled corpora, and overlap checks against pre-training sources, plus analysing and improving CQ quality including semi-automatic CQ generation or mapping to existing CQ templates. A further interpretation offered is to treat LLM behaviour as a tool-centric proxy for requirement and ontology quality: inputs that systematically yield clean extensions are pragmatically better specified, while struggles signal ambiguity or hidden assumptions. Practically, the direction is to use OntoExtend as a drafting assistant that shifts effort from manual axiom authoring to quick validation and targeted refinement, best fed with tightly scoped, precise CQs and coherent background ontologies.
