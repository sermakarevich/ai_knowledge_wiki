# ontology_ai -- for AI engineers combining LLMs with knowledge graphs

> Selection and framing for AI engineers combining LLMs with knowledge graphs. Same facts as [[../digest|ontology_ai digest]]; no new claims.

## What this means for you

- Use the LLM as a fluent drafter, not a committable writer: it recovers vocabulary (terms, classes, F1 ~59–67%) far better than structure (properties ~32%, triples/axioms ~12–15%, hierarchy closure ~17%, full axiom coverage ~2%). Plan for a grounding + validation layer around every LLM-to-graph write path ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- Ground generation in controlled semantics retrieved per request: ontology slices in context, readable names with `rdfs:comment`/`rdfs:label`, explicit domain/range and class hierarchy, and reuse-without-redeclaration prompting. This is the fix every source converges on, and it beats model upgrades: representation ablations swing 100% down to 5–19% while prompts and temperatures matter little ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- For natural-language-to-SPARQL, put the complete domain OWL ontology in the context window and make one deterministic zero-shot call (SPARQL instructions + full ontology + small domain rider + question; strip fences and reasoning traces before execution). No fine-tuning, few-shot examples, or entity linking were needed for 89.9% Match / 81.3% Exact on the revised 1,000-question DBLP-QuAD 3.1, 98/100 on SemOpenAlex (vs a published 86/100 baseline), and 100% on 21 neuroimaging competency questions ([[../topics/02-owl-rdf-sparql/digest|OWL, RDF, SPARQL knowledge representation]]).
- Prefer SPARQL over SQL on the same ontology and data, and structured tool access over raw file dumps: SPARQL reached 100% vs 57% for auto-generated SQL on a 21-question MRI benchmark, and MCP/SPARQL tool access (F1 0.717) far exceeded both unaided inference (0.431) and dumping the raw OWL file into context (0.323), which fails systematically on domain/range triples ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- Scale past context limits with live, content-conditioned retrieval: a FAISS index over ontology elements with top-k Turtle context answers all 39 test CQs with 0% EU / 2.5% industry syntax errors and <2% superfluous elements, and live Neo4j slice retrieval cuts catalog tokens ~11,200 → ~700 (~94%) ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- Make output merge-safe with deterministic normalization and dedup: stable 1-to-1 matching lifts OAEI Anatomy alignment from F1 0.182 to 0.832 at precision 0.963 (signal-weight tuning moves F1 by <0.004); six deterministic zero-inference dedup algorithms plus an embedding resolver under a non-overridable hard-conflict guard lift search recall ~70% → ~95% with zero false merges; a two-stage SEMLEG-grounded pipeline over 6,370 French legal articles preserves all relation statements through embedding fusion while compacting entities, predicates, and signatures ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- Close the loop with symbolic validation plus human review: SHACL/RDFLib syntax gates, OOPS! + Pellet/HermiT consistency checks, CQ-as-SPARQL verification, and engineer correctness/completeness ratings are what turn fluent drafts into committable changes. A five-stage clean/merge/second-pass pipeline cut hallucinations from 174 to zero on an adversarial image-only corpus ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- Treat LLMs as covering the full ontology lifecycle (requirements, implementation, publication, maintenance) in three roles — drafter of structures, domain-expert interpreter, evaluator — but expect thin evidence on practical usability: only a small subset of reviewed studies include human participants, and several omit protocols or release no code ([[../topics/01-ontology-foundations/digest|ontology foundations in AI]]).

## Decisions this informs

- **Query interface (NL-to-SPARQL vs SQL vs agents vs RAG pipelines):** choose full-OWL-in-context single-call SPARQL generation as the default where the ontology fits in context; formalism transfer outranks architecture, scale, and prompt engineering in these results ([[../topics/02-owl-rdf-sparql/digest|OWL, RDF, SPARQL knowledge representation]]).
- **Handling opaque native vocabularies:** build a clean, typed wrapper ontology shown to the model, then deterministically rewrite to native predicates before execution (e.g. federating DBLP plus OpenCitations, flattening SemOpenAlex intermediaries) without modifying the endpoint — the wrapper alone contributed ~25 Exact-match points on DBLP ([[../topics/02-owl-rdf-sparql/digest|OWL, RDF, SPARQL knowledge representation]]).
- **Ontology authoring for LLM consumption:** invest in readable names, comments/labels, and declared domain/range and hierarchy rather than a bigger model — the MRI ablation spread (100% full Turtle down to 5–19% anonymised forms) dominates model or strategy choice, and domain variation dominates too ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- **Retrieval design:** retrieve per-CQ or live content-conditioned ontology slices (FAISS top-k Turtle; Neo4j catalog slices) instead of whole-file dumps, with reuse-without-redeclaration and a token budget ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- **Merge safety:** put deterministic dedup and conflict guards (stable matching, rule-based + embedding dedup, hard-conflict guard, novel-type/predicate flagging for admin review) in front of every graph write ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- **Evaluation harness:** evaluate CQ-driven and functionally (SPARQL verification, term/axiom/hierarchy coverage, Match vs stricter Exact) rather than by lexical overlap, and distrust headline scores until benchmark determinism is checked — DBLP-QuAD 2.0's LIMIT-without-ORDER-BY references, echo queries, and undocumented OpenCitations dependency caused multi-point run-to-run variation, motivating the QuAD 3.1 revision with per-case repair logs ([[../topics/02-owl-rdf-sparql/digest|OWL, RDF, SPARQL knowledge representation]]).
- **Build-vs-benchmark bet:** join the prescribed direction of shared benchmarks plus hybrid LLM-human workflows preserving logical consistency and domain fidelity; the review of 30 papers / 41 studies finds non-standard tasks, datasets, metrics, and workflows blocking comparison (GPT, LLaMA, T5 families; OWL, free text, and CQs as inputs) ([[../topics/01-ontology-foundations/digest|ontology foundations in AI]]).

## Risks and unknowns

- **Structure unreliability:** unconstrained LLM output collapses from vocabulary to structure (properties, hierarchies, domain/range, full axioms); signature compliance in the legal pipeline reached only 50–73% from typing errors and novel domain–range reuse despite 100% JSON validity and ~99.98% class coverage ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- **Manual wrappers and missing entity resolution:** wrappers are currently manual, entity resolution is out of scope, citation queries are capped by 71% OpenCitations mapping coverage, and prose patching of the domain rider degenerates into ineffective "whack-a-mole" (concept smearing) ([[../topics/02-owl-rdf-sparql/digest|OWL, RDF, SPARQL knowledge representation]]).
- **Benchmark fragility:** headline accuracy depends on benchmark quality (deterministic references, repair logs, Match-vs-Exact scoring); single-domain or co-evolved question sets, small public samples, heuristic thresholds (0.6–0.95 bands, 0.72 retrieval floor), and possible contamination from public ontologies may overstate novel-query accuracy ([[../topics/02-owl-rdf-sparql/digest|OWL, RDF, SPARQL knowledge representation]], [[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- **Fragmented, weakly reproducible evidence base:** task definitions, datasets, metrics, and workflows differ study to study across 41 extracted studies; several primaries omit protocols or code (open screening data notwithstanding), and human-participant evidence is thin — so usability claims rest on limited support ([[../topics/01-ontology-foundations/digest|ontology foundations in AI]]).
- **CQ specificity and domain sensitivity:** industry fragments needed only minor edits while open-ended EU fragments needed moderate revision (CQ specificity drives quality), and domain variation dominates model or strategy choice ([[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
- **Coverage gaps in this synthesis:** the practitioner "when do you really need RDF/OWL" article (fetch 403) and the schema-agnostic cyber-threat-intelligence method (fetch bug) were skipped and are not covered here ([[../topics/02-owl-rdf-sparql/digest|OWL, RDF, SPARQL knowledge representation]], [[../topics/03-ontology-learning-llm-kg/digest|ontology learning and LLM knowledge-graph integration]]).
