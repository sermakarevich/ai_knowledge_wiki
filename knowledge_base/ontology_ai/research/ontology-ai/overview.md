# ontology_ai

**Research:** 8 sources, 2018–2026, focus: What is ontology in AI, how are ontologies built, represented and evaluated, and how do they combine with LLMs and knowledge graphs for a working engineer?

## Human Readable TL;DR

Think of an ontology as the agreed blueprint and parts catalog for a domain: it names the things that exist (classes, properties, relationships) and the rules for how they fit together, while a knowledge graph is the built city populated with actual buildings and streets that follow that blueprint. The research finds that large language models are fast but sloppy apprentice architects — great at drafting room names and rough sketches, terrible at load-bearing structure like hierarchies and type rules — so every working setup pairs them with guardrails: feed the model only the relevant blueprint slice, enforce naming and typing discipline, and run its output through automated inspectors plus human review. The single highest-leverage trick is putting the formal, typed ontology directly into the model's context when asking questions, which turns fuzzy natural language into precise database queries with near-perfect accuracy. Until shared benchmarks exist, treat every headline score as measured on its author's home turf and verify on your own questions before committing.

## TL;DR

Ontology engineering in AI covers a four-stage lifecycle (requirements specification, implementation, publication, maintenance) in which LLMs have been tried at every stage in three roles — ontology engineer drafting structures, domain expert supplying meaning, and evaluator judging outputs — with GPT, LLaMA, and T5 as the dominant families studied. The representation stack is RDF for triples, OWL for typed classes/properties/hierarchies with domain and range, and SPARQL for querying, and the central engineering result is that formally typed OWL semantics placed directly in the LLM context window enables reliable single-call zero-shot natural-language-to-SPARQL (89.9% Match on revised DBLP-QuAD, 98/100 on SemOpenAlex, 100% on neuroimaging and MRI competency-question sets), with deterministic wrapper ontologies extending the trick to opaque native vocabularies. Counterbalancing this is a systematic vocabulary-to-structure collapse: LLMs recover terms at F1 ~59–67% but fall to ~32% on properties, ~12–15% on triples/axioms, ~17% on hierarchy closure, and ~2% full axiom coverage, so all six integration sources converge on the same fix of retrieved ontology slices, controlled readable vocabularies, deterministic normalization and deduplication, and symbolic validation (SHACL, OOPS!, Pellet/HermiT, RDFLib) with human review. Tool structure beats raw context decisively (stable 1-to-1 matching lifting OAEI Anatomy alignment from F1 0.182 to 0.832; structured MCP/SPARQL tool access at F1 0.717 versus 0.323 for dumping the raw OWL file into context), formalism and ontology design for LLM consumption dominate model choice, and domain variation dominates model or strategy choice. The field's chief weakness is fragmentation — non-standard tasks, datasets, metrics, and workflows across the 41-study systematic review, weak reproducibility with unreleased code and protocols, thin human-participant evidence, and single-domain or co-evolved benchmarks — which motivates the shared prescription of standardized benchmarks plus hybrid LLM-human workflows.

## What is established

The evidence ledger for the claims below is [agreements.md](agreements.md); only the narrative synthesis lives here. See [agreements.md](agreements.md).

An ontology in AI is a formal, shared specification of a domain's concepts and their relationships — classes, properties, hierarchies (via constructs like `rdfs:subClassOf`), and constraints such as property domain and range with the `owl:ObjectProperty` versus `owl:DatatypeProperty` distinction — distinct from a knowledge graph, which is the populated instance data that conforms to (or is guided by) that schema. Ontology engineering is a lifecycle rather than a single authoring act, spanning requirements specification, implementation, publication, and maintenance, and the systematic review of 30 papers (41 studies, 2018–2024, screening data openly deposited) establishes that LLMs have been applied at every stage in three distinguishable roles: drafter of structures, supplier or interpreter of domain meaning, and judge of outputs, operating over heterogeneous inputs (OWL files, free text, competency questions) to produce task-specific artifacts (terms, axioms, documentation, examples). For representation and querying, the evidence establishes a concrete mechanism with large measured effects: placing the complete domain OWL ontology — declared types, domain/range, class hierarchy — plus SPARQL instructions and a small domain rider directly in context produces reliable single-call zero-shot natural-language-to-SPARQL with no fine-tuning, few-shot examples, or entity linking, reaching 89.9% Match and 81.3% Exact on the revised 1,000-question DBLP-QuAD 3.1, 98/100 on SemOpenAlex against a published 86/100 baseline, 100% on 21 neuroimaging competency questions, and 100% versus 57% for SPARQL over auto-generated SQL on an MRI benchmark, with readable names plus `rdfs:comment`/`rdfs:label` and explicit domain/range confirmed as the load-bearing features by ablations spanning 100% (full Turtle) down to 5–19% (anonymised abstract forms) while prompts and temperatures matter little. Wrapper ontologies — a clean typed namespace shown to the model and deterministically rewritten to native predicates before execution — are established as the way to extend this to uncontrolled vocabularies without touching the endpoint, contributing roughly 25 Exact-match points on DBLP and enabling federation over sources like DBLP plus OpenCitations. On the construction side, two-stage LLM induction followed by ontology-guided closed extraction is shown to scale to thousands of documents (6,370 French legal articles with 100% JSON validity and ~99.98% class coverage), retrieval-grounded per-question extension scales past context limits (a FAISS index over ontology elements answering all 39 test competency questions with near-zero syntax errors and under 2% superfluous elements), and a production extraction layer with live content-conditioned slice retrieval (~94% token cut), deterministic deduplication under a non-overridable hard-conflict guard, and multi-stage cleaning pipelines lifts search recall from ~70% to ~95% with zero false merges and drives hallucinations to zero on an adversarial corpus. The failure mode is equally well established and quantified: LLMs draft terms fluently but collapse on structure, with the competency-question benchmark across nine LLMs showing the vocabulary-to-structure gradient noted above, signature compliance of only 50–73% in the legal pipeline from typing errors and novel domain–range reuse, and systematic failure on domain/range triples when the raw OWL file is dumped into context. The remedy stack is therefore established as a package rather than a single trick: retrieved ontology slices in the prompt, controlled readable vocabularies with declared types, deterministic normalization and deduplication (embedding fusion preserving statements while compacting, canonicalization collapsing variants, stable 1-to-1 matching killing false alignments), and symbolic validators with human review, evaluated functionally through competency-question-as-SPARQL verification and axiom/term coverage rather than lexical overlap. Finally, two ordering results are established across independent studies: formalism and ontology design dominate model choice (the two strongest dense models converge near 90% Match; the stated hierarchy is formal concept transfer above architecture above scale above prompt engineering), and structure in tooling dominates signal tuning (matching-constraint ablations move F1 by ~0.65 while signal-weight tuning moves it by <0.004).

## What is contested

The open disputes are recorded in [disagreements.md](disagreements.md); in brief: whether the SLR's fragmentation verdict reflects genuine field immaturity or search-term under-coverage of BERT/T5-era work and thin novelty over prior reviews.
Whether wrappers staying manual is an acceptable engineering cost or a blocker to adoption.
Whether the binding constraint on generation quality is missing hierarchy chains, the assignment discipline, naming quality, question under-specification, entity-typing errors, or upstream data defects.
Whether OWL restrictions, SHACL-only shapes, or validated JSON is the right expressivity for LLM-built artifacts.
Whether per-question retrieval, live slice retrieval with token budgets, wrapper ontologies, or compact FAISS context is the right scaling answer.
Whether headline benchmark scores transfer to novel queries given single-domain and co-evolved question sets plus contamination risk.
Whether hybrid LLM-human workflows are validated practice or rest on thin (four-study, six-engineer) human evidence.

## What is open

See [open_questions.md](open_questions.md) for the full list; the central openings are shared standardized benchmarks with deterministic references and repair logs, hybrid LLM-human workflow designs that preserve logical consistency at automation speed, automating wrapper construction and entity resolution (currently manual and out of scope, with citation coverage capped near 71%), closing the domain–range discipline gap behind the vocabulary-to-structure collapse, scaling past context limits without quality decay, hardening heuristic thresholds and retrieval floors tuned on development corpora, resolving expressivity and evaluation-protocol choices, and reproducing results given unreleased code and incomplete protocols.
