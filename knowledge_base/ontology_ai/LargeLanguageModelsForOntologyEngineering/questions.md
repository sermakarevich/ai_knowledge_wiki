# Retrieval Practice: Large Language Models for Ontology Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review | www.semantic-web-journal.net

### Q1. What is the bibliographic and editorial record of this Semantic Web Journal article?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The Survey Article is "Large Language Models for Ontology Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review" by Jiayi Li, Maria Poveda, and Daniel Garijo, submitted 04/30/2025 with Tracking #3864-5078 and responsible editor Guilin Qi. Its status is Major Revision under the Reviewed tag, with full PDF swj3864.pdf and resources at the oeg-upm/llm4oe-slr GitHub link. See [[wiki/01-main-menu|Main menu]].

### Q2. How large is the review's evidence base and how was it derived?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The review analyzes 30 papers from which 41 distinct studies were extracted, filtered from 11,985 initially retrieved results covering 2018–2024. Review #2 explicitly records this funnel and the four research objectives mapped to four Research Questions. See [[wiki/01-main-menu|Main menu]].

### Q3. Which four ontology-engineering stages does the review cover?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The abstract lists ontology requirements specification, implementation, publication, and maintenance as the core development activities surveyed. Both solicited reviews repeat this four-stage framing when summarizing the paper's coverage. See [[wiki/01-main-menu|Main menu]].

### Q4. What roles do LLMs play in ontology engineering, with which models and input-output characteristics?
> [!tip]- Answer
> LLMs serve primarily as ontology engineers, domain experts, and evaluators, using models such as GPT, LLaMA, and T5. They process heterogeneous inputs like OWL ontologies, text, and competency questions to generate task-specific outputs such as examples, axioms, and documentation. See [[wiki/01-main-menu|Main menu]].

### Q5. What are the review's central findings about fragmentation and reproducibility?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The review observes a lack of homogenization in task definitions, dataset selection, evaluation metrics, and experimental workflows across the 30 papers. It adds that some papers omit complete evaluation protocols or code, making results hard to reproduce and methods insufficiently transparent. See [[wiki/01-main-menu|Main menu]].

### Q6. What did the two solicited peer reviews demand?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Review #1 (Anonymous, Minor Revision) praised comprehensiveness but cited scattered structure, Sections 4.1/4.2.1 redundancy, a Section 4.2 title/content mismatch needing a technical how-to summary, and Figure 2 layout fixes. Review #2 (Tianxing Wu, Major Revision) demanded clarified novelty beyond Garijo et al. 2024, better search-term coverage of BERT/T5-era work, deeper domain and human-role analysis, less Section 4/5 repetition, and a comprehensive taxonomy diagram. See [[wiki/01-main-menu|Main menu]].

### Q7. Given both reviews, what should the authors prioritize in revision and why?
> [!tip]- Answer
> They should prioritize a unifying taxonomy diagram plus de-duplicated methods and standardized evaluation framing, because both reviewers diagnose the same root defect of scattered, redundant organization. This single structural fix answers Review #1's structure complaint and Review #2's novelty, depth, and repetition concerns better than isolated textual patches. See [[wiki/01-main-menu|Main menu]].
