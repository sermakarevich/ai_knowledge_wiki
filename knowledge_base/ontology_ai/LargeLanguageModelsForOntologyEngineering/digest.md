## Key points

- The page identifies the article as a Survey Article by Jiayi Li, Maria Poveda, and Daniel Garijo, submitted 04/30/2025, with responsible editor Guilin Qi, status Major Revision, and tag Reviewed.
- The abstract states the review analyzes 30 papers on LLMs in ontology engineering across requirements specification, implementation, publication, and maintenance, with LLMs acting as ontology engineers, domain experts, and evaluators using GPT, LLaMA, and T5.

## The argument in five moves

1. The source presents itself as a Semantic Web Journal survey article (Li, Poveda, Garijo; submitted 04/30/2025; Major Revision) reviewing LLMs across the full ontology-engineering lifecycle.
2. It scopes the evidence base as 30 papers (41 extracted studies) drawn from 11,985 retrieved results over 2018–2024, centered on GPT, LLaMA, and T5 models.
3. It maps LLM roles to ontology engineer, domain expert, and evaluator, processing heterogeneous inputs (OWL ontologies, text, competency questions) into task-specific outputs (examples, axioms, documentation).
4. Its central finding is fragmentation: unstandardized task definitions, datasets, metrics, and workflows, compounded by missing evaluation protocols and code that undermine reproducibility.
5. Peer review concurs the coverage is comprehensive but demands structural tightening, de-duplication, deeper domain and human-role analysis, and a unifying taxonomy diagram.
6. The arc resolves into a forward challenge: standardized benchmarks plus hybrid LLM-human workflows as the path to reliable LLM-assisted ontology engineering.
