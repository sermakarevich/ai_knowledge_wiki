# Critical Analysis: CQ4OE: A benchmark for assessing LLM-assisted ontology generation from competency questions

## Claims vs. evidence
- Claim: no existing benchmark provides CQ-to-axiom provenance at this level. Supported within the digest's scope: each of 118 CQs links to explicit/implicit/derived terms plus a minimal sufficient TBox set (axiom kept only if its removal breaks answerability), unlike CORAL (CQs without term/axiom maps) or full-ontology overlap baselines.
- Claim: fairer requirement-scoped evaluation. Evidence is procedural, not comparative: 255 published CQs filtered to 110, plus 8 authored (3.1%), under triple-review (101/118 accepted unchanged). No head-to-head showing the old "full ontology overlap" metric actually misranks models, so fairness is argued, not measured.
- Claim: LLMs recover vocabulary better than structure. Strongly evidenced: CQ2Term global F1 59.1–66.5% vs. CQ2Onto collapse (Triple-AC 36.4% vs. Triple-G 12.4%, Axiom-AC 35.3% vs. Axiom-G 15.0%, closure F1 ~16.7%, Axioms-Full ~2.1%). Class F1 (~60–67%) roughly doubles property F1 (~32–55%) across both tasks.
- Claim: global scores hide localization failures. Well supported: CQ-conditioned coverage averages 89.2% at-least-one but only 54.8% mean and 23.5% full; Water hits 70.6% global F1 with 0% full coverage, Wine 100% at-least-one with 0% full.
- Claim: strategies matter for repair, domains matter more than models. Mixed: three strategies sit within 3pp on structural F1; multi-agent lifts Axioms-Mean 18.3%→24.2% (concentrated in AWO/ODRL) while iterative builds denser hierarchies (8.6 vs. ~4.8 closure pairs). Domain spread (Wine 48.0% to AWO 90.5% term F1) dwarfs model spread, which cuts both ways (see weaknesses).
- Claim: explainable, reusable pipeline. Substantiated as infrastructure (per-run Markdown + CSV traces, W3ID metric IDs, parallel CQ2Term/CQ2Onto dirs, leaderboard, Apache 2.0), not as validity: traces diagnose misses but strict-equivalence counting means a "miss" can be a paraphrase the alignment rejected.
- Triple-review acceptance (101/118 unchanged, 85.6%) signals careful curation, but revisions concentrate in SWO (10 of 17) — the largest, deepest source — hinting annotation difficulty scales with ontology complexity and the protocol may not transfer cheaply to new deep domains.
- Claim: multi-method alignment is necessary. Supported by the standalone-method gap: hard matching is conservative (51.7% classes, 30.0% properties) while semantic similarity reaches 73.9%/63.3%, with a 33.3-point property gap — though the aggregated fix (hard override + Top-3 mean) is asserted effective rather than ablated against simpler ensembles.
- Claim: no single model dominates. Credible across 54 runs / 162 ontologies: DeepSeek V4-Pro leads term F1 (66.5%) and class labels, Gemma 31B-IT leads property triples (41.2%), DeepSeek V4-Flash leads axiom/closure means — five models lead on at least one domain, and mean structural F1 spans only 26.7–33.7%.

## Genuinely new vs. repackaged
- Genuinely new: CQ-aligned minimal-sufficient golds — per-CQ required TBox axiom sets with explicit/implicit/derived (Ei/Ii/Ri) term annotation, e.g. AWO "Which plants eat animals?" → CarnivorousPlant ⊑ Plant plus ∃eats.Animal. This is the paper's real contribution.
- Genuinely new: the CQ2Term (99 CQs, conceptualization) vs. CQ2Onto (118 CQs, structure + reasoning) split with dual global and CQ-conditioned views (at-least-one/mean/full over terms, axioms, closure). The two-view design exposing "vocabulary without attachment" is a methodological step forward.
- Genuinely new (modest): closure-rescue coverage with directly-asserted / chain-inferred / complex-inferred tags plus BFS shortest-path explanations, and the AC-vs-G gap as an explicit over-generation diagnostic (precision 56.1% vs. recall 71.5%).
- Repackaged: the alignment stack (exact + difflib + Levenshtein + Jaro–Winkler + embeddinggemma/Ollama, Top-3-mean aggregation, τC=0.6/τP=0.7) is competent plumbing over standard similarity methods; thresholds were hand-picked on a held-out subset with no per-domain tuning.
- Repackaged: HermiT closure comparison, RDFLib/HermiT/OOPS! multi-agent repair loop, and the six source ontologies (Wine, AWO, ODRL, SAREF4WATR, VGO, SWO) are reused community assets. Baselines reuse the MASEO Generation Agent prompts at temperature 0, so "nine LLMs" is one prompting regime, not a strategy sweep.
- Repackaged: core-term selection by in/out-degree ranking plus owl2diagram inspection and the four-phase filter→augment→extract workflow are solid curation practice, not a methodological invention; likewise W3ID identifiers, Zenodo/HuggingFace archiving, and a leaderboard follow standard reproducibility playbooks.
- Borderline: per-domain model selection ("transparent basis for selecting the best-suited model per domain") is a sensible use of Fig. 2-style breakdowns, but with only 5–35 CQs per domain it risks overfitting model choice to benchmark micro-slices.
- Borderline: the embedding-cosine diagnostic over serialized axioms acknowledges strict matching may be too harsh, but since it never affects P/R/F1 it reads as an admitted-but-unresolved validity gap rather than a second scoring path.

## Weaknesses and blind spots
- Small and filtered: 118 CQs after discarding 145 of 255; 19 inference-heavy CQs (SAREF4WATR, VGO, SWO) excluded from CQ2Term entirely, so the "conceptualization" task skips exactly the hard cases.
- Depth collapse: SWO goes from source depth 15 / 8,087 axioms to a CQ-aligned gold of depth 1 / 80 axioms; the benchmark therefore under-tests the deep hierarchies LLMs fail at (VGO/SWO predicted closures 0.6–0.7 pairs vs. gold 8–12).
- Alignment is load-bearing and admitted fragile: all ontology-level metrics depend on α, with known failures on short property labels (`has_`/`is_` at 0.6–0.65) and close-but-unequal terms; manual CSV correction is offered, which breaks full automation.
- Strict equivalence with hierarchy-only rescue: non-hierarchy axioms get no partial credit, and closure rescue adds only +1.4pp mean with Closure-Full unchanged — the rescue mostly documents absence rather than recovering it.
- Floor effects weaken discrimination: Axioms-Full ~2.1% means near-zero for every model; domain variance dominating model variance suggests the benchmark ranks domains' difficulty more than models' ability.
- Single-prompt, single-temperature, contamination-uncontrolled: one MASEO prompt set, temp 0, 16k-token cap; public sources may be in pretraining, countered only by low structural scores and a promise of future private ontologies. No human-expert rating of generated ontologies and no cost/latency reporting for the three strategies.
- Uneven domain difficulty distorts averages: AWO is easiest (class F1 87.6%, closure 52.2%) while Water splits recognition vs. structure (class ~65% but Triple-G ≤3.5%, Axiom-G ≤6%); VGO/ODRL show steep AC-to-global triple drops (70.0%→25.8%, 59.7%→19.1%). AWO triples are excluded from aggregation (complex anonymous range), so cross-domain means are not strictly comparable.
- No significance or ablation analysis: with nine models, 18 (domain, strategy) cells, and F1 bands only a few points wide, there are no confidence intervals or tests; the aggregation formula, threshold choices, and closure-decomposition rules are not ablated, so we cannot tell which machinery carries the result.
- Reasoning scope is narrow: HermiT subsumption closure only; property characteristics, cardinalities, and datatype reasoning are scored by strict match with an embedding-cosine diagnostic that "does not affect strict P/R/F1" — the diagnostic admits the strict score may punish semantically close outputs without resolving it.
- Coverage reporting gap: CQ2Term excludes the 19 hardest CQs while CQ2Onto includes them, so the two tasks' CQ-conditioned numbers are not directly comparable — a model can look better on "conceptualization" partly because the hardest requirements were removed from its denominator.

## Applicability
- Directly applicable as an evaluation pattern for any requirements→schema/ontology generation: minimal-sufficient gold slices per requirement, global vs. requirement-conditioned coverage, and alignment-conditioned vs. global structural views.
- The per-run Markdown + CSV trace format is worth copying for agentic data-modeling pipelines: metric-per-axiom provenance beats a single F1 when debugging missing SubClassOf/SubPropertyOf chains or unstable property modeling.
- Not directly transferable as a gate: thresholds (τC/τP), embedding model, and HermiT-only reasoning are OWL-specific; property-heavy or deep-hierarchy domains will need retuning, and the 118-CQ scale is too small for production model selection.
- Operational caveats before copying: the embeddinggemma-via-Ollama dependency and greedy one-to-one alignment need versioning and dry-run trace review (the authors themselves recommend inspecting CSVs first); uniform thresholds across heterogeneous domains will misbehave on short formulaic property labels.
- Where not to use: open-ended knowledge-graph construction or instance-heavy (ABox) QA evaluation — CQ4OE explicitly filters out instance-level CQs and scores TBox structure, so it says nothing about fact population, retrieval grounding, or answer correctness at query time.
- **Relevance to my work**
  - AI/ML engineering: adopt CQ-conditioned at-least-one/mean/full coverage alongside global P/R/F1 for any spec-to-artifact eval; copy the AC-vs-G gap as an over-generation detector (precision≪recall ⇒ generator is spraying vocabulary).
  - Agentic systems: the zero-shot vs. iterative vs. multi-agent result (≤3pp structural gap, repair gains concentrated in 2/6 domains, denser-but-not-better iterative hierarchies) is a cautionary prior — budget repair loops only where axiom-level traces show fixable local misses, and log chain-inferred paths for auditability.
  - Elisity data platform: use CQ-style competency questions ("which devices expose X?", "which policies govern Y?") with minimal-sufficient schema/axiom slices to regression-test LLM-assisted data modeling; reuse the W3ID-style persistent metric IDs and predictions-folder-plus-one-script harness for repeatable evals.

## What this changes
- Shifts the eval question from "does output overlap the reference ontology?" to "is each requirement answerable from the output?" — the minimal-sufficient TBox slice per CQ is the idea worth stealing.
- Confirms with unusual clarity that term recovery ≠ ontology generation: expect ~60%+ class F1 but ~12–15% global triple/axiom F1 and ~2% full-CQ completeness from zero-shot LLMs, with properties and hierarchies as the failure zone.
- Downgrades repair-loop optimism: OOPS!/HermiT multi-agent polishing lifts mean axiom coverage but leaves full completeness and closure-F1 essentially flat — missing chains are not repaired into existence.
- Raises the bar for future benchmarks: any follow-up must include private/post-cutoff domains, per-domain alignment calibration, partial credit for complex axioms, and human adequacy ratings, or it will not supersede CQ4OE.
- Practical takeaway for model choice: stop asking "which ontology model is best?" and ask "which model for which domain and which layer?" — the five-models-lead-somewhere pattern plus Water/Wine localization failures argue for per-domain, per-layer (terms vs. triples vs. axioms) selection over a single aggregate ranking.
- Justifies multi-method alignment in our own evals: lexical-only matching will undercount paraphrased properties by ~30 points while pure semantic matching over-matches short labels — the hard-override plus ensemble-mean pattern is worth replicating with our own threshold calibration.
- Reframes over-generation as the default LLM failure mode (recall ≫ precision on both classes and properties): prompt and pipeline design should constrain vocabulary emission per requirement rather than encourage exhaustive enumeration, since unattached terms inflate global scores while leaving CQs unanswered.

## Verdict
- Useful harness, limited benchmark: the provenance methodology and two-view metrics are sound and reusable, but 118 CQs, collapsed depth, alignment fragility, floor-effect completeness, and contamination risk make it a diagnostic instrument rather than a ranking authority.
- Trial the method on one internal domain (write 10–20 CQs, build minimal-sufficient gold slices, report global + CQ-conditioned + AC-vs-G) before citing CQ4OE scores in any model-selection decision; do not gate releases on its leaderboard yet.
- What would upgrade this to adopt: a second release with post-cutoff/private domains, per-domain calibrated thresholds, ablated aggregation, and human-rated adequacy — until then the methodology is adoptable but the benchmark numbers are not.
- Overall judgment: **trial**
