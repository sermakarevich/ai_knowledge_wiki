# CQ4OE: A benchmark for assessing LLM-assisted ontology generation from competency questions

**Paper:** [CQ4OE: A benchmark for assessing LLM-assisted ontology generation from competency questions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26029v1) — arXiv, 22 Sep 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

Building an ontology from competency questions is like turning a customer's wishlist into a working blueprint for a house: the questions say what the house must do, and the ontology is the formal plan of rooms, doors, and wiring that makes those answers possible. CQ4OE is a standardized exam for AI assistants doing that translation job, checking not just whether they name the right rooms but whether the plumbing and electrical logic actually connect. It finds that today's models are decent at listing ingredients but shaky at assembling the full recipe, especially for relationships, hierarchies, and complete question coverage.

## TL;DR

CQ4OE is a reusable benchmark for systematic, reproducible evaluation of LLM-based OWL ontology generation from competency questions (CQs), built around CQ-aligned gold standards with explicit CQ-to-term and CQ-to-axiom provenance. It defines two complementary tasks — CQ2Term for term-level class and property prediction over 99 CQs, and CQ2Onto for ontology-level evaluation over 118 CQs covering hierarchy, property modeling, and axiom-level structure — drawn from six diverse source ontologies (Wine, AWO, ODRL, SAREF4WATR, VGO, SWO). It contributes a multi-task metric framework spanning term recovery, property characteristics, domain/range triples, TBox axioms, and HermiT hierarchy closure plus an automated explainable reporting pipeline with per-run Markdown reports and CSV traces. Baseline evaluation of nine LLMs (DeepSeek, Qwen, and Gemma families) under zero-shot, iterative, and multi-agent strategies shows that models recover explicit vocabulary far more reliably than complete, logically sound ontologies, with performance varying more by domain than by model or strategy.

## Problem & Motivation

Ontology generation from competency questions is a central yet labor-intensive phase of Ontology Engineering, where the conceptualization stage transforms natural-language requirements into classes, properties, relations, and constraints through substantial expertise and iterative cycles of analysis, modeling, implementation, and evaluation. While LLMs are already being used to suggest candidate terms, identify domain relationships, refine fragments, and support modeling decisions, current evaluation remains fragmented and unfair. Task formulations are heterogeneous and incompatible across concept extraction, completion, and full OWL generation; reference ontologies rarely specify which classes, properties, or axioms each CQ requires, so requirement satisfaction cannot be judged; and lexical or coarse structural metrics miss property modeling, logical constraints, and reasoning behavior, leaving wrong domain/range assignments, missing axioms, and flawed hierarchies undetected. Critically, scoring structural overlap against full reference ontologies is an unfair proxy because those ontologies contain knowledge beyond the input CQs, penalizing focused outputs and rewarding out-of-scope reproduction. CQ4OE addresses this gap by building, per ontology, a CQ-driven gold OWL ontology with explicit provenance linking each CQ to exactly the classes, properties, and axioms required to answer it, so evaluation measures what the stated requirements demand rather than broad domain overlap.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **CQ-aligned gold standards with explicit provenance for two tasks.** CQ4OE constructs task-specific golds from 255 published CQs across six ontologies through a four-phase workflow of core-term selection, CQ filtering and term annotation, CQ augmentation, and gold construction under triple-review adjudication. From 110 retained CQs plus only 8 manually authored CQs for uncovered core terms (118 total), it derives CQ2Term with CQ-to-term provenance over 99 CQs that have at least one explicit term, and CQ2Onto with fine-grained CQ-to-axiom provenance over all 118 CQs where each axiom is retained only if its removal would make the CQ unanswerable. Each CQ is annotated with explicit, implicit, and derived term sets, and core terms are selected by degree ranking plus manual verification, so that requirement scope is sharply delimited and fairly testable.

2. **Five-method term alignment pipeline with type-specific aggregation.** Because generated ontologies can use different labels for the same concept, CQ4OE aligns gold and predicted vocabularies before scoring using hard exact matching, difflib sequence matching, Levenshtein, Jaro–Winkler, and embedding-based semantic similarity, after excluding WordNet and information-content methods for poor coverage and taxonomy dependence. The aggregated score uses a hard-match override plus the mean of the three highest non-hard scores so no single low method vetoes a reasonable match, followed by greedy one-to-one selection with type-specific thresholds (0.6 for classes, 0.7 for properties to suppress spurious short-label property matches) that is fully traceable and manually correctable via exported CSVs.

3. **Multi-task, multi-view metric framework with global and alignment-conditioned scoring.** CQ2Term reports global precision/recall/F1 over pooled term sets plus CQ-conditioned at-least-one, mean, and full coverage, exposing models that recover vocabulary without attaching terms to the right CQ. CQ2Onto evaluates five targets — term recovery, property characteristics, domain/range triples, TBox axioms with strict structural decomposition, and HermiT hierarchy-closure over inferred subsumptions — each with global and alignment-conditioned views (except term recovery and closure, by design), plus embedding-cosine diagnostics and axiom-level and closure-recovered CQ coverage that distinguishes asserted, chain-inferred, and complex-inferred recoveries with shortest-path explanations.

4. **Automated explainable reporting and reusable, extensible infrastructure.** The open-source pipeline aligns outputs, computes all metrics, and emits per-run Markdown reports tracing matched, missing, and closure-recovered terms and axioms with per-CQ traces, alongside persistent W3ID metric identifiers, parallel CQ2Term/CQ2Onto repository directories, a single-script path for benchmarking new LLMs, a documented four-phase protocol for adding new domains, and a public leaderboard for fair, reproducible comparison.

## Key Findings

Nine LLMs tested via OpenRouter at temperature zero with MASEO Generation Agent prompting show strong vocabulary recovery but steep drops to structure, hierarchy closure, and CQ-level completeness, with no single model dominating every dimension. On CQ2Term, global term F1 spans 59.1% to 66.5% with class recovery (67.1%) well ahead of property recovery (55.2%) and a precision–recall gap indicating over-generation, while hard matching is conservative and semantic matching is generous, justifying the aggregation; CQ-conditioned coverage averages 89.2% at-least-one but only 54.8% mean and 23.5% full, with cases like Water reaching 70.6% global F1 yet 0% full coverage, proving that global scores hide requirement-localization errors. On CQ2Onto, vocabulary-to-structure collapse is stark — class F1 averaging 59.7% against property F1 31.8%, Triple-AC 36.4% versus Triple-G 12.4%, Axiom-AC 35.3% versus Axiom-G 15.0%, and closure F1 averaging only 16.7% — and the three strategies score within three points structurally while shaping hierarchies differently, with iterative generation densest and multi-agent repair (RDFLib, HermiT, OOPS! with backtracking) lifting CQ-level axiom mean coverage from 18.3% to 24.2% concentrated in AWO and ODRL without adding new chains. Three recurrent limitations emerge across models: missing SubClassOf and SubPropertyOf chains (notably shallow flat outputs where deep chains are required), unstable property modeling at nearly half of class F1 with frequent paraphrase and aliasing, and low CQ completeness with axiom-full coverage near 2% and closure rescue adding only about 1.4 mean points while leaving full coverage unchanged. Domain variation dominates model and strategy variation throughout, from easy AWO and strong-axiom Wine to structure-poor Water and chain-starved VGO and SWO.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The authors acknowledge that all ontology-level metrics depend on term alignment so errors propagate downstream, that uniform thresholds were set by empirical inspection without per-domain tuning and can still fail on short or semantically close labels, that closure rescue covers only hierarchy-related axioms decomposable into atomic SubClassOf or SubPropertyOf relations while partial credit inside complex axioms remains open, and that public source ontologies raise contamination concerns mitigated only partially by low structural scores and the novel manually curated provenance layer. They propose extending CQ4OE with additional domains including private or post-cutoff ontologies for contamination-controlled evaluation, further refining alignment procedures and evaluation metrics, and reusing the framework across models, prompting strategies, repair pipelines, and human-in-the-loop workflows via the documented annotation protocol, configurable thresholds, persistent metric catalogue, and leaderboard infrastructure.
