# Connections

- [[multi_agent_systems/RethinkingFailureAttributionInMultiAgentSystems|Rethinking Failure Attribution in Multi-Agent Systems: A Multi-Perspective Benchmark and Evaluation]] — Same-problem-different-method: also uses LLM-as-judge to validate root-cause attribution of agent failures, but for multi-agent trajectories rather than the model/harness/environment edges here; both papers converge on the finding that a single "ground truth" cause is often the wrong framing and that fault attribution needs a richer evaluation protocol than accuracy against one label.
- [[agent_harness/AgentHarnessEngineering/summary|Agent Harness Engineering]] — Shares the exact organizing dichotomy in this paper's title ("Agent = Model + Harness"): where this taxonomy diagnoses *after the fact* which side of that split caused an observed failure, AgentHarnessEngineering argues *prospectively* that most engineering leverage lives on the harness side — a natural repair-routing counterpart to the taxonomy's fault-attribution labels.
- [[RoPoLLRobustPanelOfLLMJudges/summary|RoPoLL: Robust Panel of LLM Judges]] _(unfiled)_ — Shares-technique: both papers run panels of multiple LLM judges (four frontier models here, a jury here vs. there) and study how to aggregate or threshold their verdicts; RoPoLL's finding that naive averaging is fragile to one systematically biased judge is directly relevant to this paper's own >=2/>=3/unanimity voting scheme and its documented systematic model-blaming bias among judges.
- [[CoFailureCeiling/summary|When Does Combining Language Models Help? A Co-Failure Ceiling on Routing, Voting, and Mixture-of-Agents Across 67 Frontier Models]] _(unfiled)_ — Explains a mechanism behind this paper's own weak spot: the four judges here are highly correlated (pairwise kappa up to 0.84) and share a systematic bias toward blaming the model, which is exactly the "co-failure" phenomenon this entry formalizes — correlated judges/models fail together on the same hard cases regardless of how many are pooled, capping what ensembling/unanimity voting can rescue.
- [[TowardsAScienceOfAIAgentReliability/summary|Towards a Science of AI Agent Reliability]] _(unfiled)_ — Same broad problem from a complementary angle: both argue outcome-level success/failure labels are too coarse for agent evaluation and propose a structured, multi-dimensional taxonomy instead (reliability dimensions there vs. interaction-edge/fault-side here) borrowed from mature engineering disciplines.
- [[AskDontJudge/summary|Ask, Don't Judge: Binary Questions for Interpretable LLM Evaluation and Self-Improvement]] _(unfiled)_ — Shares-technique: BinEval's move from a single holistic judge score to a decomposed checklist of yes/no questions mirrors this paper's Turn-3 "disambiguation checklist" step, which the authors show is necessary to stop judges from defaulting to a model-blaming verdict.
