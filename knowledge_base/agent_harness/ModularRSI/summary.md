# ModularRSI: Modular and Generalizable Recursive Harness Self-Improvement

**Paper:** [ModularRSI: Modular and Generalizable Recursive Harness Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14857) — arXiv, 14 Sep 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

Think of an AI coding assistant as a race car where the language model is the driver and the harness — the code that manages tools, terminal output, memory, and when to stop — is the pit crew and dashboard. Until now, teams tuned the car by practicing on the actual race track, so it was impossible to tell whether the car got genuinely better or just memorized that one track. ModularRSI instead practices on 2,000 separate training tracks, films both good and bad laps on the same track to spot exactly what the crew did wrong, and fixes each crew role separately — like coaching the tire team without confusing the fuel team — before putting everyone back together. The result is a crew that performs better on brand-new tracks and even with different drivers behind the wheel.

## TL;DR

ModularRSI is a benchmark-disjoint, contrastive, and modular framework for generalizable Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI, meaning an agent system improving its own execution code from experience) of agent harnesses for long-horizon terminal and software-engineering tasks. It replaces the usual practice of evolving on evaluation benchmarks with a curated set of 2,000 executable Harbor-format tasks fully disjoint from TerminalBench 2.0 and SWE-Bench Verified, then converts coarse task-level success or failure into localized harness signals by contrasting successful and failed trajectories for the same task and aggregating evidence across tasks. The evolvable harness, reorganized from Terminus-2, is decomposed into five functional modules — Agent Loop, Tool Use, Observation Management, Context Management, and Task Completion Detection — each evolved independently within a restricted modification scope by a Code-Modify Agent with voting, history, and validation gates, followed by a cross-module integration epoch and a frozen library for evaluation. Experiments show consistent in-domain, cross-domain, and cross-model gains, with independent modular evolution substantially outperforming joint or non-modular evolution.

## Problem & Motivation

CLI agents for complex software engineering and terminal tasks increasingly depend on their harnesses — the machinery that governs execution looping, tool interaction, context handling, and environment feedback — yet improving that machinery from experience faces three coupled credit-assignment problems. At the data level, building diverse executable long-horizon tasks with reliable environments and evaluators is costly, so existing harness RSI (Recursive Self-Improvement) methods typically evolve directly on evaluation benchmarks or subsets drawn from them, making it impossible to distinguish reusable mechanism improvements from benchmark-specific adaptation. At the trajectory level, individual or one-sided execution traces entangle systematic harness deficiencies with instance-specific reasoning and solution details, so direct optimization tends to produce task-specific patches that transfer poorly. At the mechanism level, monolithic harnesses make it unclear which component should change, and whole-harness optimization entangles unrelated mechanisms, producing changes that are hard to attribute and validate. Prior work spans model-weight adaptation, skill and context accumulation, prompt optimization, failure diagnosis, whole-harness synthesis, and specialized efforts such as TACO on observation compression, but evaluations around HarnessDev, Aspire, and S3 Gym still leave broadly generalizable, fine-grained harness improvement underexplored. ModularRSI is motivated as a response: a contrastive and modular credit-assignment framework plus a standardized benchmark-disjoint evolution resource that together turn coarse outcomes into targeted, reusable harness modifications.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Benchmark-disjoint evolution protocol with 2,000 curated tasks.** ModularRSI independently curates 2,000 executable evolution instances from external sources such as GitHub repositories, Hugging Face, Kaggle, and Linux kernel documentation, guided by category labels from the TerminalBench and SWE-Bench families but using no benchmark instances or task-specific information. Retrieved resources are converted into Harbor-format tasks with LLM filtering for environment completeness, practicality, and evaluator validity, executable validation requiring the reference solution to pass while a no-op submission fails, manual review, and semantic similarity screening against downstream benchmarks. The pool is relatively balanced with 1,000 SWE-related repository-level tasks and 1,000 Terminal-related interaction tasks across categories such as data processing, ML and science, build and dependencies, systems and OS, language and API, web and network, algorithms, security and crypto, and databases, and the evolved harness is frozen before downstream evaluation with modifications retained only after validation.

2. **Contrastive trajectory analysis with cross-task aggregation.** Instead of learning from single trajectories, ModularRSI pairs successful and failed rollouts of the same task to isolate function-level behavioral factors tied to different outcomes, aggregating evidence across tasks to surface recurring deficiencies rather than one-off failures. A Trajectory Memory stores historical trajectories and rewards per task across epochs to supply contrastive evidence when current rollouts are one-sided, and the Code-Modify Agent routes tasks into Contrastive (paired success and failure), Negative (all fail, paired with a remembered success if available, otherwise single-sided diagnosis of loops, tool misuse, recovery failure, or premature termination), and Positive (all succeed, analyzed for redundancy and efficiency) groups. Diagnoses are consolidated into structured JSON findings specifying module, evidence, rationale, and proposed change, with semantically similar diagnoses on the same function merged into candidates ranked by vote count from distinct supporting tasks.

3. **Modular decomposition with independent module-wise evolution.** The harness is decomposed into five independently evolvable functional modules — Agent Loop as the outer execution coordinator maintaining state, scheduling calls, handling retries, and deciding continuation; Tool Use for parsing model responses and executing commands; Observation Management for converting terminal output into agent-readable feedback; Context Management for maintaining and compressing conversation history; and Task Completion Detection for stop recommendations — each evolved within a restricted modification scope to reduce cross-mechanism interference. Each function carries an Evolution History of prior changes so later updates preserve evolved behavior and avoid oscillation, and an expanding function library is managed by Function Merge for redundant variants and Task-Aware Function Composition that activates only task-relevant functions selected from natural-language descriptions.

4. **Validation gates and cross-module integration.** Only validated modifications are retained through three gates: Program Check with static AST, import, protocol, discovery-contract, and self-attribute audits; Diff Review where the Code-Modify Agent rejects task-specific solutions, heuristics, or constants unlikely to generalize; and Execution Validation sampling two tasks from the current batch, with failures rolled back via recorded diffs. After independent evolution, validated module variants are combined and refined in a dedicated cross-module integration epoch that removes duplicated mechanisms, clarifies responsibilities, and repairs coordination logic, after which the function library is frozen with no further changes during downstream evaluation.

## Key Findings

With DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Preview as backbone, ModularRSI consistently improves both in-domain and out-of-domain performance while enhancing reliability, lifting TerminalBench 2.0 accuracy from 47.57 to 52.43 and SWE-Bench Verified from 73.40 to 76.45, with TB-evolved harnesses reaching 75.80 on SWE-Bench Verified and SWE-evolved harnesses reaching 49.40 on TerminalBench 2.0 alongside a TerminalBench Pass3 rise from 30.34 to 35.96 and largely monotonic gains across generations. The frozen TB-evolved harness transfers across foundation models on TerminalBench 2.0, improving GLM-5.2 from 59.55 to 61.80, MiniMax-2.5 from 41.57 to 44.94, and DeepSeek-V4-Flash from 47.57 to 52.43 across accuracy, Pass@3, and Pass3. Modularity proves decisive: independent per-module evolution plus integration at 52.43 accuracy beats the 47.57 baseline while joint all-module evolution drops to 44.19 and non-modular evolution to 46.44, and all five single-module variants individually beat baseline with Agent Loop strongest on accuracy at 50.56 and Observation Management cutting mean steps to 22.50 before integration pushes accuracy to 52.43 and Pass3 to 35.96. Under a matched benchmark-disjoint protocol on TerminalBench 2.0 with DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731, prior methods stay near the 61.79 Terminus-2 baseline while ModularRSI reaches 67.42 accuracy with 78.65 Pass@3 and 56.18 Pass3, compared with roughly one-point moves for AHE at 62.54 and Meta-Harness at 62.92. Contrastive evidence behaves as intended as the usable success-failure pair ratio falls across epochs, trajectory evidence splits into about 40.67% paired, 8.33% all-fail with historical success, 14.56% all-fail without success, and 36.00% all-succeed groups, and case studies show a persistent task checklist for NULL-filter requirements, a completion integrity guard for FFmpeg linkage checks taking a task from partial to full passes, and a robust file-write tool cutting mean episodes from 36 to 25. Data quality matters: a Medium-centered difficulty distribution outperforms a Hard and Easy mix throughout evolution and by 2.20 points on SWE-Bench Verified at 76.45 versus 74.25, and successive generations show accuracy around 52.2 with an Opus-4.8 trajectory judge near 74.0 by generation 22.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The authors explicitly note two limitations that point directly to follow-up work: no dedicated ablation isolating the contribution of contrastive trajectory analysis beyond trajectory statistics and case studies, and main evolution experiments using only a subset of the 2,000 curated instances — 120 TB-related plus 120 SWE-related instances over three epochs — due to computational cost. They propose broader comparisons and larger-scale studies as future work, which naturally includes scaling to the full evolution pool, longer evolution horizons, and dedicated ablations separating contrastive pairing, cross-task voting, modular scoping, validation gates, and integration effects. The difficulty-distribution results further suggest systematically designing evolution curricula around medium-difficulty, high-contrast tasks, while the function-library machinery invites research on merge policies, composition selectors, and conflict detection for ever-larger module variant pools.
