# Voice-Light: A Full-Duplex Cascaded Voice Agent with Causal Turn-Taking and Speculative Generation

**Paper:** [Voice-Light: A Full-Duplex Cascaded Voice Agent with Causal Turn-Taking and Speculative Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20995)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Voice-Light is like a polite but quick-thinking receptionist who keeps listening even while talking, so she can quiet down the moment you start speaking yet only truly yields the floor when she is sure you mean it. She is also like a chess player who quietly plans her next move during your pause but only says it out loud once she is confident you have finished, and anything you clearly did not hear is erased from her notebook as if it was never said. Under the hood she reuses the ears she already has for transcription to also judge turn-taking, keeping the extra brain tiny and fast. The honest headline is that her learned sense of when you are done still loses to a simple stopwatch on real conversations, so the stopwatch stays in charge while her judgment remains just one advisory voice among several.

## TL;DR

Voice-Light is a full-duplex cascaded (streaming Nemotron ASR → Qwen LLM with typed tools → Kyutai TTS) voice agent built around the rule that uncertain work may begin early but may become audible or durable only after explicit causal checks, combining immediate acoustic onset via Silero, a ~183k-parameter causal turn-taking adapter sharing the frozen Nemotron 0.6B streaming encoder, a typed hybrid controller for reversible ducking, floor-taking, interruption, backchannel resumption, and stale-generation rejection, and private speculative generation whose text and PCM carry generation identifiers until transcript and turn checks promote them. Durable conversation history contains only browser-acknowledged audio, and structured tool calls run concurrently with short audible bridges under a narrower spoken protocol. Locked evaluation on 1,673 real-conversation silence candidates shows the historical learned completion policy (step 3,500) preserved a 2.70% false-cutoff rate but reached only 12.53% end-of-turn recall versus 95.60% for Silero and 91.50% for LiveKit timing policies, so the deployed system retains a hybrid controller; a three-session, 36-turn operator microphone case study measured 758 ms median final-VAD-to-first-server-audio latency with 21 of 36 turns below 800 ms. Contributions are framed strictly as systems and evaluation contributions with released synthetic corpora, adapters, evaluation code, and deployment configuration, explicitly disclaiming a new adapter state of the art, general tool competence, or population-level interaction quality.

## Problem & Motivation

A conventional voice assistant looks like a clean serial pipeline of speech recognition, language modeling, and speech synthesis, but that abstraction hides the real interaction problem that Voice-Light tackles. In natural speech, the system must decide whether a silence is a completed turn or a within-turn hesitation, react immediately when the user starts speaking during playback without canceling on every short acknowledgment or backchannel, ensure that canceled or unheard audio can never leak back into conversation history, and distinguish text it merely generated from speech that actually reached the listener's ears. End-to-end full-duplex models avoid explicit pipeline boundaries by jointly modeling both speakers, but they sacrifice the inspectability that a cascade offers, namely intermediate text, typed tool calls, playback state, and per-component failures. Voice-Light is motivated by keeping the cascade precisely because those boundaries make overlap reversible, stale speculative work rejectable, and durable history constructible from acknowledged audio, and it poses three research questions around whether a small causal adapter reusing a persistent streaming ASR encoder can improve turn commitment over deployable timing baselines, whether a reversible controller can exploit uncertain evidence without turning every voice-activity onset into a cancellation, and how much response latency private speculation can hide in a deployed cascade.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Shared-encoder causal turn-taking adapter:** a small trainable head of approximately 183,000 parameters attached to the frozen Nemotron Speech Streaming 0.6B encoder, tapping layers 6, 12, 18, and 24 with per-tap normalization and projection to 32 dimensions fused to 64 dimensions, followed by residual causal depthwise-separable convolutions and a single-layer 64-dimensional unidirectional GRU whose convolution and recurrent state persist incrementally and reset with the ASR stream, emitting completion and yield evidence, interaction events including floor take and non-floor feedback, and future activity without loading a second speech backbone.
2. **Typed hybrid controller with reversible playback:** the meeting point of immediate acoustic reaction and learned interaction evidence, where speech onset may duck or pause playback immediately but only stronger lexical, learned, or timeout evidence commits cancellation, covering reversible ducking, floor-taking, interruption, backchannel resumption, stale-generation rejection, and conservative deadlines informed by ASR signals, transcript revisions, and playback state.
3. **Private speculative generation:** preparing downstream language-model text and TTS PCM before a final turn commitment while keeping that work private under generation identifiers until transcript and turn checks promote it, integrated with streaming ASR, Qwen generation, Kyutai synthesis of only controller-released text, and sequential tool rounds, so speculation hides downstream compute rather than serving as an early commitment.
4. **Acknowledged-audio durable history rule:** the browser streams 16 kHz microphone PCM over one WebSocket and acknowledges rendered audio ranges, and generated assistant text becomes durable conversation context only to the extent that acknowledgments show it was audible, with structured tool calls executing concurrently with audible bridge speech while causally ordered call and result records stay consistent.
5. **Synthetic corpora plus locked, gate-kept evaluation methodology:** two purpose-built training branches with a narrower spoken-tool protocol and coherent conversation timelines rather than isolated utterances, paired with explicitly separated offline protocols, frozen policies before locked tests, conversation-disjoint splits, and predefined deployment gates, where the V2 semantic-completion test split stayed sealed because no detector met the joint gate and the paper reports the learned policy's failure against timing baselines as a central result.

## Key Findings

The training-data effort produced two substantial branches whose scale and limits are carefully bounded. Spoken tool-use supervision was generated by a pinned Qwen3.6-27B-FP8 teacher as provider-neutral records across search, calculation, and time queries, yielding 3,999 conversations with 11,810 user messages, 15,308 assistant messages, and 3,498 structured calls, where synthetic search results came from the teacher rather than the live web so the corpus teaches call ordering and grounding to supplied text rather than factual retrieval. The Qwen3-1.7B rank-16 BF16 LoRA experiment trained 17.43 million parameters over 904 optimizer steps in 47 minutes on an RTX 4090 across 14,391 regrouped histories and 17.84 million rendered tokens, and on held-out synthetic states it emitted 200 of 210 expected calls with near-perfect schema validity, no-tool accuracy, and post-result continuation, which the paper treats strictly as acquisition of the generator's conversational protocol rather than general tool intelligence. The turn-taking branch rendered only user audio per unit with Qwen VoiceDesign-conditioned CosyVoice 3 voices, applied a HOLD-supervision rule requiring at least 500 ms of silence followed by resumed speech so future assistant state could not leak in, and retained V4 and V5 manifests of 1,092 and 1,097 timelines at roughly 21.8 hours each, while the locked human corpus holds 107 conversations totaling 36.30 hours across conversation-disjoint splits. Synthetic pretraining exposed the transfer gap directly, with completion AUROC of 0.9260 on 893 held-out synthetic examples collapsing to 0.5646 on 789 clean human validation labels and rising only to 0.5972 after human fine-tuning on 1,884 boundaries with 15 percent synthetic replay, which selected the deployed 750-step checkpoint reaching 65.04 percent end-of-turn recall with 3.73 percent false cutoffs and 2.0 s p95 commitment latency on validation.

The locked evaluations deliver the paper's negative result with unusual transparency. Detectors are compared as complete causal policies at their native gates rather than on identical silence, with Voice-Light scoring at 80 ms against Smart Turn at 240 ms, LiveKit at 320 ms, and Silero updating a silence proxy on 32 ms chunks, using pinned Silero VAD 6.2.1, Pipecat Smart Turn v3.2, and LiveKit v1-mini implementations. On the locked real-conversation V1 test of 1,673 causal silence candidates from 11 conversations with only 37 HOLD cases, the historical step-3,500 Voice-Light policy at threshold 0.90 with 560 ms minimum delay and 800 ms timeout held false cutoffs to 2.70 percent but crossed its learned threshold on only 205 of 1,636 end-of-turn cases for 12.53 percent recall at 770 ms mean latency including timeout actions, while Silero and LiveKit reached 95.60 and 91.50 percent recall at the same 2.70 percent false-cutoff rate with lower mean latencies around 655 ms. The V2 semantic-completion validation inventory of 1,005 soft targets and 789 clean hard labels showed the same pattern, with historical step 3,500 reaching AUROC 0.6866 and 65.80 percent recall but calibration (BCE 0.6719, Brier 0.1843) worse than a constant soft-prior baseline, and since no swept detector met the joint gate of at most 5 percent false cutoff with at least 70 percent recall and at most 800 ms p95 latency, the V2 test split remained sealed. Deployment evidence comes from three unscripted single-operator microphone sessions against a scale-to-zero Modal service in the European region with cold start excluded, where 36 measured response turns showed 758 ms median final-VAD-to-first-server-audio latency with a 932 ms mean, a 528 to 1,652 ms range, and 21 turns below 800 ms, while the 13 fully traced turns showed overlapping component medians of 502 ms endpoint commitment, 336 ms to first LLM word, 444 ms to first TTS PCM, and 95 ms server-to-browser render, with speculative promotion associated with 667 ms versus 1,513 ms without promotion as an observational split confounded with transcript stability rather than a causal speedup estimate.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper's own discussion converts the negative result into an evaluation agenda, arguing that a learned policy should replace the hybrid controller only if it improves the latency-versus-false-cancel frontier on conversation-disjoint human labels with participant-level confidence intervals, controlled overlap scenarios, and final-topology action latencies. Concretely, the locked V1 test is fragile with only 11 conversations and 37 HOLD cases where a single error swings false cutoffs by 2.70 percentage points and within-conversation candidates are correlated, possible training-source overlap leaves the Smart Turn comparison contextual only, V2 excludes 216 ambiguous cases and still lacks its planned independent human label audit, and the deployed step-750 adapter was never evaluated on the locked V1 test while its floor-take and backchannel heads remain uncalibrated against independently annotated natural conversation. The deployment study likewise calls for a genuine user study rather than three unscripted single-operator sessions, since nothing is yet established about naturalness, interruption accuracy, or latency distributions across users, accents, noise, echo, networks, or GPU types, and the system remains English-first with human audio restricted by source terms and factual generation dependent on a small language model plus external retrieval. Broader directions include measuring every learned component at the boundary where it will be used instead of inferring deployment value from training-domain metrics, independently calibrating adapter heads rather than retaining them as engineering telemetry, and extending the reproducible hybrid-system artifacts with stronger controlled turn-taking benchmarks.
