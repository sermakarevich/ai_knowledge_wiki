# SteerDuplex: Steerable Duplex Speech Dialogue Models

**Paper:** [SteerDuplex: Steerable Duplex Speech Dialogue Models (arXiv, 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12623)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Most voice AIs today are like musicians who play smoothly but ignore the conductor — they handle turn-taking and interruptions well yet fail when asked to change their tone, persona, or speaking speed mid-conversation. SteerDuplex is like an actor who actually takes direction, reliably shifting into a sarcastic tone, a pirate persona, a whisper, or short answers when the caller asks, while still knowing when to speak, pause, or yield the floor. The team trained it on real conversations plus synthetic steering examples and then coached its timing with reinforcement learning, and built a new 390-prompt exam called SteerBench to grade both what it says and how it sounds. The result steers far better than prior open models and interrupts less awkwardly, though teaching it perfect timing remains a balancing act between speaking up and holding back.

## TL;DR

SteerDuplex is a Moshi-based full-duplex speech dialogue model that adds steerability — the reliable shift of conversational behavior along tone, persona, speaking rate, and voice style in response to user instructions — on top of low-latency turn-taking, interruption handling, and backchanneling. It is fine-tuned on natural conversations plus synthetic dialogues covering instruction following, vocal delivery, reasoning, safety, and duplex interaction, followed by two-stage reinforcement learning with hybrid rewards combining verifiable interaction-timing checks and judge-based semantic feedback. Training uses a masked system-prompt prefix conditioning joint audio/text streams with temporal plus depth transformers over hierarchical audio codes, and a component-normalized GDPO objective with continuity terms that discourage short timing-satisfying but non-sustained answers. Evaluation introduces SteerBench with 390 spoken prompts and 1,067 human-authored binary audio and text rubrics spanning tone, persona, style/accent, and speed/length, complemented by Audio MultiChallenge, VoiceBench, and Full-Duplex-Bench. Supervised training lifts audio-steering average pass rate to 65.10% versus 20.55% for Moshi and 16.44% for PersonaPlex, and RL further improves source-clean interruption response from 72.5% to 82.5% while cutting synthetic pause barge-in from 26.5% to 9%, with reward probes showing timing gains must be weighed against response completeness.

## Problem & Motivation

Full-duplex spoken dialogue models already listen and speak simultaneously, enabling fluent turn-taking, backchanneling, and interruption handling, yet fluency alone does not guarantee that a model follows instructions about tone, persona, or vocal delivery. The paper argues that spoken dialogue conveys emotion, accent, and timing cues that text transcripts do not preserve, so a useful conversational partner must manage when to speak and how to sound, not just what to say. This gap is quantified starkly: under matched items and the same judge, Moshi and PersonaPlex reach audio-steering average pass rates of only 20.55% and 16.44%, showing that steerability along attributes such as tone, persona, speaking rate, and voice style remains underexplored. Duplex benchmarks assess pauses, interruptions, and backchannels as distinct behaviors, so task compliance and floor management require separate checks alongside intelligibility, timing, and task capability. SteerDuplex and SteerBench are motivated as the joint answer: a model post-training recipe that preserves general conversational capability while adding reliable steering, plus a benchmark that measures requested content and delivery with separate text and audio rubrics anchored by fixed reference clips.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Three-family capability taxonomy:** the paper organizes spoken steerability into natural-language steering (what the model says), acoustic understanding and adaptation (how it sounds, grounded by audio references), and general duplex interaction (how it participates through turn-taking, interruption handling, backchanneling, pause handling, and mid-utterance corrections). Steerability is defined as the reliable shift of such behavior in response to user instructions, distinguished from ordinary instruction following, and the taxonomy guides both training and evaluation by assessing requested content and delivery alongside timing and task capability.

2. **SteerDuplex supervised fine-tuning:** built on the public Moshi 7B backbone, the model jointly generates audio and text streams at 12.5 Hz under a masked system-prompt prefix with temporal plus depth transformers predicting text and eight hierarchical RVQ audio codebooks. Fine-tuning combines recorded conversations (including in-house two-person and Fisher English material) with synthetic speech and text examples targeting instruction following, requested delivery, reasoning, safety, and duplex interaction, establishing steering and task capability before any RL.

3. **Two-stage hybrid RL with component-normalized GDPO:** continuing from the SFT checkpoint, stage one rewards response continuity (weight 0.5, 4-second first-response target) and stage two adds a continuation-duration bonus (weight 2.0, 4-second target) on noise and user-backchannel events with dedicated backchannel sampling, each stage freezing its initial model as the KL reference. Rollout groups sampled from interaction windows (turns, interruptions, pauses, backchannels, noise, and speech-mirror scenarios) receive interaction-timing rewards (weight 1.0), a Gemini 3.6 Flash transcript judge on turn and interruption groups (weight 0.75), and a waveform-integrity gate rejecting silence, clipping, and invalid outputs, with each reward component normalized separately within its rollout group so raw scale cannot dominate. Policy loss covers text-stream actions only, including sampled padding tokens that encode pause and onset timing, while audio-codebook actions receive no direct policy loss and use an adaptive sampled-action KL penalty.

4. **SteerBench benchmark:** the evaluation set contains 390 spoken prompts (100 tone, 136 persona, 104 style/accent, 50 speed/length) combining concrete tasks with steering requests, assessed with 1,067 human-authored binary rubrics (438 audio, 629 text) that pair an axis with a binary criterion. Text and audio criteria are judged separately with audio decisions conditioned on fixed reference clips, user utterances are rendered as neutral conversational speech via Gemini TTS with stable hashed voice assignment, and aggregation uses audio-steering average pass rate (every applicable audio rubric), sample pass rate (every applicable rubric), and rubric pass rate (mean of binary decisions), with items carrying judge-call errors excluded and runs over 5% errors ruled invalid.

## Key Findings

Supervised fine-tuning supplies the main steerability gains, lifting audio-steering average pass rate to 65.10 ± 1.13%, a 44.5-percentage-point improvement over the strongest evaluated open baseline, with sample pass rates of 32%–51.11% and rubric pass rates of 63.10%–77.03% (tone 49.7/71.5, persona 51.1/77.0, style-accent 48.4/69.8, and speed-length 32.0/63.1 sample versus rubric rates). General capability is preserved or improved: Audio MultiChallenge task average pass rate rises about 7 points to 13.64 ± 0.28% average pass rate and 37.17 ± 1.33% average rubric score versus 6.64%/20.56% for PersonaPlex, VoiceBench mean reaches 40.87 ± 0.27 versus 38.55 for Moshi and 30.51 for PersonaPlex, and FDB-v2 scores 4.17 under the slow examiner versus 2.59 and 2.65 for the baselines. Reinforcement learning then refines interaction behavior on source-clean and synthetic probes: correct response after interruption rises from 72.5% to 82.5%, continuation after a user backchannel rises from 71.4% to 80.6%, synthetic pause barge-in falls from 26.5% to 9%, and synthetic interruption response rate edges from 96% to 97.7%, while background-speech recovery is effectively flat (60% to 59%), recovery after speech directed elsewhere rises modestly (42% to 48%), semantic score dips slightly (3.94 to 3.88), and mean takeover latency grows by 40 ms — better response timing without uniform improvement in every interaction measure. Capability retention tables show small deltas (AudioMC +0.74 pass rate, SteerBench rubric pass +1.47, VoiceBench +0.51, FDB-v2 mean −0.003), and reward probes reveal the central caution: promptness-only optimization attains 0.670 scalar reward but scores zero on the shared duplex diagnostic with 25 of 96 rollouts empty, single-family text, audio-rubric, and audio-quality probes each leave 41–47 empty rollouts, and continued second-stage optimization raises interruption reward (0.450 to 0.793) while continuation after backchannels falls (3.20 to 2.00 seconds) and noise-robustness reward declines (1.96 to 0.77), showing explicit continuity rewards reduce but do not eliminate the yielding-versus-continuing conflict.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper's own conclusion frames the division of labor clearly: supervised fine-tuning supplies the main steerability gains while RL refines interaction behavior, and premature yielding calls for evaluating conversational events alongside aggregate task success rather than trusting headline scores alone. Because timing rewards can be hacked through silence or truncated responses, the authors keep speech-gated interruption credit, continuity terms, waveform-validity checks, and held-out safety and capability evaluation, while noting that group normalization cannot supply gradient from a component that goes constant within a rollout group and that staging effects are not isolated by the controls. Natural next steps implied by the benchmark documentation are extending beyond single-turn English steering under explicit requests toward long-term personalization, multilingual coverage, and deeper multi-turn interaction, alongside resolving the interference between yielding and continuing objectives. Code, SteerBench, and checkpoints are slated for release upon acceptance under a research license with audio release following consent and source terms, and benchmark test material is excluded from optimization to keep future comparisons honest.
