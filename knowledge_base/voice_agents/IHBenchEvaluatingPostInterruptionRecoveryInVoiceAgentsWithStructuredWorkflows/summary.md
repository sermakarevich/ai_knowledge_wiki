# IHBench: Evaluating Post-Interruption Recovery in Voice Agents with Structured Workflows

**Paper:** [IHBench: Evaluating Post-Interruption Recovery in Voice Agents with Structured Workflows (Salimi et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19595)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine a phone agent walking you through an insurance claim step by step, like a receptionist with a checklist, when you cut in mid-sentence with "actually, that's my work address" or just a distracted "mm-hm." Most voice-AI tests only check whether the agent politely stops talking, but IHBench checks what happens next: does it remember its place on the checklist, use your correction, and pick up exactly where it left off instead of starting over or rambling? The authors built a practice course of 45 scripted calls across 10 business domains with hundreds of staged interruptions, then graded 27 voice models on both finishing the job and recovering gracefully. The headline lesson is that finishing the task and recovering gracefully are partly separate skills, brand-name closed models hold their place in long calls much better than open models, and even the simplest "mm-hm" trips up many famous models.

## TL;DR

IHBench defines post-interruption recovery as a distinct evaluation axis for voice agents executing state-machine-driven workflows and measures it across 10 enterprise domains with controlled mid-utterance interruptions. The benchmark contains 45 synthetic multi-turn conversations with 428 interruption points spanning six interruption types from cooperative (normal, filler) through directive (impatient, correction) to adversarial (pushback, topic switch), averaging 30.1 messages per conversation, each interruption shipped with a per-interruption rubric generated at construction time. Scoring is two-axis: comparative task fulfillment (TF) by randomized LLM judging against the GPT-4o Audio baseline, and absolute recovery quality (RQ) pass rate requiring all type-specific rubric criteria to be met, with GPT-5.4-mini as primary judge over three epochs per configuration and 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Across 27 audio-language model configurations from OpenAI, Google, and the open-weight community, GPT Realtime 2 (medium, thinking) leads TF at .728 while Gemini 2.5 Flash (thinking) leads RQ at .704 despite lower TF, closed-weight models dominate TF, degrade roughly 3.3x more slowly with conversation depth, and show no audio-versus-text modality gap while open-weight models lose on all three dimensions, and validation via a second judge, human studies, and comparison against AudioMultiChallenge frames RQ as a largely distinct capability axis.

## Problem & Motivation

Real-time voice agents from the GPT Realtime, Gemini, and Moshi families are moving into production customer-service, healthcare-scheduling, and enterprise workflows where interruption is the norm rather than an edge case, including corrections, impatience, topic switches, and backchannels like "mm-hm." Existing benchmarks such as Full-Duplex-Bench and its successors, FLEXI, SID-Bench, HumDial, Talking Turns, and INSTRUCT-FD answer the first question of interruption handling — whether the model detects the barge-in and stops or yields the floor — but leave unanswered the second question of what the model says next. The paper illustrates this with an insurance-claim agent cut off mid-sentence by "Actually, it was my work address, not home," which must stop, recognize the correction, integrate it, avoid repeating already-heard content, and resume at the correct workflow step, noting that failing to stop is merely awkward while stopping but restarting, ignoring the correction, or losing workflow place creates a fundamentally broken interaction. Early evidence in the paper shows these recovery failures are widespread yet invisible to timing benchmarks, with all evaluated GPT-family audio models continuing correctly after a backchannel less than a third of the time while Gemini 2.5 is markedly better and the newer Gemini 3.x line regresses sharply, which the authors frame as undertrained rather than inherently difficult behavior that targeted evaluation can surface and fix.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Post-interruption recovery as a distinct two-axis construct** — The paper decomposes recovery into six interruption types with type-specific recovery requirements and scores every interruption on two partially independent axes: comparative task-fulfillment judging and absolute criterion-based recovery-quality assessment, both powered by LLM judges.
2. **Six-type interruption taxonomy spanning intent space** — The types cover normal cut-ins, filler backchannels requiring exact continuation without repetition or acknowledgement, impatient skip requests, self-corrections of prior-turn information, adversarial pushback requiring empathetic de-escalation, and unrelated topic switches requiring a brief detour plus a steer back without blending workflows, chosen to span cooperative through directive to adversarial intents observed in real customer-service conversations.
3. **IHBench synthetic workflow-grounded benchmark** — Forty-five conversations across 10 enterprise domains (SaaS, financial services, healthcare, telecom, e-commerce, travel, education, government, subscription media, professional services) are seeded from domain-goal-intent triples and driven by an assistant-led state machine with explicit stages, skip conditions, and terminal success and hard-stop failure definitions, yielding 428 interruption points concentrated in early turns by design and by synthesis constraints.
4. **Multi-agent generation pipeline with fixed-at-construction rubrics** — A six-agent pipeline with a system-message writer, round planner, assistant and user simulators with separate interruption-handling and normal branches, verifier, and audio synthesis scripts one assistant turn plus one possibly mid-utterance interrupting user turn per round with controlled cutoff placement, overlap timing, and spoken-style grounding, fixing each item's per-interruption rubric before any model response exists and enforcing state consistency, leakage prevention, and recovery-target validity through a verify-modify loop.
5. **Validated two-judge plus human evaluation protocol** — The 27-configuration evaluation uses GPT-5.4-mini with high reasoning as primary judge, Gemini 3 Flash with thinking as an independent second judge, re-scoring on summarized context as a control, and two Prolific human studies that together establish ranking preservation, substantial inter-judge agreement, and human-level judge-human agreement, alongside TOST equivalence testing of audio versus transcript input and a submodular cross-benchmark comparison against AudioMultiChallenge.

## Key Findings

GPT Realtime 2 (medium, thinking) achieves the highest task-fulfillment win rate at .728 with RQ .624, followed by the xhigh variant and GPT Realtime 1.5, while recovery quality tells a different story with Gemini 2.5 Flash (thinking) highest at .704 despite mid-tier TF and GPT Realtime highest among GPT models at .680 with only mid-tier TF, confirming that task advancement and recovery quality are partially independent. Recovery varies sharply by interruption type: normal interruptions and topic switches are handled well across models while filler backchannels are the single largest model-to-model differentiator, with GPT-family pass rates of only 7% to 31% through restarting, acknowledging, or reinventing the utterance versus 62% to 68% for the Gemini 2.5 family and a sharp regression to 13% to 32% in Gemini 3.x. Task fulfillment degrades significantly with conversation depth with a mean logistic slope of minus .030 per turn across 26 audio configurations, and open-weight models degrade roughly 3.3 times faster than closed-weight ones with all 10 open configurations negative against a closed mean near zero, which the authors attribute tentatively to shorter training contexts without being able to separate data, scale, and modality-alignment causes. The modality analysis finds audio and text-only input statistically equivalent within plus-or-minus .02 for the six Gemini configurations while nine open-weight configurations gain about 8 points on TF and 6 points on RQ from transcripts with audio never winning anywhere, and the cross-benchmark analysis finds IHBench RQ the lowest-correlated axis in the joint matrix with four AudioMultiChallenge axes, selected second by greedy entropy selection, supporting recovery quality as a largely distinct capability rather than general long-context coherence.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The authors acknowledge that IHBench is built from synthetic English-only conversations across 10 enterprise domains with rubrics that inherit generator and judge biases, and that it evaluates only the textual content of recovery rather than prosodic or acoustic behavior such as timing and intonation after a barge-in. They propose multilingual coverage, validation against real end-user interactions in live deployment, and integration with full-duplex timing benchmarks for end-to-end evaluation of both interruption timing and recovery as natural extensions. The most actionable direction is turning the benchmark and its synthetic pipeline into a training signal by post-training on the rubrics and generated data to strengthen exactly the undertrained recovery behaviors the benchmark currently only measures, particularly filler continuation, deep-conversation robustness, and audio-modality alignment in open-weight models.
