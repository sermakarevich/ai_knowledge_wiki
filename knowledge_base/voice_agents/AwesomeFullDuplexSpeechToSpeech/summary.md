# awesome full duplex speech-to-speech

**Article:** [awesome-full-duplex-speech-to-speech](https://github.com/cyrta/awesome-full-duplex-speech-to-speech) — GitHub, 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR
This is a curated field guide to a new kind of voice AI that can listen and talk at the same time, the way a good conversational partner does instead of treating dialogue like a walkie-talkie exchange. Older voice systems work in rigid turns where you speak, then the machine thinks, then it answers, while these full-duplex systems keep their ears open even while speaking so they can handle interruptions, overlapping talk, and little "uh-huh" acknowledgements gracefully. The guide organizes everything a newcomer or researcher needs to orient themselves, spanning the models, how audio is represented, the datasets and benchmarks used to test them, the open challenges, and the commercial products already shipping this behavior. Think of it as a map of a fast-moving neighborhood, anchored by landmark arrivals like Moshi, GPT-4o Realtime, and Gemini Live that turned simultaneous conversation from a research idea into something you can actually talk to.

## TL;DR
This repository is a curated, categorized guide to full-duplex speech-to-speech conversational models that comprehensively maps the landscape across models, architectures, audio and speech representations, datasets, evaluation benchmarks, challenges, publications, and open-source and commercial projects. Its central framing is that full-duplex spoken language models process audio continuously with parallel encoding and generation inside a unified processing cycle, enabling simultaneous listening and speaking with natural turn-taking, overlapping speech, backchanneling, and interruptions. This is positioned against both traditional cascade pipelines of Speech-to-Text into LLM into Text-to-Speech and half-duplex turn-based systems limited to sequential listen-think-speak loops, with True Full-Duplex voice communication cast as a critical milestone toward human-like interaction. The guide dates the field's decisive paradigm shift to 2022–2023 and credits its acceleration to Moshi from Kyutai in 2024, the SyncLLM framework from Meta AI and the University of Washington in 2024, and commercial deployments including GPT-4o Realtime from OpenAI in 2024 and Gemini Live from Google DeepMind in 2025.

## Problem & Motivation
The problem the guide addresses is fragmentation and velocity in full-duplex speech-to-speech research, where models, representations, datasets, benchmarks, and products are appearing quickly without a single orientation point for comparing architectures or tracking what makes simultaneous human-like conversation possible. Its motivation is therefore cartographic rather than experimental, aiming to map the landscape comprehensively so readers can see how end-to-end systems that listen and speak at once differ from cascaded or turn-based alternatives and where the remaining gaps in data, evaluation, and design lie. Underlying this effort is the conviction that natural conversation demands more than accurate transcription plus synthesis, because it requires continuous audio processing that supports active listening, graceful interruption handling, and fluid timing, which sequential listen-think-speak pipelines structurally cannot deliver.

## Main Original Ideas
1. **Continuous parallel listen-and-speak framing:** the guide's core conceptual contribution is to define full-duplex speech-to-speech as unified-cycle systems with parallel encoding and generation rather than merely faster turn-taking. This distinction matters because it cleanly separates continuous simultaneous processors from cascade pipelines and half-duplex designs, giving every later comparison of models and representations a shared architectural yardstick.
2. **True Full-Duplex as an explicit milestone:** it elevates simultaneous listening and speaking with natural turn-taking, overlapping speech, backchanneling, and interruptions into a named target called True Full-Duplex voice communication. By naming the target this way, the guide turns a vague aspiration for human-like interaction into a concrete horizon that datasets, benchmarks, and systems can all be judged against.
3. **Categorized landscape taxonomy:** it proposes a stable table of contents covering models, audio and speech representations, datasets, benchmarks, challenges, publications, other learning materials, workshops and special sessions, open-source projects, and commercial products as the scaffolding for historically ordered comparison. This taxonomy is valuable because it lets a fast-moving field accumulate entries along fixed axes instead of scattering them across disconnected announcements and demos.
4. **Paradigm-shift periodization:** it attributes the field's decisive break to 2022–2023 and anchors that claim with four public reference points in Moshi, the SyncLLM framework, GPT-4o Realtime, and Gemini Live. This periodization converts a diffuse trend into a datable transition from research prototypes toward deployed simultaneous-conversation systems, which helps readers understand why the guide exists now.

## Key Findings
Within the limits of the available wiki material, which captures the repository's front matter and skeleton rather than populated tables, the substantive findings are the guide's definitional claims and its historical framing of the field. Full-duplex models are characterized as end-to-end continuous audio processors whose distinguishing behaviors are active listening, natural interruptions, instantaneous human-like backchanneling, natural turn-taking, and overlapping speech, all enabled by parallel encoding and generation.
