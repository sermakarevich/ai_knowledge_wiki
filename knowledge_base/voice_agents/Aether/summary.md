# Project Aether

**Article:** [Project Aether](https://github.com/lucifer5051/Aether) — GitHub

## Human Readable TL;DR

Think of Project Aether as teaching an ordinary gaming laptop to hold a natural phone conversation where both sides can talk and interrupt each other, like two people chatting over dinner rather than walkie-talkies. It fits a speech codec, a chat brain, an ear for transcription, and a reflex for detecting interruptions all onto a single 6 GB laptop GPU by carefully scheduling who gets the graphics chip at each moment, much like an air-traffic controller spacing out landings. When you cut in mid-sentence, a fast acoustic reflex mutes the system in tens of milliseconds instead of waiting nearly a second for the full transcription, the way a listener notices your voice before understanding your words. And because the laptop's own speaker would otherwise trick it into thinking it interrupted itself, a lightweight echo canceller subtracts the outgoing sound, like noise-cancelling headphones for the microphone.

## TL;DR

Project Aether is an independent realtime full-duplex voice AI research project that demonstrates genuine streaming conversational speech running entirely on a consumer RTX 3050 6 GB laptop GPU. The validated stack co-executes the Mimi neural audio codec at 24 kHz and 80 ms frames, NF4-quantized Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct for generation, CPU-only Sherpa-ONNX Streaming Zipformer int8 for ASR, an acoustic VAD barge-in controller, and a linear NLMS plus Geigel-DTD echo canceller. Eleven research steps (AETHER-1 through AETHER-11) progressively froze memory coexistence with 1,636.1 MB peak VRAM and 71.3% headroom, Cooperative Yielding GPU scheduling with zero deadline misses over 2,250 frames, Zipformer at 6.13% WER and 0.0598 RTF with an 80 ms cadence, VAD interruption about 755 ms faster than the ASR path, and AEC at roughly 20 dB ERLE reaching Class A production-ready status.

## Problem & Motivation

The central problem Project Aether tackles is whether true full-duplex voice interaction — continuous listening, natural interruption, backchannels, and open-speaker playback — can run on modest consumer hardware rather than server GPUs or cloud APIs. A 6 GB laptop GPU must simultaneously host a streaming neural codec and a large language model without blowing the memory budget or stalling the strict 80 ms audio frame deadlines, while CPU resources must absorb streaming recognition without contending for the GPU. Beyond raw coexistence, conversational responsiveness demands sub-100 ms interruption handling rather than the roughly 800 ms delay of waiting for finalized transcripts, and open-speaker operation introduces acoustic self-echo that naively triggers false barge-ins. The project is therefore motivated by hardware-aware execution: proving that careful scheduling, pipeline-level latency design, and lightweight signal processing can deliver natural turn-taking inside a tight consumer envelope.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Memory-coexistence baseline on 6 GB VRAM:** the project first establishes empirically that Mimi plus Qwen 1.5B NF4 fit together with only 1,636.1 MB peak allocated and ample free headroom, reframing the bottleneck from capacity to compute contention when uncoordinated generation spikes Mimi p95 latency.
2. **Cooperative Yielding GPU scheduler:** after comparing uncoordinated execution, CUDA stream priority, cooperative yielding, and fine-grained slicing, the work freezes a yielding regime in which Qwen generation pauses around Mimi frames, eliminating deadline violations while sustaining higher token throughput than prioritized streams.
3. **CPU-offloaded streaming ASR selection:** rather than accepting GPU-bound recognizers, the project surveys and benchmarks streaming ASR candidates and freezes int8 Zipformer on CPU with zero GPU VRAM and sub-0.10 RTF, explicitly trading a small accuracy gap against VRAM and SM contention under Mimi load.
4. **Acoustic VAD barge-in controller:** observing that acoustic onset precedes usable ASR partials by around 800 ms, the project implements a dedicated controller with 20 ms frames, two-frame confirmation, and 100 ms pre-roll that cancels generation in tens of milliseconds while keeping Zipformer resets sub-millisecond and leak-free.
5. **Linear AEC preprocessor with double-talk preservation:** after showing that heuristic echo gating rejects all single-talk echoes but suppresses most legitimate interruptions, the project adopts an NLMS adaptive filter with Geigel double-talk detection fed by the playback FIFO, achieving strong echo suppression without sacrificing double-talk interruption detection.
6. **Eleven-step frozen-stack research loop:** the overarching method is an incremental freeze-each-layer protocol from AETHER-1 to AETHER-11, with benchmark scripts and integration reports per step, converging on warm-started AEC weights, transient startup protection, and a five-scenario end-to-end validation suite.

## Key Findings

Memory and scheduling proved jointly feasible on the RTX 3050 laptop GPU, with 1,636.1 MB peak allocated VRAM leaving 4,379.5 MB free while idle Qwen caused no Mimi latency impact and active uncoordinated generation pushed p95 Mimi latency to 99.16 ms. Both CUDA stream priority and Cooperative Yielding removed all deadline violations, but Cooperative Yielding was selected and then sustained over 60-second runs with zero misses in 2,250 frames at 10.37 ms p50 latency and no backlog, leak, or thread growth. On recognition, Sherpa-ONNX Streaming Zipformer int8 on CPU delivered 6.13% WER at 0.0598 average RTF with two threads, while Moonshine Tiny offered slightly better accuracy at the cost of 150–250 MB VRAM and GPU contention, and Whisper and NeMo variants were eliminated. Cadence experiments showed 40, 80, and 160 ms ingestion gave identical time-to-first-partial behavior with a 470 ms readiness floor and roughly 0.8–0.9 s speech-onset to useful partial, leading to a frozen 80 ms cadence and a shift to pipeline-level latency work. The decisive latency result was that 20 ms VAD detects onset in about 45 ms and cancels interruption roughly 755 ms faster than the 800.59 ms ASR-dependent path, with FIFO clear and mute overhead under 0.02 ms, and the integrated controller validated clean cancellation with pre-roll preservation. Echo analysis confirmed that open-speaker playback reliably false-triggers VAD and that heuristic gating is unusable for full duplex, whereas NLMS plus Geigel DTD reached about 20 dB ERLE with full single-talk rejection at moderate coupling, full double-talk detection, only 90–100 ms added detection latency and under 2 ms CPU per frame, converging within 300–500 ms and holding zero Mimi misses under full concurrent stress before integration testing declared Class A production-ready status.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

As framed by the research progression, the natural next directions are hardening the defined operating envelope rather than replacing the frozen stack: shortening or masking the 300–500 ms AEC cold-start convergence, extending transient startup protection, and characterizing louder coupling and noisier rooms beyond the validated single-talk thresholds. Further work includes richer backchannel and natural turn-taking behavior on top of the barge-in controller, continued perceived-latency optimization given the fixed Zipformer readiness floor, and broader hardware validation beyond the single Windows 11 RTX 3050 plus i5-13450HX reference machine. The benchmark and report artifacts from AETHER-4 through AETHER-11 provide the harness for that follow-up, with the integrated AetherAEC and AetherBargeInController modules positioned as the extension points.
