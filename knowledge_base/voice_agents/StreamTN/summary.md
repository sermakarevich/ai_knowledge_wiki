# StreamTN: A Low-Latency Streaming Chinese Text Normalization Model for Streaming TTS in Dialogue Systems

**Paper:** [StreamTN: A Low-Latency Streaming Chinese Text Normalization Model for Streaming TTS in Dialogue Systems](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24267v1) — arXiv, 21 Sep 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

When a voice assistant speaks an answer out loud, raw text like "2024/3/5" or "H2SO4" is ambiguous because the same characters can be read many different ways, like a musician sight-reading shorthand that only makes sense with context. StreamTN acts like a simultaneous interpreter sitting between the chatbot's brain and its mouth, converting each chunk of text into speakable words after hearing just a few tokens instead of waiting for the whole sentence. It does this with two parallel assembly lines, one carrying the incoming raw text and one carrying the spoken-form output, fused together with a small adjustable head start that trades a little waiting time for much better pronunciation.

## TL;DR

StreamTN is a lightweight LLM-based Chinese streaming text normalization (TN) model built on Qwen3-0.6B for cascaded LLM-centered spoken dialogue systems (SDS), designed to incrementally convert partial LLM outputs into TTS-readable text with controllable first-packet delay. Its dual-track streaming architecture decouples raw-text input tokens and normalized-text output tokens into two parallel tracks whose embeddings are fused before the Transformer backbone, remaining compatible with standard autoregressive decoding while input lookahead is governed by a fixed token-delay parameter d. Trained with a masked negative log-likelihood objective under partial input context on a newly constructed dialogue-oriented benchmark of 95,793 training and 1,262 manually inspected test samples across 14 categories, the 4-frame configuration reaches 0.8937 Micro-F1 and 0.8931 Micro-Precision at 213 ms first-packet delay, matching a BiLSTM baseline while supporting true streaming and far exceeding rule-based and prompt-based alternatives.

## Problem & Motivation

Modern LLM-centered spoken dialogue systems follow either an end-to-end paradigm that jointly models understanding, response generation, and synthesis or a cascaded paradigm of ASR, LLM, and TTS as independent components, and cascaded systems remain widely used in practice for their controllability, modularity, ease of debugging, and flexibility in integrating retrieval or external knowledge. In such cascaded systems the LLM module's textual response feeds directly into the TTS module, but LLM outputs are full of non-standard words (NSWs) — numbers, dates, times, phone numbers, units, chemical formulas, and mathematical expressions — that are readable to humans yet ambiguous or unsuitable for direct synthesis, since a single number may be pronounced completely differently as a date, phone number, amount, or math expression. Without proper text normalization this ambiguity causes mispronunciations, unnatural prosody, or even outright synthesis failures downstream.

Existing solutions do not fit this streaming dialogue setting. Traditional industrial TN relies on handcrafted rules, regular expressions, and WFST-based grammars that are controllable but demand substantial language-specific engineering and generalize poorly to unseen patterns, while purely neural sequence models risk rare but severe unrecoverable errors that are especially damaging for TTS. Mandarin hybrid and rule-guided models such as multi-head self-attention systems and FlatTN advanced offline resources and modeling but still target sentence-level normalization rather than low-latency streaming in LLM-centered dialogue. Prompt-based LLM normalization in the style of PolyNorm is similarly mismatched because it typically waits for sufficient or complete context, adds latency and prefill cost, and suffers unstable formatting and hallucination, and fine-tuning the core dialogue LLM solely for TN would degrade its general intelligence and reasoning. Compounding this, existing TN datasets focus on conventional NSW categories, offline normalization, or multilingual TTS and miss the distribution, diversity, and latency requirements of real LLM dialogue outputs, which increasingly include scientific terms, chemical formulas, and mathematical expressions. StreamTN is motivated by the resulting dual requirement that a dialogue TN module must generate accurate, pronunciation-correct, semantically consistent TTS-readable text and must do so incrementally with low latency while both the upstream LLM and downstream streaming TTS operate token by token.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Dual-track streaming architecture on Qwen3-0.6B.** StreamTN replaces the conventional offline TN block between the LLM response module and the TTS module with a lightweight fine-tuned Qwen3-0.6B model that processes raw-text input tokens and normalized-text output tokens on two parallel tracks. The input track carries currently available raw context while the output track carries delayed normalized history, and the model generates normalized tokens autoregressively while still consuming new raw tokens, beginning after only a small number of input tokens rather than waiting for the complete response. When the raw stream ends the input track is padded and generation continues from output history until end-of-sequence, keeping the design compatible with standard autoregressive decoding with KV-cache and inspired by Qwen3-Omni and delayed-stream modeling.

2. **Delay-controlled alignment, fusion, and training objective.** The streaming behavior is formalized with a token-delay parameter d that controls how many raw tokens are observed before the first normalized token is emitted and thus governs the latency-versus-context trade-off between emitting too early with incorrect readings and waiting too long with higher first-packet TTS latency. Aligned states pad the raw track beyond length n and delay the output history by d with conceptual `<delay>` and `<pad>` symbols realized as zero vectors after embedding, fused by element-wise addition before a causally masked Qwen3 backbone to yield a streaming factorization over targets conditioned only on observed prefixes and prior history. Training uses a masked negative log-likelihood objective supervised only on valid normalized tokens that explicitly teaches normalization under partial context, while inference reuses the same alignment and latency is reported as end-to-end first-packet delay decomposed into upstream LLM waiting time and intrinsic TN computation time.

3. **Dialogue-oriented Chinese TN benchmark and data pipeline.** The authors consolidate FlatTN's fine-grained NSW scheme into ten unified types and add four new scientific categories covering simple chemical substances, complex chemical equations, simple mathematical formulas, and complex mathematical equations for 14 categories total spanning numbers, dates, time, phone numbers, units, and scientific notation. Raw inputs combine regex-filtered DuReader questions and documents from Baidu Search and Baidu Zhidao with Qwen3-32B-generated scientific samples that were manually reviewed to remove unnatural expressions, and normalized targets are produced by DeepSeek-R1-70B under category-specific pronunciation and readability guidelines with 98.2% consistency on a 5% manual check and full manual inspection and correction of all test references. The resulting resource of 95,793 training and 1,262 held-out test samples evaluates accuracy via character-level micro-averaged precision, recall, and F1 from minimum-cost Levenshtein alignment alongside robustness and streaming-inference latency, with a demonstration released online and a planned release of the trained model and benchmark.

## Key Findings

At its adopted 4-frame operating point StreamTN achieves 0.8937 ± 0.0009 Micro-F1 and 0.8931 ± 0.0022 Micro-Precision with 213 ms first-packet delay, essentially matching the strong data-driven BiLSTM baseline at 0.8941 ± 0.0015 Micro-F1 while adding true incremental processing and beating it on Micro-Precision (0.8931 versus 0.8677), and it substantially outperforms WeTextProcessing (0.7853 / 0.7586), FlatTN (0.7569 / 0.7390), and task-prompted Qwen3-0.6B without fine-tuning or streaming adaptation (0.4872 / 0.5282), which is described as susceptible to format instability, hallucination, and high prefill cost. Per-category results show the model is strongest on regular, frequently observed patterns including physical units (0.977 Micro-F1), capital amounts (0.975), time expressions (0.967), integers and decimals, fractions and percentages, and years and dates (all above 0.95), while complex mathematical equations (0.750) and chemical formulas (0.791) remain the hardest cases due to specialized notation, long verbalizations, and structural diversity, with small standard deviations indicating consistency across training seeds.

Ablations and delay sweeps confirm the design choices and quantify the latency-quality trade-off. Replacing full-parameter fine-tuning with LoRA collapses performance to 0.6335 Micro-F1 and 0.6250 Micro-Precision, indicating that low-rank adapters alone cannot learn the precise TN transformations, while adding a system prompt slightly but consistently degrades results to 0.8852 Micro-F1 and 0.8824 Micro-Precision, showing that task-specific fine-tuning learns structured patterns without complex prompting and with reduced hallucination. Increasing the streaming delay from 1 to 16 frames steadily raises Micro-F1 from 0.7030 to 0.9239 and Micro-Precision from 0.7135 to 0.9303 as more context resolves ambiguities, at the cost of first-packet delay rising from 75 ms to 756 ms, whereas the non-streaming variant is highest at 0.9639 / 0.9620 but cannot emit anything until the full sequence arrives. The authors therefore adopt the 4-frame configuration as the practical balance of quality and responsiveness, noting that additional context pushes StreamTN past BiLSTM in Micro-F1.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper itself concludes that most remaining errors concentrate in structurally complex mathematical and chemical expressions and proposes focusing future work on these difficult categories while extending StreamTN to additional languages and application domains, alongside releasing the model and benchmark to support further streaming TN research. A natural extension suggested by the reported trade-offs is adaptive or confidence-gated delay that waits longer only on ambiguous NSWs rather than using a fixed frame count, as well as targeted data augmentation and pronunciation-aware modeling for formulas and equations where character-level difficulty is highest. Further valuable directions include joint evaluation with streaming TTS quality and prosody rather than TN accuracy alone, robustness testing on live LLM output distributions beyond curated DuReader and synthetic scientific samples, and exploration of efficient full-model adaptation alternatives given how poorly LoRA performed on this precise transformation task.
