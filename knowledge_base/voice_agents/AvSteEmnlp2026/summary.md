# AV-STE: Audio-Visual Speech Token Enhancement

**Article:** [bellagodiva/av-ste](https://github.com/bellagodiva/av-ste) — GitHub /

## Human Readable TL;DR
Imagine trying to follow a friend's voice in a noisy restaurant, but you can also read their lips to fill in the words the noise drowns out. That is what AV-STE does for speech AI: it watches a small video crop of the speaker's mouth alongside the noisy audio and reconstructs the clean speech tokens that a codec language model like Moshi needs to understand and respond. Instead of trying to rebuild perfect-sounding audio, it focuses on recovering the meaning-carrying tokens, like restoring the words on a smudged page rather than reprinting the whole book. In the demo's showcased example at −5 dB of competing speech, token accuracy jumps from under 28% to about 79%, and Moshi goes from giving a confused generic reply to a coherent on-topic response.

## TL;DR
AV-STE recovers clean Mimi semantic (cb0) tokens at 12.5 Hz from noisy 16 kHz speech by fusing audio with 96×96 grayscale lip-ROI video at 25 fps through an entropy-gated cross-attention module built on AV-HuBERT, so the enhanced tokens can serve as drop-in replacements for noisy tokens in any Mimi-based TTS or codec language model such as Moshi. The release provides two checkpoints that together reproduce every semantic-token-accuracy row in the paper's main table: `avste.pt` for clean speech, non-speech background noise, and cross-dataset speaker interference, and `avste_lrs3_interference.pt` for same-corpus-style competing speakers and out-of-domain video. Both are `av_hubert_crossattn_ent` models with 4-frame lookahead and `mimi_mix_loss`, fine-tuned from the public AV-HuBERT-Large backbone `large_vox_iter5.pt` on a 20/40/40 clean, non-speech, and speaker-interference mix with SNR uniform in [−10, 10] dB. A four-clip LRS3 demo script prints semantic-token accuracy plus Mimi-decoded WER and Moshi's generated response per condition, and single-clip inference via `scripts/infer_avste.py` supports enhanced tokens, reconstructed speech, or Moshi-streaming prediction outputs.

## Problem & Motivation
Spoken dialogue systems built on neural audio codecs, such as Moshi on top of Mimi, reason over discrete semantic tokens, and background noise or competing speakers corrupt exactly those tokens, collapsing both transcription quality and the downstream model's response. Audio-only enhancement struggles most in precisely the overlapping-speech conditions that matter for conversation, while the speaker's lip movements carry complementary information that survives acoustic corruption. AV-STE is motivated by closing that gap at the token level: rather than chasing waveform fidelity, it restores the clean semantic token sequence so any Mimi-based codec language model can consume it directly, with token accuracy as the primary metric and decoded-audio WER kept as a secondary diagnostic.

## Main Original Ideas
1. **Token-level audio-visual enhancement** — framing the task as recovering the clean Mimi semantic (cb0) token sequence at 12.5 Hz from noisy 16 kHz audio plus 96×96 grayscale mouth-ROI video at 25 fps, so the output plugs straight into Mimi-based TTS and codec language models.
2. **Entropy-gated cross-attention fusion** — building the `av_hubert_crossattn_ent` model on AV-HuBERT with a 4-frame lookahead cross-attention path whose contribution is gated by entropy, letting the model lean on video when audio is unreliable, trained with a `mimi_mix_loss` objective.
3. **Two-stage interference curriculum with dual checkpoints** — fine-tuning first from `large_vox_iter5.pt` on AudioSet-sourced non-speech and cross-dataset speaker interference (`avste.pt`), then continuing to a second stage on 1–4 same-corpus LRS3 interfering speakers (`avste_lrs3_interference.pt`), with the pair covering clean speech, ambient noise, competing speakers, and out-of-domain video evaluation.
4. **Token-accuracy-first evaluation with Moshi-in-the-loop demo** — scoring primarily by the fraction of predicted cb0 tokens matching the clean reference and secondarily by Whisper-large-v3 WER on Mimi-decoded audio, demonstrated end to end through `run_demo.sh` and `compare_demo.py` tables that show both transcription recovery and Moshi's generated spoken response per noise condition.

## Key Findings
The wiki's showcased speech-noise −5 dB example moves from 27.6% token accuracy and 61.5% WER under the noisy Mimi baseline to 79.3% accuracy and 38.5% WER with AV-STE, against a clean upper bound of 100.0% and 0.0%, and Moshi's reply shifts from a generic deflection to an on-topic response about staying in the city. The four bundled LRS3 clips were deliberately chosen for clear before-and-after contrast rather than as representative averages, with the ambient-noise clips pushing the Mimi baseline as low as 0%, while the paper's Table 1 averages span the full 1321-clip noisy test split across five SNRs and noise types. Training uses a 20/40/40 clean, non-speech, and speaker-interference mix at SNRs uniform in [−10, 10] dB with Adam (0.9/0.98), a tri-stage learning-rate schedule, and gradient clipping at 5.0, differing between stages mainly in peak learning rate, warmup and decay steps, epoch budget, and interference source. On the practical side, inference requires a visible front-facing face to extract the 96×96 grayscale mouth crop at 25 fps plus 16 kHz mono audio, the `--output` extension selects tokens, waveform, or Moshi-streaming JSON, and the data and code release is complete except that LRS3, AudioSet, and Seamless Interaction themselves are license-gated third-party sources reconstructed via clip IDs and sampling scripts rather than redistributed.

## Suggestions & Future Directions
Within what the wiki snapshot supports, the natural next steps are extending the fusion beyond the current front-facing lip-ROI requirement toward more robust handling of occluded, profile, or missing video, and broadening noise and speaker-interference coverage beyond the AudioSet, LRS3, and Seamless Interaction regimes tested. Further work could tighten streaming readiness by reducing or eliminating the 4-frame lookahead, probe integration with codec language models beyond Moshi, and publish the internal data-preparation pipeline for manifests, noise pools, Mimi-logit pre-extraction, and interferer selection so the training mix is independently reproducible. A fuller release of per-condition averages with confidence intervals over the complete test grid would also complement the contrast-chosen demo clips.
