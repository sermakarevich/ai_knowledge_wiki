# AURA: Uncertainty-Routed Activation Editing for Acoustic Grounding in Speech Foundation Models

**Paper:** [AURA: Uncertainty-Routed Activation Editing for Acoustic Grounding in Speech Foundation Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23979v1)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine a brilliant court stenographer who almost always transcribes perfectly, but when the courtroom goes silent or the audio gets muffled, instead of writing nothing she invents plausible-sounding testimony. AURA is like giving her a watchful assistant who monitors how confidently her pen follows the audio — whether her attention is too fixated, too scattered, or jumping erratically — and gently nudges her hand only in those shaky moments, leaving her alone when she is tracking well. The nudge is tiny and targeted, touching just a few well-chosen spots rather than retraining her whole brain, so she stays just as accurate on clear speech while almost never inventing words during silence. When the underlying recording is fundamentally hard, like a young child speaking or heavily disfluent speech, the assistant can flag that a better hearing aid (stronger acoustic encoder) is needed on top of the nudges.

## TL;DR

AURA (Uncertainty-Routed Activation Editing) is an ultra-efficient activation-editing method that freezes a pretrained attention encoder-decoder (AED) speech foundation model and learns sparse scale-and-shift edits to decoder cross-attention heads, routed at each decoding step by cross-attention uncertainty. Each head learns a scale vector and bias vector gated by the product of a static sparse Hard-Concrete gate (where an edit may occur) and a dynamic per-step gate computed from three causal cross-attention features — Max-Prob, normalized Entropy, and Shift — so edits stay mostly dormant during confident recognition and strengthen when grounding becomes unstable. Training uses standard ASR cross-entropy plus a ramped expected-L0 sparsity loss, adding only 2d + 6 scalars per head (roughly 500× fewer trainable parameters than LoRA), with no prior hallucination-head identification stage. Across non-speech audio, imperfect-label child and adult speech, and disfluent speech, AURA sharply cuts hallucination (UrbanSound8K HRnorm from 89.18% to 0.93%) while preserving clean-speech WER, beats its static-gated JoLA ablation in 13 of 15 comparisons, and approaches or matches LoRA on MyST and TED-LIUM 3, with an AURA+Enc diagnostic showing when frozen-encoder capacity is the remaining bottleneck.

## Problem & Motivation

Attention encoder-decoder speech foundation models achieve strong automatic speech recognition performance, yet they can generate acoustically unsupported text when inputs contain no speech or only weak acoustic evidence, and more generally when the decoder drifts away from the audio. Prior interpretability and adaptation studies show that attention heads specialize into distinct functional roles and that task adaptation and hallucination concentrate in a small subset of heads, which motivates head-level activation edits over broad weight rewrites. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as LoRA still introduce millions of trainable parameters for large models, while representation-editing methods such as JoLA apply the same static intervention at every decoding step. That static behavior is limiting because hallucinations are often localized: the decoder may remain well-grounded for most of an utterance and only drift when its cross-attention pattern becomes unstable. AURA addresses this gap by intervening directly in decoder cross-attention heads, where acoustic conditioning occurs, and by routing each edit with live cross-attention uncertainty so corrections activate exactly when grounding falters.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Head-level scale-and-shift editing in decoder cross-attention** — AURA freezes all pretrained weights and introduces, per decoder cross-attention head, a scale vector Ah and bias vector vh applied as ãt,h = 1 + ĝt,h Ah ⊙ at,h + ĝt,h vh, so zero effective gates recover the frozen output exactly. By placing edits where frame-to-token acoustic conditioning happens, AURA respects the grounding path that text-oriented residual-stream interventions miss.

2. **Sparse static head selection with Hard-Concrete gates** — Each edit type uses a static Hard-Concrete gate with location parameter log α, stretch interval (γ, ζ) = (−0.1, 1.1), temperature τ = 0.33, and a linearly ramped expected-L0 sparsity penalty, initialized near zero so the model learns a small set of heads worth editing without any prior head-identification stage.

3. **Uncertainty-routed dynamic gating from cross-attention** — At every decoding step a lightweight per-head gate is computed from three causal features of the cross-attention distribution over encoder frames: Max-Prob for over-concentration and attention-sink behavior, normalized Entropy for diffuse weakly grounded attention, and Shift for abrupt frame jumps via a buffered previous argmax reset per utterance. A zero-initialized 4-scalar projection (three weights plus bias, no MLP) maps these features through a sigmoid to a per-step gate.

4. **Factorized effective gate separating where from when** — The effective gate is the product of the static and dynamic gates, so the static component decides where an edit may occur and the dynamic component decides when it should activate. This design lets AURA stay nearly dormant during confident, near-diagonal cross-attention and intervene selectively during unstable grounding.

5. **Ultra-efficient training objective and no-identification pipeline** — The total loss is standard ASR cross-entropy plus the ramped sparsity term, with the dynamic gate left unregularized, at a cost of 2d + 6 scalars per head (thousands of parameters total: 3.2k at 39M scale up to 85.8k at 1.55B scale). Unlike head-targeted hallucination methods that first identify prone heads and then fine-tune them, AURA learns sparse selection and token-level routing jointly from the adaptation objective.

## Key Findings

On non-speech hallucination with Whisper-Large-v3, adapted only on empty-transcript AudioSet, DEMAND, and MUSAN clips and evaluated on UrbanSound8K with greedy decoding, zero-shot hallucinates on nearly all inputs (89.18% HRnorm, 98.00% HRraw) while AURA lowers HRnorm to 4.39% after 5 epochs, 1.94% after 15 epochs, and 0.93% after 25 epochs, substantially without requiring head identification. Clean-speech recognition is largely preserved at short schedules (LibriSpeech test-clean/test-other WER 1.91/3.57 zero-shot versus 2.05/3.59 at 5 epochs and 2.29/3.65 at 15 epochs, rising to 4.40/4.45 at 25 epochs), and the matched JoLA ablation with the same backbone but no dynamic routing reaches similar hallucination rates only at worse WER cost (2.01% HRnorm with 4.11/4.36 WER at 15 epochs, degrading to 15.21/18.65 WER at 25 epochs), isolating the value of token-level routing for the hallucination–WER trade-off.

On speech grounding stressors with deliberately harder unfiltered protocols, AURA is the strongest representation-editing method in most comparisons and closely tracks far larger baselines. On MyST child speech it is the best ultra-efficient method across model sizes with statistically significant gains at Base, Medium (13.7%), and Large-v3 (14.2%), approaching LoRA from Whisper-small onward and even beating full fine-tuning at Medium and Large-v3, consistent with lightweight adaptation acting as implicit regularization. On TED-LIUM 3 it attains the best or tied-best ultra-efficient result at every model size (Large-v3 7.8% versus LoRA 7.9% and full fine-tuning 8.2%), though gaps among ultra-efficient methods are modest and not statistically significant, and much of the apparent gain comes from suppressing output on the 21% of test segments with empty references. On FluencyBank disfluent speech AURA again leads the representation-editing family with significant gains at Small (19.1%) and Medium (16.4%), but LoRA and full fine-tuning outperform decoder-only AURA at every size, marking a capacity boundary. The AURA+Enc diagnostic, which additionally fine-tunes all encoder parameters, consistently improves over both AURA and LoRA on MyST, is numerically strongest everywhere on FluencyBank, and adds little on TED-LIUM 3 where speech-bearing segments are already well recognized, sharpening the stated operating point that ultra-efficient decoder editing suffices when frozen acoustics are adequate while child and disfluent speech can benefit from encoder capacity.

Mechanistically, AURA beats JoLA in 13 of 15 dataset–model comparisons and ties in two, with the largest gains where the backbone has sufficient capacity for decoder-side correction. Decode-time sensitivity analysis, in which models trained with all routing features have one feature removed at inference, degrades WER in every case with entropy dominant (up to +1.7 WER at Whisper-Large-v3), indicating the learned router uses all uncertainty signals with entropy contributing most strongly and that larger models expose more informative uncertainty patterns. Qualitative Figure 2 examples on three MyST utterances show AURA turning collapsed or off-diagonal cross-attention under full fine-tuning into cleaner monotonic alignments coinciding with more grounded decoding, presented explicitly as illustrative behavior types rather than a corpus-wide alignment metric.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper's stated future work is to extend uncertainty-routed editing to speech large language models, code-switched ASR, and long-form decoding, where grounding instabilities are likely to be more severe and prolonged. A second direction follows directly from the capacity diagnostic: build principled rules for when cheap decoder-only editing is enough and when to pay for encoder adaptation, since child and disfluent speech still benefit from substantially higher-capacity updates. A third direction is deeper analysis of the routing signals themselves, including why entropy dominates at larger scales and whether richer or learned uncertainty features could further improve the hallucination–WER trade-off. More broadly, the instability of text-oriented representation-editing baselines on these tasks suggests future AED interventions should continue to respect the frame-token acoustic grounding path rather than importing position-based edits designed for text generation.
