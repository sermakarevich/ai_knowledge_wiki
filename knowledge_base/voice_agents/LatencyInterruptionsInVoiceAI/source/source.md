The difference between a bot and a real conversation is milliseconds. In production systems, speed isn't just performance. It directly affects how natural the conversation feels. What we've observed is even small delays, half a second to 1 second, start to break the flow of a conversation. Users hesitate, repeat themselves, or disengage entirely. In real world deployments, conversations are not turn-based. People interrupt, change direction mid-sentence, or correct themselves while speaking. A voice system needs to detect that in real time and adjust without breaking the interaction. This requires streaming architectures, low-latency inference, and precise handling of turn-taking. It's not just about generating a response, it's about delivering it at the right moment. Timing, pauses, and responsiveness, these are not small details. They define whether the system feels natural or not. In voice AI, conversation quality isn't just intelligence, it's timing, control, and flow.
