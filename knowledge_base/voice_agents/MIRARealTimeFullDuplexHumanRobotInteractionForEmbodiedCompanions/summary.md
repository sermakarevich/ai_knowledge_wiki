# MIRA: Real-Time Full-Duplex Human-Robot Interaction for Embodied Companions

**Paper:** [MIRA: Real-Time Full-Duplex Human-Robot Interaction for Embodied Companions (Lin et al., 2026)](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24547v1)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine a dinner companion who not only talks but nods, gestures with their hands in rhythm with their words, notices the moment you start to interrupt, and gracefully stops mid-gesture to listen — that is what MIRA gives the Astribot S1 humanoid. It works like a theater director paired with an improv dancer: one part of the system decides what to do next (keep talking, listen, apologize, greet) and hands the dancer a short cue card, while the dancer invents matching arm movements on the fly from the unfolding voice. Crucially, the dancer only commits to half a second of movement at a time while imagining further ahead, like walking with short steps while looking down the path, so stopping for a barge-in never means tripping over a long pre-planned routine. A fast reflex layer halts speech and motion within about half a second, while a slower thoughtful layer decides whether you were really talking to the robot at all and picks up the story where it left off.

## TL;DR

MIRA is a unified real-time full-duplex framework for embodied companionship that coordinates streaming dialogue, explicit embodiment-cue routing, and interruptible co-speech motion on an Astribot S1 humanoid. CORTEX, its dual-timescale interaction policy, combines a fast VAD-based interruption gate (450 ms confirmation threshold), a deliberative turn arbiter deciding IGNORE, REPLY, or INTERRUPT_AND_REPLY from transcript, vocal affect, dialogue history, and playback state, and a reactive dialogue generator that streams response text plus a compact symbolic embodiment cue (`<motion: m>`) to incremental TTS. Non-speaking cues (greeting, listen, confused, apologize, idle) route to pre-validated motion libraries with predictable timing and bounded envelopes, while `speak` opens a streaming session to ROSCO, an 8-block prefix-conditioned diffusion transformer that generates 32-DoF joint trajectories from causal audio and recent motion history with rich-motion reconstruction plus kinematic, rollout, and contrastive audio objectives. Receding-Horizon Prefix Commitment (RHPC) predicts a 50-frame window at 30 fps but commits only the leading 15 frames (0.50 s), reusing the latest 10 committed frames as prefix, which preserves training-length context, enables downstream collision checks on the look-ahead, and bounds discarded motion on barge-in. A robot-side execution bridge enforces safety through MuJoCo-based collision-aware projection, joint-limit checks, a 250 Hz control loop, and session-scoped invalidation, achieving 195 ms chunk inference (RTF 0.390), 466 ms median preemption, the highest beat-consistency and lowest FID-G among generative baselines, and robust multi-party arbitration over 199 sessions and 559 turn decisions.

## Problem & Motivation

A companion robot must treat interaction as a continuous embodied process rather than a sequence of isolated question-answer turns, constantly deciding which behavior is appropriate, when to speak, how to accompany speech with motion, and how to handle pauses, clarifications, and interruptions. The hard online constraint is that complete response audio may not yet exist when the robot must begin moving, so co-speech motion has to be generated from incremental audio while staying continuous with prior motion, and motion already released for execution cannot be revised as freely as uncommitted text or audio because it must satisfy kinematic and execution constraints. Existing systems decouple dialogue orchestration from gesture synthesis and rely on offline motion generation from complete audio, leaving linguistic content, prosodic timing, and physical safety unsynchronized, while one-way proactive frameworks such as ProAct leave open the physical commitment problem during sudden barge-ins. MIRA extends the always-on streaming full-duplex paradigm of the Audio Interaction Model from purely auditory response timing to physical embodiment, insisting that every verbal response carry an explicit embodiment decision.

The companion setting adds a uniquely visible physical-commitment problem: delayed gestures, stale motion packets, and incorrectly cancelled responses remain physically and socially disruptive even after the dialogue state has transitioned, so arbitration and execution must be bridged by an inspectable interface rather than an unconstrained end-to-end trajectory generator. MIRA therefore treats physical behavior as part of the interaction policy rather than a downstream rendering step, coupling what the robot should do next at the interaction level with how that behavior is expressed at the motion level. It follows a conservative affect paradigm in which noisy vocal affect is never mapped directly to low-level motor commands but fused with transcript semantics, dialogue history, and interaction state to select a high-level embodiment cue, preserving safety and inspectability while keeping expression legible and context-appropriate.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **MIRA unified full-duplex embodied-companion framework** — The paper proposes MIRA as a single streaming architecture linking user-intent understanding, response generation, embodiment-cue prediction, and robot motion through unified response-lifecycle management. Physical behavior is a first-class policy output: each admitted response gets a shared response identity binding TTS segments and motion messages, with cancellation paths that preempt active speech and joint trajectories at the execution boundary while retaining conversational context for subsequent arbitration.

2. **CORTEX dual-timescale interaction policy with fast gate and deliberative arbitration** — CORTEX combines streaming response generation with deliberative turn admission and a local interruption gate to arbitrate continuously between speaking, listening, and changing turns. A VAD-based Fast Interruption Gate aborts ongoing output at a 450 ms sustained-speech threshold during playback (marking the response aborted, clearing pending TTS, sending TTS-stop, ending motion, streaming audio incrementally to ASR), while the Deliberative Turn Arbiter first applies deterministic rules for empty, hesitation-only, or stop inputs and then queries a non-streaming language model over transcript, affect, history, playback status, and the preliminary rule decision to return IGNORE, REPLY, or INTERRUPT_AND_REPLY. The Reactive Dialogue Generator conditions on transcript, affect, history, and persona instructions, supports registered function calls recorded in dialogue context, emits the embodiment cue parsed separately from speech text, and streams to incremental TTS so speech and motion start early; shared connection state with a fresh identifier per admitted turn keeps asynchronous decisions coherent, and an ignored turn after an early abort can resume via continuation from saved assistant context.

3. **Explicit embodiment-cue protocol and hybrid routing** — The interface between cognition and body is a compact symbolic label serialized as `<motion: m>; response text` with finite vocabulary (speak, greeting/wave, listen, confused, apologize, idle), selected jointly from utterance, affect, intent, and history rather than one-to-one affect mapping, stripped by a parser before TTS, and defaulted deterministically to `speak` for non-empty and `idle` for empty responses on malformed output. Routing separates intent from realization: non-speaking cues dispatch to a retrieval service of pre-validated motions with predictable timing and bounded envelopes, while `speak` opens a realtime co-speech session streaming TTS audio to ROSCO, making the routing decision inspectable and the discrete behaviors deterministically safe.

4. **ROSCO prefix-conditioned diffusion transformer for streaming co-speech motion** — ROSCO-DiT generates each target rich-motion chunk conditioned on a preceding motion prefix and temporally aligned causal audio, concatenating noisy target and clean prefix tokens with a binary type indicator, projecting to 256 dimensions with sinusoidal positions, and stacking eight blocks of causally masked motion self-attention, causal audio cross-attention (each motion token seeing only same-or-earlier audio), and feed-forward layers with diffusion-timestep AdaLN modulation (shift, scale, gates g1–g3). Motion is represented per frame as a 460-dimensional vector of joint qpos, velocity, local Cartesian positions of 44 segments, and 6D rotations retargeted from BEAT to the Astribot S1 at 30 fps via GMR, though only denormalized qpos is sent to the robot; training combines rich-motion reconstruction, forward-kinematics Cartesian consistency plus collision-clearance penalties, a two-step autoregressive rollout to mitigate exposure bias, and a margin-based contrastive audio loss separating matched from cyclically shuffled audio conditions.

5. **Receding-Horizon Prefix Commitment (RHPC) streaming inference** — RHPC separates prediction horizon from physical emission stride by consuming 50 audio tokens, predicting an extended 50-frame rich-motion window, but committing only the first 15 frames and advancing 15 frames per step with the latest 10 committed frames reused as prefix and a 10-frame overlap smoothing chunk boundaries. The long horizon keeps sequence length near training to reduce train-test mismatch and provides an extended candidate for downstream kinematic projection and collision checks, while the short 0.50 s commitment means interruption terminates the session after the current segment without discarding a long trajectory. At turn start the bridge initializes prefix and audio context with zeros and feeds generated chunks back autoregressively, resampling TTS audio from 24 kHz to 16 kHz mono and transmitting only committed qpos slices.

6. **Robot-side execution bridge with collision-aware safety and failure containment** — Every qpos frame passes through a MuJoCo-evaluated projection solving constrained inverse kinematics under joint-limit and collision constraints, holding the last safe pose or invoking fallback on failure, with 30 Hz source frames consumed by a 250 Hz Orin-side joint-position loop with per-tick step limits and a brief `move_to` transition on session handoff to avoid discontinuity. On CORTEX interruption the active session is terminated with delayed commands rejected while dialogue state stays intact, and failures are contained locally with bounded scope (language errors before TTS, sentence-level interruption without invalidating the session, no new motion without sufficient audio, collision rejection independent of speech), leaving speech APIs, dialogue models, and behavior services replaceable behind the session-scoped interface.

## Key Findings

On motion quality, ROSCO achieves the highest beat-consistency score among generative baselines with competitive BeatAlign, indicating rhythmic synchronization between audio onsets and motion beats beyond frame-wise correspondence, and obtains the lowest FID-G among reported generative models, meaning its geometry-feature distribution best matches real motion. Its kinematic self-collision frame rate — measured by replaying every predicted qpos frame in the full Astribot MuJoCo model via `mj_forward` without dynamics and counting non-positive signed-distance self-contacts — is substantially lower than the 6.51% of retargeted ground truth, because GMR retargeting preserves kinematic correspondence without collision avoidance while ROSCO's collision-aware objective explicitly penalizes implausible configurations. The authors caution that this simulator proxy does not by itself guarantee hardware success, but it supports the claim of balancing audio-motion alignment with target-robot physical constraints rather than reproducing colliding retargeted trajectories.

On streaming and responsiveness, the system satisfies all three stated temporal requirements on the physical robot: motion synthesis runs faster than playback with 195 ms chunk inference against 500 ms of committed motion (emission RTF 0.390, leaving comfortable runtime margin), turn startup reaches first speech at 2.20 s and first motion UDP at 2.36 s (P50 medians) dominated by LLM first-token latency (1.62 s) and audio ingress (493 ms) rather than motion synthesis, with ASR endpointing contributing only 10 ms. Full-duplex preemption combines RHPC's 15-frame commitment bound with the VAD gate to reach 466 ms median from speech onset to abort-command dispatch, so ongoing speech and execution are promptly interruptible. Trace-based evaluation over 199 sessions, 36,448 events, and 559 turn decisions (302 REPLY, 88 INTERRUPT_AND_REPLY, 169 IGNORE) shows the intended division of labor: fillers like "Um" below the 450 ms threshold return IGNORE in about 10 ms undisturbed, wake-word "MIRA" confirms INTERRUPT_AND_REPLY 2 ms after the gate abort, bystander speech like "Just sent the location to them" triggers an early abort but is then judged non-addressed and resumed via continuation, and ambiguous "No, that will not work" is resolved to INTERRUPT_AND_REPLY from dialogue context while the physical stop had already fired without waiting for the deliberative decision.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The authors frame streaming quality as an end-to-end system property in which prefix feedback, commitment schedules, boundary preemption, and execution safeguards matter as much as diffusion capacity, and they position hybrid routing — deterministic libraries for repeatable social actions plus streaming diffusion for prosody-driven speaking — as the pattern that makes continuous deployment fluid. Three explicit limitations bound the current system and point directly to future work. First, the embodiment vocabulary is constrained, so incorporating joint semantic text conditioning alongside audio prosody could enrich gesture expressivity beyond rhythm-following motion. Second, the safety layer needs embodiment-specific kinematic tuning and collision-geometry modeling for each new robot platform, implying a porting cost for every morphology. Third, turn startup remains bounded by the cascaded ASR-to-LLM-to-TTS pipeline, so integrating native end-to-end speech-to-speech (omni) foundation models is proposed as the route to substantially compress initial response delay. Finally, while benchmarks establish technical viability, throughput, and containment, the paper calls for long-term human-subject studies in naturalistic environments to assess subjective companion dynamics and sustained engagement, which no latency or FID number can substitute.
