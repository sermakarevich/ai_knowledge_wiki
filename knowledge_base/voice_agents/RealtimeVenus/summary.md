# Realtime-Venus: A full-duplex interaction system with asynchronous delegation

**Paper:** [Realtime-Venus: A full-duplex interaction system with asynchronous delegation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13814) — arXiv, 18 Sep 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

Realtime-Venus is like a restaurant host who keeps chatting with you at the table while the kitchen cooks your order in the back, then weaves the finished dish into the conversation without missing a beat. It pairs two talkative 9B models — one that both watches and listens, one that only listens — with a behind-the-scenes helper that runs web searches and other slow tasks asynchronously while the chat stays live. Under the hood it decides every second whether to keep listening, keep speaking, or quietly hand work off, and it knows the difference between a nodding "uh-huh" and a real interruption. The result is a voice assistant that can proactively speak up when something happens on screen, survive overlapping chatter, and deliver background results once they are ready.

## TL;DR

Realtime-Venus is a proactive full-duplex interaction system built on two separately trained 9B conversational frontends — Realtime-Venus-Omni for audio–visual interaction and Realtime-Venus-Audio for spoken interaction — both adapted from MiniCPM-o 4.5 and coordinated by a dual-loop runtime with Realtime-Venus-Harness for asynchronous capability execution. Each frontend unifies continuous perception, conversational control, native speech generation, and private delegation on a shared causal timeline serialized into one-second chunks, supervised by a common post-training recipe mixing offline understanding, proactive full-duplex trajectories, and delegation workflows over 2.8M samples. Reported results show Omni leading compared online models on six of eight video benchmarks, Audio leading several audio understanding and spoken QA comparisons, and strong continuation behavior under non-interruptive overlaps alongside competitive but imperfect tool-use and delegation routing.

## Problem & Motivation

Natural interaction requires interpreting an ongoing stream of observations while deciding when and how to respond, including speaking up without being asked, yet live conversation and external computation run on very different timescales within the same session. Prior systems covered pieces of this puzzle — concurrent speech modeling in Moshi, multimodal streaming speech in Qwen2.5/3-Omni and MiniCPM-o 4.5, and retrieval or tool calls during dialogue in various spoken-agent efforts — but coordinating task capture with conversational result delivery remained fragile, since a background task needs a stable record of the request while its result must be interpreted in a conversation that may have moved on. Realtime-Venus frames this as a joint problem of full-duplex control and asynchronous delegation: the frontend must maintain a response through backchannels, revise its unspoken continuation after a correction, or start background work when external capabilities are needed, while the harness must fix evidence at the request boundary, execute over longer durations, and return results that the frontend can accept, defer, or reinterpret against changed user intent.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Dual-loop runtime with private delegation interface.** The latency-sensitive interaction loop continuously processes incoming media and controls when to listen, speak, or yield, while the capability loop is triggered by a hidden natural-language delegation request, captures the causal context available at the request boundary, executes a registered capability, and returns a polished reply through a private background channel. Perception stays active during speech and background execution, each delegation binds to its originating session plus an evidence snapshot with freshness checks and playback-aware delivery, and the frontend — not the harness — decides when and how the prepared text is spoken, which preserves stable context for background work while letting responses adapt to subsequent intent shifts.

2. **Unified causal runtime abstraction and stream serialization.** Both frontends share the same per-second transition `(s_{k+1}, O_k) = F_m(s_k, x^m_k, b_k; η_k)`, where media inputs are audio-only or audio-plus-visual, retained state carries model context and previously admitted replies, and only foreground text and speech tokens are user-facing while delegation text stays hidden. User, assistant, and background streams are interleaved into `<unit> input → output </unit>` lines governed by `<|listen|>`, `<|speak|>`, and `<|turn_eos|>` plus `<delegate>`/`<backend>` spans, with playback-synchronized text emission that emits fewer tokens when speech lags and more when capacity frees up, and a factorization that keeps acoustic generation outside the LLM while control, text, and delegation share one semantic representation so acoustically similar overlaps can yield distinct continuations.

3. **Role-conditioned overlap handling and coupled duplex–delegation supervision.** Instead of treating every overlap as a uniform stop signal, the policy distinguishes pauses and noise (stay in `<|listen|>`), short backchannels such as "yes" (hold `<|speak|>`), and floor-taking interruptions (emit `<|turn_eos|>` then stop, repair, or redirect the unspoken remainder without altering already-played audio). Each training trajectory links an observed event to both a duplex target (continue, await input, yield, stop queued output, or revise) and a delegation target (handle locally, invoke a capability for evidence or replanning, or route to tools for external information and actions), with explicit retain, cancel, revise, reconcile, retry, and fallback management so a stable-intent interruption can stop playback yet keep the plan while an intent-changing correction cancels dependent work and replans.

4. **Training-free long-video memory for hour-scale context.** Because the base model holds only a bounded rolling window, Omni attaches a decoupled external memory that archives sampled frames via motion-compensated gating with local-change protection and a consecutive-drop cap, retrieves candidates through MaxSim fine-grained matching weighted by median-absolute-deviation token importance combined with MMR-style novelty scoring, and reassembles retrieved frames chronologically with adjacent audio plus the recent short-term window. The module needs no extra training, never disturbs short-term processing, and lets the model recover relevant history far beyond its rolling context without retaining the entire session.

5. **Three-stage coupled data pipeline and unified post-training recipe.** Scenario and interaction planning expands structured seeds into event-level plans sampled across daily, informational, collaborative, and multi-party domains with backchannels, interruptions, corrections, and background speech; paralinguistic and acoustic realization renders turns into Chinese, English, and code-switched speech with hesitations, laughter, breathing, and scene-matched secondary speakers; and temporal alignment maps everything onto a shared session clock materialized as one trajectory with shared supervision for both audio-only and audio–visual training (≈72,200 sessions, ≈1,600 hours). The resulting 2.8M-sample corpus (≈70% video, ≈56% offline understanding, ≈37% proactive duplex, ≈6% delegation) trains both variants in a single sparse-supervision pass — loss only on response spans including per-second listen/speak decisions, per-sample normalized, Thinker-only with the acoustic decoder frozen — differing only in that Omni consumes video plus audio while Audio consumes the audio subset.

## Key Findings

Realtime-Venus-Omni achieves the highest scores among compared online models on six of eight streaming and offline video benchmarks, including 70.2% on StreamingBench, 64.7% on OVO-Bench, and 81.3% on Daily-Omni, improving over its MiniCPM-o 4.5 base by 2.3, 4.0, and 3.4 points on StreamingBench, OVO-Bench, and OmniPro probe accuracy while trailing the base on ProactiveVideoQA and WorldSense, which the authors read as evidence that streaming comprehension, proactive quality, and offline understanding must be judged separately. Memory augmentation helps Omni across every reported duration bin, notably +5.88 points on the 60–90-minute LVOmniBench slice and +4.76 points on the 40–60-minute LongVideoBench slice with gains across CGBench, and it also lifts MiniCPM-o 4.5 in several long settings though with a dip in one shorter bin, so the benefit is consistent for Venus but backbone- and setting-dependent in general. On audio, Realtime-Venus-Audio leads compared models on MMAU (78.0%) and MMAU-Pro (63.2%), ties the best VoiceBench AlpacaEval score (4.81), and tops Llama Questions (83.8%) and Speech CMMLU (67.8%), while ranking second on MMAR and TriviaQA and lower on MMSU, suggesting the interaction-oriented frontend retains general audio competence with remaining headroom in contextual acoustic reasoning. Full-duplex overlap tests show the clearest trade-off: Audio responds to 75% of user interruptions with continuation rates of 97%, 88%, and 86% under backchannels, other-directed speech, and background speech — best on the latter two continuations — yet trails Joy-Duplex, GPT-4o, and Gemini 3.1 Live on interruption response, confirming that strong continuation does not imply strong interruption handling. Tool use and delegation sharpen the limits: on Full-Duplex-Bench v3 Omni reaches 86.0% tool-selection F1 but only 53.1% argument accuracy and 43.0% Pass@1 (Audio 82.0%, 52.2%, 42.0%), both well below GPT-Realtime's 87.6/68.0/60.0, and on the internal delegate benchmark Omni routes better overall (75.93% vs 68.89%) because Audio over-delegates routine requests (39.44% non-delegation specificity despite 92.22% delegation recall) while Omni under-delegates external requests (68.33% recall despite 84.44% specificity), with both tied at 75.00% on reasoning.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The authors propose moving to finer-grained streaming chunks to catch brief events and sharpen response timing, extending the context window to retain relevant information and track evolving intent over longer interactions, and tackling more complex multi-step reasoning with coordinated tool use and concurrent asynchronous operations including recovery from delayed or failed calls. The results further motivate closer study of ambiguous overlaps and transition timing rather than aggregating the four overlap scenarios, plus trajectory-level diagnosis of argument grounding, retention of spoken constraints across turns, and coordination across chained calls since selection F1 alone overstates complete-episode success. The opposite delegation failure modes — Audio's trigger-happy routing versus Omni's conservative routing — suggest future work on calibrated delegation thresholds, modality-aware routing, and end-to-end evaluation of delegated execution and result integration rather than routing accuracy alone, alongside ablations that isolate which training components drive the video, audio, and duplex gains.

## Authors & Institutions

The work is presented as Venus Team, Ant Group together with Tsinghua University, with project page and code released under the Realtime-Venus name. Core contributors listed with equal contribution are Ruixiang Zhao, Hualei Wang, Renhe Sun, Enzhi Zhou, and Zihang Liu, joined by Jincenzi Wu, Xujie Song, and Kexin Shi, alongside fourteen additional contributors and project leaders Jian Liu, Yuge Huang, Junliang Xing, and Yuntao Wang as corresponding authors with advisors Weiqiang Wang, Chun Yu, and Yuanchun Shi. The system builds directly on the open-source MiniCPM-o 4.5 lineage with SigLIP2 and Whisper-Medium encoders, a Qwen3-8B backbone, discrete S3 speech tokens, and a streaming flow-matching decoder, and it is evaluated against offline families such as Qwen3-Omni, InternVL3.5, and Gemini-3.5-Flash and online and speech peers including LiveStar, MMDuet2, JoyAI systems, Freeze-Omni, Moshi, GPT-4o, Gemini Live variants, and GPT-Realtime.
