# AVTR-1: Open Stack for Real-Time Interactive Avatars

**Paper:** [AVTR-1: Open Stack for Real-Time Interactive Avatars](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22913v1) — Kravtsov, Ziganshin, Poletaev et al. (Avaturn Live), arXiv:2609.22913v1 [cs.CV], 19 Sep 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine video-calling someone whose face is generated by AI: you talk, they listen — nodding, reacting — and then answer with perfectly lip-synced speech, interrupting and being interrupted naturally. Fast AI face-generation alone cannot do this, because a real call also needs a director: someone who takes the other party's choppy incoming speech, lines it up with the video frame-by-frame, decides exactly when each frame plays, and cuts off the avatar's speech the instant you barge in. AVTR-1 is that whole production crew, released openly: a compact motion-prediction model that watches both speakers' audio (so the avatar visibly listens, not just talks), a renderer that paints the motion onto a portrait in real time, and a serving system that keeps the show running at 25 frames per second with measured, bounded delay. Its standout scientific contribution is a new test proving the avatar's listening nods and reactions actually depend on what you said — not just generic plausible fidgeting.

## TL;DR

AVTR-1 (Avaturn Live, arXiv:2609.22913v1) is an open stack for live interactive avatar conversations spanning a 153M-parameter conditional flow-matching Transformer that autoregressively predicts five-frame motion chunks (42-dim LivePortrait-derived head/expression vectors) from dual self/other audio channels, a TensorRT renderer producing five 25 fps frames per request with 0.86–2.81× real-time factors across consumer and data-center GPUs, and a worklet-based streamer (transport, conversation engine, rendering worklet over a type-routed event bus with a shared stream clock) that converts irregular voice-agent speech into gapless audio windows with a derived response-latency contribution of 305–705 ms and interruption-latency contribution of 305–505 ms, both validated against OpenAI Realtime API and Cartesia Line integrations. Training combines a 926-hour internal dyadic corpus pipeline (annotation → scene/face filtering → audiovisual speaker separation → motion-outlier rejection) with regional flow-matching/cosine/smoothness/VAD-gate losses, progressive-plus-immiscible noise, a teacher-forcing rollout curriculum, and self-distillation of the HuBERT encoder for 525 ms chunk inference (50%→12% drift error). On the SI-184 Seamless Interaction subset the paper reports dyadic-leading visual quality (FID 14.3, FVD 76.8, CSIM 0.94), competitive lip sync, and listening motion that the new Reference-Based Directed Granger Gain (R-DGG) shows is predictively dependent on speaker speech for all dyadic systems but no talking-head generator.

## Problem & Motivation

Speech-driven talking-head generation produces increasingly natural identity-faithful video, and recent work moves toward real-time dyadic systems — but fast inference alone is insufficient for live conversation, which additionally requires speech-conditioned listening and timely synchronized audio-video delivery. External voice-agent speech arrives in variable-sized fragments while chunk-based motion generators need fixed-size audio windows, so a surrounding live system must bridge the two while generating frames continuously, synchronizing them with audio, scheduling playback, and handling interruptions — decisions that shape response latency and interruption latency. Listening evaluation has a parallel gap: motion-similarity metrics cannot distinguish a listener that responds to speech from one that merely moves plausibly, so a metric isolating speech dependence is needed.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Motion/appearance split with a compact dyadic generator:** a 153M-parameter pre-norm Transformer decoder (18 layers, 512 wide, per-head QK RMSNorm, gated attention, RoPE) does conditional flow matching over five-frame 42-dim motion chunks with separate near/far motion, dual-audio, and reference conditioning paths plus a VAD-supervised audio gate, keeping per-chunk cost independent of history length.
2. **Self-distilled chunk HuBERT encoder:** the frozen-architecture bidirectional encoder is fine-tuned to reproduce its own full-context features from fixed 525 ms windows (120/200/205 ms past/present/future), cutting window-vs-full-context drift from ~50% to 12% relative L1 without architectural surgery or resumed pretraining.
3. **Streamer with stream clock and speech schedulers:** three worklets over a type-routed event bus plus a shared media timeline that absorbs overruns by shifting time; per-stream schedulers admit irregular segments into gapless 200/205 ms window pairs under four rules (left-pad, fragment-split, right-pad-at-end, one-boundary-per-window), blocking rather than substituting silence on late fragments.
4. **Derived latency model with measured validation:** RLC = Tlead + Tpresent + Twait + Tpad ∈ [305, 705) ms and ILC ∈ [305, 505) ms under real-time-arrival, observable-marker, and non-blocking conditions; OpenAI Realtime (RLC 390.4 / ILC 362.7 ms) and Cartesia Line (RLC 454.2 / ILC 393.3 ms) measurements fall inside the bounds and sum exactly, feeding a 1757 ms end-to-end reference budget dominated by the 1090 ms voice agent.
5. **R-DGG listening metric:** reference speech/motion projections fit once on ground truth and reused across systems, twin ridge regressions with/without projected speaker speech, K = 100 circular-shift correction, and participant-resampled bootstraps — validated by a ground-truth positive control and two negative-control types (talking-head generators, mismatched GT×other pairs).

## Key Findings

Renderer throughput is real-time on most tested GPUs (L40S 2.81×/TTFF 42 ms, A100 2.20×/54 ms, 4060 Ti 1.24×/99 ms, 3070 1.11×/94 ms; L4 0.99×, 3060 Ti 0.97×, 4060 0.86× just under). On SI-184 AVTR-1 leads dyadic systems on every visual metric and most conventional listening metrics while staying competitive on lip sync, though talking-head generators still take top ranks on several motion-similarity components — the finding that motivates R-DGG. R-DGG passes validation (ground truth 0.72 [0.32, 1.12] ×10⁻⁴ nats; all dyadic intervals above zero; all talking-head and GT×other intervals include zero) with AVTR-1 at 0.57 [0.19, 0.98], p = 0.002 — though overlapping dyadic intervals support no ranking among the three. The end-to-end analysis shows video-avatar deployment at 1757 ms exceeds the 1500 ms voice-to-voice target, with the difference necessarily coming out of the voice agent.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper itself suggests adaptive lead-time tracking (e.g. 99th-percentile online TTFF) instead of fixed Tlead = 100 ms; unifies nothing about single-checkpoint streaming beyond the 4-frame-lookahead-class encoder design; and scopes end-to-end latency coverage to reference values from public sources. Natural extensions are closing the sub-real-time gap on weaker GPUs, single-model handling of longer/variable response delays, human perceptual validation of listening quality alongside R-DGG dependence scores, and broader corpora since R-DGG scores compare only within one corpus.
