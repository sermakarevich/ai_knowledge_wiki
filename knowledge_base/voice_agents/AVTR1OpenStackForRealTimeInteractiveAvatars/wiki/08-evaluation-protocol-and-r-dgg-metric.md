# Evaluation protocol and R-DGG metric
**In one sentence:** On 184 Seamless Interaction speaker–listener pairs (SI-184, 11.90 hours) the paper scores visual quality, lip sync, and conventional listening metrics, then introduces the Reference-Based Directed Granger Gain (R-DGG) — the log-ratio of prediction error with versus without projected speaker speech, shift-corrected and bootstrapped — to test whether listener motion actually depends on the speaker's speech.
## Key points
- Corpus: 184 speaker–listener pairs from the conversational improvised subset of the Seamless Interaction test split (SI-184), 11.90 hours at 30 fps; per pair the speaker video is kept, the listener is generated, and output is compared with the recorded listener.
- R-DGG scored set: 1,457 listening segments from 177 videos (3.46 hours at 25 fps) — maximal listener-silent/speaker-active intervals, 0.2 s trimmed from silence boundaries, ≥4 s retained, 100 preceding in-interval frames required per scored row, common rows requiring successful tracking for ground truth and every method.
- Methods: three dyadic systems (AvatarForcing* with extended RoPE range and 30→600 s generation length, AVTR-1, DyStream) receiving both participants' audio plus four talking-head generators (Ditto, FLOAT, SoulX Lite, SoulX Pro) receiving only the generated participant's track; R-DGG uses K = 100 circular shifts.
- Conventional metrics: FID (frame distribution), FVD (video distribution), ArcFace CSIM (identity), SyncNet LSE-D/LSE-C (lip sync); rPCC (speaker–listener correlation discrepancy), PFD (joint motion distribution), SID (K-means diversity), Var (temporal variation) — computed from EMOCA and LivePortrait features at 25 fps on VAD-selected listener-silent frames (Tables 5b/6) with representation and frame-set ablations in the appendix.
- R-DGG motivation: a generated listener can move plausibly without responding to the speaker, and motion metrics cannot tell mimicry from speech-conditioned response; R-DGG measures the additional predictive information speaker speech adds after accounting for listener history and speaker motion (predictive dependence in the Granger convention — explicitly not causal influence).
- Features: 126-dim EMOCA+LivePortrait motion per participant (listener current frame is the target, its past is history); speaker motion PCA-reduced to 32 dims; both participants' speech from HuBERT Base layer 9, PCA to 64 dims (an evaluation encoder separate from the streaming encoder); 25 temporal offsets over 1–100 frames on a 25 fps grid, per-offset standardized in-interval; variance-based coordinate weights shared across methods (Equation 10).
- Reference projections: speaker speech/motion projections fit once by ridge regression on ground-truth rows where both feature extractors succeed (Equation 11) and reused to score every system so scores stay comparable; estimated with cross-fitting over identity-disjoint conversation groups.
- Scoring: two ridge regressions per system predict scaled listener motion from [history + projected speaker motion] versus the same plus projected speaker speech (Equation 12); gain ga|m = log(Em/Ea,m) in nats (Equations 13–14); positive means speaker speech reduces error beyond history and speaker motion.
- Shift correction: refit both regressions under K circular shifts of speaker features (offsets ≥4 s, sampled per pair), report Ga|m = ga|m − mean shifted gain (Equation 15); positive means speech predicts better at true timing than shifted.
- Interpretation limits: R-DGG is not listening quality — generated motion can differ from the recording yet score highly, and an over-deterministic model can outscore ground truth; near-zero proves no response only within linear ≤4 s-delay patterns; scores compare only within one corpus (transforms, projections, scaling, and correction are all corpus-specific).
- Uncertainty: 20,000 bootstrap draws resampling participants (not frames), reporting median, 2.5–97.5 percentile interval, and p = Pr(G ≤ 0).
- Validation: ground truth (cross-fitted) must sit above zero (positive control); talking-head generators and GT×other mismatched pairings (reproducible offset, repeat/crop matched, transition rows excluded) must include zero (negative controls); R-DGG is interpretable on a corpus only when all three behave.

## Why two control types
Talking-head generators test "no paired input → no gain"; GT×other tests "natural motion, wrong pairing → no gain" — together they separate speech dependence from generic motion predictability.
