# [2608.25218] TurnBench: A Multi-Domain Benchmark for Turn-Taking Dynamics in Spoken Dialogue — Digest

## 1. [[wiki/01-electrical-engineering-and-systems-science-audio|Electrical Engineering and Systems Science > Audio and Speech Processing]]

**In one sentence:** The arXiv Audio and Speech Processing (eess.AS) listing presents TurnBench (arXiv:2608.25218) as a multi-domain turn-taking benchmark pairing a 30-hour triple-annotated dyadic corpus with a standardized end-of-turn and interruption detection protocol.

## Key points
- TurnBench is listed under Audio and Speech Processing (eess.AS), cross-listed as Computation and Language (cs.CL), as arXiv:2608.25218.
- The abstract frames the gap as the lack of a consistent, linguistically grounded evaluation protocol plus hand-annotated data across diverse conversation types.
- TurnBench pairs a 30-hour hand-labeled corpus of dyadic human conversation with a standardized protocol for end-of-turn and interruption detection.
- Conversation type is a controllable experimental variable covering six distinct interaction styles, with each conversation triple-annotated.
- Benchmarking 14 heterogeneous turn-taking systems, end-of-turn recall is stable across types while interruption false positives are type-dependent and concentrated in backchannel-dense styles.
- In smooth floor transfers, human listeners begin speaking a median 151 ms before the current turn ends; no current system matches this without excessive false positives.
- The release includes the corpus, a 104-hour training set, and a public leaderboard with an interactive dataset viewer.
- Listing details: 8 pages, 2 figures, accepted to IEEE SLT 2026; v1 submitted 25 Aug 2026, v2 (camera-ready) revised 16 Sep 2026.

## The argument in five moves
1. Natural turn-taking requires real-time decisions about when to take, hold, or yield the floor.
2. Existing evaluation is limited by the lack of a consistent, linguistically grounded protocol and hand-annotated data across diverse conversation types.
3. TurnBench answers with a 30-hour triple-annotated dyadic corpus plus a standardized end-of-turn and interruption detection protocol, treating six interaction styles as a controllable variable.
4. Benchmarking 14 heterogeneous systems shows end-of-turn recall is stable while interruption false positives are type-dependent and concentrated in backchannel-dense styles.
5. Humans begin smooth floor transfers a median 151 ms early, a level no current system matches without excessive false positives, motivating the public corpus, 104-hour training set, leaderboard, and viewer release.
