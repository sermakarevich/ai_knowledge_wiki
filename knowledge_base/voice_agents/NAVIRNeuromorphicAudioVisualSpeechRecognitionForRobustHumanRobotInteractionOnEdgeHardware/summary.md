# NAVIR: Neuromorphic Audio-Visual Speech Recognition for Robust Human-Robot Interaction on Edge Hardware

**Paper:** [..](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24391v1) — arXiv, 21 Sep 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

Factory floors are like trying to hold a conversation next to a jackhammer, where ears alone fail so humans instinctively watch lips to fill in the gaps. NAVIR gives robots that same trick by combining camera-based lip reading with microphone audio in a brain-inspired chip that only fires when needed, like neurons that stay quiet until spoken to. Because the BrainChip AKD1000 chip cannot run the heavy 3D and attention machinery of normal speech AI, the authors split the job into small sequential 2D steps the chip understands. The result is a self-contained Raspberry Pi plus neuromorphic system that hears commands through industrial noise and drives a robot arm in real time while sipping power.

## TL;DR

NAVIR is an end-to-end audio-visual speech recognizer purpose-built for the BrainChip AKD1000 neuromorphic SoC, which natively supports only sequential 2D convolutional inference with no 3D convolutions, recurrence, or attention. It factorizes encoding into a per-frame visual encoder, a temporal video encoder over stacked embeddings, and a parallel MFCC spectrogram audio encoder, fuses them through a lightweight MLP predictor head trained with CTC on noise-augmented audio, decodes with grammar-constrained beam search, and recovers accuracy after hybrid 8/4/4 and 4/4/4 quantization with quantization-aware training. On GRID the quantized audio-visual model reaches 14.0% WER under noise on the unseen-speaker split and 3.3% on the overlapped-speaker split versus 22.5% and 11.8% audio-only, while on the internal industrial-command corpus it attains 98.6% command accuracy at 1.5% WER. Operation-count analysis gives a 13.17-fold spiking-over-ANN energy advantage at 27.55% mean firing rate, and on-board measurements show about 5-fold lower energy per inference than a Raspberry Pi CPU on the lip-reading model and over 100-fold lower than a laptop GPU at 14.5 inferences per second, validated in a closed-loop uFactory xArm 6 demonstration claimed as the first complete multimodal AVSR pipeline on neuromorphic hardware of this class.

## Problem & Motivation

Voice is the most natural modality for human-machine interaction, yet industrial deployment collapses because machinery, ventilation, and ambient noise sharply degrade audio-only recognition, forcing workers back to keypads, touchscreens, or physical switches that slow workflows and compromise hands-free safety. The human perceptual system compensates by integrating the visual appearance of the mouth, vividly illustrated by the McGurk illusion, and audio-visual speech recognition has shown substantial robustness gains at low signal-to-noise ratios by exploiting the same redundancy. The tension is that modern high-accuracy AVSR pipelines rely on 3D convolutions, recurrent units, attention and conformer encoders, and large transformer language models that implicitly assume GPU-class hardware and exceed the budget of embedded, battery-powered, or thermally constrained mobile-robotics platforms. Neuromorphic processors offer a way out because their sparse event-driven binary spikes replace dense floating-point multiply-accumulate operations with sparse accumulate operations so energy scales with activity rather than nominal capacity, but the commercially available BrainChip AKD1000 supports only sequential convolutional inference via the MetaTF/CNN2SNN toolchain, and multi-stream temporally structured AVSR had not previously been demonstrated at the system level on such hardware.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Factorised Akida-compatible spatio-temporal encoding.** Rather than volumetric 3D sequences, frames are encoded individually by an AkidaNet-based per-frame spatial encoder, the resulting embeddings are stacked into a time-by-features 2D representation, and a second Akida-compatible network applies temporal convolutions to yield a single video-clip embedding, while clip audio is converted to MFCCs and processed by a third AkidaNet-based spectrogram encoder, keeping the entire pipeline within sequential 2D convolutions.

2. **Lightweight fusion head with grammar-constrained beam-search decoding.** The video-clip and audio-clip embeddings are concatenated into a joint representation fed to a small MLP predictor emitting vocabulary logits with softmax probabilities, producing a clip-windows-by-vocabulary score matrix that is decoded by beam search restricted to valid prefixes of the fixed finite grammar, maintaining top-B hypotheses over blank, repeat, and legal-next-token transitions with fallback extension or shortening that guarantees a grammatically valid sentence.

3. **Noise-aware CTC training with hybrid quantization and QAT.** Models are trained with connectionist temporal classification using only sentence-level labels, with UrbanSound8K machinery noise mixed at sampled SNRs, horizontal-flip and temporal-jitter augmentation, and WER plus command accuracy as metrics, then quantized under a hybrid win/w/a scheme with 8/4/4 for the raw-input image and spectrogram encoders and 4/4/4 for the embedding-level video encoder and predictor head, followed by quantization-aware training using only l2 weight decay.

4. **Operation-count plus on-board energy characterization and Pareto analysis.** Theoretical energy follows the standard SNN literature framework parameterized by Horowitz 45 nm constants with ANN energy as MACs times 3.7 pJ and SNN energy as MACs times measured firing rate times 0.9 pJ, explicitly caveated as excluding memory and parallelism effects, complemented by five-minute-session on-board power measurements against Pi-CPU Akida, Pi-CPU Keras, and laptop-GPU baselines with stated idle floors and scope caveats, plus accuracy-versus-cost Pareto comparison against unconstrained GRID baselines.

5. **NAVIR industrial-command corpus and closed-loop edge robot validation.** A custom internal audio-visual dataset of 183 robot-manipulation commands with synonymous phrasings from about 39 words in five categories, recorded in full by two speakers for 366 recordings, is evaluated under an 80/20 unseen-sentence split alongside GRID unseen- and overlapped-speaker protocols, and the deployed Raspberry Pi plus AKD1000 system captures audio and video, runs the neuromorphic pipeline, and dispatches recognized commands to a directly connected xArm 6 arm in real time as a self-contained edge system.

## Key Findings

Under clean audio all audio-capable GRID unseen-speaker models perform comparably, but clean-trained audio models collapse to roughly 77–80% WER when noise is introduced, while noisy-audio training brings this to 21.0% float and 22.5% quantized, and fusing noisy audio with video reduces it further to 16.6% float and 14.0% quantized, confirming that the visual modality anchors recognition when acoustics degrade. The same pattern tightens on the overlapped-speaker split where noisy audio plus video reaches 2.8% float and 3.3% quantized versus 10.4% and 11.8% for noisy audio alone and 9.1% and 6.7% for video alone, with 0.7% and 0.8% under clean fusion, while quantization largely preserves accuracy and occasionally improves it by small inconsistent margins such as the 2.4-point video-only QAT gain on the overlapped split. The constrained video-only model trails unconstrained SOTA on GRID unseen speakers at 34.0% float and 35.3% quantized versus the 9.7–11.4% range, reflecting the deliberate trade of representational depth for deployability since those baselines use unsupported 3D, attention, and large-scale pretraining, yet it approaches the 1.83–4.8% overlapped range and saturates near zero error on NAVIR where video-only holds 0.5–0.7% WER at 100% command accuracy and noisy fusion holds 0.9–1.5% WER at 98.6–100% accuracy while clean-audio-only collapses to 97.5–98.7% WER. Theoretically the video-only model at 27.55% firing rate achieves 314.92 µJ per-sentence SNN inference versus a 4.15 mJ ANN baseline for a 13.17-fold gain and roughly 62.8-fold and 497-fold below LipNet and Wu et al. as ANN points with 3.1-fold and 37.8-fold fewer FLOPs, and on board the video-only Akida backend reaches 14.55 inferences per second at 0.0165 mWh per inference for 4.9-fold better efficiency than the same SNN on Pi CPU, 26-fold over the Pi float baseline, and over 100-fold over laptop GPU, while the 22-pass audio-video extension rises to 0.0894 mWh at 2.61 inferences per second, still 37-fold below laptop GPU and judged comfortably real time for commands.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The paper identifies the audio-video model's lost per-inference advantage as a generic AKD1000 toolchain mapping behavior rather than an architectural flaw, since the spectrogram encoder's larger node and sequence counts inflate passes, and proposes either a small threshold before quantization or fine-tuning under an additional l1 penalty to directly reduce incoming connections per convolutional layer and equalize the video-only and audio-video mappings without architecture changes. It frames the remaining gap to unconstrained accuracy as consistent with the chip's deliberate architectural simplicity and names sparsity-aware fine-tuning as the concrete path to close the deployment gap, with the mapping observation noted as relevant to any work deploying multiple checkpoints of the same architecture on this hardware. The discussion and appendices further scope future work around firing-rate control and spike-encoder design as the primary accuracy and energy levers under strictly convolutional constraints, alongside the acknowledged limits of single-node capacity, the high idle floor, unsupported primitives, and the small two-speaker internal NAVIR corpus that tests unseen sentences rather than unseen speakers.
