# Endpoint Anticipation for Low-Latency Spoken Dialogue

**Paper:** [Endpoint Anticipation for Low-Latency Spoken Dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13450)

## Human Readable TL;DR
Today's voice assistants wait politely until you fully stop talking before they even start thinking, like a chef who only begins cooking after the waiter finishes reading the whole order, which is why replies take a second or more instead of the quarter-second gaps humans manage. This paper teaches the system to read the signs that you are about to finish — your trailing words, intonation, and rhythm — and start drafting its answer early, the way a good sous-chef begins plating dessert while the main course is being cleared. If it guesses right, the reply is already half-ready the moment you stop, cutting wait time nearly in half; if it guesses too early, it quietly throws that draft away and starts over, trading a bit of wasted kitchen effort for much snappier conversation.

## TL;DR
The paper introduces Endpoint Anticipation, a speech-only forecasting task that predicts end-of-turn up to 2.56 s ahead so cascaded spoken dialogue systems can speculatively execute LLM and TTS pipelines on partial context instead of waiting for a reactive endpointer. It formalizes anticipation as independent binary classification per horizon h in {320, …, 2560} ms at 12.5 Hz with a trigger threshold tuning latency savings against premature triggers, and it introduces latency-vs-redundancy metrics (MRA, PAR, ERC, HEA) that separate realized savings from wasted speculative compute. Two model variants are trained — EPA-S with independent per-horizon models and EPA-M with a shared dual-stream backbone plus horizon-specific heads — on SpokenWOZ and Switchboard using frozen Mimi codec features and a 25M streaming Transformer, substantially beating an adapted VAP baseline. Integrated into the Unmute cascaded system with trigger-fork, pre-synthesis caching, and verification, EPA-M (h = 960 ms) cuts average latency from 1195 ms to 690 ms at 28.4% extra compute.

## Problem & Motivation
Cascaded architectures such as Unmute are bottlenecked by reactive turn-completion detection: the endpointer fires only after the user finishes, then ASR, LLM, and TTS run strictly in sequence, imposing a Time-to-First-Audio lower bound near 1–2 s versus roughly 250 ms human response gaps. Prior early end-of-utterance work mostly depends on streaming-ASR hypotheses or language-model confidence, keeping the ASR bottleneck in the loop, while Voice Activity Projection targets generalized two-speaker activity rather than precise fixed-horizon endpoint forecasts. The paper is motivated by shifting from reactive detection to proactive forecasting of end-of-turn signals directly from the acoustic signal, so generation can run during the user's speech and mask sequential system latency, letting modular pipelines keep complex processing while still interacting with low latency.

## Main Original Ideas
1. **Proactive fixed-horizon endpoint forecasting:** the core task reformulation predicts whether the end of turn falls within each fixed horizon h up to 2560 ms ahead, operating speech-only directly on audio and bypassing the ASR bottleneck, with the first threshold-crossing frame acting as the speculative-pipeline trigger.
2. **Latency-vs-redundancy metric family:** four metrics that disentangle success from waste — Median Realized Anticipation for actual latency savings, Premature Anticipation Rate for early triggers, Expected Redundant Computation for duration-normalized discarded compute, and Horizon Entry Accuracy for exact on-time triggering measured with a two-frame collar.
3. **Dual-stream EPA-S and EPA-M architectures:** separate streaming Transformer encoders for user and system channels whose concatenated context feeds prediction heads, learning anticipation amid backchannels and interruptions; EPA-S trains one model per horizon while EPA-M shares the backbone with horizon-specific heads, matching accuracy without horizon-specific retraining.
4. **Speculative execution integration for Unmute:** a trigger-and-fork design where the anticipated endpoint forks state, the LLM generates a short look-ahead token buffer from the partial transcript, TTS pre-synthesizes audio into a held cache, and verification either releases the cache when the endpointer confirms within the horizon or discards it and resumes anticipation, reducing system latency toward the endpointer response time alone.

## Key Findings
EPA-M dominates the adapted VAP baseline in both MRA-vs-PAR and HEA-vs-ERC spaces on SpokenWOZ: at h = 640 ms and roughly 33% ERC it achieves 640 ms MRA and 67.0% HEA against VAP's 160 ms and 19.2%, and at h = 1280 ms with about 15% ERC it holds 480 ms MRA and 22.1% HEA while VAP yields only 80 ms and 7.2%, showing VAP's generalized training cannot produce precise fixed-horizon forecasts. Anticipation proves easier on structured task-oriented SpokenWOZ than on spontaneous Switchboard conversation, with higher MRA at fixed PAR and higher HEA at fixed ERC at both 960 ms and 2560 ms horizons, suggesting the approach is currently most viable for structured applications. EPA-S and EPA-M perform comparably with only minor deviations, so the multi-target variant is preferred for deployment flexibility, and the full Unmute integration delivers a 505 ms average latency reduction (1195 ms down to 690 ms) with local LLM and TTS models, with larger gains expected for API-based models and residual latency attributed to low-anticipation turns, semantic-VAD delays, and inter-process messaging.

## Suggestions & Future Directions
The paper points to semantic edge cases as the main open frontier, such as mid-turn backtracking and late-arriving critical information that could invalidate an early forecast, alongside the observed gap between structured and open-domain spontaneous dialogue that calls for more robust modeling of spontaneity. The natural engineering directions are broader horizon coverage with deployment-friendly multi-target models, tighter coupling with semantic endpointers, and open-source release of the implementation plus the reference Unmute integration so modular pipelines can adopt speculative execution. Since the evaluation omits interruption handling as orthogonal and reports redundant compute near 28%, future work should also quantify the tradeoff under efficient inference, shared feature extractors, and API-model latencies where the savings could be larger.
