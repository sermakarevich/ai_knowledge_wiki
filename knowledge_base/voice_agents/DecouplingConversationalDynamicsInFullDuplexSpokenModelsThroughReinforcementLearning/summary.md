# Decoupling Conversational Dynamics in Full-Duplex Spoken Models through Reinforcement Learning

**Paper:** [Decoupling Conversational Dynamics in Full-Duplex Spoken Models through Reinforcement Learning (Li et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07148)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Think of a dinner-party guest who must decide both what to say and exactly when to jump into a fast, overlapping conversation. Today's full-duplex voice AIs try to learn both skills at once by copying casual human chit-chat, which makes them good at nodding along with "uh-huh" but worse at answering questions smartly — like a student who learned manners but forgot the lesson. DuplexPO splits the job the way a driving instructor separates steering from reading the map: it leaves the smart-answer brain alone and uses reinforcement learning only to coach timing instincts such as when to start, when to murmur a backchannel, and when to go quiet if interrupted. The result is an assistant that barges in less, responds faster, and still keeps its reasoning intact.

## TL;DR

The paper argues the intelligence–dynamics trade-off in full-duplex spoken language models is not fundamental but stems from coupling what to say with when to speak in a single imitation objective. It proposes DuplexPO, an RL framework that decouples real-time floor control from semantic content by restricting policy updates to short dynamics-critical windows around turn transitions, backchannels, and user barge-ins sampled from long human conversations on Fisher and Seamless-Naturalistic-HQ. Within each window the policy is scored by the Factorized Conversational Dynamics Reward (FCDR) for turn initiation, backchanneling, yielding after barge-in, and pattern regularization, and optimized with a GRPO-style group-normalized objective regularized toward the reference SFT model. Experiments report that DuplexPO achieves the best window-level initiation and yield rates with the lowest onset error on both datasets, breaks the mid-turn latency–interruption trade-off, is preferred by a blinded Gemini pairwise judge in 76.9% of Fisher and 69.3% of non-tie Seamless comparisons, and preserves or slightly improves instruction-following, QA, speech understanding, and reasoning while shifting boundary-control behavior only near speaking decisions.

## Problem & Motivation

Full-duplex spoken models that listen while speaking promise far more natural interaction than turn-based pipelines, with low latency, timely backchannels, and graceful barge-in handling, yet they perform substantially worse than half-duplex counterparts on instruction-following and reasoning benchmarks. The paper diagnoses this trade-off at two levels: at the modeling level, long structured decoding needed for reasoning conflicts with fragmented, rapidly changing contexts that demand local real-time speak, pause, and silence decisions biased toward short-range states, and at the data level, supervised fine-tuning on human dialogue corpora such as Fisher teaches interaction patterns but is dominated by casual, non-goal-oriented exchanges misaligned with a helpful instruction-following assistant. Traditional systems sidestep the issue with external orchestration such as voice activity detection or dialogue management, whereas model-level approaches like Moshi, SALMONN-omni, and SALM-Duplex expose turn-taking, backchanneling, and barge-in directly to the model, making temporal coordination part of generation itself. The central hypothesis is that conversational dynamics can instead be learned as a separate real-time decision policy from human dialogue data, so that optimizing only when to speak — turn-taking, backchanneling, and yielding — improves naturalness, responsiveness, and temporal coordination without relearning or degrading what to say.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Decoupling when to speak from what to say:** the core framing treats floor control as a targeted speech-specific alignment problem where the instruction-tuned semantic capability is frozen in spirit and only real-time boundary decisions are reshaped, using on-policy rollouts under the model's own response distribution rather than imitating heterogeneous full responses.

2. **Dynamics-critical window sampling:** instead of whole-dialogue objectives that blur credit assignment, DuplexPO selects short windows around annotated agent speaking events with a frame-level policy over a uniform grid of duration Δ (0.08 s), teacher-forcing history before each window and sampling only inside it, with formal window tuples, discretized reference intervals, lead time L and buffer B, and anti-overlap clipping that keeps supervision close to boundary events.

3. **Factorized Conversational Dynamics Reward (FCDR):** a temporally shaped, event-level reward replaces coarse rollout-level signals with interpretable onset-delay timing (early versus late starts) and three metadata-driven masks for onset, backchannel, and offset plus a regularization term, combining a bounded turn-initiation reward, a bounded backchannel overlap reward, a negated yielding-after-barge-in latency penalty, and a negated pattern-regularization penalty for undesirable interactions.

4. **Group-based GRPO optimization on windows only:** each window generates multiple rollouts scored by FCDR and normalized into clipped group advantages, optimized with a policy-gradient loss applied only to sampled tokens inside the window plus a KL penalty toward the rollout behavior policy, which is reported to give denser and more stable signal than DPO-style extremes-only preferences.

5. **Rhythm-aware data and training stack:** the pipeline combines 530K hours of two-party pseudo-dialogue speech continuation, 70K hours of spoken instruction QA plus multi-turn dialogue, synthetic barge-ins on long agent utterances with forced end-of-sequence, real-acoustics ASR-QA, and reconstructed Fisher and Seamless timing with backchannel-preserving segmentation (24.6K Fisher and 43.1K Seamless samples with leak-free session splits), built on a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone with frozen Parakeet streaming encoder and CosyVoice2 codec/generator and trained on 64 A800 GPUs.

6. **Suppressed-intent and attention diagnostics:** to explain the behavioral change, the work introduces frame-level Suppressed Intent Rate and Suppression Release Ratio for latent boundary impulses alongside attention-mass analysis, showing that RL selectively releases already-present speaking and yielding impulses near conversational boundaries rather than globally biasing eagerness or caution.

## Key Findings

DuplexPO consistently improves window-level turn-taking and backchannel behavior on both Fisher and Seamless, achieving the highest initiation and yield rates for full turns and backchannels with the lowest onset mean absolute error, while the data-matched SFT Dynamics baseline regresses across all dynamics metrics relative to the SFT Baseline, showing that supervised imitation of natural dynamics alone cannot learn robust coordination. In ambiguous mid-turn regions the paper documents a latency–interruption trade-off where commercial models and Ultravox are accurate but slow through conservative endpointing while open duplex models such as Moshi and PersonaPlex are fast but suffer high voiced interrupt rates, whereas DuplexPO preserves perfect turn-taking with the highest yield rate, lowest latency, and competitively low interrupt rate by using mid-turn evidence to distinguish true completion from disfluent continuation. These metric gains transfer to conversation-level perception: the blinded Gemini 3.0 Pro pairwise judge prefers DuplexPO overall in 20 of 26 Fisher conversations and 264 of 381 non-tie Seamless conversations, with backchannel highs of 91.7% and 82.9% and consistent turn-taking and barge-in advantages, and calibration with synthetic ±1.0 s timestamp shifts confirms the judge is genuinely timing-sensitive with stronger penalty for premature entry. On model intelligence DuplexPO preserves the SFT Baseline and yields small consistent gains across factual QA, instruction following, speech understanding, and reasoning (for example Llama Questions 75.3 vs 72.0, OpenBookQA 73.7 vs 72.2, MMSU 56.2 vs 54.9, AlpacaEval 3.68 vs 3.43, CommonEval 3.74 vs 3.48), supporting the claim that timing-only RL does not degrade semantics. Mechanistic ablations show longer lead times sharply reduce mean RL reward while buffer variation matters little, FCDR beats a SoulX-Duplug neural reward model that improves over SFT Dynamics but not reliably over the SFT Baseline, and GRPO beats a DPO-style window variant especially on yield and backchannel metrics, while suppressed-intent analysis finds selective release of latent boundary impulses and attention shifts away from the initial sink toward real-text, recent-context, and PAD waiting-state positions with higher entropy.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The authors acknowledge that factorized rewards enable interpretable credit assignment but may miss subtle pragmatic factors such as user intent, discourse content, speaker style, and culture-specific timing preferences, that human timing annotations are not unique ground truth across speakers, languages, and settings, and that restricting updates to local windows may miss long-range dialogue effects. The broader-impacts note warns that highly human-like timing could enable realistic voice phishing and calls for safeguards such as watermarking or identity disclosure in future deployments. Natural extensions framed by the ablations and appendices include richer pragmatic and content-aware rewards that complement rather than disturb fine-grained timing, evaluation of longer-horizon conversational effects beyond isolated windows, and continued calibration of automatic and judge-based dynamics metrics against human perception across more diverse speakers and languages.
