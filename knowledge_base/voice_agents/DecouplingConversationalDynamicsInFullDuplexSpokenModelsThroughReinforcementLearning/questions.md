# Retrieval Practice: Decoupling Conversational Dynamics in Full-Duplex Spoken Models through Reinforcement Learning

### Q1. What intelligence–dynamics trade-off does the paper diagnose, and what are its modeling-level and data-level causes?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Full-duplex models support simultaneous listening/speaking, backchannels, and barge-in handling but score substantially worse than half-duplex counterparts on instruction-following and reasoning. At the modeling level, long structured decoding for reasoning conflicts with fragmented, rapidly changing contexts demanding fast local speak/pause/silence decisions biased toward short-range states. At the data level, SFT on human dialogue corpora such as Fisher teaches interaction patterns but is dominated by casual, non-goal-oriented exchanges misaligned with a helpful assistant. See [[wiki/01-decoupling-conversational-dynamics|Decoupling Conversational Dynamics in Full-Duplex Spoken Models]].

### Q2. What is the paper's central decoupling hypothesis, and how does DuplexPO operationalize it?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The hypothesis is that conversational dynamics can be learned as a separate real-time decision policy: optimizing only floor-control decisions (turn-taking, backchanneling, yielding) improves dynamics while preserving instruction-following and reasoning. DuplexPO operationalizes this by sampling dynamics-critical windows around turn transitions, backchannels, and barge-ins from long multi-turn conversations and training on-policy under the model's own response distribution. It scores windows with the Factorized Conversational Dynamics Reward and optimizes with a GRPO-style objective regularized toward the reference SFT model. See [[wiki/01-decoupling-conversational-dynamics|Decoupling Conversational Dynamics in Full-Duplex Spoken Models]].

### Q3. How does DuplexPO's dynamics-critical window sampling work (Eqs. 3–6)?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Each window is a tuple Wi = (si, ei, gi, hi, ci) with discretized reference interval gi = ⌊ai/Δ⌋, hi = ⌊bi/Δ⌋ from annotated segment times (ai, bi), plus event metadata ci marking backchannels and barge-ins. The sampled support uses lead time L and buffer B via si = max(ei−1, ⌊(ai − L)/Δ⌋) and ēi = ⌊(bi + B)/Δ⌋, with anti-overlap clipping ei = min(ēi, ⌊(ai+1 − L)/Δ⌋, T). History before si is teacher-forced and the policy is optimized only on sampled actions inside [si, ei), motivated by turn transfer being decided by local timing and prosodic cues. See [[wiki/02-duplexpo-framework|DuplexPO Framework: Dynamics-Critical Windows, Reward Calculation and Prediction]].

### Q4. How is the Factorized Conversational Dynamics Reward set up, and how does DuplexPO differ from ASPIRin and ORISE-style rewards?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The policy is frame-level πθ(yt | y<t, x≤t) on a grid of duration Δ, and each window is scored by Ri = R(Wi, y[si,ei)); predicted onset ĝ i = min Ŝi gives onset delay τi = Δ(ĝ i − gi), negative when early and positive when late. Three masks from ci (m_on, m_bc, m_off) select active components in RFCD = λon m_on R_on + λbc m_bc R_bc + λoff m_off R_off + λreg R_reg. Unlike ASPIRin, which projects the action space into active-speech versus inactive-silence states, DuplexPO keeps the original action space and changes only the optimization unit to windows; unlike ORISE-style rollout-level rewards, FCDR gives event-level shaped supervision for precise temporal credit assignment. See [[wiki/02-duplexpo-framework|DuplexPO Framework: Dynamics-Critical Windows, Reward Calculation and Prediction]].

### Q5. What are the four FCDR components, their target behaviors, forms, and ranges?
> [!tip]- Answer
> R_on targets turn initiation with an indicator-times-exponential-decay form over onset delay and ranges in [0, 1], while R_bc targets backchanneling with an indicator-plus-max-overlap form against the annotated backchannel interval and also ranges in [0, 1]. R_off targets yielding after barge-in as a negated clipped latency-penalty term in [−1, 0], and R_reg is pattern regularization as a negated clipped weighted event-indicator sum in [−1, 0]. The positive pair rewards well-timed participation while the negative pair penalizes slow yielding and undesirable interaction patterns. See [[wiki/03-factorized-reward-optimization|Factorized Reward and Group-Based Optimization]].

### Q6. What is DuplexPO's GRPO-style optimization objective, and what is the four-stage training pipeline around it?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Within each window, rewards across sampled continuations are group-normalized into a clipped advantage A_i,k = clip((r_i,k − μ_i) / (σ_i + ε), −5, 5), and the loss L_GRPO = L_policy + β L_KL applies the policy gradient only to tokens inside the window with a KL penalty toward the rollout behavior policy. GRPO is claimed to give a denser, more stable signal than DPO-style preference optimization by using the full reward distribution rather than extremes. The pipeline runs pre-training, SFT, RL with DuplexPO over dynamics-critical windows, then a speech-synthesis SFT stage that freezes everything except the streaming TTS module. See [[wiki/03-factorized-reward-optimization|Factorized Reward and Group-Based Optimization]].

### Q7. What do the window-level dynamics results show, and why is SFT Dynamics an important control?
> [!tip]- Answer
> DuplexPO achieves the best overall window-level performance with the highest initiation and yield rates for both turn-taking and backchannels plus the lowest onset MAE on both Fisher and Seamless. SFT Dynamics uses the same SFT recipe plus the reconstructed dynamics-aware dialogue data, yet regresses across all dynamics metrics relative to the SFT Baseline, showing SFT alone cannot learn robust conversational coordination from natural dynamics patterns. The comparison isolates RL as the cause of the gains rather than the dynamics data itself. See [[wiki/04-conversational-dynamics-evaluation|Conversational Dynamics Evaluation]].

### Q8. How does DuplexPO break the mid-turn latency–interruption trade-off, and does the conversation-level judge agree?
> [!tip]- Answer
> In ambiguous mid-turn regions, commercial models and Ultravox get reasonable accuracy only with high latency from conservative endpointing, while Moshi and PersonaPlex get low latency but high voiced-interrupt rates from premature interruptions; SFT Dynamics gets low VIR and high yield only via long latencies. DuplexPO preserves perfect turn-taking with the highest yield rate, the lowest latency, and competitively low VIR, interpreted as learning coordinated floor control rather than a bias toward eagerness or caution. The blinded Gemini pairwise judge prefers DuplexPO in 76.9% of Fisher comparisons (n=26) and 69.3% of non-tie Seamless comparisons (n=383), with dimension-level preferences consistent with the automatic metrics. See [[wiki/04-conversational-dynamics-evaluation|Conversational Dynamics Evaluation]].

### Q9. Does DuplexPO preserve model intelligence, what mechanism explains the gains, and what limits are stated?
> [!tip]- Answer
> On Table 4 DuplexPO preserves the SFT Baseline's capability with small consistent gains (e.g. LlamaQ 75.3 vs 72.0, OBQA 73.7 vs 72.2, MMSU 56.2 vs 54.9, AlpacaE 3.68 vs 3.43), which matters because optimizing the real-time speaking policy does not degrade semantic and reasoning abilities. Mechanistically, DuplexPO releases suppressed <BOS> impulses more selectively than SFT and shifts <BOS>/<EOS> behavior near boundary-control decisions rather than globally suppressing speaking intent. Stated limits are that factorized rewards may miss pragmatics such as intent, style, and culture-specific timing, human timing labels are not unique ground truth, and local-window updates may miss long-range dialogue effects. See [[wiki/04-conversational-dynamics-evaluation|Conversational Dynamics Evaluation]].

### Q11. What is the training-data recipe in Appendix A?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Speech-continuation pre-training uses 530K hours from large text corpora converted into two-party pseudo-dialogues with randomized turn lengths, two speaker profiles, and role swapping, while instruction-following QA adds 70K hours (10K single-turn plus multi-turn up to four minutes) generated with a helpful-assistant prompt over contexts including Wikipedia and rendered with multi-speaker TTS. Synthetic barge-ins fire with probability 0.1 on agent utterances over four seconds, onset uniform over the 20%–80% span, with an 8-token (≈0.64 s) reaction delay before forced <EOS> that masks audio at inference. ASR-QA adds real acoustics via NeMo ASRSET clips with LLM-written questions, text comes from a pool of GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, and Llama3.1-70B-Instruct rendered by Chatterbox, Magpie-TTS, and Mooncast, and dynamics-aware reconstruction recovers Fisher and Seamless segments from word alignments while keeping short acknowledgments as standalone backchannels. See [[wiki/06-references|References (Continued) and Data Details]].

### Q12. How are data filtered and split, and what stack, SFT controls, and optimizer settings are used?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Non-dialogue sessions and low-quality fragments are removed, leaving 24.6K Fisher and 43.1K Seamless samples, with splits fixed at conversation/session level before reconstruction so no evaluation conversation leaks into SFT examples, RL windows, or timing annotations; Fisher evaluates on a held-out test SHAR view and Seamless on eval-clean. Training balances sources with a round-robin sampler truncated at 210 s and augments with SpecAugment plus 10K Freesound/MUSAN noise clips at probability 0.5 and 0–60 dB SNR. The stack builds on the Nemotron-VoiceChat recipe with NeMo, a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone, frozen 600M-parameter causal Parakeet streaming encoder plus 1024-dim adapter and frozen CosyVoice2 codec/generator (12.5 Hz encoder/LLM vs 25 Hz codec, two speech tokens per LLM frame) on 64 A800 GPUs. SFT Baseline excludes dynamics-aware data while SFT Dynamics adds it as the data-matched control, and optimization uses AdamW (β = 0.9, 0.98; decay 0; inverse-square-root schedule; peak LR 5×10−4 pre-training with 2,500-step warm-up and 5×10−5 SFT; bfloat16; grad-clip norm 1.0). See [[wiki/07-data-preprocessing|Data Preprocessing — Remove Non-Dialogue Sessions and Low-Quality Fragments]].

### Q13. What do the attention analysis, judge calibration, and pairwise judging protocol show?
> [!tip]- Answer
> DuplexPO redistributes attention away from the <BOS> sink (70.2% to 14.5% for Moshi 7B) toward PAD-state positions (28.0% to 77.5%), real-text tokens (1.8% to 22.5%), and recent context (last-16 mass 13.5% to 33.4%), with entropy rising from 1.63 to 3.19; PAD positions are read as non-speech/waiting states informing speak/yield decisions, though only as supporting evidence rather than mechanistic proof. Judge calibration on 26 Fisher conversations with identical transcripts but ±1.0 s agent-timestamp shifts prefers original timing (69.2% vs delayed, p=0.076; 80.8% vs advanced, p=0.002), showing stronger sensitivity to premature than delayed entry, which supports FCDR's narrower tolerance for early initiation. The Appendix F protocol judges only turn-taking, backchannel, and barge-in handling with Gemini 3.0 Pro, blinded randomized order, and JSON-schema-valid outputs, giving DuplexPO overall win rates of 76.9% on Fisher (20–6) and 69.3% on Seamless (264–117) with backchannel highs of 91.7% and 82.9%. See [[wiki/08-evaluation-protocol-appendix|Evaluation Protocol Appendix]].

### Q14. You must improve a full-duplex model's timing without harming its instruction-following: would you recommend FCDR with GRPO over a neural-reward-model or DPO-style alternative, and why?
> [!tip]- Answer
> I would recommend FCDR with GRPO because the ablations show the neural-reward-model teacher (SoulX-Duplug) improves over SFT Dynamics on some metrics but never consistently beats the SFT Baseline, whereas FCDR reaches e.g. Fisher onset MAE 0.69 with 100.0% turn-taking initiation and 100.0% backchannel yield. I would likewise prefer GRPO since it beats the DPO-style extremes-pairing variant on most window-level metrics, especially yield and backchannel behavior, because group-normalized advantages exploit the full reward distribution. The caveats to carry into deployment are lead-time sensitivity (overly long lead times sharply drop mean reward while buffer hardly matters), DPO's small edges on Seamless onset MAE and FDB-v3 VIR, and the misuse risk of realistic vishing that calls for watermarking or identity disclosure. See [[wiki/09-judge-evaluation-details|Candidate B observable rhythm summary]].
