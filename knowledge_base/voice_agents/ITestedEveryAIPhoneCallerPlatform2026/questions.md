# Retrieval Practice: I Tested Every AI Phone Caller Platform (2026)

### Q1. Which seven platforms does the video compare, and what four categories frame the comparison?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The comparison covers Vapy, Retail, Synflow, Bland, 11 Labs, Voice Flow, and Vogent, evaluated on sound, speed, cost, and integrations. The four-category frame is introduced as the cure for choice overload so buyers can match a platform to their use case. See [[wiki/01-overview|So, you've looked into AI phone]].

### Q2. Why does the video argue sound quality depends more on the speech model than on the platform?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Most platforms assemble agents from shared external speech providers, so the chosen model and specific voice dominate how a call sounds. The presenter's school-community poll found 11 Labs, Cartesia, and Rhyme the most used providers, and he demos their default voices on one real-estate script. He warns each provider has many uneven voices, so extensive listening tests matter more than the platform badge. See [[wiki/01-overview|So, you've looked into AI phone]].

### Q3. What makes Vapy's own voices stand out, and what is their main limitation?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Vapy's own uploaded voices are trained specifically for phone calls rather than audiobook or marketing recordings, and are rated the best-sounding in the test. Their drawback is narrow choice: only a few American-accent voices are available. Bland, 11 Labs, and Vogent custom voices sounded quite similar to the showcased provider voices. See [[wiki/01-overview|So, you've looked into AI phone]].

### Q4. How was the speed test controlled, and what was the latency ranking?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Every platform ran GPT-4o with default settings, the same system prompt and transcriber, the same 11 Labs voice where supported, and real phone calls over a Twilio number, repeated 10 times each. Retail AI ranked fastest at 1.79s, then Synflow at 1.88s and Vapy at 1.91s, with a clear gap to Bland (2.5s), 11 Labs (2.6s), Voice Flow (2.7s), and Vogent (3.6s). See [[wiki/01-overview|So, you've looked into AI phone]].

### Q5. How do the platforms rank on standard per-minute cost, and why do headline rates mislead?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Voice Flow is cheapest at 8 cents per minute but requires a $60/month subscription, followed by Bland and Vogent at 9 cents, Vapy, Retail, and 11 Labs at 12 cents, and Synflow highest at 13 cents. The field is described as "very similar" overall because model settings, call volume, and enterprise discounts shift the real price. Only standard rates are compared, so high-volume buyers must weigh the subscription and discount effects. See [[wiki/01-overview|So, you've looked into AI phone]].

### Q6. Which platforms offer native SMS, calendar, and CRM integrations, and where are the gaps?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Native SMS sending exists only on Retail, Vapy, and Bland, with two-way SMS text agents exclusive to Retail. Prebuilt Cal.com or Google Calendar booking comes with Retail, Vapy, Synflow, and Voice Flow. For CRM, Vapy, Synflow, and Bland integrate Go High Level natively, Synflow and Bland also cover HubSpot or Salesforce, while 11 Labs supports only API calls so CRM links must be built from scratch. See [[wiki/01-overview|So, you've looked into AI phone]].

### Q7. Evaluation: an agency wants white-labeled hosting with minimal settings, while a developer wants custom LLMs and full provider control — which platform should each pick and why?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The agency should pick Synflow for its built-in white-labeling and native workflow automations with mostly hidden model settings, which suit handover to business clients. The developer should pick Vapy as the most developer-centric option, with custom LLMs and support for almost all LLM, speech, and transcriber providers fully exposed. Neither is inherently better; the right choice follows technical expertise and whether the goal is client hosting, app integration, or handover. See [[wiki/01-overview|So, you've looked into AI phone]].
