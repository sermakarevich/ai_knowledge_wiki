# FastTurn: Unifying Acoustic and Streaming Semantic Cues for Low-Latency and Robust Turn Detection

**Paper:** [FastTurn: Unifying Acoustic and Streaming Semantic Cues for Low-Latency and Robust Turn Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01897)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine talking to a voice assistant that has to decide, mid-sentence, whether you are actually finished, just pausing to think, or only tossing in a quick "uh-huh" that it should ignore. Older systems handle this like a security guard who only checks whether anyone is making noise, so they react fast but miss the meaning, or like a secretary who waits for a full typed transcript, so they understand the words but answer too slowly. FastTurn works more like an experienced interpreter who listens to both your tone of voice and your half-finished words at the same time, guessing early when you are done while still double-checking with the sound of your voice. This lets it jump in quickly when you finish and hold back when you are merely hesitating, even with echoes or overlapping speech in the room.

## TL;DR

FastTurn is a framework for efficient turn detection in full-duplex spoken dialogue that unifies fast streaming CTC transcription with acoustic-semantic fusion to achieve low latency and robustness under overlap, noise, and echo. It is built in three steps — FastTurn-Cascaded feeding a streaming CTC prompt into Qwen3-0.6B, FastTurn-Semantic adding Conformer acoustic embeddings through an LLM adapter, and FastTurn-Unified fusing Conformer intermediate states with LLM hidden states in an MLP turn detector — trained with a four-stage pipeline of semantic pretraining, modality alignment, joint training with prompt dropout, and modality fusion. Evaluated on a four-state FastTurn test set (Complete, Incomplete, Backchannel, Wait) plus Smart Turn and Easy Turn sets, FastTurn-Unified achieves the best overall turn-detection accuracy at lower latency than the Easy Turn and Cascaded baselines, while CTC greedy decoding remains the strongest ASR readout in the reported ablations.

## Problem & Motivation

Real-time full-duplex spoken dialogue, driven by recent AudioLLM advances, requires the agent to decide continuously when to speak, yield, or interrupt while the user is still talking, and in practical deployments this is exposed as a controllable turn-detection interface between speech processing and response generation. Existing approaches split into two unsatisfying camps: VAD-based methods that infer interruption timing from acoustic energy or activity patterns are lightweight and fast but primarily capture speech presence and lack semantic understanding, while ASR-based semantic methods understand words but depend on transcript quality and decoding latency, making them fragile under speech overlap and noise. FastTurn is motivated by this gap, aiming to preserve the latency advantage of early streaming textual conditioning while adding streaming acoustic cues so that turn decisions stay accurate when lexical evidence is ambiguous and prosodic cues are critical, such as echoes, overlaps, backchannels, and mid-utterance pauses.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **FastTurn-Cascaded streaming CTC-to-LLM decisions:** FastTurn-Cascaded introduces a CTC branch with greedy decoding for fast streaming transcription, formatted as a CTC prompt and fed into the LLM (Qwen3-0.6B) for turn prediction with minimal decoding overhead. This gives low-latency semantic decisions, but because the LLM input is dominated by the CTC transcript, predictions remain sensitive to CTC errors, especially under overlap and noise.

2. **FastTurn-Semantic acoustic-augmented reasoning:** FastTurn-Semantic extends the cascaded design by extracting high-level acoustic representations from a Conformer encoder and projecting them together with the CTC prompt into the LLM input space through an LLM adapter, so the LLM reasons jointly over text and acoustics. This mitigates CTC errors while preserving the latency advantage of early textual conditioning, improving robustness in noisy and overlapping conditions.

3. **FastTurn-Unified acoustic-semantic fusion with MLP detector:** FastTurn-Unified fuses fine-grained streaming acoustic cues with LLM-conditioned semantic modeling by processing intermediate Conformer encoder hidden states through an acoustic adapter, combining them with the LLM hidden states, and forwarding the result to a multi-layer perceptron turn detector that predicts whether the current segment is a complete turn. This deep fusion is what lets the framework resolve lexically ambiguous cases where prosody carries the decision.

4. **Four-stage training with prompt dropout:** the framework is trained by semantic pretraining of the Conformer encoder plus CTC branch on ASR data and LLM fine-tuning on text-only data with turn state as a special token, followed by modality alignment of the LLM adapter under an ASR objective, joint training of LLM and adapter on acoustic embeddings plus CTC prompt with prompt dropout (p < 0.5) to prevent overfitting to the CTC branch, and final modality fusion training of the acoustic adapter and turn detector. Only adapter parameters are updated in the alignment phase while other components stay frozen, establishing speech-text alignment before full fusion of prosodic and semantic cues.

5. **Large-scale data construction and four-state evaluation:** ASR training spans over 30,000 hours of Chinese and English speech across AISHELL-1, AISHELL-2, WenetSpeech, LibriSpeech, GigaSpeech, and MLS, while turn-detection training augments the Easy Turn set with internal conversational data and synthetic corpora whose dialogue texts come from Qwen3-32B and DeepSeek-V3 synthesized by IndexTTS2, with complete and incomplete states created by forced alignment and random truncation with linguistic-incompleteness filtering. Evaluation uses a dedicated FastTurn test set combining real-world Complete (14,709 samples, 9.64 h), Incomplete (3,643, 2.15 h), and Backchannel (3,080, 0.42 h) segments with 1,000 synthesized Wait samples (0.71 h), scored with Accuracy, Miss Rate, and False Alarm Rate.

## Key Findings

Models relying solely on semantic information, such as Paraformer+Ten Turn and FastTurn-Cascaded, perform poorly on Complete and Incomplete categories with low accuracy and high miss and false-alarm rates, while models that better integrate semantic and acoustic information, such as Easy Turn and FastTurn-Unified, perform clearly better, and FastTurn-Unified achieves the best performance across all four categories (Complete 81.64% accuracy, Incomplete 81.01%, Backchannel 93.93%, Wait 98.75%). The ablation shows a steady climb from Cascaded to Semantic to Unified as transcript dependence falls and acoustic fusion deepens, confirming the value of combining semantic and acoustic features. Across test sets, Smart Turn wins only on its own simplified two-class complete/incomplete set due to data and label mismatch, FastTurn-Cascaded is strong on the clean 800-sample noise-free Easy Turn set that rewards pure semantic cues, and the echo-heavy, acoustically ambiguous FastTurn set proves hardest; on latency, Smart Turn is cheapest but collapses in complex scenarios, while FastTurn-Unified delivers lower latency than both Easy Turn and FastTurn-Cascaded at similar or better accuracy (around 120 ms on the FastTurn set versus 297 ms for Easy Turn). The English subset meets expectations but does not surpass Paraformer+Ten Turn, attributed to limited English dialogue data and optimization, and on ASR, CTC greedy decoding gives the best error rates (7.06% WER LibriClean, 9.52% TestNet, 2.33% CER AISHELL-1), with LLM autoregressive decoding improving from a 2-layer MLP adapter to 2-layer and 4-layer Transformer adapters but still showing a moderate gap under frozen-backbone alignment.

## Suggestions & Future Directions

The authors propose future work on optimizing model performance and extending to more dynamic conversational scenarios, alongside releasing the comprehensive FastTurn test set to promote research on turn-taking, interruption modeling, and speech overlap in natural dialogues. Natural follow-ups from the reported limits are to close the English gap with more English dialogue data and targeted optimization, to harden robustness further on echo, overlap, and background-noise conditions that still challenge lexical-only models, and to quantify the accuracy–latency trade-off more finely across deployment-relevant operating points. Deeper ablations of adapter depth, fusion placement, and prompt-dropout schedules could also clarify how much of the gain comes from streaming acoustic detail versus semantic conditioning.
