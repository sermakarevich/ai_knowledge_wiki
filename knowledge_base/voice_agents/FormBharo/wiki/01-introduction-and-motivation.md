# FormBharo: Designing and Evaluating a Voice Agent — Introduction and Motivation
**In one sentence:** FormBharo ("fill the form" in Hindi) is a hybrid LLM plus rule-based voice agent, piloted with ARMMAN to enroll low-income Hindi-speaking mothers in maternal care over a phone call, with a new 3,760-test benchmark showing end-to-end evaluation is required because speech errors cut form completion by up to ~41 points and turn-level accuracy does not predict completion.
## Key points
- In India almost every social benefit starts with a form, yet more than half the women in the lowest wealth quintile cannot read at all (International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS) and ICF 2021), so enrollment falls to frontline health workers signing up beneficiaries one conversation at a time.
- Documentation already consumes much of frontline workers' day, with dozens of overlapping reporting systems requiring the same data to be re-entered across apps and paper registers, capping enrollment at worker capacity rather than need.
- FormBharo fills a structured form over a phone call under tight latency and cost budgets by pairing LLMs with deterministic, rule-based validation and flow control: STT transcribes speech, LLM EXTRACT extracts form values, a rule-based layer validates/updates form state/skips inactive branches/selects the next question, REPLY phrases it, and TTS speaks it back.
- It is being piloted with ARMMAN, an NGO running large-scale maternal and child mobile-health programs in India, to enroll low-income, Hindi-speaking mothers in antenatal and postnatal care — to the authors' knowledge the first conversational voice agent piloted to fill an enrollment form for this population.
- The authors openly release FormVoiceAgentBench: human-recorded Hindi audio with 3,760 multi-turn conversation tests across 960 simulated calls, evaluating transcription, extraction, reply generation, and end-to-end form completion under real acoustic variations.
- Form completion drops by up to ~41 percentage points when LLMs receive error-prone real-speech transcripts instead of reference transcripts; rule-based controls recover many turn-level extraction errors, helping smaller, cheaper models match or surpass frontier models on form completion.
- Component-level performance does not predict end-to-end performance: GPT-5.5 leads turn-level extraction accuracy on reference transcripts (99.8%) but ranks lower on form completion, because errors both propagate and cancel across the pipeline — so model choice emerges only through end-to-end evaluation, balanced via Pareto-based weighted-sum scalarization across accuracy, cost, and latency.
- Precedent and difficulty: Kilkari's outbound prerecorded-call service reached more than 10 million subscribers and delivered ~1.2 million calls per day, and Sherwani et al. (2009) found a carefully designed speech interface achieved significantly higher task-completion rates than touch-tone among low-literate and literate users; form-filling calls compound noise, telephone-channel distortion, regional accents, speaking rates, mispronunciations, disfluencies, Hindi-English code-mixing, multi-verbalization structured values (phone numbers, dates), hesitations, mid-answer self-corrections, and branching on earlier answers.

- Paper: "FormBharo: Designing and Evaluating a Voice Agent for Conversational Form Filling in Rural India" by Aman Dalmia, Sanskriti Midha, Jigar Doshi (Artpark, Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru), arXiv:2608.06027v1 [cs.CL] 6 Aug 2026.
- Verbatim problem statement: "In India, almost every social benefit starts with a form, yet the people who need these benefits most are often unable to read or write. Reaching them requires a spoken conversation."
- Verbatim system claim: "We built FormBharo ("fill the form" in Hindi), a voice agent that fills a structured form over a phone call under tight latency and cost budgets by pairing Large Language Models (LLMs) with deterministic, rule-based validation and flow control."
- Pilot: with ARMMAN, enrolling low-income, Hindi-speaking mothers in antenatal and postnatal care; claimed first conversational voice agent piloted for this population/form-filling use.
- Benchmark: FormVoiceAgentBench pairs human-recorded Hindi audio with 3,760 multi-turn conversation tests across 960 simulated calls, covering transcription, extraction, reply generation, and end-to-end form completion under real acoustic variations.
- Key quantitative findings stated in Abstract: (1) up to ~41 percentage-point drop in form completion on real-speech vs. reference transcripts; (2) rule-based controls recover many turn-level extraction errors so smaller/cheaper models match or surpass frontier models; (3) GPT-5.5 leads turn-level extraction on reference transcripts (99.8%) but ranks lower on form completion; (4) no single model is best on accuracy, cost, and latency simultaneously, hence Pareto-based weighted-sum scalarization for the deployable configuration.
## 1 Introduction (partial)
**Covers:** §1 Introduction start (benefit access, literacy barrier, worker burden, voice precedent, challenges, FormBharo overview)
- Access premise: nearly every social benefit in India runs through a form a citizen must complete to enroll (Indus Action 2026), but the highest-need people are disproportionately low-income and less literate.
- Worker burden: enrollment falls to frontline health workers one conversation at a time; documentation consumes much of their day with dozens of overlapping reporting systems and re-entry across apps and paper registers, capping enrollment at worker capacity; these records are the administrator's source of truth for allocating resources, so enrollment errors and delays slow system response.
- Voice reach precedent: Kilkari reached more than 10 million subscribers (LeFevre et al. 2022) and delivered ~1.2 million calls per day (Bashingwa et al. 2021); early IVR relied on touch-tone input preferred over low-quality speech recognition (Patel et al. 2010), but Sherwani et al. (2009) showed a carefully designed speech interface beat touch-tone on task completion for both low-literate and literate users; LLM-powered voice agents now make real-time spoken interaction increasingly practical.
- Target interaction, verbatim: "A mother can call a number and enroll by speaking naturally with an agent in her own words and language, with nothing to read, type, or install."
- Stated hard problems: cooperative-speaker-in-quiet-room assumption breaks with background noise, telephone-channel distortion, underrepresented regional accents, unusual speaking rates, mispronunciations, disfluencies, Hindi-English code-mixing, structured values (phone numbers, dates) spoken many ways but resolving to one value, caller hesitation and mid-answer self-correction, and branching where a mis-captured field sends the agent down the wrong path.
- Hybrid framing, verbatim: "We present FormBharo, a hybrid voice agent that fills a structured form over a phone call under tight latency and cost constraints (Figure 1). A speech-to-text (STT) model transcribes the caller's speech. An LLM, EXTRACT, extracts the relevant form values. A rule-based layer then validates" (chunk ends mid-sentence here).
