# ECHO: A Matched-Contrast Benchmark for Context-Sensitive Turn-Taking in Full-Duplex Dialogue

**Paper:** [ECHO: A Matched-Contrast Benchmark for Context-Sensitive Turn-Taking in Full-Duplex Dialogue (Zhao et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17360)

## Human Readable TL;DR
Imagine you are telling a story and someone says "uh-huh" — you keep talking — but if they say "wait, stop, that's wrong," you yield the floor, even though both moments involve someone talking over you. ECHO is an exam that tests whether voice AIs can tell those two situations apart when the overlapping words sound exactly the same and only the earlier conversation differs, like hearing "this pen ran out of ink again" as friendly agreement in one story but as a real complaint interrupting a form-filling task in another. It does this by building matched pairs of Chinese dialogues that share the identical interrupting sentence but require opposite actions — KEEP talking versus YIELD — so an AI that always yields or always ignores overlaps cannot cheat its way to a good score. The headline result is that most tested voice systems are chronic over-yielders, politely stopping for mere backchannels, and this flaw stays hidden unless you grade both halves of each pair together.

## TL;DR
ECHO (Evaluating Context-conditioned Handling of Overlaps) is a paired diagnostic benchmark for Chinese full-duplex turn-taking that holds the overlapping insertion utterance lexically fixed while rewriting the preceding multi-turn dialogue context to flip its interactional role between interruption (YIELD), backchannel (KEEP), and off-talk (KEEP). Its central claim is that existing benchmarks, which evaluate independently occurring events with lexically closed non-interruptive vocabularies, reward constant-action preferences and let lexical form alone separate the classes, so evaluation must instead require joint correctness across linked pairs via pair accuracy and related pair-level metrics. Construction uses an insertion-text-matched contrastive design inspired by minimal-pair testing, with Claude-3.5-Sonnet rewriting histories under role definitions, DeepSeek-V4-Pro screening for consistency, manual review, and IndexTTS2-based dual-channel synthesis with role-dependent overlap rendering, reaching 97.45% human-evaluation accuracy on retained instances. Experiments on four speech systems find three exhibit a pronounced Yield bias concealed by interruption-only accuracy, with the bias unexplained by limited context and exposed only by class-conditioned Keep rates and pair-level metrics.

## Problem & Motivation
Full-duplex voice systems must make a harder decision than merely detecting that overlapping speech occurred: they must decide whether the overlap warrants yielding the floor to a genuine user interruption or keeping the floor through backchannels, self-talk, or third-party speech. Treating every overlap as an interruption produces fragmented, oversensitive interaction, while ignoring real floor claims leaves users unable to correct or redirect the assistant. Existing benchmarks fall short in two ways: they sample independently occurring events where the inserted utterance and its preceding dialogue vary together, so a system with a fixed action preference can score well on interruption-only tests, and their non-interruptive samples are lexically closed — for example, 81.8% of SID-Bench non-interruptive instances consist of the fifteen most frequent characters, with disjoint backchannel and turn-taking vocabularies — making the classes separable by word form alone. The motivating example makes this concrete: "This pen has run out of ink again" is an affiliative completion when the assistant is already complaining about the pen, but a genuine interruption when a faulty pen blocks a form the user was asked to fill in, so lexical content alone cannot determine the correct action. ECHO therefore asks whether a system can produce the correct action across context-rewritten instances sharing exactly the same insertion text.

## Main Original Ideas
1. **Insertion-text-matched contrastive benchmark design:** the core idea is to fix the overlapping utterance transcript (u_i^(a) = u_i^(b) = u_i) while rewriting the preceding dialogue context (c_i^(a) vs c_i^(b)) so the labels differ (y_i^(a) ≠ y_i^(b)), with each linked instance represented as (c_i^(k), u_i, y_i^(k)) and groups containing two or all three role variants, inspired by minimal-pair testing.
2. **Pair-level metrics requiring joint correctness:** ECHO pairs the design with pair accuracy and related metrics (PASR/PASRI, PRSR, PKC) that demand correct decisions on both members of a linked pair, assigning no credit to constant-action policies and exposing gaps where high sample-level accuracy coexists with poor cross-context consistency.
3. **Role-defined generation and validation pipeline:** three interaction roles defined by intended addressee and target floor action are enforced through a pipeline in which Claude-3.5-Sonnet rewrites the dialogue history up to and including the overlapped assistant utterance, DeepSeek-V4-Pro screens candidates for history–insertion–role consistency, and manual review discards incompatible examples, yielding 97.45% human-evaluation accuracy.
4. **Controlled speech synthesis with role-dependent overlap rendering:** all turns are independently synthesized with IndexTTS2 and rendered as speaker-separated dual-channel audio under a uniform pipeline of speaker-prompt RMS normalization, bounded speaking-rate normalization, forced-alignment-based overlap placement, and role-dependent rendering, where backchannel and off-talk are overlaid without modifying the assistant track while interruptions receive onset emphasis plus a fade to silence, with the faded waveform excluded from model inputs so floor release cannot leak the label.

## Key Findings
Evaluated systems reveal a widespread action-level Yield bias that single-number interruption accuracy conceals: three of the four tested speech systems respond to a substantial share of interruptions yet yield to most backchannels. Concretely, Lychee-FD, despite receiving up to five preceding dialogue turns plus the complete planned assistant utterance, keeps the floor on only 12.02% of backchannels and 8.20% of off-talk with 4.00% PASRI−B, while a text-conditioned reference observing the same dialogue but only the already-spoken assistant prefix reaches 86.34% on backchannels, showing the limiting factor is how floor decisions use available context rather than how much context is available. Among speech systems only MiniCPM-o 4.5 is balanced at 63.39%/65.57% with 54.00% PASRI−B. Pair-level metrics further expose what sample accuracy hides: the text reference reaches 82.33% macro accuracy in the three-way role space but only 66.00% PRSR, and 82.88% overall accuracy in the binary space but only 52.00% PASRI−O, while the Yield-biased systems reach at most 35.70% overall accuracy, far below the 82.33% ambiguity bound the reference establishes. The low off-talk Keep rate is consistent with side-talk difficulty reported in the original Lychee-FD study, but the comparably low backchannel rate shows over-yielding extends to system-directed feedback that never claims the floor.

## Suggestions & Future Directions
The authors conclude that reporting class-conditioned Keep rates alongside interruption accuracy is necessary for meaningful turn-taking evaluation, and they release the ECHO audio, observable dialogue text, labels, and pair metadata to support that practice. The stated limitations point directly to follow-up work: because synthesis trades ecological validity for control, pairing ECHO with real-speech benchmarks would restore naturalness, and strict acoustic control would require a waveform-reuse condition plus a gain-free interruption ablation since interruption waveforms currently receive label-dependent RMS scaling and onset emphasis and speaker-reference audio may differ across pairs. Off-talk should remain a supporting rather than primary diagnostic because its intended addressee is not always explicit in scenario labels, and cross-system comparisons should be framed as behavioral audits under supported interfaces rather than modality-matched rankings given differences in modality, streaming latency, and native output space. Finally, the balanced diagnostic distribution must not be mistaken for naturally occurring event prevalence, so future evaluations should combine ECHO-style paired diagnostics with prevalence-weighted deployment measurements.
