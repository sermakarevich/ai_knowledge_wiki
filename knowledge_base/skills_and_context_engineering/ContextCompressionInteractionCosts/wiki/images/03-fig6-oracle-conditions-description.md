**Figure 6** decomposes the cost of context compression under oracle (full‑state‑restoration) conditions into two complementary views: retrieval/execution quality (panel a) and termination/tool‑budget behavior under increasing compression (panel b).

*Panel (a) — oracle state restoration.* A dual‑axis chart over five protocol conditions (Full, Sliding, +R*, +D*, Full+R*). The left axis plots completion (%) as blue bars, all of which sit in a fairly narrow band (roughly two‑thirds to just under 90%); the right axis plots tool calls as a red line. The trend is that the *Sliding* condition is the outlier: it has the lowest completion bar but the highest tool‑call count, whereas the oracle‑restoration variants (+R*, +D*, Full+R*) recover completion to the top of the band while the tool‑call line drops to its lowest levels. In short, restoring oracle state keeps completion high and tames the accumulated tool‑call overhead that the Sliding baseline incurs.

*Panel (b) — termination & tool budget (High).* A stacked‑bar/line chart indexed by compression ratio (1×, 1.7×, 2.5×, 5×, 10%). The bars split each condition into *all_complete* (green) versus *max_turns* (red) termination, and a blue line tracks the share of runs finishing *within budget*. At low compression (≤2.5×) most runs complete (large green share) and the within‑budget line is near 100%; as compression climbs to 5× and 10× the green share shrinks sharply, the red (turn‑exhausted) share dominates, and the within‑budget line collapses to ~0. Thus higher compression pushes runs past the fixed turn horizon and out of the tool budget.

**Takeaway.** Completion is a blunt metric: it stays high across most oracle conditions but falls at strong compression, while the tool‑budget/turn‑exhaustion curve exposes the real cost — reacquisition overhead grows with compression until it exhausts the run's horizon and violates a reference execution budget that the full‑context agent never approaches. Termination is governed by the fixed turn limit, with the within‑budget curve serving as an auxiliary diagnostic of accumulated interaction cost.
