**Figure 3** presents a two‑panel diagnostic of how a "Full" setting compares with a 5×‑compressed setting across six model–regime cells (DeepSeek‑H/L, Qwen‑H/L, GPT‑5.5‑H/L), arguing that the effect lives in a 2‑D space rather than on a single success axis.

**Panel (a) – "interaction cost vs completion (not the same axis)."** This is a qualitative phase map (no regression fitted). The x‑axis is the change in retrieval cost, *Δ retrieval* (extra tool calls, Full − 5×), spanning roughly 0–60; the y‑axis is the change in completion, *Δ completion* (percentage points, Full − 5×), spanning roughly −30 to +20. Each point is one cell, with 95% bootstrap CIs on both axes; marker fill encodes whether the retrieval difference survives Holm correction and border thickness encodes High‑ vs Low‑IR regime. The six points scatter across the plane rather than along a diagonal: DeepSeek‑High sits at high retrieval growth with a completion *loss* (upper‑left region), GPT‑5.5‑High and the two Low cells show high retrieval growth with roughly flat completion, and Qwen shows little retrieval growth with a mild completion drop. Notably, the vertical (completion) error bars are wide and all cross zero — completion changes are non‑significant in every cell — while most horizontal (retrieval) bars clear zero.

**Panel (b) – "tool decomposition: Full vs 5×."** A grouped bar chart of total tool calls per cell (y‑axis, 0 to ~80+), with each group showing Full vs 5× side by side. Bars are stacked into a light lower segment (retrieval calls) and a small dark upper segment (execution calls). Across all six cells the 5× bars are shorter, and the reduction is concentrated in the light retrieval segment; the dark execution segment stays small and nearly constant between Full and 5×.

**Takeaway.** Compression sensitivity is two‑dimensional: retrieval/interaction cost and task completion are related but *not* interchangeable outcomes (cost can rise while completion is unchanged, or fall while completion drops slightly). The extra tool calls incurred by the Full setting are almost entirely *retrieval* calls, with execution cost essentially flat — so the cost difference is driven by how much the system retrieves, not by how much it executes.
