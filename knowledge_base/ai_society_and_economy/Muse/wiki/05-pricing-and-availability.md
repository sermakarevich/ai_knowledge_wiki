# Pricing and Availability
**In one sentence:** Muse started in the United States (US) only, is free for most needs, and offers $20 per month and $100 per month paid plans for heavier use.
## Key points
- At launch Muse is available only in the United States (US), not worldwide.
- Muse works on Apple iOS, Google Android, the web at muse.ai, and inside WhatsApp.
- Support for Meta's Artificial Intelligence (AI) glasses is coming soon, not yet available.
- Meta offers a free tier, and chief AI officer Alexandr Wang said the vast majority of users should be able to do what they need within the free tier.
- Meta offers two paid plans at $20 per month and $100 per month for heavier use and power users.
- There is no advertising inside Muse, but Meta is exploring commerce opportunities that could bring future revenue.
- Reuters places Muse in the context of AI infrastructure investments forecast to exceed $130 billion this year and Chief Executive Officer (CEO) Mark Zuckerberg's plan for personal superintelligence, described in his 6,500-word manifesto.
## Where you can use it
Muse is rolling out in the US only at first. This means people outside the US do not get it at launch.

You can talk to Muse like messaging another person. The sources list these access points: Apple iOS, Google Android, the web at muse.ai through the dedicated Muse app, and directly in WhatsApp.

Support for Meta's AI glasses is coming soon. The official announcement says coming soon to AI glasses, Axios says support coming soon for Meta's AI glasses, and Reuters says smart glasses support soon.
## What it costs
Muse is free for most of what people need. Meta also offers two subscription plans for people who want to do more.

The two paid plans cost $20 per month and $100 per month. Reuters describes them as a free basic version plus $20 per month and $100 per month subscriptions for heavier usage.

In simple words: light everyday use is free, heavy use can be paid.
## Why paid plans exist
Alexandr Wang, Meta's chief AI officer, said the paid plans help Meta pay for computer power. His words to Axios were: For the vast majority of users, they should be able to do what they need to within the free tier, but for real power users, you know, those subscription tiers help us cover the compute costs.

Compute costs here means the cost of the computers and electricity needed to run the Artificial Intelligence (AI) agent, especially when it keeps working in the background.
## Ads and future money plans
There is no advertising within Muse. Advertising means paid ads shown to users.

Wang said the company is exploring commerce opportunities that could generate additional revenue. Commerce opportunities means future ways to earn money when people buy things through the agent.

This fits the wider plan described by Reuters: Zuckerberg wants to offer personal superintelligence to the billions of people who use Meta's services daily, and this is part of Meta's attempt to earn money beyond advertising and build a business from its AI infrastructure investments.
## Big cost context
Reuters says Meta's AI infrastructure investments are forecast to exceed $130 billion this year. Infrastructure here means the large computer systems and data centers needed to build and run AI.

Axios says Muse was long in development and was touted as a key next step by CEO Mark Zuckerberg in his recent 6,500-word manifesto. Wang also described Muse as an early but big step toward Meta's broader goal of personal superintelligence, meaning an AI helper built for each person to reach goals and pursue interests.
