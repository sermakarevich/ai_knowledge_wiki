# Muse Announcement and Capabilities
**In one sentence:** Meta announced Muse on September 8, 2026 as a personal AI (Artificial Intelligence, software that can learn and do tasks) agent that carries out tasks across a person's apps instead of only answering questions.
## Key points
- Meta announced Muse on September 8, 2026, and Reuters reports it rolled out on that Tuesday despite internal concerns about its handling of sensitive personal data.
- Meta says Muse does not just answer questions but actually does the work, such as sending an email, booking travel, filling out forms, and negotiating on a person's behalf.
- Reuters reports Muse can autonomously send emails, sell a car, and book travel, and can connect to a person's apps across email, calendar, payments, health, shopping, and the smart home.

- Axios reports Muse works through a chat interface like a text thread, and Reuters reports it is reached through a dedicated Muse app or WhatsApp, while Axios adds that users can name their agent and create an avatar.

## Announced on September 8, 2026
Meta announced Muse on September 8, 2026. Axios reports Meta announced it on that Tuesday as a personal AI agent built on the latest generation of models developed under chief AI officer Alexandr Wang. Reuters reports Meta rolled it out on Tuesday, September 8, and describes it as a long-touted AI assistant that can autonomously send emails, sell a car, and book travel on a person's behalf, despite internal concerns that the technology mismanages its access to sensitive personal data.
## Does work, not just answers
Meta says Muse is a personal AI agent that does not just answer questions but actually does the work. Meta says people just tell Muse what needs to get done and it takes action. Meta says it helps people stay on top of things, takes tasks and projects off their plate, and turns long-term goals into action plans. Meta says once a person shares a goal, Muse helps develop a personalized plan, coordinates time and resources, and then advances the work on its own.
## What the agent can do
Meta says Muse can handle tasks like sending an email or booking travel, and can also take on big long-term goals. Meta says it can open a browser, fill out forms, and negotiate on the person's behalf. Meta says it gets better results with less effort, giving examples such as selling a car for more, lowering a bill, or adjusting a training plan as the rest of someone's life shifts. Reuters reports it can autonomously send emails, sell a car, and book travel. For payments, Meta says Muse can check out with Link built by Stripe, and says it is the first AI agent covered by Link's purchase protections, which include free coverage for damaged or lost items, price drops, no-fee returns, and a return guarantee on eligible purchases. Meta says Shop Pay is coming soon as another way to pay.
## Connected apps across daily life
Reuters reports Muse is designed to access a person's apps across categories like email, calendar, payments, health, shopping, and the smart home. Meta says Muse works across the apps a person uses daily, learning from conversations and getting sharper along the way. Meta says each person stays in control, decides how much access Muse gets, and chooses which apps it connects to. Reuters likewise reports people choose which apps it connects to and can revoke access at any time.
## Background, long-running work
Meta says that for tasks that take more time, Muse keeps working after people close the app, and comes back when something changes or when it needs approval, like before it sends an email or makes a purchase. Axios describes Muse as designed to be more proactive and longer-running than typical chatbots. Reuters reports each Muse agent runs on its own virtual machine, a cloud-based emulation of a personal computer, which lets it keep carrying out requests in the background even when the person is not actively using it. See also [[02-secure-vm-architecture|Secure VM Architecture]].
## Memory and proactive suggestions
Meta says Muse remembers what matters to a person, so it can make suggestions without being asked and act on details the person only mentioned once. Meta gives examples: turning a recipe video the person saved on Instagram into a grocery list, suggesting a menu for a dinner party, and remembering friends' dietary restrictions before sending the invites. Axios notes the design goal of being more proactive than a typical chatbot.
## Muse Spark model
Meta says Muse is powered by Muse Spark, its most capable model to date, built for real-world agent work. Axios reports the agent is built on the latest generation of models developed under chief AI officer Alexandr Wang, and notes the product was long in development and was presented by CEO Mark Zuckerberg as a key next step in his recent 6,500-word manifesto.
## OpenClaw lineage and internal codename Hatch
Reuters reports Muse is modeled on the open-source AI agent OpenClaw. Reuters reports the Muse agent was known inside Meta under the codename Hatch. As context on agent risks, Axios reports that Meta's own Summer Yue went viral after posting on X about an OpenClaw agent deleting files from her Mac after she gave it access to her email.
## Personal superintelligence vision
Meta says personal superintelligence will be one of the most transformative technologies of a lifetime, and calls Muse a first step: an agent that takes on more of the work so people can focus on what matters to them. Axios reports Wang described Muse as an early but big step toward Meta's broader goal of personal superintelligence, quoting him as saying the future vision is to develop personal superintelligence that helps people accomplish goals, pursue passions, and build things they never would have built without the technology. Reuters reports the Muse agent is the centerpiece of CEO Mark Zuckerberg's plan to offer personal superintelligence to the billions of people who use Meta's services daily.
## How you talk to it
Meta says talking to Muse works just like messaging another person, in the Muse app or directly in WhatsApp. Meta says it was built to work for billions of people worldwide with no learning curve, so anyone can use it out of the box with no technical experience required. Axios reports Muse exists in a chat interface similar to a text thread. Reuters reports it is available through a dedicated Muse app or WhatsApp. Meta says it is rolling out in the US on iOS, Android, and muse.ai, with support for AI glasses coming soon. Axios likewise reports it is initially available in the US on iOS, Android, and the web, with support coming soon for Meta's AI glasses.
## Customizable name and avatar
Axios reports users can name their agent, create an avatar, and customize how it communicates.
## Walkthrough: selling a car for more
Meta uses selling a car as a concrete example of getting a better result with less effort. In plain steps, the person tells Muse the goal in normal chat language, Muse builds a personal plan, then it opens its own browser, fills out forms, and negotiates on the person's behalf. For slow tasks Muse keeps working after the app is closed, then returns when something changes or when it needs approval, for example before it sends a final email or makes a purchase. Reuters confirms the same headline capability, saying Muse can autonomously send emails, sell a car, and book travel on a person's behalf.
## Walkthrough: dinner-party invites from a saved video
Meta gives a dinner-party example that shows memory plus unprompted help. The person once saves a recipe video on Instagram. Later Muse can turn that saved video into a grocery list, suggest a menu for the dinner party, and remember friends' dietary restrictions before it sends the invites. Dietary restrictions here means foods friends cannot or will not eat. The point Meta stresses is that the person mentioned those details only once, and Muse still acts on them without being asked again. Approval still applies: Meta says Muse comes back for approval before sensitive actions such as sending an email.
## Walkthrough: honeymoon planning help praised in testing
Reuters reports one internal tester praised Muse as the third participant on a three-week honeymoon in Indonesia, for arranging travel plans and ground transport. Ground transport here means cars, vans, or other local travel on the ground. This example matters because it shows the same long-running pattern: a big goal is shared once, Muse coordinates time and resources, and it keeps advancing the work in the background. It is a tester report quoted by Reuters, not a Meta promise, and Reuters pairs it with mixed results from other testers. See [[06-press-critique|Press Critique]] for the full mixed picture.

## Lowering a bill and adjusting a training plan
Meta lists two more better-results examples besides the car sale.

A training plan here means an exercise schedule.
As life shifts means when work, travel, or family time changes.
Both examples show long-term goals that need many small steps over time.
Muse advances that work on its own and returns for approval before sensitive actions.
