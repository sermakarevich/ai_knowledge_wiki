# Geopolitics, people and society, and risks AI
**In one sentence:** AI is becoming a source of geopolitical power led by the US in notable models (55% vs 23% for China) amid chip and mineral controls, reshaping how people think and relate (personal queries 73% of consumer ChatGPT use in June 2025), creating system-wide risks distinct from bottlenecks, with feedback loops that can accelerate or slow change.
## Key points
- Over half of notable AI models released since 2022 were developed in the United States (55%), compared with 23% in China (Epoch AI; McKinsey Global Institute analysis).
- The US and China compete with different strengths — the US may lead in frontier models while China may be ahead in manufacturing deployment — and use policy leverage: US restrictions on advanced-chip exports vs Chinese controls on critical-mineral exports.
- In June 2025, personal queries made up nearly three-quarters (73%) of consumer ChatGPT interactions, compared with about half in June 2024 (Aaron Chatterji et al., NBER working paper 34255, September 2025).
- AI could change how people think, learn, work, and relate, spurring creativity and enjoyment but also contributing to cognitive overload and burnout via work requiring more oversight, judgment, and context-switching.
- People's experiences with AI shape their views of the technology, influencing how businesses use it and governments regulate it.
- In the report's framework, risks are potential harms, distinct from bottlenecks that slow AI development and adoption; they arise from resources AI requires, models themselves, how people and organizations use them, and broader changes from community pressure to errors, cyberattacks, and fraud.
- The system does not move in one direction: economic gains can fund more R&D and infrastructure (accelerating progress), while people's AI experiences can change business behavior and government policy (speeding or slowing adoption).

## Geopolitics: 55% vs 23%
**Covers:** p. 24 exhibit "Geopolitics — 55% vs 23%" + "What to know / Metrics to watch"

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Notable AI models released since 2022 developed in the United States | Over half — 55% |
| Notable AI models released since 2022 developed in China | 23% |
| Source | Epoch AI; McKinsey Global Institute analysis |

What to know: "The United States and China are competing for an AI advantage, each with different strengths. Both countries are also using policy tools to gain leverage, from US restrictions on advanced-chip exports to Chinese controls on critical-mineral exports, with consequences for global trade, alliances, and national security. There is no single measure of AI leadership—the US may lead in frontier models, while China may be ahead in manufacturing deployment—but the technology is becoming an important source of economic and military power."

Metrics to watch: Notable AI model performance by country; data center capacity by country or region; cross-border model downloads and usage; AI hardware trade flows; and trade restrictions on chips, minerals, and other AI-related technologies.

What to know: "AI could change how people think, learn, work, and relate to one another, with consequences for relationships, mental well-being, and how people spend their time. It could spur creativity and enjoyment, but work that requires more oversight, judgment, and context-switching could also contribute to cognitive overload and burnout." Footnote 5: "People & Organization Blog, 'Five principles for designing brain-powered organizations,' blog post by Jacqueline Brassey, Julie Goran, Alexis Krivkovich, and Kate Smaje, McKinsey, July 13, 2026." "These experiences will shape people's views of the technology, influencing how businesses use it and governments regulate it."

Metrics to watch: AI use for health, professional, relationship, and financial guidance; employee burnout and well-being measures; and surveys of public trust, concern, and excitement about AI.

## Risks
**Covers:** p. 24 exhibit "Risks" + "What to know / Metrics to watch"

What to know: "In our framework, risks are potential harms, distinct from bottlenecks that can slow AI's development and adoption. They can arise from the resources AI requires, the models themselves, how people and organizations use them, and the broader changes AI produces—from pressure on communities and the environment to model errors, cyberattacks, and fraud."

Metrics to watch: Model errors and safety failures; cyberattacks and fraud involving AI; and environmental and community impacts.

## Feedback across the system
**Covers:** p. 24 exhibit "Feedback across the system" + "What to know"

What to know: "The system does not move in one direction. Economic gains can fund more R&D and infrastructure, accelerating progress. At the same time, people's experiences with AI can change business behavior and government policy, which can speed or slow adoption."

Framing: "Changes in one part of the AI system can feed back into others, amplifying or slowing change."
