# Time saved, $756B investment scale, and competing beyond models
**In one sentence:** AI is saving workers measurable hours, drawing massive investment into chips-to-power capacity whose payoff depends on returns, and pushing competitive advantage from models themselves toward data, relationships, and integration as about one-third of firms build rather than buy software.
## Key points
- Nearly one-third of US workers who used AI at work in the previous week said it saved them three or more hours; an additional 31% reported saving one to two hours.
- How freed-up time is used — more paid work, learning, entrepreneurship, leisure, or other activities — will help determine AI's effects on economic growth and well-being.
- Combined capital investment and R&D spending by seven leading AI-related companies was $756B in 2025, twice as much as in 2022.
- Funding comes from public markets, private credit, governments, and venture capital, flowing toward parts of the AI system needing capacity: chips, data centers, and electricity generation.
- The McKinsey Global Institute-classified AI foundation added about $500 billion in revenue from 2022 to 2025, and delivered a return on invested capital about 1.5 times that of other industries between 2022 and 2024.
- About one-third of survey respondents report their organization used agentic coding tools to build its own software instead of purchasing it.
- Best AI models alone may not confer a lasting edge because the technology is widely available and leaders may not stay ahead; firms may compete on proprietary data, customer relationships, and integration effectiveness.
- AI may reshape competition by changing company-customer-supplier relationships, reducing some scale advantages, and requiring governance structures to manage risks as use expands.

## $756B — Combined investment and R&D by seven AI leaders
**Covers:** center panel of p. 23 key-data exhibit (McKinsey Global Institute analysis)

| Figure | Detail |
|---|---|
| $756B | Combined capital investment and R&D spending by seven leading AI-related companies in 2025, twice as much as in 2022 |
| ~$500B | Revenue added 2022–2025 by industries MGI classifies as the AI foundation |
| ~1.5x | Return on invested capital 2022–2024 in those arenas relative to other industries |

> "What to know: As AI investment has grown, so have its financing needs. Funding comes from a wide range of sources, including public markets, private credit, governments, and venture capital. Capital tends to flow toward parts of the AI system that need more capacity, financing everything from chips and data centers to electricity generation."

> "Whether these investments ultimately pay off will depend on the returns they generate."

The McKinsey Global Institute defines the "AI foundation" as semiconductors, cloud services, and AI software and services. The seven companies are Nvidia, Oracle, TSMC, Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon.

Metrics to watch: AI infrastructure investment as a share of GDP; venture, private-credit, and government investment in AI; revenue growth and return on invested capital in AI foundation industries; depreciation of AI chips by generation; and the share of AI investment going to the largest companies.

Source: McKinsey Global Institute analysis; see Kweilin Ellingrud, "The race takes off in the next big arenas of competition," McKinsey Global Institute, March 26, 2026.
## 1/3 — Build vs. buy and competing beyond models
**Covers:** right panel of p. 23 key-data exhibit (McKinsey "state of AI in 2026," August 25, 2026)

| Figure | Detail |
|---|---|
| ~1/3 | Share of survey respondents reporting their organization used agentic coding tools to build its own software instead of purchasing it |

> "What to know: Using the best AI models may not by itself give companies a lasting edge because the technology is widely available and leading models may not stay ahead for long. Companies may increasingly compete on proprietary data, customer relationships, and how effectively they integrate AI into their work. AI may also shift the competitive landscape by changing relationships among companies, customers, and suppliers, and by reducing some advantages of scale. As AI use expands, governance structures are needed to manage the risks."

Metrics to watch: Performance differences between AI leaders and laggards; new-business formation and average employment at entry; distribution of firms by enterprise size; shifts in gross value added and employment by industry; and governance, audit, and cybersecurity readiness across firms.

Source: Dan Tinkoff, Lieven Van der Veken, and Michael Chui, with Tara Balakrishnan, "The state of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI," McKinsey, August 25, 2026.
