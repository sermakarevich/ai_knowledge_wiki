# 47% trust, 89% vs 46% adoption, 2.5% productivity — and downstream economic exhibits
**In one sentence:** Institutions and public trust lag fast individual AI adoption (47% trust; 89% of organizations use AI but only 46% move beyond pilots), aggregate productivity gains remain modest so far (2.5% annual growth since late 2022), while task-level exhibits point to large future effects on innovation, demand, jobs, and time use.
## Key points
- Fewer than half of survey respondents (47%) trust companies that use AI to protect their personal data (Ipsos AI Monitor 2026).
- In 2026, 89% of organizations used AI in at least one business function, but of these only 46% had progressed beyond pilots (McKinsey "state of AI in 2026").
- Individual professional adoption is faster than organizational scaling: in early 2026 more than 80% of US physicians (81% of 1,692 surveyed, Jan 15–Feb 2, 2026) reported using AI professionally, more than twice the 38% share in 2023.
- Average annual US nonfarm labor productivity growth between late 2022 (ChatGPT launch) and the first half of 2026 was 2.5%, slightly above the 2.1% long-run average since 1947, but too soon to tell whether structural or normal volatility.
- AI has shortened drug-discovery timelines by as much as 80%, and pharmaceutical companies using AI could bring 1.5 times as many new drugs to market for the same cost.
- The report cites ~$30T in 2024 e-commerce sales as an example of the enormous new-demand market an earlier general-purpose technology (the internet) enabled.
- By 2035, 72% of US workers could see AI reinvent how they spend more than 15% of their working time; jobs are more likely to change than be replaced outright in the near future.
- Mechanism repeated across exhibits: institutions change more slowly and unevenly than physical infrastructure can be built and far more slowly than AI capabilities improve, while broad productivity gains require workflow redesign spreading across firms and industries.

## Institutional stance: 47% trust
**Covers:** pp. 21 exhibit "47% Less" + "What to know / Trajectory / Metrics to watch"

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Share trusting companies using AI to protect personal data | Less than half — 47% |
| Source | Ipsos AI Monitor 2026 |

What to know (verbatim substance): "Institutional stances are broad and often hard to quantify. They include regulations, liability rules, professional standards, company policies, social norms, and public attitudes toward AI. They can shape how the technology develops and spreads—and change in response to its effects."

Trajectory: "Institutions tend to change more slowly and unevenly than physical infrastructure can be built, and far more slowly than AI capabilities improve."

Metrics to watch: AI-related laws and regulations; company AI governance policies; professional standards for AI use; surveys of public trust in AI.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|

| Of those, progressed beyond pilots | 46% |

| Source (org figures) | Dan Tinkoff, Lieven Van der Veken, and Michael Chui, with Tara Balakrishnan, "The state of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI," McKinsey, August 25, 2026 |
| Source (physicians) | "2026 Physician Survey on Augmented Intelligence," American Medical Association, March 2026 |

What to know: "Individuals are adopting AI tools quickly, including in their professional lives... Organizations tend to move more slowly because they must integrate the technology into existing systems, redesign complex workflows, and sometimes overcome resistance to new ways of working."

Metrics to watch: share of firms piloting, scaling, and fully embedding AI; AI usage intensity by industry and business function; worker and consumer use; share of workflows redesigned around AI.

## Productivity growth: 2.5%
**Covers:** pp. 21 exhibit "2.5%" + "What to know / Metrics to watch"

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Average annual US nonfarm labor productivity growth, late 2022–H1 2026 | 2.5% |
| Very long-run average since 1947 | 2.1% per year |
| Assessment | Slightly above long-run average, but too soon to tell whether structural or normal volatility |
| Sources | US Bureau of Labor Statistics; McKinsey Global Institute analysis |

What to know: "AI has already made some individual tasks quicker and easier. Broader productivity gains require organizations to redesign workflows around the technology and for those changes to spread across businesses and industries."

Metrics to watch: total factor productivity growth; output per capita; output per hour worked; firm- and task-level studies of time use and output.

## Innovation: up to 80% faster discovery, 1.5x drugs
**Covers:** pp. 22 exhibit "Innovation — Up to 80%"

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Drug-discovery timeline shortening from AI | As much as 80% |
| New drugs per same cost at pharma companies using AI | 1.5 times as many |

What to know: "AI is already accelerating parts of scientific discovery, from identifying drug candidates to designing new materials. Over time, it could also help create entirely new products, services, and businesses, much as earlier general-purpose technologies have."

Metrics to watch: new products and services developed with AI; share of companies using AI in product and service development; patents granted; R&D productivity in AI-enabled fields such as pharmaceuticals and materials science.

## New demand: $30T e-commerce analogy
**Covers:** pp. 22 exhibit "New demand — $30T"

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Approximate value of e-commerce sales in 2024 | $30T — "an enormous market enabled by an earlier general-purpose technology, the internet" |
| Sources | UN Trade and Development, "E-commerce: Domestic and international sales" (dataset), last updated May 21, 2026; World Bank, World Development Indicators, 2026 |

What to know: "Like earlier general-purpose technologies, AI is likely to help people create entirely new products—and perhaps even new wants and needs... It could help people find goods and services that better match what they want and could lower prices for some goods and services, including those it provides, encouraging people to consume more."

Metrics to watch: revenue from new AI-enabled products and services; estimates of consumer value from AI products and services.

## Jobs: 72% reinvented time
**Covers:** pp. 22 exhibit "Jobs — 72%"

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| US workers who could see AI reinvent how they spend >15% of working time, by 2035 | 72% |
| Source | McKinsey Global Institute analysis |

What to know: "Most jobs combine tasks that AI can perform with others it cannot. In the near future, jobs are more likely to change than to be replaced outright. As AI takes on more routine work, the skills workers need will change, potentially increasing the value of judgment, creativity, and working effectively with AI."

Metrics to watch: employment and wage growth in AI-exposed occupations; share of occupations requiring AI skills; occupational transition rates.
