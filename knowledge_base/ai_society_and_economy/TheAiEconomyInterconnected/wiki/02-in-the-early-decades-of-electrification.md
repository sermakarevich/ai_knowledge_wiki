# General-purpose technologies outpace the systems needed to realize their potential

**In one sentence:** Like electrification and the internet before it, AI's big productivity gains are delayed because model capabilities improve exponentially while physical infrastructure builds linearly and companies reorganize unevenly, so out-of-step parts create bottlenecks such as the 2023 chip-packaging crunch and the electricity/grid-connection wall.

## Key points

- Electrification's big productivity gains arrived only after factories were physically redesigned around the electric motor, following an earlier sequence of first generating enough power, then expanding the grid.
- The internet followed the same staged pattern: first fiber-optic networks were built, then new business models were needed, and finally companies reorganized around the technology.
- AI model capabilities are improving exponentially, doubling every few months by some recent measures, while physical infrastructure builds out more linearly over years and company-level change is uneven and slower.
- When AI's parts move out of step, bottlenecks slow advancement or deployment (e.g., 2023 demand for AI chips outpacing manufacturing) and mismatches create risks when governance or cybersecurity lags powerful new tools.
- By 2023, a year after ChatGPT's launch, demand for AI chips outgrew packaging capacity: buyers faced lead times of up to 11 months for a finished H100-based server because only TSMC could fuse Nvidia's H100 with memory at the required scale.
- Early generative-AI hurdles shifted from frontier chips and computing power to electricity supply and grid connections, with the next constraints expected in applications, workforce skills, and organizational workflows rather than hardware.
- A single data center can consume as much power as a small city, and when local grids cannot absorb that demand, new power lines and substations — with their own permits, land, and queues — must be built before the facility turns on.
- Microsoft's Azure was short on operating capacity for several quarters due to electricity: on an October 2025 earnings call its CFO said the company had AI chips it could not deploy for lack of powered, ready-to-occupy data center space; the median new US power plant now takes about five years from interconnection request to commercial operation, up from less than two years in 2008.

## Electrification and the internet as precedents

In the early decades of electrification, "it was difficult simply to generate enough power to run factories and industrial machinery. Later, as that hurdle eased, the limits of the power grid became apparent."

"The big productivity gains arrived only once factories were physically redesigned around the electric motor, a step that came later still."

"The internet's path was much the same: First fiber-optic networks had to be built, then new business models were needed, and finally companies had to reorganize around the technology."

## Mismatches: AI's parts move on different timelines

"AI faces a similar challenge, with many necessary parts coming together on vastly different timelines and with different shapes of change."

"Model capabilities are improving exponentially, doubling every few months by some recent measures. Physical infrastructure builds out more linearly, often taking years. Change at companies may occur more unevenly and can take longer, as businesses develop new products and ways of working."

"When the parts move out of step, it can cause bottlenecks that slow down technological advancement or deployment—such as when demand for AI chips outpaced the ability to manufacture them in 2023."

"For organizations, a period of rapid adoption can just as easily be followed by a slowdown or reversal if a product fails or causes unintended harm, or if deployment of the technology does not deliver productivity gains soon enough."

"At other times, a mismatch can create risks, such as when corporate governance or cybersecurity lags behind the adoption of powerful new tools."

"Early in the generative-AI build-out, one of the first hurdles was access to frontier chips and computing power. As those bottlenecks eased, attention shifted to electricity supply and grid connections. The next constraints are more likely to involve applications, workforce skills, and organizational workflows than hardware."

## Bottlenecks: the 2023 chip-packaging crunch

"By 2023, only a year after OpenAI launched ChatGPT, demand for AI chips had outgrown the capacity to package them with the memory they require."

"Nvidia, which designs most of the processors that frontier AI runs on, had built exactly the chip for the job, the H100. The problem was fusing it with memory in a single package, so that the hardware could work fast enough."

"Only one company could do this at the scale AI now demanded: TSMC, the Taiwanese firm that manufactures chips for most of the world's major technology companies, including Nvidia and Apple. But in 2023, demand for that packaging outpaced even TSMC's ability to keep up."

"Buyers reportedly faced lead times of up to 11 months for a finished H100-based server. The fix was more capacity, and TSMC built faster than it ever had."

## Electricity as the next constraint

"Other hurdles emerged in a completely different part of the system: electricity. Building and connecting enough power capacity—a challenge for utilities and grid operators, not chipmakers—is another constraint, one familiar from the early era of electrification."

"A single data center can consume as much power as a small city, and when local grids cannot handle that much new demand, the utility has to build new power lines and substations before the facility can turn on—construction that requires its own permits and land, behind a long queue of other projects waiting for the same thing."

"Several hyperscalers, the handful of companies building AI at the largest scale, have run into this wall. On an October 2025 earnings call, Microsoft's CFO told investors that the company's cloud computing platform, Azure, which is used to handle AI workloads, had been short on operating capacity for several quarters. Electricity was the obstacle. Microsoft had AI chips it could not deploy because it lacked powered, ready-to-occupy data center space."

"The median new US power plant now takes about five years from interconnection request to commercial operation, up from less than two years in 2008."
