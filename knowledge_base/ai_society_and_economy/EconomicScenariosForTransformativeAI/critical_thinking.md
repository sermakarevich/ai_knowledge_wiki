# Critical Analysis: Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI

## Claims vs. evidence
(1) Modest scenario (+1.6% GDP by 2030, +0.1pp unemployment) -- **strong**. Anchored to Acemoglu 2025 and OECD Filippucci et al. 2024-2025 magnitudes, with conservative m=0.14 and d=0.10 mid-2026 anchors from Census BTOS and late-2025 Claude-use proxy. This is the only scenario disciplined by observed diffusion rather than assumed acceleration.
(2) Extreme scenario (15.4%/yr growth, labor share 60→45.2%, 17.9% cognitive unemployment) -- **weak**. Result is driven by jointly assumed disruptiveness parameters with no 2026 empirical basis: psi=0.90 automation share, rho=0 reinstatement, mu=0.04 search discount, theta_H=0.50 posting speed. Table 6 shows the fragility: moving wage rigidity Xi from 0.5 to 0 shifts cognitive wage from -11.5% to -42.2% and unemployment from 17.9% to 2.6%. Table 5 shows capital elasticity epsilon=1 to infinity moves extreme GDP from +21.3% to +43.3% and flips average wage from -9.2% to +30.1%. The headline is an assumption bundle, not a finding.
(3) Median survey respondent implies substantial scenario (+8.6% GDP, -4.2% cognitive employment) -- **suggestive at best**. August 2026 Morning Consult panel (n=10,980, only 3,259 answer all five model items) asks stated expectations about capability timing, adoption bins, and hypothetical re-employment months. Confusion between capability and workplace adoption is baked in: 24% already claim AI can build a software product, 19% claim it runs an online business today. Mapping is mechanical (task counts x 0.95 x 0.62 wage-bill share, bin midpoints, mu=0.17 x 3/months) and unasked parameters are fixed at substantial values, so convergence to substantial is partly constructed.
(4) Ideas channel adds <1% labor productivity by 2030 even in extreme -- **unsupported as lower bound**. Follows from semi-endogenous Jones 1995 setup where research stays bottlenecked by physical tasks plus assumed fishing-out 2.86 and research share path 3.5% to 4.1% of GDP. The paper admits it omits research-automation feedbacks and cannot generate Aghion-Jones-Jones 2019 or Davidson et al. 2026 explosive paths, then labels the result a lower bound without quantifying the omitted term.
Calibration split: normal-times labor flows are credible (CPS 2010-19 separations 0.84%/1.84% per month, mu-bar=0.17 from Carrillo-Tudela and Visschers switching matrix, matching curvature 1.27 from den Haan et al. 2000, filling rate 0.65 from JOLTS via Davis et al. 2013). AI-path parameters are not calibrated in the same sense -- they are scenario assumptions with literature decoration. Conflict note: Anthropic-affiliated authors using Claude conversation data (Massenkoff-McCrory 2026, Appel et al. 2025 mode-of-use) to anchor m and psi while Claude is credited as research assistant creates correlated measurement and advocacy risk the paper does not address.

## Genuinely new vs. repackaged
Core is repackaged task-based automation theory: Zeira 1998, Autor-Levy-Murnane 2003, Acemoglu-Autor 2011, Acemoglu-Restrepo 2019 for displacement vs reinstatement, plus Diamond-Mortensen-Pissarides matching and Jones-Romer semi-endogenous growth. Sigma=0.5 complements, rho=0.5 from Acemoglu-Restrepo history, and so-so-technologies framing are direct imports.
What is new is packaging, not theory: (a) a single monthly simulation mapping five measurable inputs (m, d, a, psi, re-employment time) to GDP, wages, shares, and unemployment through 2030; (b) disciplining the input ranges with one contemporaneous survey rather than literature cherry-picking; (c) a public explorer at anthropic.com/institute/econ-scenarios. Useful scaffolding, but no new identification, estimation, or mechanism.

## Weaknesses and blind spots
Acknowledged but underplayed: no probabilities attached, so modest vs extreme cannot be weighted for decisions; 2030 cutoff truncates exactly when ideas compounding and capital-stock dynamics steepen (Figure 2 still rising in 2030); two-group worker split (62.4% cognitive in 2025 CPS) hides within-group dispersion the paper itself notes (sales/office cross-occupation rate 28% vs managers/professionals 10%).
Silent: capital-supply epsilon=3 baseline is asserted on global-compute-financing grounds, yet Tables 5-6 prove it decides whether wages rise or fall -- the load-bearing parameter gets one paragraph. No demand side beyond slow real-wage adjustment, no AI-deflation or product-variety gains, no household production (cites Nguyen et al. 2026 then drops it). No tenure/scarring losses despite citing Jacobson et al. 1993 and Huckfeldt 2022. No policy reaction function -- 9%-of-GDP transfer arithmetic is stated with no precedent, no tax-base analysis when labor share falls 15 points. No non-US labor markets, no cycles, no finance shocks, no catastrophic risk by design.

## Applicability
Works: near-term structured comparison of stated assumptions for the US, 2026-2028, when diffusion and task-level gains can actually be measured against the explorer inputs. Good as a consistency check on consultancy forecasts.
Fails: long-run AGI restructuring, cross-country extrapolation, distributional planning within cognitive occupations, and any use of the extreme 15%/yr path as a forecast -- capital build (+56.3% stock), wage-setting, and re-employment frictions are assumed, not estimated, for that regime.
**Relevance to my work** --
- AI/ML engineering: trial the explorer logic to bound agent-capability claims -- require m, d, a, psi numbers before accepting productivity pitches, and default to modest a=0.30 until trial data clears it.
- Agentic systems: use psi=0.5 vs 0.9 distinction in roadmap reviews -- current Claude API automation share near 0.75 does not imply autonomous-task share 0.9 economy-wide; do not plan hiring on extreme displacement.
- Elisity data platform: watch adoption diffusion d, not benchmark capability -- Census firm-use vs instance-use gap (18% firms vs 10% instances) is the right metric for enterprise data-product rollout timing.
- General planning: ignore survey-median-as-forecast usage -- wide IQRs (GDP +3% to +19%, a in [0.09, 1.02]) make the median a talking point, not a plan input.

## What this changes
If claims hold: plan for two worlds at once -- baseline budgets on ~1.5pp extra productivity growth (substantial: 5.4%/yr vs 2.0% no-AI) while hedging tail risk of rapid cognitive reallocation (4.5% cognitive unemployment in substantial, 17.9% in extreme). Retraining and income-bridge design move from HR topic to balance-sheet topic when 4 points of labor share shift in four years.
If only partially true -- the likely case: modest GDP arithmetic plus substantial-rate churn in specific roles (software, office, sales support). What survives is the measurement agenda: track task reach, instance diffusion, per-task gain, automation share, and months-to-re-employment quarterly. The GDP level numbers will be wrong; the five-parameter dashboard is still the right instrument panel.

## Verdict
The normal-times calibration is solid but the transformative headlines rest on jointly assumed psi, rho, mu, and epsilon values that sensitivity tables show can flip wages from -9% to +30% and unemployment from 3% to 24%. The survey adds timeliness, not identification, and the 2030 horizon with fixed capital-supply and no policy response makes the extreme path illustrative arithmetic. Keep the framework as a consistency tool, discard the point forecasts. **watch** -- strongest reason is that the single parameter deciding whether workers gain or lose (capital-supply elasticity) is asserted rather than measured.
