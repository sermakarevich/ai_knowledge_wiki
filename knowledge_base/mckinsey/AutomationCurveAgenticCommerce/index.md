# The automation curve in agentic commerce

McKinsey's Deepa Mahajan, Hannah Mayer, Katharina Schumacher, and Roger Roberts (with Katharina Giebel, QuantumBlack) argue agents could mediate $3 trillion to $5 trillion of consumer commerce by 2030, driven by decision-grade usefulness, open transaction rails (Model Context Protocol, Agent-to-Agent, Agent Payments Protocol, Agentic Commerce Protocol, Universal Commerce Protocol plus the December 2025 Agentic AI Foundation), and intent shifting upstream. Their six levels run from programmed replenishment (level 0, ~23 percent Subscribe & Save penetration) through assist and assemble (levels 1–2) to authorize, autonomize, and networked autonomy (levels 3–5), each with a retailer readiness price from verifiable data to API-first merchandising to limitable, auditable, reversible authorization. The sting is the curve-not-ladder thesis: delegation accelerates for low-regret utility goods, plateaus where human involvement is the product (luxury, milestones), and goes selective amid trade-offs — so value migrates to agent-readable, dependable executors as the funnel compresses, and retailers should place autonomy where it enhances experience, economics, and trust.
