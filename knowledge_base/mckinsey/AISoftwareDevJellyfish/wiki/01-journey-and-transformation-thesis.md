# Journey and the transformation thesis

**In one sentence:** Engineer-turned-founder Andrew Lau, who cofounded Jellyfish in 2017, argues that artificial intelligence (AI) is the biggest transformation in software development since the invention of the internet and will completely redefine the software development life cycle within three years.

## Key points

- Andrew Lau describes himself as an engineer by training who caught the entrepreneurial bug about 15 years ago and, after founding and working at a few companies, cofounded Jellyfish in 2017.
- Lau states plainly that with AI in software development the company is seeing another tailwind, and that he fundamentally believes the software development life cycle will be completely redefined within three years, which he calls exciting and scary for the industry.
- He frames the change as one of the biggest transformations since the invention of the internet, explicitly larger than the earlier agile and cloud transitions.
- The 20-year stable pattern he says is ending runs from requirement generation and issue tracking through integrated development environment (IDE) development, source control, continuous integration and continuous development (CI/CD), testing, deployment, and post-deployment observation.
- The shift is human as much as technical because roles, skills, and collaboration models will all evolve, redefining what people actually do rather than merely speeding up existing tasks.
- The interview was conducted by McKinsey Senior Partner Martin Harrysson of the Bay Area office and Partner Prakhar Dixit of the Seattle office, published December 10, 2025, and edited for length and clarity.

---

## Lau's background in his own words

Lau compresses his story into two sentences: an engineer by training who caught the entrepreneurial bug about 15 years ago, then several foundings and company stints before cofounding Jellyfish in 2017. The framing matters because it positions him as a practitioner-turned-measurement-vendor rather than an outside analyst. Jellyfish's business — a Software Engineering Intelligence platform used, per its own materials, by more than 500 companies — is the lens for every data claim later in the interview.

## The three-year redefinition claim

The interview's load-bearing prediction is Lau's statement that he fundamentally believes the software development life cycle will be completely redefined within three years, calling that prospect exciting and scary for the industry. Note the scope: not coding alone but the whole life cycle, which sets up the later systems-thinking argument that local speedups without pipeline-wide change create downstream friction.

## Bigger than agile or cloud

Lau's comparison set is deliberate — agile and cloud were the two largest workflow transformations of the past two decades, and he claims this change exceeds both because it redefines what people actually do. The 20-year pattern he recites (requirements, issue tracking, IDEs, source control, CI/CD, testing, deploying, observing) is the baseline he expects to look very different within three years. The human half of the claim — roles, skills, and collaboration models all evolving — anticipates the closing section on the spec-writing human.
