# Speed gains and caveats

**In one sentence:** McKinsey's lab study found developers complete coding tasks up to twice as fast with generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, but the gains collapse on complex tasks and for junior developers.

## Key points

- Documenting code functionality for maintainability took half the time with generative AI assistance, the largest saving among the three task types studied.
- Writing new code took nearly half the time with AI assistance, while optimizing existing code, known as code refactoring, took nearly two-thirds of the unassisted time.
- Time savings shrank below 10 percent on tasks developers rated high in complexity, for example when they lacked familiarity with a required programming framework.
- Developers with less than one year of experience sometimes took 7 to 10 percent longer with the tools than without them, showing the gains depend on existing skill.
- Code quality did not fall as speed rose when developer and tool collaborated, and automated reviews rated readability of AI-assisted code marginally better.
- Maintaining quality still depends on the developer, who must understand what makes code good and prompt the tool toward the right outputs.

---

## The headline numbers

### Up to two times faster overall

The article's central claim is that "software developers can complete coding tasks up to twice as fast with generative AI." The three task-level figures behind it are: documentation in half the time, new code in nearly half the time, and refactoring in nearly two-thirds the time (Exhibit 1).

### Where the gains evaporate

Two caveats bound the headline. First, complexity: on tasks perceived as highly complex, savings fell to less than 10 percent. Second, experience: junior developers with under a year of experience saw no benefit and in some cases needed 7 to 10 percent more time with the tools than without them.

## Quality held steady

### No speed-for-quality trade-off — with collaboration

"Using these tools did not sacrifice quality for speed when the developer and tool collaborated." Automated review of the submissions found readability, which the article notes matters for reusability, was "marginally better in AI-assisted code."

### Quality remains the developer's job

The article closes the quality discussion with a condition: "to maintain code quality, developers need to understand the attributes that make up quality code and prompt the tool for the right outputs." The tool does not supply judgment on its own.
