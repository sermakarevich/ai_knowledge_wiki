# Retrieval practice: the agentic organization

> [[index|Wiki]] | [[summary|Summary]] | [[digest|Digest]]

## Paradigm and evolution

1. What three things unite "side by side at scale at near-zero marginal cost" in McKinsey's definition of the agentic organization?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Humans and Artificial Intelligence agents — both virtual and physical.
</details>

2. Along what spectrum of increasing complexity are organizations deploying virtual Artificial Intelligence agents?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
From simple tools augmenting existing activities, to end-to-end workflow automation, to entire Artificial Intelligence-first agentic systems — with physical agents (smart devices, drones, self-driving vehicles, early humanoid robots) emerging in parallel.
</details>

3. Why does the task-length doubling trend (seven months since 2019, four months since 2024, two hours now, possibly four unsupervised days by 2027) force the "act now" conclusion — and what breaks if leaders wait for perfect clarity?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Because capability grows exponentially while operating models evolve linearly: waiting means the gap between what the technology can do and what the organization can absorb keeps widening, so late movers get shaped by the era (or disrupted by Artificial Intelligence-native rivals) instead of shaping it.
</details>

4. A colleague claims most companies already run modern digital operating models. What three coexistence numbers refute that?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
89 percent still live in the industrial age, 9 percent run agile or product-and-platform models from the digital age, and only 1 percent act as a decentralized network.
</details>

## Business model

5. Name the three sources of agentic-era competitive advantage in the business-model pillar.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Closer customer reach via Artificial Intelligence channels for real-time hyperpersonalization; Artificial Intelligence-first streamlined processes; a walled garden of proprietary data as the superpower.
</details>

6. What did the European utility's multimodal assistant (three million customers) achieve, and why does that result generalize to the ecosystem economy?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
It significantly cut average handling times while boosting satisfaction, response speed, and resolution without a human; it shows assistants becoming 24/7 negotiating concierges, letting whoever owns customer contact expand beyond traditional industry boundaries.
</details>

7. If you removed the proprietary-data moat and let every firm use the same public models, what breaks in the article's business-model argument?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
The durable edge disappears: with "an intern with the internet in its pocket" available to all, differentiation collapses to whoever continuously captures and refines unique consented data (behavior, usage, sensor streams) into personalized products — without it, Artificial Intelligence-native rivals compete purely on cost and speed.
</details>

## Operating model

8. What is the article's anchor supervision ratio, and which end-to-end processes illustrate it?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Two to five humans supervising an agent factory of 50 to 100 specialized agents — e.g., onboarding a customer, launching a product, or closing the books.
</details>

9. What distinguishes a work chart from an org chart, and why does the shift matter?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
An org chart maps hierarchical delegation; a work chart maps agentic networks exchanging tasks and outcomes. The shift matters because fixed hierarchies cannot coordinate machine-speed agent populations — only flat structures with high context sharing keep agentic teams in sync instead of chaotic.
</details>

10. Transfer: your company pilots agents inside unchanged departments with hand-offs between silos. Predict the outcome using the article's logic.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Automation layered onto existing processes without end-to-end redesign: local efficiency gains but no decoupling of cost from growth, plus chaos risk from agents lacking shared context — the "not automation as usual" warning.
</details>

## Governance

11. Name the three control-agent types and the DevSecOps analogy behind them.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Critic agents challenge outputs, guardrail agents enforce policy, compliance agents monitor regulation — mirroring how DevSecOps embedded automated checks into digital delivery.
</details>

12. What is the oversight "sweet spot," and what ultimately caps agentic scale?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Enough oversight to manage risk without pulling agents back to human speed; scale is ultimately capped by how much oversight capacity humans can provide, making governance itself a potential productivity bottleneck.
</details>

## Workforce, people, and culture

13. How do Human Resources systems and performance management change in the agentic organization?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Human Resources systems become a repository of agents and agentic workflows alongside humans; performance management shifts from task completion to how well people orchestrate agents, unlock value, and deliver outcomes.
</details>

14. Why must culture serve as an "ethical compass," not just glue — what goes wrong if values aren't embedded in agentic systems?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Without embedded values and long-term purpose, agents optimize short-term efficiency at the expense of cohesion and trust; winners preserve cohesion and identity while transforming at pace.
</details>

## Technology and data

15. What does agent-to-agent dialogue replace, and what is its biggest practical payoff?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
It replaces traditional middleware-and-Application-Programming-Interfaces integration requiring heavy programming and custom connections; the payoff is faster, cheaper integration of legacy, cloud, and even drones — plus experimenting (test, scale, deprecate) without months of engineering.
</details>

16. Why can locking in one model vendor leave technology outdated "in a matter of weeks," and what architecture answers that?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Because large language models and Artificial Intelligence products evolve extremely fast; the answer is separating the agentic structure, logic, and data from the vendor landscape while walling in proprietary context and knowledge.
</details>

## Getting started

17. List the five concrete first steps for leadership teams.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Put agentic Artificial Intelligence on the top-team agenda; outline the Chief Executive Officer's vision; ramp up an Artificial Intelligence center of excellence; upskill people; rewire one or two lighthouse domains to launch quickly and learn live.
</details>

18. Your board proposes fifty small Artificial Intelligence pilots across every department. Using the article, argue why one or two lighthouse domains beat that plan.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
Fifty pilots repeat the "more pilots than Lufthansa" trap — disconnected from value drivers, with Artificial Intelligence everywhere but the profit-and-loss statement; one bold end-to-end domain builds the learning muscle (learning only by doing), proves the full value potential, and funds the scaling enablers for the rest.
</details>
