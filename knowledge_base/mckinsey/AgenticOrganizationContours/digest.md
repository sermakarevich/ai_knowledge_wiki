## 01 — The agentic paradigm and the evolution of operating models

# The agentic paradigm and the evolution of operating models

- The agentic organization is defined as the new paradigm in which humans and Artificial Intelligence agents, virtual and physical, work side by side at scale at near-zero marginal cost.
- Virtual agents are being deployed along a spectrum of rising complexity, from tools that augment existing activities to end-to-end workflow automation to entire Artificial Intelligence-first agentic systems.
- Physical agents are emerging in parallel as companies build "bodies" for Artificial Intelligence such as smart devices, drones, self-driving vehicles, and early humanoid robots that let Artificial Intelligence interface with the physical world.
- The reliably completable task length for Artificial Intelligence doubled roughly every seven months since 2019 and every four months since 2024, reaching about two hours at the time of writing, with a trajectory toward four unsupervised days of work by 2027.
- That trajectory implies an accelerated evolution from an intern-level employee needing constant supervision to a mid-tenure employee operating independently to possibly a senior executive shaping strategy.
- Organizational paradigms coexist today: 89 percent of organizations still live in the industrial age, 9 percent run agile or product-and-platform models from the digital age, and only 1 percent act as a decentralized network.
- The paradigm's promise depends on continued growth in Artificial Intelligence capabilities plus factors such as regulation, so leaders cannot wait for perfect clarity and must act now rather than be shaped by the era.

## 02 — Pillar 1: the business model

# Pillar 1: the business model

- Agentic-era advantage rests on three moves: getting closer to customers via Artificial Intelligence channels for real-time hyperpersonalization, streamlining processes to become Artificial Intelligence-first, and building a walled garden of proprietary data as the superpower.
- Artificial Intelligence-native start-ups and agentic companies can disrupt industries with a fundamentally different level of productivity measured as revenue per employee, cost decoupled from growth, and greater speed to market and innovation.
- Consumers are already bypassing shops, apps, and search engines for Artificial Intelligence-native interfaces such as ChatGPT, and every consumer could soon have a low-cost Artificial Intelligence personal assistant that negotiates with other agents around the clock.
- One European utility's multimodal Artificial Intelligence assistant serves three million customers and significantly cut average handling times while boosting satisfaction, response speed, and call resolution without a human.
- If today's Artificial Intelligence is "an intern with the internet in its pocket," tomorrow's edge comes from walled data gardens the public internet doesn't offer — consented proprietary streams of customer behavior, product usage, and sensor data.
- One global bank's agent factory runs know-your-customer processes with ten agent squads and achieved a substantial positive impact on output quality and consistency, while another bank cut legacy-modernization time and effort by up to 50 percent using human-overseen agent squads.
- Going forward, most if not all processes can be reimagined as Artificial Intelligence-first, with humans and traditional Information Technology systems selectively reintroduced in the loop or above the loop.

## 03 — Pillar 2: the operating model

# Pillar 2: the operating model

- How organizations are built and operate will evolve as much as the products or services they deliver, with work reimagined as Artificial Intelligence-first and operating models becoming flat networks of empowered, outcome-aligned agentic teams.
- An agentic team is a small multidisciplinary human group that owns and supervises the underlying Artificial Intelligence workflows to deliver a clear end-to-end business outcome across marketing, product management, technology, data, and operations.
- In McKinsey's experience, a human team of two to five people can already supervise an agent factory of 50 to 100 specialized agents running an end-to-end process such as onboarding a customer, launching a product, or closing the books.
- At a European automaker and a public-sector organization, agent squads are reverse-engineering and modernizing legacy systems while humans steer and validate, and in product development agents gather feedback, analyze data, test features, and even run campaigns.
- Humans sit mostly above the loop steering outcomes and selectively within the loop where human contact matters, with traditional Information Technology systems reintroduced only where needed.
- Proliferating agents without context, steering, and orientation is a recipe for chaos, so winning models pair flat decision structures with high context sharing and alignment across agentic teams.
- Organization charts built on hierarchical delegation pivot toward agentic networks or work charts built on exchanging tasks and outcomes.

## 04 — Pillar 3: governance

# Pillar 3: governance

- Governance cannot remain a periodic, paper-heavy exercise; as agents operate continuously, it must become real-time, data-driven, and embedded, with humans holding final accountability.
- Just as DevSecOps embedded automated checks into digital delivery, agentic organizations embed control agents into workflows: critic agents challenge outputs, guardrail agents enforce policy, and compliance agents monitor regulation.
- Every action can be logged and explained in real time, from data privacy to financial thresholds to brand voice, under an Artificial Intelligence governance framework spanning the agent life cycle from discovery and initiation to decommissioning.
- Rather than line-by-line reviews, compliance officers and leaders define policies, monitor outliers, and adjust the level of human involvement to balance speed and scale with security and control.
- The central challenge is finding the sweet spot of oversight: enough to manage risk without pulling agents back to human speed, and companies that get the balance right capture more of the agentic advantage.
- Ultimately the scale of agentic adoption is capped by how much oversight capacity humans can provide, making governance itself a potential bottleneck to productivity.
- Humans remain essential but their oversight changes in nature — from reviewing execution to setting policy, watching exceptions, and tuning involvement up or down.

## 05 — Pillar 4: workforce, people, and culture

# Pillar 4: workforce, people, and culture

- In the agentic organization, humans move from executing activities to owning and steering end-to-end outcomes, demanding new profiles with different skills and a culture that provides cohesion and purpose.
- As agents take on execution, people increasingly define goals, make trade-offs, and steer outcomes, changing how companies plan for a hybrid workforce, whom they hire or borrow, and how they deploy human or Artificial Intelligence talent.
- Human Resources systems stop tracking only human employees and become a repository of agents and agentic workflows as well.
- Performance management anchored in task completion gives way to systems tracking how well people orchestrate agents, unlock value, and deliver outcomes.
- The whole talent system must be rethought, from career paths to incentives to leadership models, and measurement shifts from tasks completed to value unlocked.
- Culture acts as both operating glue and ethical compass: pioneering organizations stress orchestration to align teams on shared context and outcomes, find the right human–Artificial Intelligence mix since not everything needs agentic Artificial Intelligence, and build trust between humans and agents.
- The culture compass embeds values and long-term purpose into agentic systems so companies do not chase short-term efficiency at the expense of cohesion and trust — and winners will be those who preserve cohesion and identity while transforming at pace.

## 06 — Pillar 5: technology and data

# Pillar 5: technology and data

- In the agentic organization, technology and data get democratized, supported by an agentic Artificial Intelligence mesh, with agent-to-agent protocols making integration across systems, machines, and humans easier and cheaper.
- Agentic platforms provide reusable, high-performing atomic agents and data products with technical safety guardrails that prevent technical-debt and cybersecurity buildup while unlocking democratization and innovation.
- Agent-to-agent protocols redefine interactions between humans, agents, Information Technology systems, and devices by replacing heavy middleware-and-APIs integration work with agent-to-agent dialogue above system complexity.
- That dialogue layer lets organizations integrate legacy systems, cloud platforms, and even machines such as drones into cohesive workflows faster and cheaper, and test, scale, or deprecate capabilities without months of engineering effort.
- Successful scalers balance build-versus-buy on sources of distinctiveness and competitive advantage, avoiding technology or vendor lock-in to adapt quickly to a fast-evolving offering landscape.
- Locking in one model or vendor can leave technology outdated in weeks since large language models and Artificial Intelligence products evolve so fast, so organizations must wall in proprietary context, institutional knowledge, and nonpublic data.
- The required architecture separates the agentic structure, logic, and data from the underlying vendor landscape, keeping competitiveness independent of whichever model is current.

## 07 — Getting started: how leaders begin

# Getting started: how leaders begin

- The most frequent executive question is "How do I start," covering how to set a North Star vision without clarity on the future, assess data, technical, and governance maturity, prioritize by value and feasibility, bring the organization along, and scale faster than rivals.
- The clear and present danger is ending up with "more pilots than Lufthansa" — disconnected from value drivers, with Artificial Intelligence everywhere except the profit-and-loss statement — or with public-relations fiascos.
- Companies seeking advantage should think boldly, move fast, and go deep, making coherent choices across the 15 themes laid out in the article with a different executive mindset.
- The first radical shift is from linear to exponential: technology develops exponentially while operating models evolve linearly, so leaders must replace functional silos with cross-functional autonomous agentic teams and redesign incentives and support processes.
- The second shift is from technology-forward to future-back: delegating the transformation to the technology leader like a software deployment will not suffice, since leaders must envision the future organization and work backward — learning only by doing, not by reading books or talking about it on the golf course.
- The third shift is from threat to opportunity: executives must continuously engage employees on what the technology unlocks for them as professionals, overinvesting in upskilling beyond basic literacy plus change management, incentives, budget, communications, and performance management.
- Concretely, leadership teams start by putting agentic Artificial Intelligence on the top-team agenda, outlining the Chief Executive Officer's vision, ramping up an Artificial Intelligence center of excellence, upskilling people, and rewiring one or two lighthouse domains to launch agentic processes quickly and learn live.

## The argument in five moves

1. Artificial Intelligence agents, virtual and physical, constitute a paradigm shift on the scale of the industrial and digital revolutions.
2. Advantage accrues to Artificial Intelligence-native business models built on hyperpersonalization, Artificial Intelligence-first cost structures, and proprietary data moats.
3. Structure must flatten into outcome-aligned agentic teams where tiny human cores supervise large agent factories.
4. Governance, talent systems, and technology must all be rebuilt for continuous machine-speed operation with humans above the loop.
5. Leaders win by moving now — bold lighthouse bets, three mindset shifts, and learning live — because adapters outpace waiters.
