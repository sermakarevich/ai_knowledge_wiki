# Pillar 1: the business model

**In one sentence:** In the agentic era, competitive advantage comes from Artificial Intelligence-native channels delivering real-time hyperpersonalization, Artificial Intelligence-first processes that decouple cost from growth, and a walled garden of proprietary data that public models cannot replicate.

## Key points

- Agentic-era advantage rests on three moves: getting closer to customers via Artificial Intelligence channels for real-time hyperpersonalization, streamlining processes to become Artificial Intelligence-first, and building a walled garden of proprietary data as the superpower.
- Artificial Intelligence-native start-ups and agentic companies can disrupt industries with a fundamentally different level of productivity measured as revenue per employee, cost decoupled from growth, and greater speed to market and innovation.
- Consumers are already bypassing shops, apps, and search engines for Artificial Intelligence-native interfaces such as ChatGPT, and every consumer could soon have a low-cost Artificial Intelligence personal assistant that negotiates with other agents around the clock.
- One European utility's multimodal Artificial Intelligence assistant serves three million customers and significantly cut average handling times while boosting satisfaction, response speed, and call resolution without a human.
- If today's Artificial Intelligence is "an intern with the internet in its pocket," tomorrow's edge comes from walled data gardens the public internet doesn't offer — consented proprietary streams of customer behavior, product usage, and sensor data.
- One global bank's agent factory runs know-your-customer processes with ten agent squads and achieved a substantial positive impact on output quality and consistency, while another bank cut legacy-modernization time and effort by up to 50 percent using human-overseen agent squads.
- Going forward, most if not all processes can be reimagined as Artificial Intelligence-first, with humans and traditional Information Technology systems selectively reintroduced in the loop or above the loop.

## The three-part advantage

The article states the business-model thesis compactly: in the agentic era, companies gain competitive advantage by getting closer to customers via Artificial Intelligence channels to offer real-time hyperpersonalization, streamlining processes to become Artificial Intelligence-first, and building a walled garden of proprietary data as their superpower. The disruption warning follows immediately — Artificial Intelligence-native start-ups and agentic companies can potentially disrupt industries with a fundamentally different level of productivity (revenue per employee), cost decoupled from growth, and greater speed to market and innovation.

## Artificial Intelligence-native channels and hyperpersonalization

Consumers are already bypassing shops, apps, and search engines in favor of Artificial Intelligence-native interfaces such as ChatGPT. McKinsey projects that in the future every consumer could have a low-cost Artificial Intelligence personal assistant. The proof point is a European utility provider that rolled out a multimodal Artificial Intelligence assistant to its three million customers: it significantly reduced average handling times, boosted customer satisfaction, improved response speed, and resolved more calls without a human. These assistants will not just respond — they are personal concierges that negotiate with other agents 24/7, continuously learning from user behavior and market signals to generate ever-evolving hyperpersonalized products. The same shift unlocks the ecosystem economy, in which companies that own customer contact grow by meeting customer needs beyond their traditional business model and industry boundaries.

## Artificial Intelligence-first processes and the bank of tomorrow

The article's signature illustration is the bank of tomorrow: when a customer wants to buy a house, a personal Artificial Intelligence concierge activates a series of agentic workflows — a real estate agent suggests properties, a mortgage underwriting agent tailors offers to the customer's financial profile, compliance agents check bank policy, a contracting agent finalizes agreements, and another agent fulfills the loan — all overseen by an agentic team of human supervisors, mortgage experts, and Artificial Intelligence-empowered frontline employees. The bank can even extend into furnishing, renovations, and energy upgrades, becoming a network of agentic teams. Real deployments back the vision: banks already run mortgage and compliance processes with agent squads, insurers are reinventing claims and underwriting while reimagining themselves as Artificial Intelligence-native, and telcos use agents in customer service and beyond. One global bank's agent factory manages know-your-customer processes with ten agent squads with substantial positive impact on quality and consistency of output; another bank used humans overseeing agent squads to modernize legacy core systems with up to 50 percent reductions in time and effort. The punchline is that this is not automation layered onto existing processes — it is end-to-end redesign with humans above the loop for strategic oversight, with the potential to bring marginal cost toward the cost of compute.

## Proprietary data as the differentiator

The article's most quotable line anchors this section: if today's Artificial Intelligence is "an intern with the internet in its pocket," tomorrow's edge will come from the walled data gardens that the public internet doesn't offer. Companies outperform by continuously capturing and refining unique, consented, proprietary data — streams of customer behavior, product usage, and sensor data — and converting it into differentiating personalized products and processes. Artificial Intelligence itself accelerates the build-up of data foundations and data products as well as data-quality improvements.
