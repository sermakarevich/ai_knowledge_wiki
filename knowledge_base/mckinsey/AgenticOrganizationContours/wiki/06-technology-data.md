# Pillar 5: technology and data

**In one sentence:** Technology and data get democratized through an agentic Artificial Intelligence mesh of reusable atomic agents and agent-to-agent protocols, while architecture must separate the agentic structure, logic, and data from a fast-churning vendor landscape to avoid lock-in.

## Key points

- In the agentic organization, technology and data get democratized, supported by an agentic Artificial Intelligence mesh, with agent-to-agent protocols making integration across systems, machines, and humans easier and cheaper.
- Agentic platforms provide reusable, high-performing atomic agents and data products with technical safety guardrails that prevent technical-debt and cybersecurity buildup while unlocking democratization and innovation.
- Agent-to-agent protocols redefine interactions between humans, agents, Information Technology systems, and devices by replacing heavy middleware-and-APIs integration work with agent-to-agent dialogue above system complexity.
- That dialogue layer lets organizations integrate legacy systems, cloud platforms, and even machines such as drones into cohesive workflows faster and cheaper, and test, scale, or deprecate capabilities without months of engineering effort.
- Successful scalers balance build-versus-buy on sources of distinctiveness and competitive advantage, avoiding technology or vendor lock-in to adapt quickly to a fast-evolving offering landscape.
- Locking in one model or vendor can leave technology outdated in weeks since large language models and Artificial Intelligence products evolve so fast, so organizations must wall in proprietary context, institutional knowledge, and nonpublic data.
- The required architecture separates the agentic structure, logic, and data from the underlying vendor landscape, keeping competitiveness independent of whichever model is current.

---

## The agentic Artificial Intelligence mesh

The pillar's headline is democratization: in the agentic organization, technology and data get democratized, supported by an agentic Artificial Intelligence mesh. To scale responsibly, organizations must adopt agentic platforms and architectures such as that mesh, which provide reusable, high-performing atomic agents and data products equipped with technical safety guardrails — preventing buildup of technical debt or cybersecurity risks while unlocking unprecedented levels of democratization and innovation.

## Agent-to-agent protocols

Agent-to-agent protocols ease interactions and integrations by redefining how humans, agents, Information Technology systems, and devices relate. Rather than relying on traditional integrations such as middleware and Application Programming Interfaces that require heavy programming and custom system-to-system connections, agent-to-agent protocols let systems use agents to communicate with other systems. Moving to agent-to-agent dialogue above the underlying system complexity lets organizations integrate legacy systems, cloud platforms, and even machines such as drones into cohesive workflows more quickly and at lower cost. McKinsey stresses the experimentation payoff above all: new capabilities can be tested, scaled, or deprecated without months of engineering effort.

## Dynamic sourcing and the separation architecture

The sourcing doctrine is to balance build-versus-buy decisions on sources of distinctiveness and competitive advantage, avoiding technology or vendor lock-in to adapt quickly to a fast-evolving offering landscape. The rationale is clockspeed: historically, business-critical platforms were built in-house or selected through rigorous sourcing with multiyear implementations, intended to stay stable for decades — but large language models and Artificial Intelligence products evolve so fast that locking in one solution or vendor can mean technology outdated in a matter of weeks. At the same time, organizations must wall in proprietary organizational context, institutional knowledge, and nonpublic data for competitiveness. The resolution is architectural: separate the agentic structure, logic, and data from the underlying vendor landscape.
