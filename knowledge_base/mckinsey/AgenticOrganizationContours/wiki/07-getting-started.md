## The executive starting question

McKinsey reports that the most frequent question from executives is "How do I start," and unpacks it into five sub-questions: how to create a North Star vision without clarity on what the future holds, how to assess maturity and upgrade needs for data, technical, and governance foundations, how to set priorities for value and feasibility, how to bring the organization along in skills and mindset, and how to scale faster than rivals for competitive advantage. The failure mode has a memorable name — the clear and present danger of ending up with "more pilots than Lufthansa," disconnected from value drivers, seeing Artificial Intelligence everywhere but in the profit-and-loss statements, or ending up with public-relations fiascos.

## Think boldly, move fast, go deep

Building on transformation experience, McKinsey argues that companies wanting competitive advantage from the agentic organization should think boldly, move fast, and go deep. The journey to agentic leadership demands a different mindset to reach a coherent set of choices and actions across the 15 themes laid out in the article. Three radical shifts make the step change in how to transform for the agentic era. From linear to exponential: while technology develops exponentially, organizations and operating models typically evolve linearly, which caps capturable value — so leadership teams must take bold stances, replacing functional silos with cross-functional autonomous agentic teams, redesigning incentives and support processes, and investing in required capabilities. From technology-forward to future-back: delegating the agentic transformation to the technology leader as with a software deployment will not suffice; leaders must envision the future organization with its full value potential from Artificial Intelligence-first processes and a hybrid human–agent organization, then work backward to starting points. The learning doctrine is blunt — you can only learn by doing, not by reading books or talking about it on the golf course — and boldly reimagining one end-to-end domain builds the organization's learning muscle while leaders plan the scaling enablers beyond the first lighthouse in parallel. From threat to opportunity: leaders may feel apprehension about agentic Artificial Intelligence's operational impact, so executives must continuously engage employees about the new possibilities for organizational growth and purpose and for employees as professionals, overinvesting in upskilling beyond basic literacy as well as change management, incentives, budget, communications, and performance management.

## The five concrete first steps

Concretely, leadership teams can start with five steps: making agentic Artificial Intelligence a prominent part of the top-team agenda, outlining the Chief Executive Officer's vision for creating an agentic organization, ramping up an Artificial Intelligence center of excellence, upskilling people, and rewiring one or two lighthouse domains to launch agentic processes quickly and learn live.

## What sets the pace, and who wins

Many factors will influence adoption pace: model development, computing-power availability, robotics progress, regulatory changes, societal understanding and acceptance, and the human appetite for risk and change. McKinsey closes with certainty on one point: while its insights will evolve with the technology, the organizations that adapt and learn faster will be the early winners in the agentic era — and the agentic organization may be leaders' key to competitive advantage through decentralized, outcomes-focused agentic networks.
