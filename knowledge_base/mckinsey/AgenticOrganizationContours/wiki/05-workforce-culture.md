# Pillar 4: workforce, people, and culture

**In one sentence:** Humans move from executing activities to owning and steering end-to-end outcomes, which rewires hiring, deployment, measurement, and the talent system itself, while culture becomes the glue and ethical compass holding the hybrid workforce together.

## Key points

- In the agentic organization, humans move from executing activities to owning and steering end-to-end outcomes, demanding new profiles with different skills and a culture that provides cohesion and purpose.
- As agents take on execution, people increasingly define goals, make trade-offs, and steer outcomes, changing how companies plan for a hybrid workforce, whom they hire or borrow, and how they deploy human or Artificial Intelligence talent.
- Human Resources systems stop tracking only human employees and become a repository of agents and agentic workflows as well.
- Performance management anchored in task completion gives way to systems tracking how well people orchestrate agents, unlock value, and deliver outcomes.
- The whole talent system must be rethought, from career paths to incentives to leadership models, and measurement shifts from tasks completed to value unlocked.
- Culture acts as both operating glue and ethical compass: pioneering organizations stress orchestration to align teams on shared context and outcomes, find the right human–Artificial Intelligence mix since not everything needs agentic Artificial Intelligence, and build trust between humans and agents.
- The culture compass embeds values and long-term purpose into agentic systems so companies do not chase short-term efficiency at the expense of cohesion and trust — and winners will be those who preserve cohesion and identity while transforming at pace.

---

## From execution to outcome ownership

The pillar's thesis is a role reversal: in the agentic organization, humans move from executing activities to owning and steering end-to-end outcomes, and that shift demands new profiles with different skills plus a culture that provides cohesion and purpose. As agents absorb execution, people increasingly define goals, make trade-offs, and steer outcomes. The organizational consequences cascade through workforce planning for a hybrid workforce, whom companies hire or borrow, how human and Artificial Intelligence talent gets deployed, and how success is measured.

## Rewiring the talent system

Two institutional changes stand out. First, Human Resources systems, which today track human employees, become a repository of agents and agentic workflows too — the agent population enters the system of record. Second, performance management anchored in task completion gives way to systems that track how well people orchestrate agents, unlock value, and deliver outcomes. McKinsey is explicit that in this paradigm the talent system itself must be rethought, from career paths to incentives to leadership models.

## Culture as glue and ethical compass

Culture becomes both the operating glue and the ethical compass of the agentic organization. Pioneering agentic organizations highlight the need for orchestration — aligning teams around shared context and outcomes, identifying the right mix of human and Artificial Intelligence capabilities (as not everything needs agentic Artificial Intelligence), and building trust between humans and agents. The culture compass embeds values and long-term purpose into agentic systems so companies do not chase short-term efficiency at the expense of cohesion and trust. Early pioneers show that clarity, decisive leadership, and continuous learning are critical — but what differentiates winners is the ability to preserve cohesion and identity while transforming at pace.
