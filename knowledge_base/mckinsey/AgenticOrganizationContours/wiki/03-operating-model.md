# Pillar 2: the operating model

**In one sentence:** Work and workflows get reimagined as Artificial Intelligence-first while structure pivots from hierarchical org charts to flat networks of small outcome-focused agentic teams, where two to five humans supervise an agent factory of 50 to 100 specialized agents.

## Key points

- How organizations are built and operate will evolve as much as the products or services they deliver, with work reimagined as Artificial Intelligence-first and operating models becoming flat networks of empowered, outcome-aligned agentic teams.
- An agentic team is a small multidisciplinary human group that owns and supervises the underlying Artificial Intelligence workflows to deliver a clear end-to-end business outcome across marketing, product management, technology, data, and operations.
- In McKinsey's experience, a human team of two to five people can already supervise an agent factory of 50 to 100 specialized agents running an end-to-end process such as onboarding a customer, launching a product, or closing the books.
- At a European automaker and a public-sector organization, agent squads are reverse-engineering and modernizing legacy systems while humans steer and validate, and in product development agents gather feedback, analyze data, test features, and even run campaigns.
- Humans sit mostly above the loop steering outcomes and selectively within the loop where human contact matters, with traditional Information Technology systems reintroduced only where needed.
- Proliferating agents without context, steering, and orientation is a recipe for chaos, so winning models pair flat decision structures with high context sharing and alignment across agentic teams.
- Organization charts built on hierarchical delegation pivot toward agentic networks or work charts built on exchanging tasks and outcomes.

---

## Artificial Intelligence-first workflows

The operating model of the agentic era is anchored around reimagined Artificial Intelligence-first workflows, with humans and Information Technology systems selectively reintroduced in Artificial Intelligence-native design. The article gives concrete early-adopter evidence: at a European automaker and a public-sector organization, squads of agents are reverse-engineering and modernizing legacy systems while humans steer and validate the work. In product development, agents can gather feedback, analyze data, test features, and even run campaigns. The human position is explicit — mostly above the loop to steer and direct outcomes, and selectively within the loop where human contact matters.

## The agentic team and the agent factory

Structure pivots to small, outcome-focused agentic teams. McKinsey defines an agentic team as a smaller group of multidisciplinary humans who own and supervise the underlying Artificial Intelligence workflows, organized to deliver clear end-to-end business outcomes covering the full functional value chain of marketing, product management, technology, data, and operations. The anchor number of the whole article appears here: in McKinsey's experience, a human team of two to five people can already supervise an agent factory of 50 to 100 specialized agents running an end-to-end process such as onboarding a customer, launching a product, or closing the books. The corollary is that agentic Artificial Intelligence extends the scope and autonomy of a product team more than ever before.

## From org charts to work charts

The article warns that proliferation of Artificial Intelligence agents without the right context, steering, and orientation can be a recipe for chaos. Winning operating models therefore empower agentic teams with flat decision and communication structures that operate with high context sharing and alignment across agentic teams so they move in sync. The structural punchline is a pivot in the unit of organization: away from organization charts based on traditional hierarchical delegation and toward agentic networks or work charts based on exchanging tasks and outcomes.
