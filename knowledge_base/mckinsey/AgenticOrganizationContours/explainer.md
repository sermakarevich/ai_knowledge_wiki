# Explainer: the agentic organization

> [[index|Wiki]] | [[summary|Summary]] | [[digest|Digest]]

## What it is

Imagine a company where, alongside every team of people, there is a much larger team of tireless digital helpers that do the routine work — drafting, checking, scheduling, analyzing — while the humans act more like coaches and captains, setting direction and making the judgment calls. McKinsey, a global management consulting firm, calls this setup the agentic organization: humans plus Artificial Intelligence agents working side by side at a cost close to zero for each extra task. The article argues this is as big a change in how companies are organized as the factory system or the internet era were.

## Why it matters

Most companies today still run like factories of the past: departments, hierarchies, and slow approval chains, even though the technology around them improves at breakneck speed. McKinsey's numbers say 89 percent of organizations still operate in that industrial style. Meanwhile Artificial Intelligence capabilities keep doubling in what they can reliably do — from minutes-long tasks a few years ago to hours-long tasks now, possibly days-long tasks by 2027. Companies that reorganize around this new capability early gain a lasting edge, while those that only sprinkle chatbots on top of old structures get little return and risk being undercut by younger competitors built for this world from day one.

## How it works

The article organizes the change into five building blocks. First, the business model: reach customers through Artificial Intelligence assistants that personalize everything in real time, run processes where software does the bulk of the work, and guard your own unique data as your crown jewel. Second, the operating model: replace big departments with small mission teams — as few as two to five people — each directing a factory of 50 to 100 specialized agents that carry a process from start to finish. Third, governance: since agents work around the clock, rule-checking must be automatic and instant, with special watchdog agents reviewing the work and humans setting the rules and handling exceptions. Fourth, people and culture: employees shift from doing tasks to steering outcomes, performance gets judged on results unlocked rather than boxes ticked, and shared values keep everyone — human and machine — pulling in the same direction. Fifth, technology: build on shared platforms of reusable agents connected by common communication standards, and keep your core knowledge independent of any single software vendor whose product may be outdated within weeks.

## Where it is used

The article's flagship example is a bank of the future, where buying a house triggers a relay of agents handling property search, mortgage tailoring, compliance checks, contracting, and loan fulfillment under human supervision. Real early cases already exist: a European utility's assistant serves three million customers with shorter waits and happier callers, a global bank runs customer background checks with ten squads of agents, and another bank modernized old computer systems in half the usual time with agents overseen by people. Similar agent squads are modernizing systems at a European carmaker and a public-sector body.

## Takeaways

Reorganize around outcomes, not departments; keep humans in charge of goals, trade-offs, and exceptions while agents execute; make oversight automatic and instant rather than periodic; treat your proprietary data as the true competitive moat; and start now with one or two bold pilot areas instead of dozens of disconnected experiments.

## Jargon decoder

- **Agentic organization:** a company redesigned so humans and Artificial Intelligence agents create value together, the article's name for the next era of business organization.
- **Agent factory:** a large set of specialized agents, typically 50 to 100, supervised by a tiny human team to run one process end to end.
- **Above the loop:** the human role of steering and overseeing work rather than doing each step, as opposed to being in the loop of every action.
- **Hyperpersonalization:** tailoring products and services to each individual in real time, here done by agents that learn continuously.
- **Walled data garden:** a company's private stock of data not available on the public internet, which becomes its edge as public models commoditize.
- **Agentic Artificial Intelligence mesh:** a shared platform of reusable agents and data that teams across the company can draw on.
- **Agent-to-agent protocols:** common languages letting agents and systems talk to each other without expensive custom-built connections.
- **Lighthouse domain:** one business area chosen for the first bold end-to-end transformation, meant to teach the organization how to change.
