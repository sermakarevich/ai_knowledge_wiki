# The State of AI in 2026: On the Road to ROI — Summary

**Article:** [The state of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai) — McKinsey QuantumBlack, August 25, 2026

McKinsey's ninth annual global AI survey (1,719 respondents, 97 countries, fielded May 4–June 8, 2026) finds organizations deepening their use of AI — more enterprise-wide scaling, more agentic tools, more in-house software building — while enterprise-level profit impact has barely moved. Individual employees report real personal benefit; companies overall do not yet show it on the bottom line, except for a small "high performer" segment that transforms workflows rather than bolting AI onto existing ones.

## Key takeaways

- **Adoption keeps widening.** 44% of respondents now report AI scaling across their enterprise (up from 38%), led by large ($1B+ revenue) organizations, which scale agentic AI at nearly double the rate of smaller ones (40% vs. 22%).
- **Coding agents are changing build-vs-buy.** 32% of respondents say their organization decided against buying a software product or feature because it could be built in-house with agentic coding tools.
- **Individual gains, flat enterprise impact.** 80% say AI has improved their own productivity, but only 37% of organizations report any positive EBIT impact — unchanged from 2025.
- **Costs are starting to bite, but investment keeps rising.** ~20% of respondents say operating costs (including tokens) constrain their AI use; 60% still expect to increase AI investment next year.
- **High performers (6% of respondents) look different.** They pursue growth/innovation alongside efficiency, redesign workflows (74% vs. 25% of others), invest more, and manage AI risk more actively.
- **Workforce fears were overstated — but are rising again.** Only 14% report actual AI-driven head-count declines in the past year, versus the 32% who expected declines a year ago; 39% now expect declines in the coming year.

## Bottom line

Conviction in AI is outrunning proven financial return. The organizations most likely to close that gap are the ones treating AI as a reason to redesign how work gets done — not just another tool layered onto the existing org chart.
