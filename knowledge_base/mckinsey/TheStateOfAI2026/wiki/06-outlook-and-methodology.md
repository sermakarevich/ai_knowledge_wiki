# Outlook and Survey Methodology

**In one sentence:** Organizations' conviction in AI is outpacing the financial returns they can attribute to it, and closing that gap requires transforming workflows, not just adopting tools; this page also covers how the underlying survey was conducted.

## Key points

- Organizations keep scaling AI deployments more broadly and deeply, expanding use of more capable technologies and increasing investment, even though the share reporting meaningful EBIT (earnings before interest and taxes — a measure of core profit) impact has stayed essentially unchanged.
- AI capabilities are giving organizations more options to build rather than buy enterprise software, while also forcing them to develop new disciplines for managing AI's costs.
- Companies are becoming more willing to make organizational changes that could enable bottom-line effects, with more respondents expecting AI to reshape their business over the next three years than a year ago.
- Organizations continue to plan further increases in AI investment despite the unchanged near-term financial-impact numbers.
- As AI becomes embedded in more business processes and employees grow more fluent with it, only organizations that transform their businesses (not just adopt AI tools) are likely to convert individual productivity gains into lasting enterprise-level financial performance.
- The survey behind these findings ran online from May 4 to June 8, 2026, across 1,719 respondents in 97 nations, weighted by each nation's contribution to global GDP.

---

## The closing argument

Organizations' conviction in AI is growing faster than the immediate financial returns they can attribute to it. Companies continue to scale AI deployments more broadly and deeply across the enterprise, expand their use of increasingly capable technologies, and increase their investments — even as the share reporting meaningful EBIT impact remains essentially unchanged so far. Organizations are discovering that AI capabilities not only give them more options for building rather than buying enterprise software but also increasingly require them to develop new disciplines for managing AI's costs. At the same time, companies are becoming more willing to make the kinds of organizational changes that can enable bottom-line effects: more expect AI to reshape their business over the next three years than did a year ago, and they continue to plan to invest more.

As AI becomes embedded in more business processes and employees become increasingly fluent in using it, the organizations that translate individual productivity gains into lasting enterprise-level financial performance are likely to be those that transform their businesses, not just adopt AI tools.

## About the research

The online survey was in the field from May 4 to June 8, 2026, and garnered responses from 1,719 participants in 97 nations representing the full range of regions, industries, company sizes, functional specialties, and tenures. Thirty-six percent of respondents say they work for organizations with more than $1 billion in annual revenue. To adjust for differences in response rates, the data are weighted by the contribution of each respondent's nation to global GDP.

The report's authors are Dan Tinkoff (global leader of QuantumBlack, AI by McKinsey; senior partner, New Jersey office), Lieven Van der Veken (global leader of QuantumBlack, AI by McKinsey; senior partner, Lyon office), Michael Chui (senior fellow, Bay Area office), and Tara Balakrishnan (associate partner, Seattle office). This survey is part of an annual McKinsey Global Survey series on the state of AI running since roughly 2017, with prior editions published annually from 2020 through 2025.
