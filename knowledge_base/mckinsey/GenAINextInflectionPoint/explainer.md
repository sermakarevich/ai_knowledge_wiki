# Explainer — Gen AI's next inflection point

## What it is

In August 2024, four McKinsey consultants reported on a survey of 592 employees about generative artificial intelligence (gen AI) — the chatbots and copilots that write, summarize, and code on demand. Their headline finding is a mismatch: workers have already woven these tools into their jobs, but their employers have barely reorganized around them. The article is a playbook for closing that gap by redesigning how the company operates, training people role by role, and governing the whole effort like any major transformation.

## Why it matters

Think of a company that hands every employee a power drill but never redesigns the assembly line: lots of enthusiastic drilling, no faster factory. That is where the survey says most firms stand — 91 percent of employees use gen AI at work, yet only 13 percent of organizations have deployed it in six or more real use cases. Because competitors that do reorganize will compound productivity, personalization, and service advantages, standing still gets riskier the longer the lag lasts.

## How it works

The playbook has three moves. First, pick whole workflows — product development, marketing, customer service — and rebuild them end to end around gen AI instead of sprinkling disconnected pilots; Walmart, for example, aimed its entire effort at just two areas, shopper experience and employee experience. Second, retrain the workforce in place rather than trying to hire your way out, since the shift touches every role: engineers learn to supervise code-writing systems, marketers learn machine-assisted personalization, and managers get AI coaching assistants. One executive's budgeting rule captures the emphasis: five dollars on people for every one dollar on technology. Third, make it stick with a central oversight team that vets ideas and kills weak ones, clear accountability and progress reviews, and deliberate culture work — leaders visibly using the tools, plain explanations of why change is happening, training, and bonuses tied to AI-driven results.

## Where used

The article illustrates each move with field cases: Walmart's shopping-list generators and associate assistant apps; Asian banks that mapped spare capacity role by role before designing retraining; a European phone company whose AI coach graded service agents on 20 skills and cut handling time by a tenth while lifting satisfaction by a fifth; and a multinational bank that folded AI use into performance reviews. The pattern generalizes to any large organization where work crosses department boundaries.

## Takeaways

Individual enthusiasm cannot substitute for institutional change, and tools alone create no value without redesigned processes. The winning unit of change is the end-to-end workflow, the winning talent strategy is reskilling in place, and the winning governance is centralized but business-led. Start now, because the penalty for lagging grows with each wave of the technology.

## Jargon decoder

Gen AI means AI systems that generate text, images, or code from a prompt. A domain is a complete workflow or customer journey, such as resolving a support case, rather than a single task. Early adopters are firms with six or more deployed gen AI use cases; experimenters have one to five. A center of excellence is a central expert team that sets standards and spreads lessons. The influence model is McKinsey's four-lever formula for changing behavior: leaders model it, people understand it, skills enable it, and incentives reinforce it.
