## Key points

- The most-cited enabler of future gen AI adoption is better integration into existing systems at 60 percent of respondents, framing stickiness as an infrastructure problem, not an enthusiasm problem.
- Governance starts with a centralized structure overseeing AI adoption, sometimes led by a chief AI officer: 91 percent of early adopters report some governance structure versus 77 percent of experimenters.
- A gen-AI-dedicated center of excellence aligns vision with execution and runs the experiment portfolio — deciding which experiments to scale or stop on priorities and risks — as a multinational bank's business-led unit of technology, AI, and risk experts demonstrates.
- That bank also hardwired adoption by integrating active gen AI use into performance evaluations, creating formal commitment rather than optional experimentation.
- Transformation discipline means defined infrastructure, roles, and measurement criteria, accountability inside business units, and a regular monitor-and-adjust cadence.
- Mindset and behavior change runs through the influence model's four tenets, where early adopters lead on all fronts: role modeling 89 versus 70 percent, conviction 95 versus 88, capabilities 86 versus 63, and enabling operating model 79 versus 52.
- The closing warning is temporal: with employees already asking for more and some firms capturing value, "by gen AI's next inflection point, the downside of lagging behind — and missing out on gen AI's potential benefits — may be even greater."

## The stickiness problem

"Our survey indicates that the most useful enabler of future adoption is better integration of gen AI into existing systems, cited by 60 percent of respondents. To make gen AI changes stick, organizations need the right infrastructure to support continuous change and win over hearts and minds." Infrastructure and persuasion are presented as joint requirements, addressed by the three moves below.

## Governance: centralize, then run the portfolio

"The first step is establishing the right governance for gen AI," meaning "a centralized structure that oversees the organization's AI adoption, sometimes with a chief AI officer leading these efforts." Adoption data: "Nearly all early-adopter respondents (91 percent) say they have implemented some governance structure for gen AI, compared with a smaller share (77 percent) of experimenters." The recommended vehicle — "a centralized model with a gen-AI-dedicated center of excellence" — "helps align AI vision with execution" and "facilitates the implementation of strategy, continuous measurement, adaptation to new insights, and further experimentation — specifically, which experiments to scale or to stop, based on priorities and risks."

## The bank that operationalized governance

"To enhance its productivity with gen AI, a leading multinational bank identified the processes with the highest potential for improvement. This exercise enabled the development of a clear strategy and road map and of a business-led center of excellence, including experts in technology, AI, and risk management. The center of excellence evaluates use cases, implements AI guardrails, tracks metrics, and shares knowledge across the organization. What's more, the bank integrated active use of gen AI into performance evaluations, ensuring a formal commitment to AI integration."

## Run it like a transformation

"The second step is treating these changes like a true transformation. This means defining the transformation's infrastructure, roles, and measurement criteria; ensuring accountability within business units; and implementing a regular cadence to monitor progress — and adjusting as needed."

## Mindsets: the influence model in a gen AI key

"Third is addressing employee mindsets and behaviors across the organization," grounded in "extensive transformation research": "changing mindsets and behaviors is vital to any successful transformation." Early adopters "focus more than others on the four tenets of the influence model": role modeling 89 versus 70 percent; fostering understanding and conviction 95 versus 88; building capabilities 86 versus 63; reinforcing new ways of working via an enabling operating model 79 versus 52. In gen AI terms: leaders visibly using AI (a CEO streamlining workflows, executives using AI analytics in reviews); communicating the why through town halls and success-story videos; training from data analysis to internal AI boot camps with online-education partners; and wiring AI goals into performance metrics, KPIs, and bonuses — e.g., sales targets with AI-driven segmentation and lead generation plus rewards for effective AI use.

## The time to act is now

"No matter where an organization is on its gen AI journey, the time for making transformational change is now. Employees are already asking their organizations for more, and some companies have begun moving from experimentation to value capture." The mechanism for capturing momentum: "addressing organizational barriers to adoption, which requires no less than fundamentally transforming the company's operations and preparing people for continuous change."
