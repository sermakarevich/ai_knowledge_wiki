**In one sentence:** Companies should translate gen AI vision into value by transforming whole domains — end-to-end workflows like product development or customer service — rather than scattering one-off use cases, because domain-level change spanning organizational boundaries delivers greater returns.

## Key points

- Full gen AI benefits, from faster innovation and higher productivity to better employee and customer experience, arrive only through transformative changes to the operating model: processes, ways of working, capabilities, and culture.
- The prescribed unit of transformation is the domain — a specific workflow, process, journey, or occasionally an entire function such as product development, marketing, or customer service.
- Domain-level implementation beats one-off solutions because domains often span organizational boundaries, letting one integrated effort capture end-to-end value across silos.
- In software development, gen AI can compress delivery from months to days via prompt-based proofs of concept, automated requirements, self-writing code, and continuous testing, recasting engineers from task completers into systems designers.
- In marketing, gen AI enables true personalization at scale — Netflix-style hyperpersonalized previews and Spotify-style playlists — which can dissolve the silo between creative and analytics teams while lifting content productivity and funnel ROI.
- In customer service, AI-empowered humans working alongside gen AI agents become empathetic problem solvers and experience supervisors, balancing commercial and empathetic mindsets in closer partnership with product teams.
- Gen AI also transforms cross-cutting domains such as team management, where coaching prompts at managers' fingertips shift time from administrative to-dos toward check-ins and soft-skill development.

---

## Why the operating model, not the tool

"Companies can only reap gen AI's full benefits, which range from faster innovation and enhanced productivity to improved employee and customer experience, when they use technology to make transformative changes." The article insists on "holistic changes to the operating model, including key processes, ways of working, capabilities, and culture" — tool distribution without these changes reproduces the piecemeal trap the CIO anecdote diagnosed.

## The domain as the unit of transformation

"To start, companies should prioritize the right unit of transformation by focusing on specific domains, such as product development, marketing, and customer service. This domain-based approach allows for end-to-end, technology-led transformation that integrates multiple use cases within a single value-creating workflow, process, journey, or, occasionally, entire function." The economic logic: "Since domains often span organizational boundaries, implementing gen AI and other technologies at the domain level can deliver greater value than one-off solutions."

## Software development: days instead of months

"Gen AI can revolutionize work by delivering higher-quality, resilient products much faster; think days instead of months." Preconditions include changes across the product life cycle and closer product–engineering collaboration. Mechanisms named: comprehensive product data, prompt-based proofs of concept, automated requirements shortening ideation-to-prototyping and enabling more iterations, plus "self-writing code, autogenerated user guidance, and continuous code testing" that "transform engineers from task completers to systems designers."

## Marketing: personalization at scale, silos dissolved

"Gen AI could (finally) enable the vision of true personalization at scale," with Netflix and Spotify cited as early movers via hyperpersonalized previews and playlists. Payoffs listed: engagement, loyalty, seamless brand integration into customers' lives, content-creation productivity, and "ROI across the sales and marketing funnel." Organizational consequence: "the marketing organization's silos could break down, especially between the creative and analytics teams."

## Customer service: centers of delight

"Gen AI can transform teams into centers of customer delight by proactively addressing issues and offering new products, all at reduced costs." The future role: "AI-empowered humans will work with gen AI agents, using real-time trends and customer insights to become empathetic problem solvers and supervisors of customer experience." Agents and supervisors will "make more use of technology, apply systems thinking, balance empathetic and commercial mindsets, and work more closely with the customer experience and product teams."

## Cross-cutting domains: management itself

"Gen AI is also set to revolutionize cross-cutting domains, such as performance management and team management," notably by putting "coaching prompts at managers' fingertips" with easier access to employee resources — shifting managerial time "less on administrative to-dos, more on checking in with team members and developing soft skills."
