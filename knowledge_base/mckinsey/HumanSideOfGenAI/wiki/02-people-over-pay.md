# People over pay: what gen AI talent wants and why it may leave

**In one sentence:** Fifty-one percent of gen AI creators and heavy users plan to quit within three to six months — not for higher pay, which ranks only seventh as an attractor, but for workplace flexibility, meaningful work, caring leaders, and well-being — while 55 percent already report clinical-level burnout, far above the 32 percent global sample rate.

## Key points

- The 51-percent quit intent among creators and heavy users dwarfs the broader workforce figure of 34 percent planning to leave (itself down from 40 percent in 2021).
- The article explicitly busts the myth that these workers leave for better compensation elsewhere: pay ranks seventh as a reason to take a job, versus third for the broader workforce.
- The top stay factors for stayers (n = 475) are workplace flexibility at 40 percent, meaningful work at 33 percent, health and well-being support at 31 percent, and safe workplace at 29 percent.
- The top attraction factors are relational: reliable and supportive people at 50 percent, caring and inspiring leaders at 45 percent, meaningful work at 39 percent, and inclusive community at 37 percent.
- Among stayers, 72 percent feel engaged at work (versus 63 percent overall) — yet 55 percent report clinical burnout versus 32 percent globally, an engaged-but-exhausted paradox.
- McKinsey analysis notes high disengagement and dissatisfaction can cost companies millions of dollars a year, making retention of this scarce group financially material.
- "People won't come just for the money, and they certainly won't stay for it" — the retention lever is flexibility plus belonging, care, and reliability in the work community.

---

## The flight risk in numbers

### A sobering 51 percent

"Of self-identified gen AI creators and heavy users, 51 percent of respondents to our latest survey say they plan to leave in the next three to six months." Context: in 2021, "40 percent of respondents across jobs, industries, and geographies said they planned to quit"; that figure "has since dropped to 34 percent" — but the gen AI vanguard remains far above it. "Early creators and heavy adopters, in particular, wield power when it comes to job choice and shaping their careers."

### The compensation myth, busted

"Many company leaders believe that workers in these groups are leaving at higher rates because they can find better compensation elsewhere. Yet an examination of the employee-value-proposition (EVP) factors that resonate most with these segments busts the myth, once again, that compensation is a primary motivator." The pandemic-era shift persists: "Employees increasingly value relational elements such as caring leaders and coworkers, as well as support for health and well-being, more than compensation (though pay is always important)."

## What makes them stay

### Flexibility first, relationships close behind

"They stay in their jobs when they are given flexibility, and they leave when they aren't." Supporting factors: "meaningful work, support for health and well-being, reliable and supportive coworkers, and a safe workplace environment." Exhibit 2 full ranking (top-3 reason to stay, n = 475): flexibility 40, meaningful work 33, well-being 31, safe workplace 29, career development 27, reliable people 27, compensation package 23, caring leaders 23, sustainable expectations 21, inclusive community 18, resource access 15, geographic ties 15.

### Engaged but exhausted

"When we dig deeper into self-identified heavy users and creators who are staying in their jobs, we find that a healthy 72 percent report feeling engaged at work, compared with 63 percent in our total survey sample. However, a worrying 55 percent report clinical levels of burnout, a much higher rate than that of the global sample at 32 percent. In other words, companies may not be getting the productivity and engagement they expect from these workers."

## What attracts them to a new job

For the broader workforce the top four take-a-job factors resemble the stay factors, but for heavy users and creators "there is a stronger emphasis on relationships with managers and peers, and on a sense of community more broadly." Exhibit 3 (n = 962, started current job 2015 or later): reliable people 50, caring leaders 45, meaningful work 39, inclusive community 37, flexibility 35, well-being 20, compensation 17, career advancement 16, sustainable expectations 15, safe workplace 12, resources 7, geography 7.
