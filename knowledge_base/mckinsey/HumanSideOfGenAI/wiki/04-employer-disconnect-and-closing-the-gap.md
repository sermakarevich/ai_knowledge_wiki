# The disconnect and how leaders close it: build in-house, humanize the job

**In one sentence:** Fifty-seven percent of employers plan to close gen AI skill gaps in-house through upskilling, reskilling, and redeployment versus 30 percent via hiring and contracting — yet the very people they must retain plan to leave — so leaders must answer three questions (reimagine jobs as human-centric, redefine flexibility around results, listen continuously) against a backdrop where gen AI may automate up to 30 percent of work activities by 2030.

## Key points

- Employers favor in-house capability building at 57 percent (upskilling 28, reskilling 23, redeploying 6) over externally driven routes at 30 percent (hiring 19, contracting 11), per n = 3,112 responding employers.
- The strategy contains a contradiction: "it's hard to reskill and upskill people when they are looking to leave," with 51 percent of creators and heavy users eyeing the exit.
- McKinsey research puts the automation horizon at up to 30 percent of business activities across occupations by 2030, framing the urgency.
- "Thriving stars" — high performers who inspire others and spur collaboration, innovation, and better decisions — make up as little as 4 percent of organizations and must be placed where they multiply performance.
- Redefining flexibility means judging outcomes not hours: the article openly asks whether meaningful weekly work could fit in 20 hours and whether the 40-hour week remains the benchmark.
- Mapping gen AI workers onto EVP factors shows care for family gaining the most importance and compensation losing the most, with feeling valued by a manager, development access, and meaningful work rising.
- Only about 4 percent of respondents fear job displacement (7 percent among 18-to-24-year-olds) — an undercurrent of worry the authors recast as an opening for leaders to start an honest dialogue.

---

## The build-versus-buy split

### In-house wins the survey

"Our survey of executives found that most organizations plan to build their gen AI capabilities internally, through upskilling, reskilling, and redeploying talent, more than by external hiring and contracting (Exhibit 5)." The nuance: "some subpopulations, such as programmers and software engineers, may be best brought in through hiring while other types of workers, such as associates and customer experience specialists, will benefit more from upskilling and reskilling."

### The retention contradiction

"The problem is that if companies want to build gen AI skills with the employees they already have, they need to retain the very people who, according to the survey, have indicated that they plan to leave in the next three to six months."

## The three leadership questions

### 1. Reimagine jobs to be more human-centric

"Begin by defining which tasks people should do, which tasks gen AI can do, and how humans should manage other people as well as gen AI usage itself. Technological skills such as coding will be the baseline for many jobs, but social–emotional skills and higher cognitive skills will be the differentiators for creative, collaborative work in the future." "Thriving stars" enter here: "Workers who perform at high levels and inspire others — we call them 'thriving stars' — help spur collaboration, innovation, and better decision making. However, they make up as little as 4 percent of organizations."

### 2. Redefine flexibility around results

"As jobs change, companies will need to look at worker outcomes according to the results achieved, not by hours spent. The benchmark for output will have to shift. For instance, some written code may be longer, but it may not necessarily be better or more user friendly." The provocation: "could an employee's meaningful work in a given week be completed in as little as 20 hours? And if that's the case, is the 40-hour workweek still the benchmark? Rather than filling hours with tasks to get to a specific number in a given week, companies can focus on ways to emphasize the distinctive, creative part of a job that makes it meaningful."

### 3. Listen instead of assuming

"Talking with employees rather than leading by assumption. Creating a constantly evolving dialogue can help with both problem-solving and morale. This is particularly relevant as the gen AI talent pool expands." On displacement fears: "Survey respondents overwhelmingly express enthusiasm about the integration of gen AI into their workplaces, though approximately 4 percent say they are concerned about job displacement (rising to 7 percent for workers aged 18 to 24). This undercurrent of worry presents an opportunity for leaders to engage workers about the potential changes gen AI will bring."

## The EVP gap behind it all

"This gap between what employees say they want in a job and what employers are willing to offer them has been central to the workplace experience since the pandemic erupted." Exhibit 6 shows gen AI workers diverging most on care for family (up) and compensation (down), with "feeling valued by a manager, having access to development opportunities, and doing meaningful work" also rising, while "advancement opportunities, on the other hand, are not as highly valued."
