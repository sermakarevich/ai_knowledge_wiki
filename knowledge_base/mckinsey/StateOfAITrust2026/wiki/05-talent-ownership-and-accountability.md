# Skills gaps and accountability

**In one sentence:** Nearly 60 percent name knowledge and training gaps as the top barrier to implementing responsible Artificial Intelligence, while organizations with explicit ownership score 2.6 on maturity against just 1.8 for those without clear accountability.

## Key points

- Nearly 60 percent of respondents cite knowledge and training gaps as the primary barrier to implementing responsible practices, up from about 50 percent last year, making it the leading blocker.
- Executive support has improved, yet firms still lack the skills, awareness, and operational muscle to embed responsible Artificial Intelligence consistently across teams.
- Organizations with clear ownership, especially Artificial Intelligence-specific governance roles or internal audit and ethics teams, average 2.6 on maturity, the highest of any accountability structure.
- Organizations with no clearly accountable function average just 1.8, a 0.8-point gap that the article ties to explicit ownership and decision rights.
- The training gap widening year over year suggests demand for responsible-Artificial Intelligence skills is growing faster than supply or internal upskilling.
- Audit and ethics teams scoring at the top implies independent oversight functions adapt well to Artificial Intelligence governance when given the mandate.
- Together the two findings prescribe the fix: named owners plus broad workforce training, since leadership support alone does not reach the teams doing the work.

## The knowledge barrier

### Training gaps widen

"Nearly 60 percent of respondents cite knowledge and training gaps as the primary barrier to implementing responsible Artificial Intelligence practices, up from about 50 percent last year." The article notes that "while executive support has improved, the data suggests that organizations continue to struggle with building the skills, awareness, and operational muscle required to embed responsible Artificial Intelligence consistently across teams." Support from the top is necessary but not sufficient; the bottleneck sits in the middle layers and practitioner ranks.

## Ownership and maturity

### Named owners outperform

"Organizations that assign clear ownership for responsible Artificial Intelligence — particularly through Artificial Intelligence-specific governance roles or internal audit and ethics teams — exhibit the highest average maturity levels, with an average score of 2.6." Against that, "organizations without a clearly accountable function lag behind materially (scoring an average of just 1.8), reinforcing the importance of explicit ownership and decision rights." The 0.8-point spread is among the largest single-factor differences in the survey, making accountability the highest-leverage organizational intervention the article identifies.
