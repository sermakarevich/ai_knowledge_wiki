# Trust as business enabler and the road ahead

**In one sentence:** Respondents report positive business, efficiency, and customer-trust outcomes from Artificial Intelligence trust work more often than negative ones, regulatory pressure as a motive is fading, and the article closes by arguing early investment in trustworthy innovation decides who captures long-term agentic value.

## Key points

- Respondents report improvements in business outcomes, operational efficiency, and customer trust more frequently than negative outcomes from trust initiatives.
- The perceived influence of some regulatory frameworks has declined, signaling a move from compliance-led to value- and performance-driven motives for trust work.
- The closing argument warns that governance and risk gaps will grow more costly as systems become more autonomous and embedded in critical workflows.
- Organizations lacking accountability, controls, and monitoring face slower adoption, higher incident impact, and weaker stakeholder trust.
- Trust capability cannot be bought and installed; it demands a combined program of policies, processes, people, and technology.
- The prize is framed as building foundations for "agents, robots, and people to work together in new ways", the human-machine collaboration of the agentic era.
- The verdict: "building a trustworthy innovation engine early will define those who capture long-term value from Artificial Intelligence in the agentic era."

---

## From compliance to value

### The enabler shift

"Respondents report improvements in business outcomes, operational efficiency, and customer trust more frequently than negative outcomes." At the same time, "the perceived influence of some regulatory frameworks has declined, suggesting a shift from compliance-led motivation toward value- and performance-driven adoption of Artificial Intelligence trust." Trust work is being justified by returns rather than by fear of the regulator — consistent with the investment finding that large responsible-Artificial Intelligence spenders see earnings impact above 5 percent.

## Looking ahead

### Trust as the enabler of scale

The article warns: "As Artificial Intelligence systems become more autonomous and embedded in critical workflows, gaps in governance and risk management will become increasingly costly." Firms that skip the foundations "risk slower adoption, higher incident impact, and diminished stakeholder trust." The alternative: treat trust "as a core business capability, rather than as a compliance requirement", which leaves organizations "better positioned to scale Artificial Intelligence adoption to its full potential". The capabilities "require a concerted combination of policies, processes, people, and technology to build the foundations needed for agents, robots, and people to work together in new ways". Final line: "Building a trustworthy innovation engine early will define those who capture long-term value from Artificial Intelligence in the agentic era."
