# Questions — State of AI trust in 2026: Shifting to the agentic era

## Q1. What was the survey's sample and how is maturity scored?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

About 500 organizations surveyed December 2025–January 2026, respondents with direct governance, risk, or investment responsibility; scored 0–4 across five responsible-Artificial Intelligence dimensions with four maturity levels. See [[wiki/01-survey-and-maturity-model|Survey design and the AI Trust Maturity Model]].
</details>

## Q2. What are the headline overall-maturity numbers?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Average 2.3 in 2026, up from 2.0 in 2025; only ~30 percent reach level 3+ in strategy, governance, and agentic controls. See [[wiki/01-survey-and-maturity-model|Survey design and the AI Trust Maturity Model]].
</details>

## Q3. Why does the article say the agentic era changes the trust problem?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Because systems now do things, not just say things — unintended actions, tool misuse, operating beyond guardrails — so failures carry material operational consequences rather than just wrong outputs. See [[wiki/01-survey-and-maturity-model|Survey design and the AI Trust Maturity Model]].
</details>

## Q4. Which industries and regions lead, and what gap is global?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Technology, media, and telecom plus financial services lead by industry; Asia–Pacific leads by region; governance and agentic controls trail data and technology in every region. See [[wiki/02-maturity-by-industry-region-and-investment|Maturity by industry, region, and investment]].
</details>

## Q5. What investment threshold separates leaders, and what payoff is reported?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

$25 million or more into responsible Artificial Intelligence: significantly higher maturity and far likelier material benefits including earnings impact above 5 percent. See [[wiki/02-maturity-by-industry-region-and-investment|Maturity by industry, region, and investment]].
</details>

## Q6. What is the top barrier to scaling agentic Artificial Intelligence, and what trails it?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Security and risk concerns, cited by nearly two-thirds; regulatory uncertainty and technical limitations rank well behind. See [[wiki/03-barriers-and-top-risks|Blockers to agentic scale and the top-cited risks]].
</details>

## Q7. Which two risks top the relevance ranking, at what shares?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Inaccuracy at 74 percent and cybersecurity at 72 percent. See [[wiki/03-barriers-and-top-risks|Blockers to agentic scale and the top-cited risks]].
</details>

## Q8. Where is the awareness-versus-mitigation gap widest?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Intellectual property infringement and personal privacy — risks widely rated relevant but rarely actively mitigated. See [[wiki/04-awareness-mitigation-and-incidents|The mitigation gap and incident response]].
</details>

## Q9. Incidents are flat at ~8 percent, so why is the article worried?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Because response quality deteriorated: almost 60 percent of affected respondents rate their organization's response satisfactory or negative, meaning expected harm per incident rises as systems grow more complex. See [[wiki/04-awareness-mitigation-and-incidents|The mitigation gap and incident response]].
</details>

## Q10. What blocks responsible-Artificial Intelligence implementation most, and how did it move?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Knowledge and training gaps at nearly 60 percent, up from ~50 percent last year, despite improved executive support. See [[wiki/05-talent-ownership-and-accountability|Skills gaps and accountability]].
</details>

## Q11. What is the maturity spread between owned and ownerless governance?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

2.6 average with explicit ownership (Artificial Intelligence-specific roles or audit/ethics teams) versus 1.8 with no accountable function. See [[wiki/05-talent-ownership-and-accountability|Skills gaps and accountability]].
</details>

## Q12. What evidence supports calling trust a business enabler?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Positive business, efficiency, and customer-trust outcomes reported more often than negative ones, plus fading influence of regulatory frameworks as a motive — value-driven rather than compliance-driven adoption. See [[wiki/06-trust-as-business-enabler|Trust as business enabler and the road ahead]].
</details>

## Q13. Transfer: your firm pilots agents well but cannot get sign-off for production. What does this article prescribe?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Treat the blocker as a trust deficit, not a capability gap: name an accountable owner, fund training and monitoring, close the mitigation gap on the flagged risks, and run incident-response drills — the levers the survey ties to maturity — before adding more agents. See [[wiki/05-talent-ownership-and-accountability|Skills gaps and accountability]] and [[wiki/06-trust-as-business-enabler|Trust as business enabler and the road ahead]].
</details>

## Q14. Transfer: where should a fixed governance budget go first — model upgrades or oversight — and why?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Oversight: the survey shows deployment confidence, not model capability, gates scale (two-thirds cite security/risk), and $25M+ responsibility investors see the earnings payoff, so assurance buys more deployment than better models do. See [[wiki/02-maturity-by-industry-region-and-investment|Maturity by industry, region, and investment]] and [[wiki/03-barriers-and-top-risks|Blockers to agentic scale and the top-cited risks]].
</details>
