# State of AI trust in 2026: Shifting to the agentic era

**Article:** [State of AI trust in 2026: Shifting to the agentic era](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/tech-forward/state-of-ai-trust-in-2026-shifting-to-the-agentic-era)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine giving office keys to a brilliant new hire who works at lightning speed but sometimes misunderstands instructions. That is what companies are doing with Artificial Intelligence agents, software that takes actions on its own rather than just answering questions. McKinsey asked about 500 organizations how well they supervise these new workers and found the supervision is falling behind: grades improved slightly, but only a third of firms do oversight well. The good news is that companies treating supervision as an investment instead of a chore report better profits and happier customers.

---

## TL;DR

McKinsey's 2026 Artificial Intelligence Trust Maturity Survey scores roughly 500 organizations on responsible Artificial Intelligence practices across five dimensions, adding agentic Artificial Intelligence governance for the first time. Average maturity rose to 2.3 from 2.0, yet only about 30 percent reach level 3 or higher in strategy, governance, and agentic controls. Security and risk worries block agentic scale for nearly two-thirds of respondents, mitigation lags awareness nearly everywhere, and named ownership plus serious investment separate the leaders from the rest.

---

## Problem & Motivation

As Artificial Intelligence moves from experimentation to scaled deployment across core functions, systems are gaining autonomy: they recommend, trigger actions, and interact with other systems on their own. This shifts the failure mode from saying the wrong thing to doing the wrong thing, with materially larger consequences. Trust and the responsible practices behind it therefore become the precondition for capturing Artificial Intelligence value rather than an optional extra. The article addresses the gap between rapid agentic adoption and the governance needed to run it safely, using a fresh survey to show where organizations stand and what separates leaders.

---

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Agentic governance as a fifth maturity dimension.** The 2026 model adds agentic Artificial Intelligence governance and controls to the classic four of strategy, risk management, data and technology, and governance, making autonomous-system oversight a scored discipline for the first time.
2. **Doing-versus-saying risk framing.** The article recasts the trust problem around autonomous action — unintended actions, tool misuse, operation beyond guardrails — rather than output correctness alone, which reorders priorities toward monitoring and response.
3. **Trust spending as value engine.** The finding that investors of 25 million dollars or more show higher maturity and earnings impact above 5 percent reframes responsibility budgets from an innovation tax into a driver of returns.
4. **The readiness diagnosis.** Flat incident frequency near 8 percent paired with deteriorating response confidence shows the binding constraint is not detecting risks but responding to them as system complexity grows.

---

## Key Findings

| Finding | Number |
| ------- | ------ |
| Average maturity 2026 vs 2025 | 2.3 vs 2.0 on 0–4 scale |
| Organizations at level 3+ in strategy, governance, agentic controls | ~30 percent |
| Top barrier to scaling agentic Artificial Intelligence (security/risk) | ~two-thirds |
| Inaccuracy / cybersecurity cited as highly relevant | 74 / 72 percent |
| Organizations reporting Artificial Intelligence incidents | ~8 percent |
| Incident experiencers rating response satisfactory or negative | ~60 percent |
| Knowledge/training gaps as top implementation barrier | ~60 percent, up from ~50 percent |
| Maturity with explicit ownership vs none | 2.6 vs 1.8 |
| Investment threshold tied to higher maturity and value | $25M+, earnings impact above 5 percent |

- Maturity leaders by industry are technology, media, and telecom plus financial services; Asia–Pacific leads by region while governance trails technology everywhere.
- The awareness-to-mitigation gap spans nearly every risk category and is widest for intellectual property infringement and personal privacy.
- Positive business, efficiency, and customer-trust outcomes are reported more often than negative ones, and regulatory frameworks carry less motivational weight than before.

---

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. Establish explicit accountability for responsible Artificial Intelligence through dedicated governance roles or empowered audit and ethics teams.
2. Close the skills gap with broad training that reaches practitioner ranks, since executive support alone has not moved the needle.
3. Convert risk awareness into active mitigation, starting with the widest gaps in privacy and intellectual property controls.
4. Build incident response capacity for agent-driven failures, treating readiness as the priority given flat frequency but falling confidence.
5. Fund trust as a value program with policies, processes, people, and technology combined, and benchmark progress with the maturity model; open questions include how smaller firms replicate these results affordably and whether the investment-value link survives controlled causal testing.
