# Reconfiguring work: change management in the age of gen AI

**Article:** [Reconfiguring work: change management in the age of gen AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/reconfiguring-work-change-management-in-the-age-of-gen-ai)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine a company buys every employee a professional kitchen oven, then wonders why the food is no better — because nobody rewrote the recipes, relabeled the ingredients, or taught the cooks new dishes. That is the article's picture of generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) at work today: two-thirds of companies own the oven, yet almost none taste the difference. The fix it prescribes is a five-part kitchen rebuild led from the top: declare what the new restaurant will serve, earn cooks' trust with labeled ingredients and safety rules, rewrite recipes around the oven, decide which stations go fully automatic and which keep human chefs with super-tools, and get the whole staff inventing dishes together.

---

## TL;DR

Erik Roth argues that Gen AI adoption has stalled at experimentation because leaders treat a capability as a tool, and offers a five-step chief-executive-led change program: an outcome-based North Star assuming humans plus agents, trust via grounded answers plus accessible-but-governed data, workflow redesign through a three-phase agent-to-swarm evolution, a structural split into minimum viable organizations and augmented teams, and mass participation driven by superusers and visible leader use. Evidence comes from McKinsey surveys (participation-rate economics, generational expertise splits) and two worked cases: McKinsey's internal Lilli platform at 92 percent adoption with nearly 17,000 employee-built agents, and Singtel's academy training over 10,000 staff. The core result is a decision architecture for converting pilots into company-wide value, with trust positioned as the gating precondition for scale.

---

## Problem & Motivation

The article addresses the gap between broad Gen AI adoption and missing business impact: two-thirds of global companies use Gen AI somewhere, yet leaders cannot convert potential into bottom-line value, and employees already use the technology three times more than their leaders realize. This matters because Gen AI behaves unlike any prior enterprise software — its natural-language ease invites shallow piloting while its compounding agentic abilities demand deep reconfiguration — so license-rollout habits actively waste the investment and leave ungoverned shadow use exposed. Without a people-first change approach, experimentation never becomes acceleration and the promised growth era never arrives.

---

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Capability, not tool.** Gen AI must be managed as a new medium of thinking, working, and creating rather than software to install, which reframes adoption from compliance into inhabiting a new way of working.
2. **Outcome-based North Star.** A simple, bold, model-proof vision of humans and agents jointly delivering business outcomes gives reconfiguration a destination that survives each frontier-model release.
3. **Trust before scale.** Grounded answers, accessible data, and explicit governance with human checkpoints form the load-bearing precondition: untrusted output means untrusted decisions means no scale.
4. **MVO-plus-augmented portfolio.** The enterprise should be split deliberately — ultra-lean minimum viable organizations for repetitive logic-based work, superpowered human teams where judgment and contact matter — instead of automated uniformly.
5. **Middle-out mass participation.** Change travels through millennial superusers mentoring peers and visibly tool-using leaders, with federated employee-built agents scaling adoption at zero marginal tech debt.

## Key Findings

| Claim | Evidence |
| ----- | -------- |
| Adoption is broad, value elusive | Two-thirds of global companies use Gen AI; impact "remains elusive" |
| Shadow use outruns leadership | Employees use Gen AI 3x more than leaders realize |
| Participation predicts returns | ~2% typical involvement; ≥7% doubles chances of positive excess shareholder returns; 21–30% at top performers |
| Enthusiasm skews millennial | 62% of ages 35–44 report high AI expertise vs 50% of 18–24s vs 22% of over-65s |
| Habit beats rollout (Lilli) | Jul 2023 launch; 92% ever-used, 74% regular, >30% time saved on gathering/synthesis, ~19M prompts |
| Federation scales free | ~150 centrally built vs ~17,000 employee-built agents with no added tech debt |
| Structured skilling works (Singtel) | Oct 2024 academy with two universities trained >10,000 staff |

- Bolting Gen AI onto unchanged processes delivers incremental impact at best; redesign must be jointly owned by business and technology teams.
- The most trusted platforms reveal their sources and ground answers in institutional knowledge plus external information.
- Even fully autonomous agent swarms keep residual human roles for safety, effectiveness, and higher-value redeployment.

---

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. Write a North Star now for the human-plus-agent company, segmented by which units become lean automated organizations and which stay human-powered.
2. Stand up the trust apparatus first: open the data, ground the models, and install oversight committees with human checkpoints before scaling workflows.
3. Rebuild one high-value, high-feasibility workflow end-to-end with employee co-designers rather than spreading thin pilots.
4. Measure participation against the 7 percent floor and cultivate superuser networks with tailored training and visible executive use.
5. Open questions the article leaves hanging: what reskilling actually absorbs MVO-displaced workers, how liability works for grounded-but-wrong answers, and what the full cost ledger looks like.
