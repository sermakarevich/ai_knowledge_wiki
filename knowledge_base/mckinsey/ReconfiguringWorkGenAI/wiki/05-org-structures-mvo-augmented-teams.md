# Step 4: Organizing as MVOs Plus Augmented Teams

**In one sentence:** Once generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) is embedded in workflows, chief executives must split the enterprise deliberately — minimum viable organizations (MVOs, ultra-lean highly automated units) for repetitive logic-based work, and Gen AI-augmented human teams where judgment and contact matter — and rebuild talent around both.

## Key points

- Only some business units should evolve into MVOs — "extremely lean and highly automated workflows" — while others stop at phase two as augmented teams whose humans gain "digital superpowers"; both paths force CEOs to modify structures and communicate impacts clearly.
- MVOs fit repetitive or logic-based work best, exemplified by invoice processing automated end-to-end — matching, approvals, entries — with near-zero human touch and only a small exception-handling team.
- Enabling an MVO requires robust AI operations and monitoring plus a rethought talent strategy: operators skilled in managing AI systems, data analysis, and exception handling, with emerging roles such as AI workflow optimizer and automation product owner.
- Workers displaced from MVO-ified workflows must be reskilled into other parts of the organization, and CEOs should hunt specifically for areas where a lean AI-first model cuts costs and improves speed or accuracy dramatically.
- Some functions must never become MVOs: sales teams using Gen AI for upsell suggestions and tailored marketing can carry larger portfolios at higher conversion, and service agents resolve issues faster — but removing humans would erode customer experience and damage the brand.
- The step's decision rule is therefore portfolio logic, not ideology: automate to MVO where work is repetitive and human contact adds no value; augment where the human touch is the product.

---

## The structural fork

" As gen AI becomes embedded into workflows, CEOs will need to decide how different parts of the enterprise are structured." The fork is explicit: "Only some business units may evolve into MVOs, which are extremely lean and highly automated workflows. Other parts of the company, meanwhile, will remain in step two of the AI evolution, giving human teams digital superpowers so they can accomplish more than ever." The step warns that structure must follow: "Both trajectories will require CEOs to modify organizational structures, communicating clearly with employees about how the changes will impact them" — restructuring without honest communication destroys the trust built in Step 2.

## Where MVOs win: the invoice-processing archetype

" MVOs will work best when handling repetitive or logic-based work. For example, consider a routine back-office process such as invoice processing. With gen AI, a company could automate invoice matching, approvals, and entries with near-zero human touch, maintaining just a small team to handle exceptions." The example is chosen to be universal (every firm processes invoices) and to illustrate the residual human role: exceptions, not routine throughput. The selection criterion generalizes: "CEOs should identify areas where a lean, AI-first model could dramatically cut costs and improve speed or accuracy."

## Talent for the MVO world

" To enable an MVO, companies must, of course, invest in robust AI operations and monitoring. But just as importantly, CEOs must rethink their talent strategies." The MVO operator is a new profile: "highly skilled in managing AI systems, data analysis, and exception handling," with "roles such as AI workflow optimizer or automation product owner" emerging "as critical positions." The displaced are not discarded: "team members who formerly completed tasks in the now-MVO workflow will need to be reskilled to deliver value in other parts of the organization" — linking Step 4 back to the reskilling engine of Step 5.

## Where humans stay: augmented customer-facing teams

" On the other hand, some functions should never evolve into full MVOs but instead stop at phase two and remain augmented teams." The evidence is already visible: "Sales teams, for instance, are already using gen AI to analyze customer data and get tailored suggestions for upselling or personalized marketing content in seconds. This could allow a single salesperson to manage a larger portfolio with much higher conversion rates. Customer service representatives are using gen AI to resolve issues more quickly and consistently." The boundary principle: "taking humans entirely out of the loop in these customer-facing roles would not make sense, as it could erode the customer experience in ways that damage the company's brand."

**Covers:** the MVO-versus-augmented-teams fork, the invoice-processing MVO archetype, MVO talent and reskilling, and the customer-facing augmented-team cases with the brand-risk boundary.
