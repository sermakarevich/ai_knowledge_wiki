# Step 1: A North Star Based on Outcomes, Not Tools

**In one sentence:** Chief executives (CEOs, the top decision-makers) must define a simple, bold, technology-proof vision of business outcomes achieved by humans and generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) agents working together — because treating Gen AI as just another tool to install guarantees it changes nothing.

## Key points

- Viewing Gen AI as "just another tool that employees will be required to use" is incomplete; employees already experience it as a capability, a new way of thinking, working, and creating.
- The reconfigured end-state is humans and Gen AI agents working together seamlessly, and forward-thinking CEOs are writing North Star plans for that world today even though it has not yet arrived.
- A good North Star is simple enough to be universally understood but bold enough to inspire teams, and it must absorb and activate frontier AI models and their new capabilities within a reasonable time frame.
- Delivering the North Star requires two components: a well-resourced change management plan and a full reimagining of end-to-end workflows — ambition without both is decoration.
- The article sketches the trajectory concretely: discrete-task agents first, then agent swarms delivering complete business outcomes, with parts of the firm becoming minimum viable organizations (MVOs, ultra-lean units where agent swarms do most work under minimal human checks).
- CEOs should segment the company in the vision itself — high-touch customer functions staying human-powered while back-office operations become MVOs — weighing both the pace of technology and their ability to mobilize people.

---

## Tool versus capability

The step's core conceptual move is reclassifying Gen AI. Tools are adopted; capabilities are inhabited. "CEOs are learning that this view is incomplete — and that employees see AI as more of a capability than a tool." If leadership frames Gen AI as software to install, employees comply minimally; if it is framed as the medium in which future work happens, reconfiguration becomes legitimate. This framing does the rhetorical work of justifying everything that follows — governance, workflow redesign, restructuring, reskilling.

## Design rules for the North Star

Three criteria are stated. Simplicity ("simple enough to be universally understood") prevents the vision from becoming a slogan only executives can parse. Boldness ("bold enough to inspire teams") prevents it from collapsing into the next quarter's efficiency target. Durability ("accommodate the evolution of technology and be able to absorb and activate frontier AI models") prevents it from expiring with each model release — a real risk given the article's own observation that agentic capabilities are advancing from discrete tasks toward spreadsheets and web navigation.

## The two required components

" Managing the magnitude of change that such a North Star represents will require two critical components: a well-resourced change management plan and a full reimagining of end-to-end workflows." Note the deliberate pairing: the people-mobilization track and the work-redesign track must run together. Leaders must simultaneously track "the pace of technological change and the ability to mobilize their people toward this future state" — moving faster than people can absorb, or slower than technology demands, both break the plan.

## The envisioned trajectory: agents to swarms to MVOs

The article makes the abstract concrete: "a company could start by deploying gen AI agents that complete discrete tasks and then evolve into deploying agent swarms capable of achieving complete business outcomes." In the end-state, "parts of the organization could become minimum viable organizations (MVOs) where agent swarms oversee most work, with as few humans as possible to check their outputs," while "other parts of the company would retain larger numbers of human workers equipped with technology-enabled superpowers." The segmentation guidance is explicit: "high-touch customer functions remaining more human powered, while back-office operations become MVOs."

**Covers:** the tool-versus-capability reframe, North Star design rules, the twin requirements of change plan plus workflow redesign, and the agents-to-swarms-to-MVO trajectory with human/MVO segmentation.
