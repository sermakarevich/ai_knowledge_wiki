# Explainer: Reconfiguring Work in the Age of Gen AI

## What it is

A McKinsey playbook for bosses, written by Erik Roth in August 2025, that says: getting real value from generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI, software that writes, codes, and reasons from plain-language requests) is not a technology project but a people project. It lays out five moves — set a bold vision, build trust in the data, redesign how work flows, reshape which teams are automated versus human, and turn employees into co-inventors — that together convert scattered experimenting into company-wide results.

## Why it matters

Most companies already pay for Gen AI and most see little for it: two-thirds use it somewhere, yet few feel it in their profits. Meanwhile employees are secretly using it three times more than their bosses think, which means the energy for change exists but runs outside any plan — risking mistakes, leaks, and wasted money. Whoever channels that energy first, the article argues, unlocks a growth era where dull repetitive work disappears into machines and people spend their days on judgment, customers, and creation.

## How it works

Think of it like rebuilding a restaurant kitchen around a powerful new oven rather than just plugging the oven into the old kitchen. First the owner declares what the new restaurant will serve (the North Star, a vivid picture of humans plus AI agents jointly delivering outcomes). Then the kitchen earns cooks' trust by labeling every ingredient and putting safety rules on the wall (grounding answers in company knowledge, opening data, governing use with human spot-checks). Then recipes get rewritten so the oven sits at the center, evolving from one helper robot to robot teams to near-self-running stations (solo agents, supervised groups, autonomous swarms). Then the owner decides which stations go fully automatic and which keep human chefs with super-tools (minimum viable organizations for back-office repetition, augmented teams for customers). All along, the whole staff — not a 2 percent task force — learns, invents dishes, and teaches each other, with star users mentoring peers and leaders visibly cooking with the new gear themselves.

## Where used

McKinsey ran this recipe on itself with Lilli, its internal Gen AI platform: leaders ask "Have you asked Lilli?" in every meeting, new hires learn it on day one, and staff have built nearly 17,000 of their own AI helpers on top of 150 official ones — reaching 92 percent adoption and answering almost 19 million questions. Singapore Telecom (Singtel) took the classroom route, opening an AI Acceleration Academy with two national universities in October 2024 to train over 10,000 staff. The article's other concrete pictures are invoice processing running with near-zero humans and salespeople carrying bigger portfolios through AI-generated upsell hints.

## Takeaways

- Pilots are cheap; value comes only from redesigning work and mobilizing people.
- Trust precedes scale: grounded answers, open-but-governed data, humans checking outputs.
- Automate selectively — full autonomy for repetitive back-office work, superpowered humans where customers feel the difference.
- Participation is measurable: push past 7 percent of staff involved, aim for the 21–30 percent zone of top performers.
- Bet on millennials as carriers: the 35–44 cohort reports the highest AI expertise and makes the best peer mentors.

## Jargon decoder

- **Gen AI agents / agent swarms:** AI programs that carry out tasks on their own; a swarm is many such programs cooperating toward a full business result.
- **North Star:** a short, vivid, long-lived statement of the future company everyone can repeat.
- **MVO (minimum viable organization):** a radically slimmed-down unit where AI does almost everything and a handful of people supervise.
- **Augmented team:** regular staff made far more productive by AI helpers, with humans still in charge.
- **Two-in-the-box:** a partnership where a business person and a technology person jointly own each redesign.
- **Human-in-the-loop:** a required human checkpoint before AI output becomes a decision.
- **Hallucination:** AI stating something false with total confidence.
- **TSR (total shareholder returns):** how much richer shareholders got, counting price gains plus dividends.
- **Superusers / ambassadors:** enthusiastic early adopters who teach colleagues and spread new habits.
- **Tech debt:** future repair work created by quick-and-dirty technology choices; the article claims employee-built agents add none.
