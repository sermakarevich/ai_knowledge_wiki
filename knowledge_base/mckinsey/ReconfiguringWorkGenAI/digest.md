# Digest: Reconfiguring Work

## The Setup: Piloting Is Easy, Value Is Hard

**In one sentence:** Two-thirds of companies already use generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI, meaning AI that creates text, code, and other content from plain-language prompts), yet almost none see company-wide bottom-line impact, because Gen AI fails like no earlier enterprise software — handing people the technology without changing how work happens produces experimentation without value.

- Two-thirds of global companies use Gen AI, but these deployments behave nothing like past enterprise software projects, and leaders remain unclear how to convert potential into meaningful value.
- Gen AI's natural-language interface plus growing reasoning and agentic capabilities let it interpret large volumes of information, write code, answer queries, and — in the most advanced agents — build spreadsheets and navigate web pages.
- Employees already use Gen AI three times more than their leaders realize, creating a shadow-adoption gap between grassroots experimentation and enterprise strategy.
- The article's prescription is a novel, non-linear change management approach that turns employees from Gen AI experimenters into Gen AI accelerators via five CEO-led steps.
- That approach demands employees become active participants — experimenting, co-creating products, continually reskilling — while acknowledging some will need extra support.
- The promised payoff is an era of growth in which reconfigured, fully Gen AI-enabled organizations give employees AI superpowers that strip away tedious, repetitive tasks.

## Step 1: A North Star Based on Outcomes, Not Tools

**In one sentence:** Chief executives (CEOs, the top decision-makers) must define a simple, bold, technology-proof vision of business outcomes achieved by humans and generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) agents working together — because treating Gen AI as just another tool to install guarantees it changes nothing.

- Viewing Gen AI as "just another tool that employees will be required to use" is incomplete; employees already experience it as a capability, a new way of thinking, working, and creating.
- The reconfigured end-state is humans and Gen AI agents working together seamlessly, and forward-thinking CEOs are writing North Star plans for that world today even though it has not yet arrived.
- A good North Star is simple enough to be universally understood but bold enough to inspire teams, and it must absorb and activate frontier AI models and their new capabilities within a reasonable time frame.
- Delivering the North Star requires two components: a well-resourced change management plan and a full reimagining of end-to-end workflows — ambition without both is decoration.
- The article sketches the trajectory concretely: discrete-task agents first, then agent swarms delivering complete business outcomes, with parts of the firm becoming minimum viable organizations (MVOs, ultra-lean units where agent swarms do most work under minimal human checks).
- CEOs should segment the company in the vision itself — high-touch customer functions staying human-powered while back-office operations become MVOs — weighing both the pace of technology and their ability to mobilize people.

## Step 2: Trust Through Data, Governance, and Enterprise Wisdom

**In one sentence:** Nobody scales what nobody trusts, so leaders must ground generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) in the company's own knowledge, open up data access, and wrap both in governance with human checks — because untrusted Gen AI output means untrusted Gen AI decisions and zero chance of scale.

- Foundational trust is the precondition for scale: if employees do not trust Gen AI output, they will not trust the decisions it makes, and the technology cannot scale.
- The most trusted platforms are grounded in organizational context — they reveal how answers are derived and which sources were used — and should be augmented with institutional knowledge such as proprietary research, customer interaction logs, and decades of engineering know-how, plus dynamic external information.
- Leaders must treat data accessibility as a first-class work stream, because Gen AI's ability to consume unstructured data inside the firm's "marketplace of information" is a distinct competitive advantage for surfacing hidden insights and patterns.
- That advantage is hard to capture: training models on structured and unstructured data for high-quality trustworthy answers remains challenging, and the practices and frameworks for monitoring response quality are still emerging.
- Governance must be explicit and C-suite-owned: the chief executive with the chief information officer (CIO, the top technology executive) and chief data officer (CDO, the top data executive) prioritizes accessible data, then wraps it in an AI oversight committee, acceptable-use policies, compliance and risk guidelines built with risk and legal teams, and human-in-the-loop checkpoints.
- Human-in-the-loop checkpoints exist to catch hallucinations (confident false outputs), biases, and data leakage before they reach decisions.

## Step 3: Reimagining Workflows Toward AI Teams

**In one sentence:** Bolting generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) onto existing processes yields incremental gains at best, so business and technology teams must jointly rebuild workflows around Gen AI through three phases — solo agents, supervised agent groups, autonomous swarms — while employees co-design the change and leaders make daily use a habit.

- Bolting Gen AI onto existing processes "will not encourage widescale uptake and will deliver incremental, if any, impact," because Gen AI is a capability — a new way of thinking, working, and creating — not another enterprise software tool.
- Redesign must use a "two-in-the-box" approach pairing business teams (who guarantee the new way of working delivers business results) with technology teams (who guarantee technical feasibility).
- The evolution runs in three phases: stand-alone agents for discrete tasks, groups of agents completing end-to-end processes under human oversight, and fully automated agentic swarms acting independently as minimum viable organizations (MVOs, ultra-lean units delivering full business outcomes).
- Even in the final swarm phase people are not eliminated: a few employees keep MVOs safe and effective while many others are redeployed to higher-value activities.
- In the first two phases, leaders must involve employees directly — inviting them to build their own agents and flag where Gen AI belongs — and start with work streams where value is clear, feasibility is high, and required investment is manageable.
- McKinsey's own Lilli platform proves the habit thesis: since its July 2023 launch, 92 percent of global staff have used it, 74 percent use it regularly, it saves over 30 percent of time on information gathering and synthesis, and it has answered nearly 19 million prompts.

## Step 4: Organizing as MVOs Plus Augmented Teams

**In one sentence:** Once generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) is embedded in workflows, chief executives must split the enterprise deliberately — minimum viable organizations (MVOs, ultra-lean highly automated units) for repetitive logic-based work, and Gen AI-augmented human teams where judgment and contact matter — and rebuild talent around both.

- Only some business units should evolve into MVOs — "extremely lean and highly automated workflows" — while others stop at phase two as augmented teams whose humans gain "digital superpowers"; both paths force CEOs to modify structures and communicate impacts clearly.
- MVOs fit repetitive or logic-based work best, exemplified by invoice processing automated end-to-end — matching, approvals, entries — with near-zero human touch and only a small exception-handling team.
- Enabling an MVO requires robust AI operations and monitoring plus a rethought talent strategy: operators skilled in managing AI systems, data analysis, and exception handling, with emerging roles such as AI workflow optimizer and automation product owner.
- Workers displaced from MVO-ified workflows must be reskilled into other parts of the organization, and CEOs should hunt specifically for areas where a lean AI-first model cuts costs and improves speed or accuracy dramatically.
- Some functions must never become MVOs: sales teams using Gen AI for upsell suggestions and tailored marketing can carry larger portfolios at higher conversion, and service agents resolve issues faster — but removing humans would erode customer experience and damage the brand.
- The step's decision rule is therefore portfolio logic, not ideology: automate to MVO where work is repetitive and human contact adds no value; augment where the human touch is the product.

## Step 5: People as Change Agents

**In one sentence:** Reconfiguration lives or dies on mass participation — involving far more than the usual 2 percent of employees, mobilizing millennial superusers as mentors, having leaders visibly use the tools, and letting staff build their own agents — as McKinsey's Lilli (92 percent adoption, ~17,000 employee-built agents) and Singtel's 10,000-person academy show.

- Participation is the performance lever: typically only about 2 percent of employees join transformation efforts, but involving at least 7 percent doubles the chances of positive excess total shareholder returns (TSR, stock gains above the market), and top performers involve 21 to 30 percent.
- Gen AI ambassador programs matter more than in past technology rollouts because everyone is still discovering use cases together, so "superusers" who drive uptake must be found and supported as cultural change agents.
- Enthusiasm skews millennial, not Gen Z: 62 percent of employees aged 35 to 44 report high AI expertise, versus 50 percent of 18-to-24-year-olds and 22 percent of baby boomers over 65 — so CEOs should deploy millennial champions as peer mentors and practice-group leads.
- Change must run middle-out, not just top-down or bottom-up, with CEOs leading by example through visible personal use of Gen AI tools.
- McKinsey's adoption playbook — user segmentation with tailored training, superuser Lilli Clubs, one-on-one coaching for senior leaders, self-guided "ask Lilli" help — plus a federated agent model produced ~150 centrally built agents and nearly 17,000 employee-built ones with no added tech debt.
- Singtel's skills-first counterpart, the October 2024 AI Acceleration Academy with Nanyang Technological University and the National University of Singapore, trained more than 10,000 employees across roles to use data and Gen AI in their own workflows.

## The argument in five moves

1. Adoption without reconfiguration produces pilots without value.
2. Name an outcome-based North Star that assumes humans plus agents.
3. Earn trust with grounded answers, open data, and real governance.
4. Rebuild workflows around Gen AI, then split the firm into MVOs and augmented teams.
5. Win through mass participation led by superusers and role-modeling leaders.
