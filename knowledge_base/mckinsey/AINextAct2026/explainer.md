# AI's next act — Plain-language explainer

## What it is

In January 2026, the consulting firm McKinsey asked seven of its artificial intelligence (AI) leaders what the year ahead holds for companies using AI. Their shared answer: the age of impressive demos is over, and the age of making AI actually work inside real companies has begun. The excitement moves from chatbots that answer questions to background agents that finish jobs — closing support tickets, clearing order exceptions, chasing approvals — while leaving a receipt for everything they did.

## Why it matters

Most companies spent the last few years running small AI experiments, and very few turned those experiments into everyday operations that save money or serve customers better. The article argues 2026 is when that bill comes due: company boards will demand proof that AI spending produces results, competitors that get AI working will suddenly pull ahead, and programmes built on shaky data and old computer systems will fail their reviews. For anyone whose job touches budgets, staffing, or customer service, this is the year AI stops being an experiment and starts changing how work flows.

## How it works

The leaders describe a practical recipe rather than a single breakthrough. First, give software agents narrow, well-fenced jobs instead of total freedom — less freedom per agent, more finished work. Second, aim those agents at the gaps between tasks: the handoffs, approvals, and escalations where work currently sits waiting for days. Third, prove the agents are trustworthy with layered testing — exact repeatable checks, statistical accuracy measurement, and safety rails — instead of grading AI with more AI alone. Fourth, fix the foundations underneath: modern systems, clean data, trained staff, and redesigned workflows. Wrap all of that in a background coordination layer, the so-called Agentic operating system, that sends the right agent to each event and calls in a human only when judgment is truly needed.

## Where it is used

The advice targets large UK organisations across industries, with named relevance to life sciences, technology leadership, and any enterprise running ticket queues, exception handling, or approval chains. The competitive warning is cross-industry: in each sector, one company is expected to scale these agent workflows first and force rivals to follow.

## Takeaways

- Fund workflow redesign and foundations before buying bigger models, because value tracks redesign quality, not model spending.
- Convert last year's pilots into production systems with testing, controls, and staff training, or shut them down.
- Measure coordination time — handoffs, approvals, escalations — as the productivity metric that agents actually move.
- Build trust evidence deliberately: audit trails, accuracy statistics, and agent identity controls are what earn deployment permission.
- Watch competitors, because one visible scaled deployment in your industry is the signal to accelerate.

## Jargon decoder

- **Agentic workflows:** multi-step jobs carried out by AI agents that use tools, check results, and pass work along, rather than single chatbot answers.
- **AI that settles:** Patnaik's phrase for agents that finish a business outcome end to end instead of just discussing it.
- **Audit trail:** a step-by-step record of what an agent did, so its work can be checked later.
- **Humanware:** Smaje's term for the people side — roles, skills, training, and change management — that makes AI results repeatable.
- **LLM-as-judge:** using one AI model to grade another's output; useful but insufficient on its own.
- **Agentic observability:** tracking and explaining what agents are doing across company and third-party platforms.
- **Identity and Access Management for agents:** giving each software agent its own ID card and permissions, like employee access badges.
- **Agentic OS:** Sagar-Jones's vision of a background layer that notices events and dispatches the right agents automatically.
- **AI natives:** companies built around modern data and AI practices from the start, versus traditional firms modernising legacy systems.
- **Digital trust:** the confidence of customers, regulators, and staff that AI is used safely and fairly, treated here as a condition for growth.
