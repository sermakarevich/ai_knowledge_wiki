# AI's next act: McKinsey AI leaders on the year ahead — Digest

The whole source at medium depth: every section's headline claim and key points, in order. ~10 min. Descend into a wiki page only where you need the detail.

## 1. [[wiki/01-shift-from-possible-to-viable|From technically possible to operationally viable]]

**In one sentence:** The article frames 2026 as the year the enterprise artificial intelligence (AI) conversation shifts from what AI can demo to what AI can run reliably at scale, making execution rather than experimentation the competitive differentiator.

- The January 2026 framing piece declares that discussion inside organisations is shifting decisively from what is technically possible to what is operationally viable at scale.
- Leaders are moving beyond isolated pilots to confront governance, trust, and end-to-end integration, which reframes AI as an operating challenge rather than a technology showcase.
- The article's closing synthesis names execution as the clear 2026 differentiator, with advantage going to sustained investment in fundamentals over incremental experimentation.
- Organisations that win will hard-wire AI into redesigned processes, build confidence through robust governance and controls, and invest in human and technical foundations.
- The piece is a compiled outlook, not original research: seven McKinsey technical leaders contribute one perspective each, assembled by Rohit Rathod, a Senior Client Development Advisor in the London office.
- Its target reader is the UK enterprise leader deciding where 2026 AI budgets go, and its consistent message is to fund foundations and workflow redesign before new model spending.

## 2. [[wiki/02-autonomy-shrinks-by-design|Autonomy shrinks by design]]

**In one sentence:** Debasish Patnaik argues 2026 will not be the year of fully autonomous agents but the year enterprises deliberately narrow agent autonomy, shifting from chat interfaces to agents that settle work end to end with audit trails while the real productivity gains come from automating coordination.

- Patnaik, a Senior Partner who leads QuantumBlack, AI by McKinsey for the UK, predicts 2026 is the year autonomy shrinks by design rather than the year of fully autonomous agents.
- Enterprises will move from "AI that chats" to "AI that settles", meaning agents that close tickets and clear exceptions end to end with audit trails instead of merely conversing.
- The real productivity lift comes from automating coordination — handoffs, approvals, escalation — rather than automating individual tasks.
- Business value will correlate less with spending on AI models and more with how well organisations redesign everyday workflows.
- The prescribed operating envelope is controlled environments with verifiable actions and hard guardrails, which is what makes narrowed autonomy safe to scale.
- The claim reframes the agent debate from maximizing autonomy to right-sizing it: less freedom per agent, more completed work per workflow.

## 3. [[wiki/03-boards-own-outcomes-pilots-to-production|Boards own outcomes as pilots convert to production]]

**In one sentence:** Kate Smaje argues that boards and C-suite teams are stepping up to own end-to-end AI outcomes instead of delegating experiments, making 2026 the year 2025's pilots convert into production at scale with evaluation, controls, and the human layer that makes impact repeatable.

- Smaje, a Senior Partner and Global Leader for Technology and AI, reports a real step up in engagement and ownership of AI by boards and C-suite teams.
- Top teams are starting to own end-to-end outcomes rather than delegating experiments and individual use cases downward.
- Her 2026 prediction is categorical: organisations convert 2025's pilots into production at scale instead of running another round of experiments.
- Production at scale requires three named ingredients: evaluation, controls, and the "humanware" on top.
- "Humanware" — the people, roles, skills, and change layer — is what makes AI impact material and repeatable rather than a one-off pilot result.
- The perspective moves accountability upward: AI stops being an innovation-team hobby and becomes a board-owned operating commitment.

## 4. [[wiki/04-fundamentals-and-the-audit-moment|Fundamentals and the audit moment]]

**In one sentence:** Alex Sukharevsky warns that AI value will not materialise without modern architecture, quality data, capabilities, operating model, and change management, predicting a tougher audit moment in which programmes fail on enablers and economics rather than model quality while AI natives pull away from traditional firms.

- Sukharevsky, a Senior Partner and AI and digital leader, states that significant value will not materialise without the fundamentals even as AI technology advances fast.
- He enumerates five fundamentals: modern information technology (IT) architecture, high-quality data, capabilities, operating model, and change management.
- His 2026 prediction is a tougher "audit moment" in which programmes fall short not because models underperform but because enablers and economics were never in place.
- The gap between AI-native and traditional organisations will become more visible, shifting competitive dynamics as natives convert the same models into more value.
- The strategic focus moves from scaling AI to secure, ethical scaling built on digital trust, making trustworthiness a growth condition rather than a compliance afterthought.
- The perspective is the article's hardest warning: model quality is no longer the binding constraint, so model-first budgets will fail their audits.

## 5. [[wiki/05-trust-assurance-and-agentic-foundations|Trust, assurance, and agentic foundations]]

**In one sentence:** Dave Kerr and Rory Walsh argue that 2026 confidence in AI will be built through layered trust-and-assurance practices and serious investment in evaluations, observability, and agent identity management, because only provably reliable systems earn deployment in real operations.

- Kerr, a Partner leading Agentic deployments, says the defining question shifts from "can it work?" to "can we trust it, consistently, in the real world?"
- Real confidence comes through trust and assurance, with wider deployment of autonomous systems gated on proof of reliability, consistent behavior, and strong preventive controls.
- He demotes the fashionable large language model (LLM)-as-judge technique to one tool inside a broader regime combining deterministic tests, statistical measures of accuracy, and robust safeguards.
- Walsh, a Partner in QuantumBlack Labs, predicts enterprises will tackle the foundational enablers for building, governing, and operating Agentic workflows under business, security, and regulatory requirements.
- Moving beyond proofs of concept and single deployments will require considerable investment, ending the era when agentic progress looked cheap.
- Walsh names three concrete foundation topics: at-scale evaluations, agentic observability across first- and third-party platforms, and Identity and Access Management for agents.

## 6. [[wiki/06-agentic-os-and-competitive-wake-up-calls|The Agentic OS and competitive wake-up calls]]

**In one sentence:** Emily Sagar-Jones sketches an Agentic operating system (OS) that deploys the right agents autonomously in the background while escalating to humans only when necessary, and David Champagne predicts visible scaled deployments will jolt laggards from experimentation into production.

- Sagar-Jones, a Principal Data Scientist at QuantumBlack, names the Agentic OS — an operating layer that anticipates needs, responds to events, and coordinates across systems — as her most exciting 2026 prospect.
- The OS deploys the right agents autonomously in the background of everyday work and escalates to humans only when necessary, making selective escalation its core design principle.
- Greater entrusted autonomy raises the stakes on robust security, governance, and guardrails, pairing each step of freedom with a matching control.
- Champagne, a Senior Partner leading QuantumBlack in Life Sciences, predicts 2026 wake-up calls: one company per industry will scale agentic workflows and produce a step-change in speed, cost, or service levels.
- Once that advantage is visible, competitors will be jolted from experimentation to production, so diffusion happens through demonstrable competitive threat rather than persuasion.
- Together the two perspectives describe the endgame and its trigger: background autonomous coordination as the destination, visible rival success as what forces everyone onto the road.

## The argument in five moves

1. The enterprise AI question changes from what is possible to what is viable at scale.
2. Autonomy is deliberately narrowed so agents settle work with audit trails instead of chatting.
3. Boards take ownership and convert pilots to production with evaluation, controls, and humanware.
4. Programmes face an audit moment where missing fundamentals, not weak models, cause failure.
5. Trust is proven through layered assurance and agent infrastructure, then scaled under competitive pressure toward an Agentic OS.
