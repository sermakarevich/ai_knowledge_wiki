# Fundamentals and the audit moment

**In one sentence:** Alex Sukharevsky warns that AI value will not materialise without modern architecture, quality data, capabilities, operating model, and change management, predicting a tougher audit moment in which programmes fail on enablers and economics rather than model quality while AI natives pull away from traditional firms.

## Key points

- Sukharevsky, a Senior Partner and AI and digital leader, states that significant value will not materialise without the fundamentals even as AI technology advances fast.
- He enumerates five fundamentals: modern information technology (IT) architecture, high-quality data, capabilities, operating model, and change management.
- His 2026 prediction is a tougher "audit moment" in which programmes fall short not because models underperform but because enablers and economics were never in place.
- The gap between AI-native and traditional organisations will become more visible, shifting competitive dynamics as natives convert the same models into more value.
- The strategic focus moves from scaling AI to secure, ethical scaling built on digital trust, making trustworthiness a growth condition rather than a compliance afterthought.
- The perspective is the article's hardest warning: model quality is no longer the binding constraint, so model-first budgets will fail their audits.

---

## Technology advances, value does not follow automatically

The core claim separates two curves: "while AI technology is advancing fast, significant value won't materialise without the fundamentals." The five fundamentals named are "modern IT architecture, high-quality data, capabilities, operating model, and change management." Each is a known enterprise weakness — legacy estates, fragmented data, thin AI skills, project-based operating models, underfunded change — so the sentence reads as a checklist of where 2026 programmes will actually be won or lost.

## The audit moment

The memorable forecast is institutional: "We'll see a tougher 'audit moment' where programmes fall short not because the models underperform, but because the enablers and economics weren't in place." An audit moment means finance and the board start asking what the AI spend returned, and the predicted answer is that the models were fine while everything around them — data pipelines, integration architecture, unit economics — was not. Economics sitting alongside enablers signals that cost discipline, not just technical readiness, will decide which programmes survive scrutiny.

## Natives pull away, trust becomes the scaling condition

Two structural consequences follow. First, "the gap between AI natives and traditional organisations will become more visible, competitive dynamics will shift," because natives already possess the fundamentals and therefore harvest each model generation faster. Second, "the focus will move from scaling AI to secure, ethical scaling built on digital trust," which reframes trust from a brake on speed into the condition that permits speed: only organisations customers, regulators, and employees trust get to scale at all.

**Covers:** Alex Sukharevsky perspective — five fundamentals, audit moment, enablers-versus-models failure mode, native-versus-traditional gap, secure ethical scaling on digital trust.
