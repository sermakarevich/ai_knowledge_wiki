# Autonomy shrinks by design

**In one sentence:** Debasish Patnaik argues 2026 will not be the year of fully autonomous agents but the year enterprises deliberately narrow agent autonomy, shifting from chat interfaces to agents that settle work end to end with audit trails while the real productivity gains come from automating coordination.

## Key points

- Patnaik, a Senior Partner who leads QuantumBlack, AI by McKinsey for the UK, predicts 2026 is the year autonomy shrinks by design rather than the year of fully autonomous agents.
- Enterprises will move from "AI that chats" to "AI that settles", meaning agents that close tickets and clear exceptions end to end with audit trails instead of merely conversing.
- The real productivity lift comes from automating coordination — handoffs, approvals, escalation — rather than automating individual tasks.
- Business value will correlate less with spending on AI models and more with how well organisations redesign everyday workflows.
- The prescribed operating envelope is controlled environments with verifiable actions and hard guardrails, which is what makes narrowed autonomy safe to scale.
- The claim reframes the agent debate from maximizing autonomy to right-sizing it: less freedom per agent, more completed work per workflow.

---

## Fully autonomous agents versus shrunken autonomy

The quote opens against consensus expectations: "Everyone expects 2026 to be the year of fully autonomous agents; when what is more likely is it's the year autonomy shrinks by design." The contrarian structure matters because it tells enterprise buyers not to plan around unbounded agents. Shrinking by design means the enterprise chooses the boundaries — which systems an agent may touch, which actions need approval, what evidence each action must leave — before granting any autonomy at all.

## From "AI that chats" to "AI that settles"

The article's most quotable distinction is the move "from 'AI that chats' to 'AI that settles', closing tickets and clearing exceptions end-to-end with audit trails." Chatting produces text that a human must still act on; settling produces a finished business outcome — the ticket closed, the exception cleared — plus the audit trail proving how it happened. The audit trail is load-bearing in this argument: without verifiable evidence of each step, a settled outcome cannot be trusted, governed, or learned from.

## Automating coordination, not tasks

Patnaik locates the productivity prize precisely: "the real productivity lift will come from automating coordination (handoffs, approvals, escalation), not just tasks." Individual tasks were the robotic process automation era; coordination is the work between tasks, where enterprise time actually dies waiting in queues. An agent that chases an approval, routes an exception to the right owner, and escalates with full context removes days of latency that no faster task execution could recover.

## Value follows workflow redesign, not model spend

The economic claim is explicit: "The business value of AI will correlate less with how much you spend on AI models, and more on how well organisations redesign everyday workflows." Model spending buys capability; workflow redesign buys outcomes, because value only appears when the redesigned process lets agents act in "controlled environments with verifiable actions and hard guardrails." This is the same fundamentals thesis Sukharevsky states elsewhere in the article, expressed here as a spending rule.
