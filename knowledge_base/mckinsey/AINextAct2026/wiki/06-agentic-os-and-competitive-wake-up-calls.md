# The Agentic OS and competitive wake-up calls

**In one sentence:** Emily Sagar-Jones sketches an Agentic operating system (OS) that deploys the right agents autonomously in the background while escalating to humans only when necessary, and David Champagne predicts visible scaled deployments will jolt laggards from experimentation into production.

## Key points

- Sagar-Jones, a Principal Data Scientist at QuantumBlack, names the Agentic OS — an operating layer that anticipates needs, responds to events, and coordinates across systems — as her most exciting 2026 prospect.
- The OS deploys the right agents autonomously in the background of everyday work and escalates to humans only when necessary, making selective escalation its core design principle.
- Greater entrusted autonomy raises the stakes on robust security, governance, and guardrails, pairing each step of freedom with a matching control.
- Champagne, a Senior Partner leading QuantumBlack in Life Sciences, predicts 2026 wake-up calls: one company per industry will scale agentic workflows and produce a step-change in speed, cost, or service levels.
- Once that advantage is visible, competitors will be jolted from experimentation to production, so diffusion happens through demonstrable competitive threat rather than persuasion.
- Together the two perspectives describe the endgame and its trigger: background autonomous coordination as the destination, visible rival success as what forces everyone onto the road.

---

## The Agentic OS vision

Sagar-Jones describes "the Agentic OS becoming a reality — an operating layer that anticipates needs, responds to events, and coordinates across systems by deploying the right agents autonomously in the background of everyday work, escalating to humans only when necessary." Three verbs define the layer's intelligence: anticipate rather than await instructions, respond to events rather than polling, coordinate across systems rather than serving one application. Escalation "only when necessary" is the human-in-the-loop bargain made precise — humans handle judgment calls and exceptions while routine coordination disappears into the background.

## More autonomy demands more guardrails

The vision comes with its price stated upfront: "This shift will increase the level of autonomy entrusted to AI, while placing importance on robust security, governance, and guardrails." Entrusted is the operative word — autonomy is granted, not seized, and the grant is conditional on the security and governance apparatus holding. This directly echoes the Patnaik and Kerr perspectives: the OS is what shrunken-by-design autonomy and trust-and-assurance practices are meant to make possible.

## The wake-up-call diffusion mechanism

Champagne predicts the competitive trigger: "2026 will bring a few wake-up calls for corporates: a company in an industry will successfully scale agentic workflows and create a step-change in speed, cost, or service levels." Step-change matters — marginal gains do not jolt anyone, while a visible leap in speed, cost, or service rewrites what customers expect from every player. The diffusion logic follows: "When that advantage is visible, it will jolt everyone else from experimentation to production." Adoption here is driven by fear of falling behind, which is historically a faster enterprise motivator than any internal business case.
