# Boards own outcomes as pilots convert to production

**In one sentence:** Kate Smaje argues that boards and C-suite teams are stepping up to own end-to-end AI outcomes instead of delegating experiments, making 2026 the year 2025's pilots convert into production at scale with evaluation, controls, and the human layer that makes impact repeatable.

## Key points

- Smaje, a Senior Partner and Global Leader for Technology and AI, reports a real step up in engagement and ownership of AI by boards and C-suite teams.
- Top teams are starting to own end-to-end outcomes rather than delegating experiments and individual use cases downward.
- Her 2026 prediction is categorical: organisations convert 2025's pilots into production at scale instead of running another round of experiments.
- Production at scale requires three named ingredients: evaluation, controls, and the "humanware" on top.
- "Humanware" — the people, roles, skills, and change layer — is what makes AI impact material and repeatable rather than a one-off pilot result.
- The perspective moves accountability upward: AI stops being an innovation-team hobby and becomes a board-owned operating commitment.

---

## Ownership moves to the top

The diagnosis is behavioral, not technical: "We're seeing a real step up in engagement and ownership around AI by boards and C-suite teams. They're starting to own end-to-end outcomes rather than delegating experiments and individual use cases." Delegation was rational when AI meant contained experiments; it fails when AI rewires workflows across functions, because only the top team can adjudicate the trade-offs between speed, risk, and investment that end-to-end outcomes demand.

## 2025 pilots become 2026 production

The forecast sets the year's conversion target: "2026 should be the year organisations convert 2025's pilots into production at scale." The verb convert is doing the work — pilots are inputs to be transformed, not assets to be admired, and scale is the pass criterion. This directly answers the article's framing question of operational viability: a pilot proves possibility, while production at scale proves viability.

## Evaluation, controls, and "humanware"

Smaje names the conversion stack precisely: "evaluation, controls, and the 'humanware' on top that makes the impact material and repeatable." Evaluation proves the system works, controls keep it working safely, and humanware — training, role redesign, adoption, accountability — is what carries a result from one team to every team. Repeatable is the key adjective: without the human layer, each deployment is a bespoke heroic effort, and impact cannot compound across the organisation.
