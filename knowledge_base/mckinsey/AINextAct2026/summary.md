# AI's next act: McKinsey AI leaders on the year ahead

**Article:** [AI's next act: McKinsey AI leaders on the year ahead](https://www.mckinsey.com/uk/our-insights/uk-insights/ais-next-act-mckinsey-ai-leaders-on-the-year-ahead)

## Human Readable TL;DR

Imagine a company that spent years test-driving cars in empty parking lots and now has to merge onto a real motorway. That is where big companies stand with artificial intelligence in 2026, according to seven of McKinsey's AI leaders: the fun demo phase is over, and winners will be the firms that give their software helpers narrow, well-fenced jobs, prove they can be trusted, and fix the unglamorous plumbing underneath. Bosses and boards have to take charge, turn last year's experiments into everyday operations, and watch rivals closely, because one competitor getting this right will force everyone else to catch up fast.

---

## TL;DR

McKinsey's January 2026 outlook compiles predictions from seven of its AI leaders for United Kingdom (UK) organisations, converging on one thesis: 2026 shifts from technical possibility to operational viability at scale, making execution the differentiator. The leaders prescribe deliberately narrowed agent autonomy with audit trails, board-owned conversion of 2025 pilots into production, heavy investment in data and architecture fundamentals ahead of a tougher audit moment, and layered trust-and-assurance regimes over fashionable single-metric evaluation. The collection offers no new data or quantified results; its value is the convergence of practitioner forecasts on workflow redesign, governance, and competitive pressure as the year's decisive forces.

---

## Problem & Motivation

Enterprises have spent several years running isolated AI pilots that rarely graduate into production systems delivering measurable business value, while governance, trust, and integration challenges grow more complex with each deployment. The article addresses the resulting leadership question for 2026: what must change for AI to become operationally viable at scale rather than merely technically impressive. This matters because boards are about to audit AI spending, AI-native competitors are converting the same models into value faster, and misallocated budgets will show up as failed programmes within the year.

---

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Autonomy shrinks by design** — rather than racing toward fully autonomous agents, enterprises should deliberately narrow what agents may do, moving from chat interfaces to agents that settle tickets and exceptions end to end with audit trails in controlled, guardrailed environments.
2. **Coordination is the productivity prize** — the largest gains come from automating the work between tasks, namely handoffs, approvals, and escalation, instead of speeding up individual tasks.
3. **Boards own end-to-end outcomes** — AI accountability moves from delegated experiments to C-suite ownership, with 2026 defined as the year 2025 pilots convert to production at scale under evaluation, controls, and the human layer.
4. **The audit moment** — programmes will soon be judged and found wanting not on model quality but on missing enablers and economics, exposing the gap between AI natives and traditional firms.
5. **Assurance-gated deployment** — autonomous systems earn real-world use only through proven reliability and layered evaluation, with judge models subordinated to deterministic tests, statistical accuracy measures, and safeguards.
6. **The Agentic operating system** — a background layer that anticipates needs, dispatches the right agents across systems, and escalates to humans only when necessary, paired at every step with security and governance.

## Key Findings

The article presents expert forecasts rather than measured results, so there are no quantitative comparisons to tabulate. Its qualitative findings are:

- The enterprise conversation has shifted from what AI can do to what AI can run reliably at scale.
- Business value tracks workflow redesign quality more closely than model spending.
- Production at scale requires evaluation, controls, and humanware together; any one alone leaves impact unrepeatable.
- Model quality is no longer the binding constraint on value; architecture, data, capabilities, operating model, and change management are.
- Trust evidence — reliability proof, consistent behavior, preventive controls — gates wider deployment of autonomous systems.
- Agent infrastructure planning now includes at-scale evaluations, cross-platform observability, and agent identity management.
- Visible scaled deployments by one firm per industry will force competitors from experimentation into production.

---

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. Redirect AI budgets from model spending toward workflow redesign, evaluation infrastructure, and change capacity.
2. Convert or close the 2025 pilot portfolio, gating each production deployment on trust evidence and repeatability.
3. Build the agentic foundations early: at-scale evaluation, observability across first- and third-party platforms, and identity and access controls for agents.
4. Establish board-level ownership of end-to-end AI outcomes with audit-ready economics before the audit moment arrives.
5. Monitor each industry for the first visible scaled agentic deployment and treat it as the signal to accelerate.
6. Open questions the article leaves unanswered: what the audit moment's pass criteria should be, how resource-constrained firms fund the foundations, and how to measure coordination productivity rigorously.
