# Unlocking the value of AI in software development — Retrieval practice

## Research and the performance gap

1. How many companies were surveyed, how many assessed impact, and how were top and bottom performers defined?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Nearly 300 senior leaders from publicly traded companies were surveyed on adoption; 100 assessed impact across four outcomes. Top performers are the top quintile across the four metrics, bottom performers the bottom quintile.</details>

2. State the four outcome metrics with their exact improvement ranges, plus the overall performance gap.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Team productivity, customer experience, and time to market improved 16 to 30 percent; software quality improved 31 to 45 percent. The gap between top and bottom performers is 15 percentage points.</details>

3. Why would comparing quintiles on outcomes be more informative than comparing adopters against non-adopters?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Because nearly everyone has adopted something, so the interesting variation is maturity of use; quintiles on outcomes isolate what converts tooling into results rather than restating that tools exist.</details>

4. A team lead says "we deployed Copilot to everyone, so we should expect top-performer gains." What breaks in that reasoning?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The article's thesis is that gains require overhauling processes, roles, and ways of working; deployment without life-cycle breadth, role redesign, and the three enablers predicts shallow, not top-quintile, results.</details>

## End-to-end use cases

5. What are the two headline numbers on scaling use cases, and which stages do they span?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Top performers are six to seven times more likely to scale four or more use cases; nearly two-thirds of leaders report 4+ at scale versus 10 percent of bottom performers — spanning design, coding, testing, deployment, and adoption tracking.</details>

6. How does Cursor's "internal lab" loop work, and what does Plan Mode add?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Teams trial workflow fixes on their own pain points and productize the winners, with sprints balancing features against process improvement. Plan Mode plots changes before implementation, starting from customer feature requests in the editor or team chat.</details>

7. Why does layering Bugbot review before human review matter more than replacing human review outright?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Layered gates expand coverage with minimal disruption while keeping human judgment as the final check; replacing review would trade throughput for unvalidated pre-production code.</details>

## AI-native roles

8. What share of teams use AI for refactoring, modernization, and testing, and how much time does it save weekly on average?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>More than 90 percent of surveyed teams, saving an average of six hours per week.</details>

9. Name the three tools cited as evolving from autocomplete to autonomous multi-file work, and the mechanism behind that evolution.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, and Google's Jules agent; the mechanism is reasoning-driven agents that plan tasks plus richer contextual information.</details>

10. Contrast the new AI-specific skill sets the article assigns to developers versus product managers.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Developers build scale, security, and testing skills around full-stack fluency and spec communication; product managers build strategy, customer-insight, and governance skills around design, prototyping, QA, and responsible AI.</details>

11. What breaks if an enterprise keeps specialist front-end/back-end/QA silos while deploying agents everywhere?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Handoffs multiply exactly where agents collapse steps, so the Cursor lesson — merged full-stack ownership with a per-release responsible individual — predicts coordination overhead will eat the six-hours-a-week savings.</details>

## Upskilling and measurement

12. What is the 57-versus-20 statistic, and what training formats does it compare?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>57 percent of top performers invest in hands-on workshops and one-on-one coaching versus 20 percent of bottom performers; most companies offer only on-demand courses.</details>

13. Which two outcome metrics do top performers track, at what rates, and which vanity metrics do laggards watch instead?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Quality improvements at 79 percent and speed gains at 57 percent; laggards watch tool usage frequency and code acceptance rates, which barely correlate with performance.</details>

14. Apply Shaukat's test to this dashboard: "AI wrote 60% of merged lines, acceptance rate 35%." What is missing?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Everything about what the code achieves — security, maintainability, usefulness, defect and reliability effects — which is why the article calls such counts adoption vanity metrics rather than impact evidence.</details>

## Change management and roadmap

15. What are the review-linkage figures for top versus bottom performers, and what asymmetry inside them matters most?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Nearly eight in ten top performers link gen-AI goals to both PM and developer reviews, versus 10 percent of bottom performers for developers and none for product managers; the zero for laggard PMs matters because the PM role is the one being rewired.</details>

16. Why does the article prefer behavior-based incentives over outcome-based ones for individuals?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Behaviors like surfacing automation opportunities or improving testing velocity are within individual control and build the foundation for impact, while team outcomes are not fully controllable by any one person.</details>

17. Recite the closing three-step roadmap in order and explain why ambition comes first.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Set ambitious leadership-uniting goals, develop a holistic operating-model blueprint fitted to context, then create a detailed road map for structures, workflows, metrics, and incentives. Ambition first supplies the energy and alignment that a blueprint and road map alone cannot generate.</details>
