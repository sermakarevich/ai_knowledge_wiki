# AI-native roles in the life cycle

**In one sentence:** As AI absorbs refactoring, modernization, and testing for over 90 percent of surveyed teams and saves six hours a week, product managers shift toward design, prototyping, quality assurance, and responsible AI while engineers shift toward full-stack fluency, spec communication, and architectural judgment.

## Key points

- AI has evolved from assistive coding aids to fully integrated collaborators taking on core tasks such as refactoring, modernization, and testing across the product development life cycle (PDLC).
- More than 90 percent of all surveyed software teams use AI for these activities, saving an average of six hours per week.
- The tooling mechanism is a move from basic autocomplete to reasoning-driven agents that plan tasks, backed by richer context — for example GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, and Google's Jules agent now execute long-running multi-file refactoring and modernization autonomously.
- Product managers spend less time on feature delivery and more on design, prototyping, quality assurance (QA), and responsible AI implementation, building skills in strategy, customer insight, and governance.
- Software engineers focus more on full-stack fluency, structured communication of specs, and architectural and systems trade-offs, building skills in scale, security, and testing.
- At Cursor, front-end, back-end, and QA boundaries have merged into full-stack ownership with a dedicated responsible individual per release, small cross-team pods, and product managers testing, designers prototyping in code, and business teams querying product data directly.

---

## From autocomplete to reasoning agents

The article marks the transition explicitly: "moving from basic autocomplete to reasoning-driven agents that plan tasks, supported by greater contextual information." Better orchestration plus more context is what lets tools graduate from inline completion to autonomous multi-file work. The head-count context matters: "as engineering head count growth slows, developers are expected to pair technical fluency with product, design, and business understanding" — fewer, broader engineers rather than more specialists.

## The new role bargains

Product work moves upstream and toward assurance: less feature-delivery coordination, more design, prototyping, QA, and responsible AI practice. Engineering work moves toward integration and judgment: full-stack fluency, crisp specs that agents can execute against, and systems trade-offs that agents cannot own. Both roles add AI-specific skills on top of traditional strengths rather than replacing them, and the article projects a future in which teams "operate as orchestrators of parallel and asynchronous AI agents" while enterprises generate custom software on demand, making "problem framing and intent specification critical skills."

## Cursor's team shape

Cursor's structure shows the merger in practice: "Traditional boundaries between front end, back end, and QA have merged into broader full-stack responsibilities." Coordination is explicit — "Each release has a dedicated responsible individual (DRI) who coordinates development, testing, and bug resolution" — while functional walls thin: "Product managers test features internally, designers prototype directly in code, and business and data teams use the platform to query product data." Machine learning, infrastructure, and product work happens in small pods with projects spanning teams.

## Truell on intent and validation

Cursor CEO and cofounder Michael Truell projects the endpoint: "Over the next decade, AI-assisted programming will let developers specify intent through a mix of formal programming languages and natural language, freeing them to focus on designing the logic of their software." The management metaphor follows: "Some individual contributors may spend part of their time as engineering managers directing a 'junior' team of asynchronous agents — a new type of work that could demand an entirely new skill set." And the quality corollary: "AI will likely play a role in code review and testing, and validation will accelerate by orders of magnitude."
