# Change management and the transformation roadmap

**In one sentence:** Nearly eight in ten top performers wire generative AI goals into product-manager and developer performance reviews (versus 10 percent and zero among laggards) and pair that accountability with behavior-based incentives plus a three-step transformation roadmap of ambitious goals, operating-model blueprint, and detailed rollout plan.

## Key points

- The third enabler is change management: nearly eight in ten top performers link generative AI (gen AI) goals to both product-manager and developer reviews, versus 10 percent of bottom performers for developers and none for product managers.
- Leading organizations incentivize behaviors that drive impact — identifying automation opportunities, improving velocity through AI-enabled testing, enhancing quality via model-assisted review — rather than raw tool usage.
- Behavior-based metrics work because they build the foundation for impact while avoiding judging individuals on outcomes they cannot fully control.
- High performers additionally expect teams to connect AI-enabled work to broader outcomes such as productivity, quality, or customer experience, building impact awareness without tying compensation to metrics outside individual influence.
- The article closes with three overarching moves: set ambitious leadership-uniting goals, develop a holistic future-operating-model blueprint tested to context, and create a detailed road map redefining structures, workflows, metrics, and incentives.
- Only this strategic, comprehensive posture — treating AI as a transformative catalyst for the whole development process — unlocks productivity at scale, because sustainable organizational change does not happen overnight even as tools accelerate.

---

## Accountability through reviews

"Top performers embed AI adoption directly into performance evaluations." The 80-versus-10-and-zero split is the starkest gap in the article, and the asymmetry inside it matters: even laggards occasionally set developer AI goals, but none set them for product managers — the role whose rewiring the article identifies as load-bearing. "By aligning individual objectives with the organization's AI strategy, companies create accountability and encourage employees to integrate AI into their day-to-day workflows."

## Behaviors, not usage

"Leading organizations focus incentives on the behaviors that drive impact — not just usage." The three exemplar behaviors — surfacing automation opportunities, accelerating velocity through AI-enabled testing, improving quality through model-assisted review — are all contributions an individual controls, unlike team throughput or defect rates. "These behavior-based metrics create the foundation for impact while avoiding the pitfall of judging individuals on outcomes they cannot fully control." The companion expectation closes the loop upward: teams narrate how their work connects to productivity, quality, or customer experience, which "builds awareness of impact without tying compensation to metrics outside individual influence."

## Beyond mandates to capability

"Embedding these expectations into performance systems moves companies beyond one-off mandates, making AI adoption a sustained organizational capability that continuously drives innovation and measurable value." The contrast is mandate versus capability: mandates decay when attention moves on, while review-embedded expectations compound because every cycle re-asks the question.

## The three-step roadmap

The closing passage prescribes sequence: "They set ambitious goals that unite leadership and energize the organization; they develop a holistic blueprint for the future operating model, tested and refined to fit the organization's context; and they create a detailed road map that redefines team structures, workflows, metrics, and incentives to unlock productivity at scale." Ambition first for energy and alignment, blueprint second for fit, road map last for execution detail. The final verdict: "Only by approaching AI in such a strategic, comprehensive way can software teams hope to harness its full potential as a force for innovation, efficiency, and value creation in software development."

**Covers:** enabler 3 — the review-linkage gap, behavior-based incentives, the mandate-to-capability argument, and the closing three-step transformation roadmap.
