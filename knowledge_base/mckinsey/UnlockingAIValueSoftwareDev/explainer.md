# Unlocking the value of AI in software development — Plain-language explainer

## What it is

McKinsey studied nearly 300 large companies using artificial intelligence (AI) to build software and found a split: most get modest help, while the best get dramatically better results — faster teams, happier customers, quicker releases, and far fewer defects. The article is a field guide to what the best do differently. The short version is that winners rebuild how software gets made around AI instead of just handing programmers a new tool.

## Why it matters

Software runs the modern economy, yet there are never enough skilled programmers and big projects are hard to coordinate. AI coding assistants promised relief, but simply installing them produced thin returns for most firms. The stakes are the gap the study measured: leaders pulled 15 percentage points ahead of laggards, with quality up by roughly a third and speed up by roughly a quarter. Any organization paying for AI licenses without changing how it works is buying the smaller outcome.

## How it works

Winners make two big shifts backed by three supports. First, they spread AI across every stage of building software — planning, writing code, testing, shipping, and tracking how features perform — instead of confining it to code suggestions, reaching four or more uses at scale far more often than peers. Second, they rewrite job descriptions: product managers do more designing, prototyping, and quality checking, while engineers become versatile generalists who write crisp instructions for AI and judge its work. The three supports are hands-on coaching rather than video courses, measuring results like defect rates and delivery speed rather than how often the tool is clicked, and tying AI goals to performance reviews so the new habits stick.

## Where it is used

The showcase example is Cursor, a young company whose programmers develop its AI coding product with the product itself. They plan changes with the assistant before writing code, run several AI helpers in the background while they work, preview results in a browser inside the editor, and let an automated reviewer check code before a human signs off. The same pattern applies anywhere software is built inside larger firms — banks, retailers, healthcare, and energy companies were all in the survey — wherever testing, modernizing old code, and shipping features consume engineering time.

## Takeaways

Treat AI as a reason to redesign the software process end to end, not as a faster keyboard. Train people with workshops and personal coaching, judge the effort by better products shipped sooner, and put AI expectations into reviews and incentives. Sequence the change as bold goals first, a redesigned way of working second, and a detailed rollout plan third.

## Jargon decoder

- Product development life cycle: the full journey of a software feature from idea and design through coding, testing, release, and follow-up.
- Agentic AI: AI systems that carry out multi-step tasks on their own, such as refactoring many files, rather than just suggesting the next few lines.
- Prompt engineering: the skill of breaking a problem down and describing it so clearly that an AI model produces useful work.
- Paved-path life cycle: Cursor's term for workflows where the easiest way to build software is also the officially approved way, because team rules are built into the tools.
- Dedicated responsible individual: one person assigned to coordinate everything about a single release, from building to testing to fixing bugs.
- Adoption metrics versus outcome metrics: counts of tool usage, such as how often suggestions are accepted, versus measures of results, such as fewer defects or faster releases.
