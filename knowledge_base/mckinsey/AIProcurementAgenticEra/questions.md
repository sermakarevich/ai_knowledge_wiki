# AI in procurement: redefining value creation — Retrieval Practice

Answer from memory before re-reading. Answers are collapsed below each question.

## Vision and the hybrid workforce

**Q1 (core recall):** What three outcomes does the article say agentic AI turns procurement into a strategic driver of?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Growth, sustainability, and resilience — procurement's mandate expands beyond transaction tasks to all three.</details>

**Q2 (why / breaks-if):** Why do the authors insist the power lies in the orchestration of outcomes rather than the automation of steps?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Because automating steps only speeds up existing handoffs, while orchestration means agents carry a commercial objective end to end — opportunity identification, sourcing, commercial strategy, post-award tracking — which is what closes the loop where contracted value usually leaks.</details>

**Q3 (transfer):** A supplier announces its own AI pricing agents. Using the article's two-sided-market point, what should the buying team do?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Treat agent adoption as non-optional and compete on better data, clearer negotiation objectives, and tighter human oversight, since both sides automating raises the bar for everyone.</details>

## Measurable impact

**Q4 (core recall with numbers):** Name the four quantified case results: the efficiency and value-capture gains in autonomous sourcing, the time cut and savings in telco negotiation, the leakage cut in pharma, and the inventory and EBIT effects at the aircraft maker.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Autonomous sourcing: 20–30% staff efficiency gain plus 1–3% more value capture. Telco long-tail negotiation: up to 90% less analysis/email time, 10–15% savings across vendors. Pharma compliance: 4% less value lost to leakage. Aircraft maker: 30% less active inventory, ~$700M EBIT boost.</details>

**Q5 (why / breaks-if):** What breaks if a team copies the pharma compliance agents but keeps checking invoices manually once a quarter?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The value evaporates: leakage compounds between reviews, the agents' continuous checking is what catches mismatches early, and batch manual review reintroduces the exact delay the case eliminated.</details>

## Data as a strategic asset

**Q6 (core recall with numbers):** What share of available data do procurement functions use today, and what four domains must the common data spine join up?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Less than 20 percent. The spine must unify spend, suppliers, contracts, and market benchmarks as a single source of truth.</details>

**Q7 (why / breaks-if):** Why does the article sequence digital links between existing tools before building the full data spine?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Links deliver connectivity without replacing systems, buying time and early agent value, while the spine is the heavier end state; reversing the order stalls agents for years behind a replacement program.</details>

## Agents and teaming

**Q8 (core recall):** What are the three specialist agent roles named in the article, and what three new capabilities must staff build?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Agents specialize in importing data from unstructured sources, analyzing data, or conversing in natural language. Staff must build prompt engineering, scenario evaluation, and change management.</details>

**Q9 (transfer):** A pilot wires one agent into each existing purchasing app without changing workflows. Which rewired-model warning does this violate, and what happens?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>It repeats the narrow hardwired-tool trap: isolated agents improve interfaces but never orchestrate outcomes, so gains stay local, data stays siloed, and the pilot never compounds into end-to-end returns.</details>

## Integration, advantage, roadmap

**Q10 (core recall):** State the article's closing verdict on what the four shifts do to procurement, and list the five roadmap steps.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Verdict: the shifts fundamentally reposition procurement as a competitive advantage for the enterprise. Roadmap: activate no-regret agents, define the long-term vision around business outcomes, scale fast in high-impact categories, build a cross-functional data/AI/procurement/change team, invest in immediate capability building.</details>

**Q11 (evaluation):** Which of the article's numbers would you demand primary-source evidence for before citing, and why? (Links to the critique.)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>See [[critical_thinking|Critical Analysis]]: the $700M EBIT and 90% time-cut figures are the most selection-sensitive — single unnamed cases with no baselines or controls — so treat them as existence proofs of upside, not expected values. The 20–30% / 1–3% / 10–15% / 4% / 30% figures share the same caveat to a lesser degree.</details>
