# Agents as infrastructure and human-agent teaming

**In one sentence:** Procurement should stop bolting narrow AI tools onto existing steps and instead run factories of specialized agents as operating infrastructure, with staff guiding and coaching digital counterparts while agents absorb repetitive transactional work.

## Key points

- Early AI use cases were narrow in scope, amounting to improved interfaces on existing tools with hardwired links that a rewired model must break.
- The replacement is factories of agents run as operating infrastructure, with teams of agents assembled per workflow from specialists in importing unstructured data, analyzing it, or conversing in natural language.
- Procurement tasks draw each workflow's agent team from diverse data sources, so the unit of design becomes the workflow's agent roster rather than any single tool.
- Humans and agents work side by side with staff guiding and coaching digital counterparts while agents take over most repetitive transactional work.
- Freed capacity redirects toward strategic decision-making, orchestration, and oversight, which redefines the procurement job around judgment rather than processing.
- The transition demands new staff capabilities in prompt engineering, scenario evaluation, and change management, making reskilling part of the infrastructure rather than an afterthought.

---

## From hardwired tools to agent factories

The article contrasts early AI use cases, which were narrow in scope and amounted to improved interfaces on existing tools, with the rewired alternative: break those hardwired links and run factories of agents as operating infrastructure. The factory metaphor implies standardized production of agent capability — reusable agent types, assembled on demand — instead of one-off pilots wired into single applications.

## Specialist agents assembled per workflow

Agents are designed for specific tasks such as importing data from unstructured sources, analyzing that data, or conversing in natural language. Procurement tasks then involve teams of such agents, assembled to meet each workflow's requirements and drawing on diverse data sources. Two design consequences follow. First, the workflow designer thinks in rosters: which importer, which analyst, which conversationalist does this sourcing event need. Second, unstructured intake becomes first-class work rather than a preprocessing chore, since supplier documents, bids, and market reports arrive unstructured by nature.

## Guiding and coaching digital counterparts

"In a rewired procurement function, humans and AI agents will work side by side," with staff guiding and coaching digital counterparts while agents take over most repetitive transactional work. Coaching language reframes supervision as training: reviewers correct agent outputs, encode the corrections as rules or examples, and watch subsequent behavior improve. The freed human capacity goes to strategic decision-making, orchestration, and oversight — the three activities where accountability cannot be delegated.

## The new capability stack

The article names three capabilities procurement personnel must build: prompt engineering, scenario evaluation, and change management. Prompt engineering is how staff steer agents precisely; scenario evaluation is how they stress-test agent recommendations against cost, service, and risk trade-offs; change management is how they carry the wider organization through newBuying behaviors. An organization that buys agents without building these three skills gets automation without adoption.
