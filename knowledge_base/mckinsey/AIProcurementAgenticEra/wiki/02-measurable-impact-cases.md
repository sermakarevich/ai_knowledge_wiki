# Measurable impact, delivered fast

**In one sentence:** Four field cases show agentic AI already delivering double-digit efficiency and savings gains in procurement, with pilots reaching scale in under a year on top of only a few key datasets.

## Key points

- A chemicals company piloting autonomous sourcing in consumables automated tender preparation, supplier identification and prequalification, bid analysis, and supplier query handling, lifting procurement staff efficiency by 20 to 30 percent and value capture by 1 to 3 percent.
- A telecommunications player using AI agents for long-tail specialized software negotiations cut analysis and email time by up to 90 percent and achieved 10 to 15 percent savings across vendors.
- A pharmaceutical company deploying agents for invoice-to-contract compliance cut the value lost through leakage by 4 percent via automatic delivery tracking and invoice and purchase-order checks against contract terms.
- An aircraft original equipment manufacturer automating order execution and inventory levels from production planning data cut active inventory by 30 percent and boosted earnings before interest and taxes by around $700 million.
- Organizations can move from prototype to pilot in weeks and from pilot to scale in under a year given a foundation of a few key datasets and defined use cases, with no massive enterprise resource planning replacement or multiyear information technology program required.
- The article's speed claim reframes the adoption barrier as data readiness and use-case definition rather than systems replacement, which is what makes the results reproducible instead of anecdotal.

## Autonomous sourcing in consumables

A chemicals company is piloting AI agents for autonomous sourcing in the consumables category. Its agents automate tender preparation, supplier identification and prequalification, and competing-bid analysis, while another agent routes, tracks, and synthesizes supplier queries and clarifications during sourcing exercises. The measured result is 20 to 30 percent higher procurement staff efficiency alongside 1 to 3 percent higher value capture. Note that one search extract describes this same case as a tech company sourcing external services, so the industry and category labels carry an [unverified] flag in the source file; the mechanism and the numbers are consistent across extracts.

## AI-guided long-tail negotiation

A telecommunications player applies AI agents to price negotiations across long-tail spend on specialized software products, a segment where human negotiators rarely have time for full preparation. The agents prepare a comprehensive prenegotiation fact base, make real-time suggestions during negotiations, evaluate trade-offs between cost, service levels, and risk, and automatically generate counteroffers to supplier proposals. Negotiating teams cut time spent on analysis and emails by up to 90 percent, and AI-guided negotiations produced 10 to 15 percent savings across vendors.

## Compliance and leakage control

A pharmaceutical company deployed agents to enforce invoice-to-contract compliance. The agents track supplier delivery performance and automatically check invoices and purchase orders against contract terms. The new approach cut value lost through leakage by 4 percent. This case matters because it shows agents creating value after the award rather than in sourcing, in the exact zone where procurement's claimed savings usually evaporate.

## Inventory and order automation

An aircraft original equipment manufacturer uses agents to automate order execution and inventory levels based on production planning data. Active inventory fell 30 percent and earnings before interest and taxes rose by around $700 million. This is the largest absolute number in the article and the clearest example of procurement agents moving an enterprise-level financial metric rather than a function-level productivity metric.

## Why speed is the point

The article stresses that these results arrive fast: from prototype to pilot in weeks and from pilot to scale in under a year, given a few key datasets and defined use cases, without massive enterprise resource planning replacements or multiyear information technology programs. The implication is that the binding constraint is data readiness plus a sharply defined workflow, not budget cycles or platform migrations, which sets up the rewired-model argument that data infrastructure is the real investment.
