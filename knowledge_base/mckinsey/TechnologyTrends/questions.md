# Retrieval Practice: Technology Trends
### Q1. What is the central thesis of McKinsey's Technology Trends Outlook 2026, and how are its 14 trends grouped?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The central thesis is that the technology story has moved off the screen into the physical world, with AI acting as accelerant across all trends while energy, hardware, talent, and cybersecurity constraints decide who can deploy it. The 14 trends fall into three groups: AI revolution (trends 01–04), compute and connectivity frontiers (05–09), and cutting-edge engineering (10–14). Two trends are new this year: agentic software development and AI for scientific discovery and engineering. See [[wiki/01-overview|Technology Trends Outlook 2026 — Overview]].
### Q2. Why do only about one-quarter of companies get meaningful acceleration from agentic software development, despite its nearly $1 trillion value potential?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Gains are highly skewed: 80 percent of engineers see only ~3 percent acceleration while the top 20 percent see a 55 percent boost, and in 30 percent of companies productivity actually fell after adoption. More code does not equal more product — one study found coding activity rose 180 percent but shipped releases only 30 percent. Capturing value requires rewiring the full product development life cycle with change management, verification of agent outputs, and small human–agent pods rather than just generating more code. See [[wiki/02-agentic-software-development|Agentic software development]].
### Q3. Why does agentic AI show broad adoption but thin enterprise value, and what makes it so expensive to run?
> [!tip]- Answer
> 89 percent of organizations regularly use AI with a majority at least experimenting with agents, yet only 37 percent attribute any positive EBIT impact to AI programs. A single agentic workflow can consume five to 30 times more tokens than a standard chatbot query through repeated reasoning loops, tool calls, retrieval, and orchestration, and 93 percent of respondents report exceeding AI budgets. The direction is from point agents to orchestrated end-to-end multi-agent workflows, with the biggest gains coming from redesigning workflows around human–agent collaboration. See [[wiki/03-agentic-ai|Agentic AI]].
### Q4. How is AI changing scientific discovery from prediction into closed-loop systems, and what remains the strategic bottleneck?
> [!tip]- Answer
> AI is moving from AlphaFold-style prediction and simulation into closed-loop labs where AI proposes hypotheses and robots run experiments, as in the Berkeley A-Lab and Ginkgo's GPT-5 cloud-lab work that cut cell-free protein synthesis costs 40 percent across six cycles. Firms deploy AI across experiment design, molecular optimization, toxicity prediction, and clinical-trial design, backed by $12.5 billion in equity investment through H1 2026. Scientific data is the strategic bottleneck, addressed via biobanks like the 490,640 UK Biobank whole genomes, consortium pooling, federated learning, and proprietary data generation. See [[wiki/04-ai-for-scientific-discovery|AI for scientific discovery and engineering]].
### Q5. Why has scaling AI become a physical-stack problem, and how are model architectures responding?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Data centers could account for 14 percent of US power demand by 2030 (up from 3 percent in 2022) with nearly $7 trillion in build-out capex, while bottlenecks span processors, high-bandwidth memory, transformers, packaging, lithography, and skilled labor. Economics are shifting from training to inference, making cost per token and performance per watt central. Architectures respond with small language models like Phi-4-mini (3.8B vs 175B parameters), compression, reasoning models, multimodal and world models, diffusion models, and neurosymbolic AI. See [[wiki/05-ai-infrastructure-and-models|AI Infrastructure and Model Architectures]].
### Q6. What is driving the shift from general-purpose GPUs to application-specific semiconductors, and who is leading it?
> [!tip]- Answer
> As enterprises embed AI in core workflows, diverse latency, throughput, and efficiency needs make workload-specific optimization central, with McKinsey estimating inference will be 30–40 percent of all data-center demand by 2030 and the majority of AI compute. Hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft) codesign custom silicon with chip firms — e.g., AWS Inferentia-based instances deliver up to 2.3× throughput at up to 70 percent lower cost per inference. System performance now depends on moving and packaging data: high-bandwidth memory, TSMC CoWoS packaging, chiplets, silicon photonics, and high-speed interconnects. See [[wiki/06-application-specific-semiconductors|Application-specific semiconductors]].
### Q7. How has AI compressed the cybersecurity defense window, and what architectural shift does defense require?
> [!tip]- Answer
> More than three-quarters of vulnerabilities are now classified as zero day, and AI agents can autonomously chain dozens of exploitation steps — one sanctioned test escalated a single low-severity flaw into full unauthorized file access. Threat cycles that once spanned weeks or months have collapsed, with public-application exploitation up 44 percent in a year fueling 49 percent more ransomware groups. Defense must shift from perimeter scanning to zero-trust continuous runtime inspection with automated containment, plus unified identity governance since machine identities now outnumber humans roughly 100 to one. See [[wiki/07-cybersecurity-and-trustworthy-systems|Cybersecurity and trustworthy systems]].
### Q8. Why is advanced connectivity now sold on reliability and sovereignty rather than data transport, and what role do satellites play?
> [!tip]- Answer
> AI workloads spreading to factories, stores, hospitals, and vehicles demand lower latency, stronger reliability, and higher uplink capacity — gen AI traffic was 26 percent of uplink versus ~10 percent typical — so telcos sell AI-ready fiber, software-defined slicing, and resilient networks. 5G stand-alone is going live (AT&T nationwide) with commercial slicing products from Verizon Business and Vodafone. Satellite is a complement, not a substitute: Starlink neared 10,000 satellites with 225 operator partnerships across 88 countries, expected to capture close to 20 percent of the addressable fiber market in hard-to-reach areas. See [[wiki/08-advanced-connectivity|Advanced connectivity]].
### Q9. What form-factor shift defines immersive reality in 2026, and where is adoption actually landing?
> [!tip]- Answer
> The category is shifting from heavy VR/MR immersion to lightweight AI smart glasses that augment everyday tasks — smart-glass shipments surged 167 percent YoY in Q1 2026 to ~2.25 million units, and Meta sold over seven million AI glasses in 2025. AI turns the device into a context engine with on-device vision, audio, and gesture understanding, plus on-device inference cutting latency and cloud dependence. Adoption stays at experimentation (score 2), deepening only in manufacturing, healthcare, field service, and training where devices beat phones at a specific job. See [[wiki/09-immersive-reality-technologies|Immersive-reality technologies]].
### Q10. Why does quantum stay at experimentation despite heavy government funding, and where is adoption most advanced?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Organizations still test quantum via codevelopment programs and selected field deployments, with few scaling quantum computing in core workflows — broad commercial utility awaits error correction and scalable architectures. Hiring has contracted and concentrated in specialized R&D, with quantum scientists and engineers now the largest role and 59 percent of postings in R&D. Governments accelerate anyway: US $2 billion Commerce letters of intent plus 2026 executive orders, China's 12,000-km quantum communication network, and Europe's Quantum Europe Strategy. The most advanced adoption is in government and defense — post-quantum-cryptography migration and quantum sensing. See [[wiki/10-quantum-technologies|Quantum technologies]].
### Q11. What is blocking robotics from moving past demos, and how does robotics-as-a-service change the economics?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Fine manipulation is the bottleneck: the NIST Task Board benchmark rates in-hand manipulation and grasping among the hardest capabilities to achieve and measure, limiting dependable production use. Robotics-as-a-service shifts $150,000–$500,000 humanoid unit costs from capital outlay to per-robot/per-hour/per-task operating expenditure, with Locus Robotics passing five billion warehouse picks as scalability proof. Dark factories accelerate via AI, robotics, and digital twins, though most value comes from brownfield integration, and adoption stays at experimentation (score 2) with China holding ~2 million industrial robots. See [[wiki/11-future-of-robotics|Future of Robotics]].
### Q12. How is mobility moving from pilots to constrained commercial scale, and where is value shifting?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Autonomy narrows to repeatable domains: Aurora's driverless Dallas–Houston trucking, Waymo's 15 million-plus 2025 trips, Walmart's million-plus drone deliveries, and Starship's 9 million-plus ground-robot deliveries. Automotive value shifts to software, with the software and electronics market growing at 5.9 percent CAGR to $548 billion by 2035 versus ~1 percent for vehicles overall. Scale now depends on charging networks, regulation like the FAA's proposed BVLOS rule, and public trust, with hiring pivoting from build to run — 37 percent of postings in operations. See [[wiki/12-future-of-mobility|Future of Mobility]].
### Q13. In space technologies, what is scaling, what is still nascent, and what anchors the space economy?
> [!tip]- Answer
> LEO broadband is moving mainstream via Starlink and OneWeb plus Amazon Leo's 300-plus satellites, while direct-to-device (AST SpaceMobile's BlueBird) is nascent and risky after the BlueBird 7 launch loss. Earth observation value now comes from workflow integration — hyperspectral sensing, AI analytics, and data fusion for emissions, agriculture, and disaster response. Defense demand anchors the economy, shown by the US Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture Tranche 1 launch, while orbital compute is only early experimentation with no evidence it competes with terrestrial cloud. See [[wiki/13-future-of-space-technologies|Future of space technologies]].
### Q14. Why is biomanufacturing capacity the strategic control point in life sciences, and how is the field shifting to scale-up?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Precision diagnostics like liquid biopsy and companion diagnostics are entering clinical workflows, and outcome-led food and agriculture (gene-edited crops, precision fermentation) gain traction — but scaling cell culture, fermentation capacity, quality evidence, and treatment-center readiness constrains growth. The response is pilot plants and shared infrastructure, e.g., BioMADE transitioning Lygos's pilot facility into a nationally available multiuser site. Talent confirms the pivot: operations plus general/administrative exceed a third of postings while clinical-trials expertise leads demand, and adoption scores 4, scaling in progress. See [[wiki/14-future-of-life-sciences-and-bioengineering|Future of life sciences and bioengineering]].
### Q15. Why is energy-tech deployment fragmenting into regional pathways instead of one uniform transition?
> [!tip]- Answer
> Adoption scores 4 (scaling) but splits by region because progress depends on local power costs, grid constraints, policy design, and industrial priorities rather than emissions targets alone. Hydrogen, ammonia, and synthetic fuels advance only where policy, low-cost power, infrastructure, and offtake align; the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism enters its definitive regime in 2026, reshaping supplier decisions. Talent shows deployment mode: postings skew to operations and non-R&D roles, with sharp shortages in carbon (0.2×) and transmission/grid (0.2×) skills. See [[wiki/15-future-of-energy-and-sustainability|Future of Energy and Sustainability Technologies]].
### Q16. (Evaluation) As a technology leader with limited capital, which two trends would you prioritize for near-term investment and which would you deliberately defer, judging by the report's adoption and talent signals?
> [!tip]- Answer
> A defensible judgment is to prioritize cybersecurity and trustworthy systems plus energy and sustainability technologies, since both score adoption 4 (scaling in progress) with demand tilted to non-R&D roles, meaning deployment is underway and spending buys operational capability, not research. Defer quantum and immersive reality, both stuck at adoption score 2 (experimentation) with contracting or narrowing hiring, since returns there remain proofs of concept. This recommendation follows the report's own logic that winners are decided by securing energy, hardware, talent, and trust to operate in the real world. See [[wiki/01-overview|Technology Trends Outlook 2026 — Overview]].
