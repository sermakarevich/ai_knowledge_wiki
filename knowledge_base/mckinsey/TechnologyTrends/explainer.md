# Technology Trends — In Plain Language

## What is this about?

This report looks at 14 big technology trends for 2026.

The main message is simple: technology has left the screen.
It is now about the real world — robots, power plants, chips,
hospitals, cars, satellites, and factories.

Artificial Intelligence (AI, computer systems that learn from data)
is the thread that connects almost all of them.
It speeds up coding, science, robots, security, and chip design.

The report groups the 14 trends into three buckets:

- AI revolution (4 trends): smart coding helpers, smart agents,
  AI for science, and AI computers and models.
- Compute and connectivity frontiers (5 trends): special chips,
  security, networks, virtual reality glasses, and quantum computers.
- Cutting-edge engineering (5 trends): robots, transport,
  space, life sciences, and energy.

Two trends are new this year: systems that write software on
their own, and AI that helps discover drugs and materials.

## Why does it matter?

Because the gap between promise and real results is large.

Almost 9 in 10 companies use AI somewhere, but only about
1 in 3 sees a clear profit gain from it.
Buying tools is easy. Changing how people work is hard.

At the same time, AI has hit physical limits:

- Power: AI data centers (big buildings full of computers)
  may use 14% of all US electricity by 2030, up from 3% in 2022.
- Hardware: there are shortages of chips, memory,
  transformers, and machines that make chips.
- Money: building data centers worldwide could cost
  almost $7 trillion by 2030.
- People: some fields lack experts in automation
  and in running AI reliably.

Whoever solves power, chips, people, and safety first
will win, not whoever has the smartest demo.

Safety matters too. Over three-quarters of known security
holes are "zero day" flaws, meaning attackers already know
about them before they are publicly reported.
Fake identities from bots and agents now outnumber
real human users by about 100 to 1.

## How does it work?

Think of it as three layers working together.

First layer: smart helpers do the work.
Coding assistants plan, write, test, and fix programs.
Work agents plan and run multi-step office tasks,
like handling orders across several systems.
Science AI suggests new molecules or materials,
and robots in automatic labs test them day and night.

Second layer: heavy machines make it possible.
Training (teaching a model) used to be the main cost.
Now daily use, called inference (asking a trained model
questions), is the bigger cost.
So companies build special chips for specific jobs
to get more answers per watt of electricity.
They also pack memory closer to chips, improve cooling,
and shrink models so they can run locally on
a phone, laptop, or factory computer.

Third layer: safety and connections hold it together.
Old security trusted everything inside the office network.
New security trusts nothing by default — every person,
device, and software agent is checked again and again.
This is called zero-trust (never trust, always verify).
New networks use software control, 5G slices
(dedicated lanes on a mobile network), fiber cables,
and satellites to stay fast and reliable.
Hospitals, factories, and cars need this reliable link
before they trust AI with real tasks.

## Where can this be used?

- Software teams: let agents handle boring code and tests,
  while people focus on what to build and whether it works.
  Best results come from small human-plus-agent teams.
- Drug and materials labs: use AI to design candidates,
  then test them in automatic labs. This can cut
  experiment costs by up to 40% in early trials.
- Factories and warehouses: rent robots by the hour
  instead of buying them. This is called Robotics-as-a-Service
  (RaaS, robots rented like a subscription).
- Hospitals: use blood tests that find cancer signals
  (liquid biopsy, a blood test that looks for tumor traces)
  and computer models of organs to plan treatment.
- Transport: driverless trucks on fixed routes, delivery
  drones for light packages, and electric cars, which were
  1 in 4 cars sold worldwide in 2025.
- Energy and farms: track methane leaks from satellites,
  price carbon on imports, and grow gene-edited crops
  that give more yield with less waste.
- Offices and field work: lightweight smart glasses give
  translation, instructions, and remote expert help
  without leaving the real world.

## Conclusions & takeaways

1. AI is no longer just chat on a screen. It is an helper
   for building things in the physical world.
2. Tools alone do not raise profit. Winners redesign
   teamwork, checks, and rules around humans plus agents.
3. Growth is limited by electricity, chips, and skilled people,
   not by ideas. Plan power and hardware first.
4. Attacks are faster and automatic now. Build continuous
   checks, protect machine identities, and prepare
   for quantum-safe codes (new codes that even
   future quantum computers cannot easily break).
5. Progress is uneven. Networks, security, transport,
   life sciences, and energy grow where they solve
   a clear paid problem. Robots, quantum computers,
   smart glasses, space computers, and frontier AI
   are still mostly experiments.
6. Practical rule: start small, measure profit or time saved,
   secure power and data, train people to supervise agents,
   and only then scale up.

## Jargon decoder

| Term | What it means in plain words |
| ---- | ---------------------------- |
| AI agent | A program that plans and does multi-step tasks with little human help |
| Inference | The daily use of a trained AI model, when you ask it questions |
| ASIC (Application-Specific Integrated Circuit) | A chip built for one job only, so it is faster and saves power |
| Zero-trust | A safety rule: check every user and device each time, trust no one by default |
| Zero day flaw | A security hole that attackers already use before it is publicly known |
| RaaS (Robotics-as-a-Service) | Renting robots by the hour or task instead of buying them |
| LLM (Large Language Model) | An AI trained on huge amounts of text to read, write, and answer |
| 5G slicing | Splitting one mobile network into private lanes with guaranteed speed |
| Liquid biopsy | A blood test that looks for tiny traces of disease such as cancer |
| Quantum-safe code | New encryption (secret-code methods) built to resist future quantum computers |
| Digital twin | A live computer copy of a factory or machine used to test changes safely |
| HBM (High-Bandwidth Memory) | Super-fast memory stacked close to the chip to feed AI with data |
