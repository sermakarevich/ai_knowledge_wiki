# Future of Mobility
**In one sentence:** Autonomous mobility is moving from pilots to constrained commercial scale — corridor trucking, retail drone routes, sidewalk robots, shared micromobility and electric boats — while value shifts to software, jobs shift from build-out to operations, and scale now depends on charging, regulation, and public trust rather than proving the tech works.
## Key points
- Autonomous trucking is scaling first in fixed corridors and industrial sites: Aurora began commercial driverless trucking on public roads between Dallas and Houston, while Kodiak and Atlas Energy Solutions expanded driverless operations in the Permian Basin.
- Automotive value is shifting to software: centralized computing, connected architectures and over-the-air updates, with McKinsey estimating the global automotive software and electronics market grows at 5.9% CAGR to $548 billion by 2035 versus ~1% for the overall vehicle market.
- Drone delivery is moving from demos to structured retail operations pending a US FAA proposed BVLOS (beyond-visual-line-of-sight) rule; Walmart passed 1 million drone deliveries across 66 stores in four states but is constrained to 2.8 pounds, versus Amazon Prime Air targeting 5 pounds or less and Keeta Drone in China focusing within 3 km across 70 set routes.
- Ground delivery robots are gaining in short-distance food, grocery and campus use: Serve Robotics built 2,000+ robots by end-2025, Starship completed 9M+ deliveries with 2,700+ robots across 270+ locations and expects 12,000 robots by end-2027, and Neolix passed 10,000 cumulative L4 vehicle deliveries in 300+ cities in China.
- Electric air taxis (eVTOL) are progressing toward certification but unscaled: Joby completed 850+ flights in 2025 toward FAA certification flight testing, yet still face air-traffic integration, cost, noise and public acceptance.
- Shared micromobility is consolidating into profitable urban mix: Lime surpassed 1 billion lifetime rides in 2025, Seattle bike/scooter share hit 10 million rides in 2025 (+60% YoY), with commuting now a key trip purpose.
- Mobility hiring has pivoted from build to run: postings remain well below the 2022 peak and fell 2024–2025, steepest in electrical/software/systems/test engineering, while technicians (largest role) and operations managers held up; 37% of 2025 postings are operations, and AI (0.3×) and optimization (0.1×) talent supply falls short of demand.
- Adoption is regionally split (score 3—Piloting): China EVs >50% of 2025 car sales with 4.7M public chargers (>65% global, >75% of global growth); EU e-car sales +30% with ~1.3M chargers; US e-car sales −2% with ~235,000 chargers; India 2.3M EV sales and e-car sales +75%, SE Asia e-car sales more than doubled.

## Autonomous trucking: early deployment in constrained settings
Driverless trucking is finding an early commercial foothold along constrained routes and in repeatable logistics settings, rather than nationwide autonomy. Aurora began commercial driverless trucking operations on public roads between Dallas and Houston. Kodiak and Atlas Energy Solutions expanded driverless operations in the Permian Basin. Freight autonomy is more likely to scale first in fixed corridors, semistructured industrial environments, and hub-to-hub networks, where safety and operating economics are easier to establish.
**Covers:** pp.105–106, autonomous trucking

## Software-defined vehicles
Automotive vehicles are being built around centralized computing, connected architectures, and over-the-air updates, allowing automakers to improve functionality and offer software-enabled features. McKinsey analysis estimates the global automotive software and electronics market could grow at a 5.9 percent compound annual growth rate and reach $548 billion by 2035, compared with roughly 1 percent annual growth for the overall vehicle market.
**Covers:** pp.105–106, software value

## Drone delivery: pilots to retail expansion
Autonomous delivery via drones is beginning to move from proof-of-flight demonstrations toward more structured operations, supported by regulation. In 2025, the Federal Aviation Administration proposed a rule to enable routine BVLOS drone operations, allowing drones to fly beyond the operator's direct line of sight without case-by-case waivers. Walmart's expansion of drone deliveries to 66 stores across four states, following more than one million completed drone deliveries, shows concentration on small-payload delivery.

| Operator | Payload / range constraint |
|---|---|
| Walmart | 2.8-pound delivery weight limit |
| Amazon Prime Air | eligible packages weighing five pounds or less, delivered in 60 minutes or less |
| Keeta Drone (China) | deliveries within three kilometers among 70 set routes |

In practice, drone delivery is still concentrated in suburban or office-dense areas where a central pickup station is easier to manage than true door-to-door residential delivery.
**Covers:** pp.105–106, drone delivery

## Ground-based autonomous delivery
Ground delivery by mobile robots is gaining traction in short-distance food, grocery, and campus delivery. Serve Robotics built more than 2,000 delivery robots by the end of 2025. Starship Technologies announced in late 2025 that it had completed more than nine million deliveries via a fleet of over 2,700 robots across more than 270 locations, had partnered with Uber Eats to expand autonomous sidewalk delivery across Europe and the United States, and expects 12,000 robots in service by end-2027. In China, Neolix said it has surpassed 10,000 cumulative L4 autonomous-vehicle deliveries in more than 300 cities, with commercial use across express freight, fresh food, cold chain, and supermarkets.
**Covers:** p.106, ground delivery

## Electric air taxis (eVTOL)
Urban air taxis, or electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) vehicles, are progressing from prototypes toward certification, operations planning, and infrastructure development; commercial flights could happen in a few years. Joby completed more than 850 flights across its electric air taxi fleet in 2025 and positioned itself for Federal Aviation Administration certification flight testing. Other players are testing eVTOL aircraft in local operating conditions and planning early launch markets. Passenger eVTOL services still face certification plus air traffic integration, cost, noise, and public acceptance before scale.
**Covers:** p.106, air taxis

## Shared micromobility
After market consolidation, the industry is entering a phase focused on steady, profitable growth via cost management and unit economics. Shared bikes and scooters are increasingly treated as part of the urban mobility mix rather than a stand-alone novelty. Lime surpassed one billion lifetime rides in 2025. Seattle's bike and scooter share program reached a record ten million rides in 2025, with trips up more than 60 percent from the prior year as the city added protected bike lanes and expanded parking infrastructure. Use has shifted toward utilitarian trips, with commuting now a key purpose alongside leisure, errands, and appointments.
**Covers:** pp.106–107, micromobility

## Maritime mobility
Autonomous electric boats are being deployed on predictable routes where infrastructure can be integrated with public transit and port systems. Stockholm expanded service for Candela's electric hydrofoil ferry following a successful pilot, showing how electric propulsion and hydrofoil technology can reduce energy consumption and enhance passenger experience. Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority and NYK Group collaborated to advance trials of port-to-port maritime autonomous surface ships. The segment remains in early operational, route-specific deployment.
**Covers:** pp.106–107, maritime

## Talent and labor markets: demand
Mobility job postings remain well below their 2022 peak and continued to decline from 2024 to 2025. The steepest pullbacks came in engineering roles such as electrical, software, systems, and test engineers as the industry moves from build-out toward operations. Roles for technicians, now the single largest role, and operations managers held up better, pointing to a market focused on running and maintaining mobility systems at scale. Over a third of mobility job postings are in operations, the highest share of any trend.

| Business function share, 2025 | % |
|---|---|
| Operations | 37 |
| R&D | 29 |
| General and administrative | 13 |
| Other | 11 |
| Sales and marketing | 10 |

## Talent and labor markets: skills availability
Fleet management, the most in-demand skill, is well supplied, and Python expertise exceeds openings. However, in artificial intelligence and optimization, supply falls short of open roles, which could constrain autonomous-vehicle expansion.

## Adoption developments across the globe
Adoption score: 3—Piloting. Organizations are deploying mobility technologies in several specific business use cases but mostly launching pilots to test feasibility. EVs and micromobility are moving toward maturity, while autonomous vehicles, drones, eVTOL aircraft, and maritime autonomy remain selective and use-case specific.

- China: top market for EVs, batteries, public charging, robotaxis. Electric cars >50% of annual car sales in 2025; most public charging globally (>65% at end-2025); charging grew from ~3.4M points end-2024 to >4.7M end-2025, over 75% of global growth. Excels in manufacturing scale, cost competition, integrated industrial policy.
- Europe: shaped by regulation, sustainability, urban policy. EU electric-car sales +30% in 2025; public network ~1.3M charging points in 2025; micromobility integrated into transit; active in shared mobility and maritime electrification.
- United States: strides in robotaxis, autonomous trucking, drone delivery, software-defined vehicles, start-ups; EV adoption uneven. Electric-car sales −2% YoY in 2025; public charging stock ~235,000 points end-2025.
- Middle East: early launch market for regulated advanced mobility (air mobility, smart-city, digital transport), driven by urban agendas, infrastructure investment, government innovation programs.
- India and Southeast Asia: lower-cost two-/three-wheelers, buses, high-utilization fleets. India 2.3M annual EV sales in 2025, e-car sales +75% YoY; SE Asia e-car sales more than doubled (Indonesia +125%); affordability, density, utilization favor lightweight shared electric models.
- Latin America/Caribbean and Africa: earlier stage; LatAm e-car sales ~350,000 (+70% YoY 2025), constrained by charging, affordability, power reliability, availability. Select advances in electric buses, motorcycles, fleets.
- Cross-region rule: adoption strongest when solving a clear operating problem (fuel costs, underserved areas, emissions); slower when depending on novelty or missing infrastructure.

> 'The next challenge is not proving that autonomous and electric mobility work. It's building the charging networks, regulatory frameworks, and public trust needed to scale them. Commercial success will depend not just on ecosystem coordination but also on advances in vehicle technology.'
> — Ruth Heuss, senior partner, Berlin
**Covers:** pp.108–110, adoption + quote

## Underlying technologies
- Advanced air mobility: electric aircraft for short-range passenger/cargo plus supporting infrastructure.
- AI and simulation: train, test, validate autonomous systems before/during deployment.
- Autonomous-driving software: perceive environment, decide, drive with limited/no human input.
- Batteries and electric powertrains: replace internal combustion across passenger, freight, shared vehicles.
- Charging infrastructure: physical + digital systems delivering electricity, from public/depot chargers to battery-swapping networks.
- Drone and autonomous delivery: unmanned aerial and ground vehicles transporting goods.
- Fleet management and mobility platforms: operations software to run shared, autonomous, electric services at scale.
- Grid integration: manage connection between EVs, charging networks, power grid.
- Maritime mobility: electric propulsion, autonomous navigation, port systems for lower-emission waterways.
- Materials and circularity: lightweight/recyclable materials, battery recycling, processes reducing weight and life-cycle emissions.
- Sensors and positioning: lidar, radar, cameras for object identification, localization, safe navigation.
- Software-defined vehicle architectures: computing/connectivity platforms allowing post-factory updates.
**Covers:** pp.110–111, underlying technologies list

## Key uncertainties
- EV infrastructure and supply chains: how incentives, battery costs, charging availability evolve; whether charging, grid capacity, battery supply chains keep pace, especially for fleets and heavy-duty transport.
- Affordability and regional competitiveness: whether lower-cost EV platforms broaden adoption across markets with different trade policies, preferences, charging infrastructure.
- Public acceptance of autonomous vehicles: must demonstrate safety/reliability; robotaxis must scale beyond dense urban markets; trucking must move beyond constrained routes and private industrial settings.
- Scalability of emerging delivery models: drones and ground robots proven in early deployments but broader viability uncertain; depends on regulation, economics, battery performance, community acceptance.
- Mobility-system software risk: more connected/software-defined means more exposure to failures and cyberattacks; a single over-the-air update can reach an entire fleet.
**Covers:** pp.111–112, uncertainties

## Big questions
- Which mobility technologies are ready to scale now, and which should remain in pilots until safety, economics, infrastructure, and customer acceptance improve?
- Where should autonomy be prioritized first across robotaxis, trucking, industrial sites, ports, public transit, and advanced driver-assistance systems?
- How should companies and governments coordinate infrastructure for electrified, autonomous, multimodal mobility?
- Which players will capture value as mobility becomes software-defined: automakers, battery makers, semiconductor companies, software providers, fleet operators, charging networks, or infrastructure owners?
- How should companies manage trust, safety, cybersecurity, and operational reliability as mobility systems become more autonomous?
**Covers:** pp.111–112, big questions
