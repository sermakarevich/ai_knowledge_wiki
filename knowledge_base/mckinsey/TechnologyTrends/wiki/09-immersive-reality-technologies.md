# Immersive-reality technologies (Trend 08)

**In one sentence:** Immersive reality is shifting from heavy VR/MR immersion to lightweight AI smart glasses that augment everyday tasks with on-device context-aware AI, while enterprise use deepens in manufacturing, healthcare, and field operations but hiring narrows toward lean R&D and adoption stays at experimentation (score 2).

## Key points
- The category is shifting from immersion to augmentation: lightweight smart glasses that keep users anchored in the physical world (visual prompts, translation, navigation, remote support) are the center of gravity, e.g. Meta's Ray-Ban Display with cameras, microphones, speakers, full-color in-lens display, compute, AI, and gesture input.
- Smart glasses are the clearest growth pocket: global shipments surged 167 percent year over year in Q1 2026 to ~2.25 million units; EssilorLuxottica reported Ray-Ban Meta AI glasses sales grew more than 200 percent YoY in H1 2025, and Meta sold more than seven million AI-powered smart glasses in 2025; devices over 400 grams cause fatigue, while the new lighter generation crosses the all-day wearability threshold.
- AI turns the device into a context engine: on-device/near-device vision, audio, spatial mapping, eye/hand/gesture tracking let models understand what the user sees, hears, and does (e.g. Meta's late-2025 update: Conversation Focus in noise, music recommendations from gaze, visual AI in lens); Google announced Gemini-powered glasses at I/O 2026 (audio first, display later); Apple reportedly shelved a Vision Pro revamp to prioritize AI smart glasses.
- Real-time AI inference is moving onto the device, cutting latency for gesture/eye tracking, object recognition, and translation and enabling autonomous use without constant cloud (e.g. Brilliant Labs' Halo processes voice, vision, and sensor data locally).
- Platform competition moved beyond headset hardware into software/OS stacks: Samsung Galaxy XR (Oct 2025, first Android XR device, co-developed with Google and Qualcomm) represents an open multi-OEM approach versus Meta's vertically integrated stack (Ray-Ban Display + neural wristband); enterprise adoption concentrates in manufacturing, healthcare, field service, training, logistics, design, and safety-critical operations (RealWear Arc 3 179-gram assisted-reality headset + Ari OS Oct 2025; Aena VR+haptic firefighter training; Medivis FDA-cleared AR cranial navigation).
- Hiring is well below the metaverse-era surge and still narrowing (total postings down again in 2025), with declines in software developers, project managers, and network engineers but momentum tilting toward machine-learning and test engineers; R&D is 51% of 2025 postings; VR (49%) and ML (36%) anchor skill demand while 3D skills are scarcest (0.8x availability ratio); adoption score is 2—Experimentation with steady-not-explosive scale expected only where devices beat phones/laptops at a specific job.

## Latest developments

**Covers:** Technology Trends Outlook 2026, pp. 76–78 (Recent developments involving immersive-reality technologies)

- Shift from immersion to augmentation: devices help users stay anchored in the physical world, especially lightweight smart glasses; immersive reality is "more about making everyday tasks easier through visual prompts, contextual assistance, translation, navigation, and remote support."
- Meta Ray-Ban Display launch (Sept 30, 2025) demonstrates the shift: cameras, microphones, speakers, full-color in-lens display, compute, AI, gesture input in familiar eyewear form factor, "useful even without a fully immersive visual experience."
- Global smart-glasses category: +167% YoY in Q1 2026, ~2.25 million units shipped (IDC, updated July 1, 2026).
- EssilorLuxottica H1 2025: Ray-Ban Meta AI glasses sales grew >200% YoY (press release July 28, 2025); CNBC (Feb 11, 2026) reports EssilorLuxottica more than tripled Meta AI glasses sales in 2025.
- Apple shelved Vision headset revamp to prioritize Meta-like AI smart glasses (Bloomberg, Oct 1, 2025) — reinforces shift to lighter, more wearable devices.
- Wearability threshold: headsets over 400 grams historically too heavy for long wear without fatigue; by 2026 a new lighter generation crossed the threshold, making all-day frequent use plausible while remaining socially present (capture media, hear audio, ask AI questions).
- AI as context engine: wearables become local context engines using on-device/near-device vision, audio, spatial mapping, gesture data to understand surroundings in real time. Meta late-2025 AI glasses update (v21, Dec 16, 2025): focus on the person the wearer is speaking to even in noise, music recommendations from gaze, visual AI responses in lens while staying present. Google I/O 2026 (May 19, 2026): Gemini-powered glasses coming, audio first then display.
- Data engine for physical AI: glasses capture real-world spatial context, human actions, environmental responses harder to obtain than language data; General Intuition building environment foundation models needing deep spatial/temporal reasoning, using action-labeled video datasets (TechCrunch, Oct 16, 2025: $134M seed).
- On-device inference: reduces latency, enables gesture/eye tracking, object recognition, real-time translation without full cloud reliance; Brilliant Labs' Halo processes voice, vision, sensor data locally (Business Wire, Mar 5, 2026).
- Platform battle beyond hardware — assistants, dev tools, app stores, device management, enterprise security:
  - Samsung Galaxy XR (Oct 22, 2025), first Android XR device, open multi-OEM platform co-developed with Google and Qualcomm.
  - Meta: >7M AI smart glasses sold in 2025 + Ray-Ban Display with in-lens screen and neural wristband = vertically integrated hardware-software-AI stack.
- Enterprise deepening in manufacturing, healthcare, field service, training, logistics, design, safety-critical operations:
  - RealWear Arc 3 + Ari OS (Oct 28, 2025) for frontline workers (manufacturing, logistics, warehousing, healthcare, pharma, robotics): 179-gram assisted-reality headset with see-through display; Ari OS enables natural-language app control and AI-assisted workflows.
  - Aena pilot: VR headsets + haptic gloves for airport firefighters, maintenance, marshallers (Passenger Terminal Conference, Mar 19, 2026).
  - Medivis cranial navigation platform with FDA clearance (Dec 16, 2025): AR maps patient imaging into the operative field for real-time surgical guidance.

## Adoption developments across the globe

**Covers:** p. 79

- Adoption score: 2—Experimentation. Organizations test functionality/viability with small-scale prototypes and selective deployments; few scale broadly, though adoption advances in healthcare, manufacturing, retail, design, training, field operations, industrial assistance.
- Form factor matters: smart glasses gain momentum (fit daily routines, less behavioral change); headsets stay relevant where immersion is the value (simulation, spatial design, gaming, high-stakes visualization).
- Geography: US remains key early consumer market (platform players + retail availability). Consumer adoption more geographically uneven (product availability, camera norms, privacy expectations, language support, regulation vary); Meta's 2026 expansion adds Chile, Colombia, Japan, Korea, Peru, Singapore for Ray-Ban Meta and Oakley Meta glasses. Enterprise adoption less regionally differentiated (manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, training, field service across majors) but pace depends on budgets, safety requirements, device management, data governance, measurable ROI.
- Outlook: steady rather than explosive; scale is clearest when tech solves a specific problem better than smartphone/tablet/laptop; less clear when mainly novelty, entertainment, or screen replacement.

## Key uncertainties

**Covers:** p. 80

- Incremental value over smartphones and enterprise ROI: devices don't always justify wearing/managing another device.
- Hardware development: weight, heat, battery life, comfort, durability still constrain all-day use despite new form factors.
- Privacy and social acceptability: capture of images, audio, location, biometrics, eye movements, gestures plus contextual info on bystanders raises surveillance, misuse, consent, and social-friction concerns.
- AI reliability and safety: misinterpretation of real-world environments creates safety/legal/operational risks in healthcare, industrial, transportation, field service.
- Ecosystem fragmentation and regulation: competing OSes, dev tools, hardware standards; rules on workplace monitoring, accessibility, content moderation, privacy shape scaling speed.

## Big questions about the future

**Covers:** pp. 80–81

- Which form factor — AI glasses, display glasses, assisted-reality headsets, or high-fidelity VR/MR headsets — will reach meaningful scale first?
- Which workflows deliver measurable ROI, and which remain compelling but hard to scale?
- How to design experiences that augment work without creating distraction, fatigue, or safety risks?
- What role will AI agents play as spatial devices understand context, suggest actions, coordinate across apps?
- How should leaders prepare for digital interfaces moving from screens/phones into persistent, wearable, context-aware systems?
