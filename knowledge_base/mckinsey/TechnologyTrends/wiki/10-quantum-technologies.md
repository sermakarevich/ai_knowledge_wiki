# Quantum technologies
**In one sentence:** Quantum remains at experimentation (Adoption score 2) with hiring shifting toward specialized R&D talent, while governments accelerate funding and post-quantum-cryptography migration even as enterprise use stays limited to proofs of concept.
## Key points
- Adoption score is 2 — Experimentation: organizations test quantum via codevelopment programs and selected field deployments, with few companies scaling quantum computing in core workflows.
- Talent demand has contracted from its earlier peak and concentrated in specialized R&D: quantum scientists and engineers are now the single largest hiring role, while demand fell for software developers, solution architects, project managers, and security analysts.
- By business function in 2025, 59% of quantum postings are R&D, versus 8% sales and marketing, 12% operations, 12% general and administrative, and 9% other.
- Cloud computing (required in 94% of postings) and quantum computing (70%) sit at the center of skill demand; both are keeping pace with demand, alongside well-supplied Python, machine learning, algorithms, C++, and cybersecurity skills.
- The US Department of Commerce announced letters of intent with 9 companies for $2 billion, plus June 2026 Executive Orders 14413 (quantum innovation) and 14412 (cryptographic security); NIST finalized its first 3 post-quantum encryption standards in August 2024.
- China has built an operational quantum communication network spanning more than 12,000 kilometers of fiber across 80 cities and demonstrated a Beijing–Stellenbosch satellite quantum key distribution link of more than 12,900 kilometers.
- Europe amended the EuroHPC Regulation (January 2026), launched a Quantum Europe Strategy to lead by 2030, commits over €400 million via the EU Quantum Technologies Flagship, and France announced €3.3 billion total investment; Euro-Q-Exa was inaugurated in Munich in February 2026.
- Across geographies the most advanced adoption is in government and defense — post-quantum-cryptography migration and quantum sensing for navigation and signals intelligence — while financial services, pharmaceuticals, and logistics remain experimental with hybrid quantum–classical proofs of concept.

## Talent and labor markets — Demand
**Covers:** Technology Trends Outlook 2026, pp. 86–87, "Quantum technologies: Demand"

Quantum technology hiring has contracted from its earlier peak even as investment accelerated. The pullback is concentrated in software developers, solution architects, project managers, and security analysts, suggesting more selective hiring. Demand strengthened for quantum scientists and engineers (now the single largest role), postdoctoral researchers, and research technicians, underscoring continued focus on specialized R&D and operational hardware scaling.

Job postings by title, 2022–25 (thousands, ~0–8 scale): tracked roles are quantum scientist/engineer, scientist, software engineer, software developer, security analyst, solution architect, postdoctoral researcher, research technician, data scientist, and project manager.

Job postings by business function, 2025, % share:

| R&D | Sales and marketing | Operations | General and administrative | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 59 | 8 | 12 | 12 | 9 |

Definitions from source: R&D = roles that create or improve products, technology, or IP; Sales and marketing = roles driving demand, customers, revenue; Operations = roles that produce, deliver, support, or run the core product/service; General and administrative = internal enabling functions; Other = mixed or nonstandard titles.

## Adoption developments across the globe
**Covers:** Technology Trends Outlook 2026, pp. 87–89, "Adoption developments across the globe"

Adoption score: 2 — Experimentation. Organizations are testing functionality and viability through codevelopment programs and selected field deployments. More organizations are investing in use-case development, internal capability building, and quantum-as-a-service offerings.

- United States: strengths in private-sector development, venture investment, and government-backed commercialization; Commerce Department $2 billion letters of intent with 9 companies; Trump administration June 2026 executive orders signal intensifying federal commitment to quantum as strategic infrastructure; post-quantum-cryptography adoption accelerating after NIST finalization.
- China: national strategic priority with strength in quantum communication and sensing; operational fiber network over 12,000 km across 80 cities; world's longest intercontinental satellite-based quantum key distribution link (Beijing to Stellenbosch, South Africa, over 12,900 km); restricted access to Western quantum hardware accelerating domestic qubit platforms and supply chains.
- Europe: strong research foundation moving into commercialization; EuroHPC Regulation amended January 2026; Quantum Europe Strategy goal of EU quantum leadership by 2030; EU Quantum Technologies Flagship commits over €400 million plus Digital Europe Programme funding; France total announced investment €3.3 billion with pledge to be a top-three quantum market; Euro-Q-Exa inaugurated Munich February 2026; Airbus and Boehringer Ingelheim codeveloping quantum solutions with technology providers.
- Australia, Canada, Japan, South Korea: national programs via research institutions and government-backed initiatives, concentrated in hardware development, quantum sensing, and post-quantum security; adoption elsewhere mostly nascent and policy-led rather than commercially driven.
- Sector pattern: most advanced adoption in government and defense (post-quantum-cryptography migration; quantum sensing for navigation and signals intelligence); enterprise (financial services, pharmaceuticals, logistics) experimental — proofs of concept, hybrid quantum–classical solutions, internal capability building, not production deployment.

> "The next milestone for quantum is not simply building larger computers. Progress increasingly depends on integrating hardware, error correction, software, and classical computing into reliable systems that can solve real-world problems."
> — Henning Soller, partner, Frankfurt

Cited milestones in chunk: Toshiba breakthrough bringing quantum communications to existing national-scale telecom infrastructure (April 24, 2025); Infleqtion and Royal Navy demonstration of world's first quantum optical clock on underwater autonomous submarine for GPS-free navigation (October 28, 2025); QuantumDiamonds €152 million next-gen quantum-based chip inspection facility in Munich (December 15, 2025); France €1.55 billion for quantum and semiconductor development (May 2026 reporting).

## Underlying technologies
**Covers:** Technology Trends Outlook 2026, p. 90, "Underlying technologies"

| Technology | Description in source |
|---|---|
| Hybrid quantum–classical computing | Workflows using quantum processors for selected subproblems while classical computers handle most tasks, often through cloud platforms |
| Logical qubits | Imperfect qubits that have been error corrected to enable longer and more accurate calculations |
| Quantum communication infrastructure | Fiber, satellite, photonic, and telecom systems to transmit or protect information in high-assurance networks |
| Quantum control systems | Equipment and electronics to prepare, manipulate, and read out qubits with sufficient precision for reliable operation |
| Quantum-safe security | Encryption and key-distribution methods designed to protect sensitive data from future quantum-enabled attacks |
| Quantum sensing systems | Devices measuring time, motion, gravity, magnetic fields, and other physical signals with very high precision |
| Quantum software, compilers, and orchestration tools | Tools translating problems into quantum instructions and coordinating work across quantum and classical systems |
| Qubit hardware platforms | Platforms holding basic units of information, built via superconducting circuits, trapped ions, neutral atoms, photonics, spin-based systems, and topological designs |

> "With the latest advancements in quantum computing, adopting quantum cryptography has become a strategic business decision today, even as broader quantum use cases continue to mature."
> — Martina Gschwendtner, associate partner, Munich

## Key uncertainties and big questions
**Covers:** Technology Trends Outlook 2026, p. 91, "Key uncertainties / Big questions"

Major uncertainties:
- Fault-tolerant-computing timelines: progress is real but broad commercial utility depends on error correction, control systems, and scalable architectures arriving on commercially relevant timelines.
- Hardware architecture: no modality has solved all core scaling challenges; different architectures may suit different workloads or parts of the stack.
- Ecosystem maturity: quantum software, middleware, cloud access, talent, and enterprise integration remain immature versus classical stacks.
- Commercial scaling: supply chains, specialized components, and sustained customer demand will need to expand before quantum becomes a repeatable business opportunity.

Big questions for companies and leaders:
- How will value accrue across the quantum stack, from hardware and cloud platforms to software, systems integration, or companies that own the use cases?
- How quickly will paid codevelopment turn into scaled deployment, and which industries will be first beyond one-off experiments?
- What are the biggest constraints to commercialization, including talent, supply chains, and durability of post-quantum security approaches?
