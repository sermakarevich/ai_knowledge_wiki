# Technology Trends Outlook 2026 — Overview
**In one sentence:** McKinsey's sixth-edition 2026 outlook argues the technology story has moved off the screen into the physical world, with AI acting as accelerant across 14 trends grouped in three categories while energy, hardware, talent, and cybersecurity constraints decide who can actually deploy it.
## Key points
- The report covers 14 trends in three groups — AI revolution (4 trends), compute and connectivity frontiers (5), cutting-edge engineering (5) — with two new trends this year: agentic software development and AI for scientific discovery and engineering.
- AI underpins and amplifies the other ten trends beyond its four dedicated ones, reshaping software development, robotics, scientific discovery, infrastructure, cybersecurity, and semiconductors.
- Energy technologies alone drew nearly $200 billion in investment in 2025, and spending on AI infrastructure doubled in a single year, as AI scale runs into power and hardware limits.
- US data centers running AI workloads alone are projected to consume as much electricity by 2030 as California does today, with transformer lead times over two years and more than 2,500 gigawatts of energy projects stalled in grid queues worldwide.
- More than three-quarters of all cybersecurity vulnerabilities are currently classified as "zero day," compressing the defense window from days/weeks to near-zero as AI helps both attackers and defenders.
- Inference is overtaking training as the dominant AI workload, driving application-specific chips codesigned by hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft) for more output per watt at lower cost.
- Talent signals split the field: in connectivity, cybersecurity, energy, life sciences, and mobility more than half of listings are non-R&D roles (deployment underway), while in all four AI trends plus application-specific semiconductors over 75 percent of jobs are R&D (still early).
- Five trends are on pace to at least double investment in 2026 vs 2025: agentic software development, AI infrastructure and model architectures, AI for scientific discovery and engineering, future of space technologies, and future of robotics.

## Introduction: off the screen into the physical world
**Covers:** Introduction, pp. 1–2

> "The technology story of 2026 has moved off the screen and into the physical world."

- Innovation is accelerating in power grids and chips underpinning the data-center boom, intelligent robots embodying AI, agentic systems discovering new compounds, and launch pads sending thousands of satellites into orbit.
- "AI needs energy to scale. That's one reason energy technologies alone drew nearly $200 billion in investment in 2025, among the highest capital influx in any technology domain. And spending on AI infrastructure doubled in a single year."
- Defining questions are not only what technology can do but who can build the hardware and assemble the skilled workforce to deploy AI in the real world.
- Four trends are AI-specific; AI also underpins the other ten: autonomous bots interacting with the world, coders made "many magnitudes more productive," life-sciences discovery "many times faster," plus optimized infrastructure, changed cybersecurity operations, and new semiconductor innovation.
- Beyond AI, trends are shaped by economic, geopolitical, and workforce transitions: therapeutics, diagnostics, genomics, brain science; robotics/mobility/immersive-reality convergence; sustainability for surging data-center power demand; quantum glimpse at otherwise infeasible problems.
- Challenges: no proven road maps for deploying AI at scale, workforces needing upskilling, legacy systems, exposed networks, shortages in energy, talent, and capital — requiring "rewiring operational models from the inside out."

## How we chose the trends and research methodology
**Covers:** Sidebars "How we chose the trends" and "Research methodology," pp. 3–4

- Report seeks technology trends shaping next year, decade, century — "key enablers of change," list "meant to be useful, not perfect or complete."
- Selection: quantitative plus qualitative — high innovation plus wide-ranging, significant potential impact (multiple sectors/geographies, economic or societal value, landscape-reshaping); in-depth interviews with leaders, engineers, scientists, practitioners.
- Six tangible measures: search-engine queries (Google Trends), news articles (Factiva), patents (Google Patents, granted), research publications (The Lens), equity investment (PitchBook), talent demand (McKinsey Organizational Data Platform, primarily English-speaking countries).
- Scores: innovation = patents + research; interest = press + search; each indexed 0–1 relative to trends studied. Investment = flow of funds (VC, corporate/strategic M&A incl. joint ventures, private equity incl. buyouts and PIPE, public incl. IPOs; excludes corporate capital and opex).
- Changes for 2026: last year's AI trend is now "AI infrastructure and model architectures"; digital trust/cybersecurity is now "cybersecurity and trustworthy systems"; bioengineering is now "life sciences and bioengineering"; two new trends (agentic software development; AI for scientific discovery and engineering); cloud and edge computing removed as matured.
- Enterprise adoption scored 1–5 via expert interviews: 1 Frontier innovation, 2 Experimentation, 3 Piloting, 4 Scaling in progress, 5 Fully scaled.

## Five cross-cutting themes
**Covers:** "New and notable," pp. 4–5

1. **Machines are being given more autonomy.** AI transformed screen workflows and is entering an agentic era completing end-to-end digital tasks; "Agentic AI is becoming the connective tissue of the enterprise." Physical AI adds perception, reasoning, action across robotics, mobility, wearables — arriving first in manufacturing and logistics, pointing toward hospitals, construction, farms, city infrastructure; "dark factories" with no humans present, driverless real-time vehicle decisions.
2. **AI is generating breakthroughs faster than we can absorb them.** Biopharma AI proposes thousands of drug candidates in the time it took for a handful, but cannot compress wet-lab validation, trials, regulatory review; software AI produces code faster than humans can review, test, deploy securely ("brittle code"); materials compounds arrive faster than labs can synthesize/validate. "AI is the most powerful accelerant in recent history. Its speed is also its biggest obstacle."
3. **The cyber defense window has compressed.** Cat-and-mouse once played over days/weeks; AI eliminated the buffer — "More than three-quarters of all cybersecurity vulnerabilities are currently classified as 'zero day.'" AI helps defenders find/fix faster and attackers find/exploit faster. Example: Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview identified thousands of flaws, so instead of public release Anthropic gave gated access via Project Glasswing to identify, validate, and patch.
4. **Hardware and software are being codesigned for differentiated AI workloads.** General-purpose chips gave way to workload-optimized chips; inference overtaking training as dominant workload; new chips deliver more output per watt at lower cost. Hyperscalers invest heavily: "Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft are increasingly partnering with semiconductor firms to codesign custom silicon" and some explore offering chips externally — "the customer is becoming an alternative supplier."
5. **AI is hungry, and the grid is not ready.** Data centers can be built faster than transmission lines, substations, transformers; transformer lead times over two years in many markets; "More than 2,500 gigawatts of energy projects are stalled in grid queues worldwide." Securing reliable compute is becoming as much competitive advantage as talent or capital.
