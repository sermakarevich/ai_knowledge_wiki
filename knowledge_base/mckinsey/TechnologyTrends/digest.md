# Technology Trends — Digest

## 1. [[wiki/01-overview|Technology Trends Outlook 2026 — Overview]]
**In one sentence:** McKinsey's sixth-edition 2026 outlook argues the technology story has moved off the screen into the physical world, with AI acting as accelerant across 14 trends grouped in three categories while energy, hardware, talent, and cybersecurity constraints decide who can actually deploy it.
## Key points
- The report covers 14 trends in three groups — AI revolution (4 trends), compute and connectivity frontiers (5), cutting-edge engineering (5) — with two new trends this year: agentic software development and AI for scientific discovery and engineering.
- AI underpins and amplifies the other ten trends beyond its four dedicated ones, reshaping software development, robotics, scientific discovery, infrastructure, cybersecurity, and semiconductors.
- Energy technologies alone drew nearly $200 billion in investment in 2025, and spending on AI infrastructure doubled in a single year, as AI scale runs into power and hardware limits.
- US data centers running AI workloads alone are projected to consume as much electricity by 2030 as California does today, with transformer lead times over two years and more than 2,500 gigawatts of energy projects stalled in grid queues worldwide.
- More than three-quarters of all cybersecurity vulnerabilities are currently classified as "zero day," compressing the defense window from days/weeks to near-zero as AI helps both attackers and defenders.
- Inference is overtaking training as the dominant AI workload, driving application-specific chips codesigned by hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft) for more output per watt at lower cost.
- Talent signals split the field: in connectivity, cybersecurity, energy, life sciences, and mobility more than half of listings are non-R&D roles (deployment underway), while in all four AI trends plus application-specific semiconductors over 75 percent of jobs are R&D (still early).
- Five trends are on pace to at least double investment in 2026 vs 2025: agentic software development, AI infrastructure and model architectures, AI for scientific discovery and engineering, future of space technologies, and future of robotics.

## 2. [[wiki/02-agentic-software-development|Agentic software development (Trend 01)]]
**In one sentence:** Agentic software development — LLM (Large Language Model) systems that autonomously plan, write, test, fix, and deploy code — could unlock almost $1 trillion in value, but only about one-quarter of adopters get meaningful acceleration because value requires rewiring the full product development life cycle (PDLC), change management, verification, and small human–agent teams rather than just more code.
## Key points
- Agentic software development means LLM-powered systems autonomously performing planning, coding, testing, bug-fixing, and deployment, shifting engineers from hands-on coders to agent supervisors/orchestrators.
- Potential value is almost $1 trillion when deployed successfully, but only one-quarter of companies using the tools achieved meaningful PDLC acceleration (defined as more than a quarter of their teams achieving twofold or greater productivity gains).
- Gains are highly skewed: 80 percent of engineers using AI tools see only ~3 percent acceleration while the top 20 percent see a 55 percent boost; in 30 percent of companies productivity fell after adopting agentic AI tools.
- More code does not equal more product: in one study AI tools raised coding activity by 180 percent but shipped releases rose by only 30 percent; 46 percent of developers distrust AI accuracy vs 33 percent who trust and only 3 percent who highly trust it.
- Capital and interest are converging: investment in agentic coding-tool companies was negligible through 2023, ~$5 billion in 2025, and crossed $61 billion in H1 2026 (mainly the $60 billion Cursor acquisition); job postings rose +221% with $4.9 billion equity investment in 2025.
- The operating model must shift from two-week sprints and synchronous assistance to asynchronous delegation, after-hours agents, the "agentic factory" across the full software development life cycle (SDLC), and human–agent pods of small human teams supervising agent fleets.
- Bottlenecks move "to the edges" (what to build, whether it works): top performers are 6–7x more likely to embed AI across 4+ life-cycle stages; developers spend only ~one-tenth of the workday writing code; AI agents can accelerate modernization by 40–50 percent and cut costs up to 40 percent, while AI now accounts for a third of companies' spending to introduce new capabilities.

## 3. [[wiki/03-agentic-ai|Agentic AI]]
**In one sentence:** Agentic AI has moved from experimentation to major enterprise investment as systems that plan and execute multistep workflows, but measurable EBIT impact remains elusive while costs, integration, governance, and new token/identity economics decide whether it becomes enterprise connective tissue.
## Key points
- Agentic AI means systems that plan and execute multistep workflows with limited human direction, reasoning, using enterprise systems, coordinating with other agents, and adjusting as context evolves.
- Adoption is broad but value is thin: 89 percent of organizations regularly use AI with majority at least experimenting with agents, yet only 37 percent attribute any positive EBIT impact from AI programs.
- Agents are compute-intensive and costly: a single agentic workflow can consume five to 30 times more tokens than a standard chatbot query via repeated reasoning loops, tool calls, retrieval, and orchestration; 93 percent of respondents report exceeding AI budgets.
- Capability is improving: agent-building tools from Anthropic and OpenAI improved tool use and computer interaction, LangChain's LangGraph adds orchestration and human-in-the-loop controls; METR estimates Claude Opus 4.6 had a 50 percent time horizon of roughly 12 hours on software engineering tasks by May 2026.
- The direction is from point agents to orchestrated end-to-end workflows where multiple agents coordinate across systems, with biggest gains from redesigning workflows around human-agent collaboration plus new governance, security, and infrastructure.
- Scoring signals conviction off a small base: equity investment reached $9.9 billion in 2025 and already $6.7 billion by mid-2026; job postings rose +952% in 2024-25.
- Talent demand is R&D-heavy and orchestration-scarce: hiring leans 78% R&D, led by software engineers, ML engineers, data scientists, solution architects; cloud computing required in roughly four in five postings, while orchestration supply is only 0.2x demand.
- Adoption score is 3 — Piloting: North America leads, China integrates agents into industrial/consumer platforms, Europe is more cautious on regulation/privacy, and digitally mature sectors with cloud-native data infrastructure progress fastest.

## 4. [[wiki/04-ai-for-scientific-discovery|AI for scientific discovery and engineering]]
**In one sentence:** AI — from AlphaFold-style deep learning through LLMs (large language models, AI systems trained on massive text to understand and generate language), agentic workflows, and robotics — is moving from prediction and simulation into closed-loop discovery systems that accelerate drug, materials, and engineering R&D, though economics, validation, and scale-up bottlenecks remain.
## Key points
- Deep learning models solved long-standing challenges in protein structure prediction (e.g., AlphaFold) and established AI as a credible scientific tool; attention now shifts to physical AI, LLMs, and autonomous experimentation.
- Bringing a new drug to market still takes more than ten years and costs $1.3 billion to $2.6 billion with high clinical failure rates, so firms deploy AI across experiment design, molecular optimization, toxicity prediction, candidate generation/evaluation, and clinical-trial design.
- Dozens of AI-discovered or AI-assisted candidates are reportedly in development globally; Anthropic launched Claude Science (beta AI workbench) plus an internal AI drug-discovery program focused on neglected diseases.
- In materials and engineering, AI searches large pools for batteries, semiconductors, and catalysts; physics-informed AI and deep learning surrogates approximate costly fluid-dynamics and structural simulations but still require high-fidelity validation and physical testing.
- Momentum is capital-led: equity investment rose from nearly $8 billion ($7.9B) in 2025 to $12.5 billion through H1 2026; talent demand +18% (2024–25); headline rounds include Isomorphic Labs $2.1B Series B, Earendil Labs $787M, Fathom $47M Series A, PhysicsX $300M at $2.4B valuation, Periodic Labs $300M seed (2025).
- R&D organizations use LLMs as orchestration/coordination layers over simulations and tools (MCP-SIM memory-coordinated physics-aware simulation example), and shift toward closed-loop labs where AI proposes hypotheses and robots run experiments (Berkeley A-Lab; Ginkgo + GPT-5: 6 cycles, 40% cost reduction in cell-free protein synthesis).
- Scientific data is the strategic bottleneck: biobanks (e.g., 490,640 UK Biobank whole genomes), consortium pooling, federated learning (e.g., Lilly TuneLab, built on $1B+ research investment), and proprietary data generation (e.g., Recursion) expand training data.

## 5. [[wiki/05-ai-infrastructure-and-models|AI Infrastructure and Model Architectures]]
**In one sentence:** AI has shifted from experimentation to expensive industrial-scale deployment where the binding constraints are physical — power, chips, memory, supply chains, and inference cost — while efficiency-focused model architectures (reasoning, multimodal, world models, SLMs, diffusion, neurosymbolic) and custom hardware decide who can scale economically.
## Key points
- Capital expenditure to build data centers could reach nearly $7 trillion worldwide by 2030, and data centers are expected to account for 14 percent of total US power demand by 2030, up from just 3 percent in 2022.
- Data centers can often be built faster than the energy systems needed to power them, so power availability and time-to-power are structural constraints holding back AI scaling, with AI-related demand already raising electricity pricing and grid stress in some regions.
- Bottlenecks include shortages of processors, high-bandwidth memory, transformers, skilled labor, construction materials, water, electrical equipment, semiconductor packaging, and lithography (ASML as the only supplier of EUV machines for 3-nm scale and below).
- The economics are shifting from training to inference: cost per token and performance per watt/per dollar are central, addressed via custom accelerators, higher-density racks, memory hierarchy, power delivery architectures, compression, and inference orchestration.
- Small language models (e.g., Microsoft Phi-4-mini at 3.8 billion parameters vs 175 billion for the GPT-3 version behind early ChatGPT), model compression, and optimized inference frameworks enable local/edge/enterprise deployment where latency, privacy, regulation, or cost make frontier-cloud access less attractive.
- New model classes expand AI beyond content generation toward systems that perceive, reason, simulate, plan, and act: reasoning architectures allocating extra compute to hard problems, multimodal models, world models, diffusion models for simulation/design/science, autonomous agents, and vision-language-action (VLA) models for robotics.
- Geopolitics is direct leverage: China halted gallium, dysprosium, terbium, and yttrium shipments to Japan for months in 2026; the US weighed restrictions on Chinese open-weight models; TSMC escalated planned US investment to $265 billion (from $65B, then $165B in March 2025) targeting 2- and 3-nm chips in Arizona.
- Adoption gap persists: 89 percent of organizations regularly use AI in at least one business function but only 37 percent attribute any positive EBIT impact at enterprise level; talent demand nearly tripled since 2022 (R&D-heavy, 77% of 2025 postings) with the sharpest shortage in CI/CD (continuous integration and continuous delivery).

## 6. [[wiki/06-application-specific-semiconductors|Application-specific semiconductors]]
**In one sentence:** AI compute is shifting from general-purpose GPU training clusters to heterogeneous, workload-specific inference systems built on custom ASICs, chiplets, silicon photonics, and advanced packaging, driven by hyperscaler codesign and geopolitical supply-chain pressure.
## Key points
- Application-specific semiconductors are purpose-built chips (notably ASICs and specialized accelerators) optimized for distinct AI workloads, increasingly codesigned with software, memory, networking, and data-center operating models.
- The shift is driven by inference: as enterprises embed AI in core workflows and hyperscalers commercialize AI services, diverse latency/throughput/efficiency needs make power efficiency and workload-specific optimization central; McKinsey estimates inference will be 30–40 percent of all data-center demand by 2030 and the majority of AI compute.
- Investment surged to $41.6 billion in 2025 versus $7.5 billion in 2024, with news mentions, searches, and equity investment all rising 2022–2025; patent filings were the strongest innovation signal, while research publications did not accelerate (much innovation sits in private firms).
- Hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft) codesign custom silicon with chip firms: Anthropic plans to use up to one million Google Cloud TPUs for Claude; AWS deploys Trainium (training) and Inferentia (inference), with Inf1-based EC2 Inf1 instances delivering up to 2.3× higher throughput and up to 70 percent lower cost per inference than comparable EC2 instances.
- System performance now depends on moving and packaging data, not just compute: high-bandwidth memory (HBM, e.g. SK hynix 12-layer HBM4E), TSMC chip-on-wafer-on-substrate (CoWoS) packaging, chiplets, silicon photonics, and high-speed interconnects (e.g. Astera Labs, Lightmatter, Lumentum) are strategic differentiators.
- Adoption score is 4 — Scaling in progress: North America anchors development (hyperscalers, chip designers, start-ups); Taiwan leads advanced-node manufacturing and advanced packaging while diversification efforts (including into India) accelerate; China invests in domestic packaging, memory, manufacturing, and reported DUV lithography progress; Europe leans on tools, photonics research, advanced manufacturing, and new packaging.
- Talent demand is R&D-heavy (82% of 2025 postings) and recovering from the post-2022 contraction, shifting from traditional hardware/design/optical roles toward research engineers and solution architects; sharpest shortages are in GPU and optimization skills (0.1× availability), while C++ (9.2×) and machine learning (4.3×) are well supplied.
- Key constraints are AI workload stabilization (how many chip architectures the market can sustain), fab-investment versus obsolescence economics, HBM/packaging/networking bottlenecks, power/cooling limits, and supply-chain resilience under export controls and manufacturing concentration.

## 7. [[wiki/07-cybersecurity-and-trustworthy-systems|Cybersecurity and trustworthy systems]]
**In one sentence:** AI is compressing the vulnerability-to-exploitation window and enabling autonomous chained attacks at scale, forcing enterprises to shift from perimeter defense to zero-trust continuous runtime inspection, quantum-safe cryptography, and blockchain-based trust layers.
## Key points
- More than three-quarters of all cybersecurity vulnerabilities are classified as "zero day," meaning an exploit already exists by the time of public disclosure.
- Advanced AI agents can autonomously discover and exploit vulnerabilities and chain dozens of separate exploitation steps into one attack path — demonstrated when an agent escalated a single low-severity flaw into complete unauthorized file access in a sanctioned test of 1,060 autonomous attacks.
- Historically weeks-or-months threat cycles have shrunk dramatically: exploitation of public-facing applications surged 44 percent in a single year, fueling a 49 percent increase in active ransomware and extortion groups (IBM 2026 X-Force Threat Intelligence Index).
- Defense is shifting to continuous control-point architectures: continuous runtime inspection, granular data classification, automated containment, and zero-trust (never trust a user, device, or system even inside the network), including custom-integrated security for machine-to-machine and agent identities.
- Machine identities (bots/agents) now outnumber human users by roughly 100 to one, making identity a central security pillar with unified governance, password-free authentication, and context-aware access.
- Investment was $77.5 billion in equity in 2025 and roughly $63.0 billion already by mid-2026 (below 2022 level); searches rose meaningfully, news mentions stayed low, research/patent activity rose modestly — a mature field in sustained deployment.
- "Harvest now, decrypt later" plus NIST finalized post-quantum cryptography standards moved quantum-safe migration from speculative research to active enterprise-planning priority ahead of Q-Day; adoption remains early-stage.
- Consolidation is accelerating around AI-powered platforms bundling identity, cloud, and automated detection: Google completed Wiz acquisition; CrowdStrike→SGNL, Cisco→Astrix Security, Palo Alto Networks→Portkey, Proofpoint→Acuvity, Cyera→Oasis Security ($1 billion), Keyfactor→InfoSec Global.

## 8. [[wiki/08-advanced-connectivity|Advanced connectivity]]
**In one sentence:** Advanced connectivity is a mature but strategically selective frontier where AI workloads are driving demand for AI-ready fiber, software-defined and autonomous networks, 5G stand-alone with slicing, and complementary satellite coverage, with value going to providers who sell reliability, resilience, and sovereignty rather than basic data transport.
## Key points
- Connectivity is no longer just moving data from point A to point B; it is an infrastructure stack supporting real-time intelligence, distributed computing, and demanding enterprise workloads as AI spreads to factories, stores, hospitals, vehicles, and towers.
- Advanced connectivity led all technology trends in patent filings (5G-Advanced, optical transport, software-defined networking); equity investment was $60.5 billion in 2025, below the 2022 peak of $73.8 billion, anchored by HPE's $13.6 billion acquisition of Juniper Networks.
- AI is reshaping network requirements toward lower latency, stronger reliability, local connectivity, and higher uplink capacity: gen AI traffic was 26 percent of uplink traffic versus roughly 10 percent in typical mobile networks (Ericsson, June 2025 mobility report).
- Telcos are responding with AI-native automation (AIOps) plus software-defined capabilities such as network slicing and cloud-based functions, while Cisco's Unified Edge moves compute, networking, storage, and security closer to sites for real-time inference and agentic workloads.
- 5G stand-alone is moving into broader operation (AT&T nationwide deployment announced October 2025), and slicing is becoming a commercial product: Verizon Business launched SLA-backed 5G slice fixed-wireless, Vodafone Business launched a commercial slicing service in the UK.
- Security, resilience, and sovereignty are now core design requirements: submarine cables carry 99 percent of intercontinental internet traffic (EU 2025 Action Plan on Cable Security), driving redundancy, route diversity, and multi-orbit plus direct-to-device complements.
- Nonterrestrial connectivity is integrating into the telecom stack but remains a complement, not a substitute: Starlink had almost 10,000 active satellites as of August 2026, with 225 publicly announced operator–satellite partnerships across 88 countries as of January 2026; executives expect LEO to capture close to 20 percent of the addressable market for existing fiber in hard-to-reach areas.
- Hiring has shifted from pure build-out to serving customers: job postings remain below 2022 levels with a modest 2025 uptick, network engineers still anchor hiring, momentum shifting to software engineers and sales/support roles; cloud computing is required in 73 percent of postings, while CI/CD talent is scarcest (0.1× availability-to-demand); adoption score is 4, scaling in progress.

## 9. [[wiki/09-immersive-reality-technologies|Immersive-reality technologies (Trend 08)]]
**In one sentence:** Immersive reality is shifting from heavy VR/MR immersion to lightweight AI smart glasses that augment everyday tasks with on-device context-aware AI, while enterprise use deepens in manufacturing, healthcare, and field operations but hiring narrows toward lean R&D and adoption stays at experimentation (score 2).
## Key points
- The category is shifting from immersion to augmentation: lightweight smart glasses that keep users anchored in the physical world (visual prompts, translation, navigation, remote support) are the center of gravity, e.g. Meta's Ray-Ban Display with cameras, microphones, speakers, full-color in-lens display, compute, AI, and gesture input.
- Smart glasses are the clearest growth pocket: global shipments surged 167 percent year over year in Q1 2026 to ~2.25 million units; EssilorLuxottica reported Ray-Ban Meta AI glasses sales grew more than 200 percent YoY in H1 2025, and Meta sold more than seven million AI-powered smart glasses in 2025; devices over 400 grams cause fatigue, while the new lighter generation crosses the all-day wearability threshold.
- AI turns the device into a context engine: on-device/near-device vision, audio, spatial mapping, eye/hand/gesture tracking let models understand what the user sees, hears, and does (e.g. Meta's late-2025 update: Conversation Focus in noise, music recommendations from gaze, visual AI in lens); Google announced Gemini-powered glasses at I/O 2026 (audio first, display later); Apple reportedly shelved a Vision Pro revamp to prioritize AI smart glasses.
- Real-time AI inference is moving onto the device, cutting latency for gesture/eye tracking, object recognition, and translation and enabling autonomous use without constant cloud (e.g. Brilliant Labs' Halo processes voice, vision, and sensor data locally).
- Platform competition moved beyond headset hardware into software/OS stacks: Samsung Galaxy XR (Oct 2025, first Android XR device, co-developed with Google and Qualcomm) represents an open multi-OEM approach versus Meta's vertically integrated stack (Ray-Ban Display + neural wristband); enterprise adoption concentrates in manufacturing, healthcare, field service, training, logistics, design, and safety-critical operations (RealWear Arc 3 179-gram assisted-reality headset + Ari OS Oct 2025; Aena VR+haptic firefighter training; Medivis FDA-cleared AR cranial navigation).
- Hiring is well below the metaverse-era surge and still narrowing (total postings down again in 2025), with declines in software developers, project managers, and network engineers but momentum tilting toward machine-learning and test engineers; R&D is 51% of 2025 postings; VR (49%) and ML (36%) anchor skill demand while 3D skills are scarcest (0.8x availability ratio); adoption score is 2—Experimentation with steady-not-explosive scale expected only where devices beat phones/laptops at a specific job.

## 10. [[wiki/10-quantum-technologies|Quantum technologies]]
**In one sentence:** Quantum remains at experimentation (Adoption score 2) with hiring shifting toward specialized R&D talent, while governments accelerate funding and post-quantum-cryptography migration even as enterprise use stays limited to proofs of concept.
## Key points
- Adoption score is 2 — Experimentation: organizations test quantum via codevelopment programs and selected field deployments, with few companies scaling quantum computing in core workflows.
- Talent demand has contracted from its earlier peak and concentrated in specialized R&D: quantum scientists and engineers are now the single largest hiring role, while demand fell for software developers, solution architects, project managers, and security analysts.
- By business function in 2025, 59% of quantum postings are R&D, versus 8% sales and marketing, 12% operations, 12% general and administrative, and 9% other.
- Cloud computing (required in 94% of postings) and quantum computing (70%) sit at the center of skill demand; both are keeping pace with demand, alongside well-supplied Python, machine learning, algorithms, C++, and cybersecurity skills.
- The US Department of Commerce announced letters of intent with 9 companies for $2 billion, plus June 2026 Executive Orders 14413 (quantum innovation) and 14412 (cryptographic security); NIST finalized its first 3 post-quantum encryption standards in August 2024.
- China has built an operational quantum communication network spanning more than 12,000 kilometers of fiber across 80 cities and demonstrated a Beijing–Stellenbosch satellite quantum key distribution link of more than 12,900 kilometers.
- Europe amended the EuroHPC Regulation (January 2026), launched a Quantum Europe Strategy to lead by 2030, commits over €400 million via the EU Quantum Technologies Flagship, and France announced €3.3 billion total investment; Euro-Q-Exa was inaugurated in Munich in February 2026.
- Across geographies the most advanced adoption is in government and defense — post-quantum-cryptography migration and quantum sensing for navigation and signals intelligence — while financial services, pharmaceuticals, and logistics remain experimental with hybrid quantum–classical proofs of concept.

## 11. [[wiki/11-future-of-robotics|Future of Robotics (and start of Future of Mobility)]]
**In one sentence:** Robots are moving from scripted demos to RaaS-funded, AI-driven dark-factory deployment, but fine manipulation, real-world reliability, and optimization talent remain the bottlenecks, while mobility is entering selective scaling led by EVs (21M sales in 2025) and repeatable L4 robotaxi service.
## Key points
- Fine manipulation is the bottleneck: the NIST Task Board benchmark notes in-hand manipulation and grasping are among the most difficult capabilities to achieve and measure, limiting moves from demos to dependable production use.
- Robotics-as-a-service (RaaS) shifts humanoids priced at $150,000–$500,000 per unit from capital outlay to per-robot/per-hour/per-task operational expenditure, with Locus Robotics surpassing five billion warehouse picks as a scalability proof point and a feedback loop aligning deployer and operator on uptime.
- Dark factories are accelerating via AI, robotics, and digital twins: Haier, Midea, and Xiaomi expanding in China, Hyundai, LG, and Samsung integrating AI-driven lines in South Korea, though most value will come from brownfield integration, not greenfield showcases.
- Robotics hiring declined from earlier highs with a 2025 uptick tilted to machine learning (largest role), automation, and research; 63% of 2025 postings are R&D, and optimization is scarcest at 0.1× talent-to-demand versus 9.2× for PyTorch and 4.6× for deep learning.
- Adoption score is 2 — Experimentation: China held ~2 million industrial robots in 2024 and 54% of annual installations worldwide and centers robotics in its 15th Five-Year Plan, while manufacturing and logistics lead and construction remains hardest due to dynamic, safety-critical sites.
- Future of mobility is selective scaling: global electric-car sales grew >20% in 2025 to 21 million units (one in four cars sold), China captured >half of sales, Europe grew >30%, the US declined 2% after federal tax credits ended in September, and electric heavy-freight truck sales tripled to >200,000 units.
- Autonomy is narrowing to repeatable domains: Waymo reported >15 million trips in 2025 and >20 million lifetime trips, Baidu Apollo Go reported 3.4 million fully driverless rides in Q4 2025 and >20 million cumulative rides by February 2026, and UNECE adopted the first global automated-driving framework in June 2026; 49% of experts expect mass-market private vehicles to center on L2 through 2035.
- The EV bottleneck is shifting to energy access: >7 million public charging points by end-2025, transformer/switchgear lead times >2 years in some markets, Chinese LFP packs ~€64/kWh and NMC ~€82/kWh with up to 40% lower battery costs than global peers; mobility equity investment was $63.5 billion in 2025 (−11%).

## 12. [[wiki/12-future-of-mobility|Future of Mobility]]
**In one sentence:** Autonomous mobility is moving from pilots to constrained commercial scale — corridor trucking, retail drone routes, sidewalk robots, shared micromobility and electric boats — while value shifts to software, jobs shift from build-out to operations, and scale now depends on charging, regulation, and public trust rather than proving the tech works.
## Key points
- Autonomous trucking is scaling first in fixed corridors and industrial sites: Aurora began commercial driverless trucking on public roads between Dallas and Houston, while Kodiak and Atlas Energy Solutions expanded driverless operations in the Permian Basin.
- Automotive value is shifting to software: centralized computing, connected architectures and over-the-air updates, with McKinsey estimating the global automotive software and electronics market grows at 5.9% CAGR to $548 billion by 2035 versus ~1% for the overall vehicle market.
- Drone delivery is moving from demos to structured retail operations pending a US FAA proposed BVLOS (beyond-visual-line-of-sight) rule; Walmart passed 1 million drone deliveries across 66 stores in four states but is constrained to 2.8 pounds, versus Amazon Prime Air targeting 5 pounds or less and Keeta Drone in China focusing within 3 km across 70 set routes.
- Ground delivery robots are gaining in short-distance food, grocery and campus use: Serve Robotics built 2,000+ robots by end-2025, Starship completed 9M+ deliveries with 2,700+ robots across 270+ locations and expects 12,000 robots by end-2027, and Neolix passed 10,000 cumulative L4 vehicle deliveries in 300+ cities in China.
- Electric air taxis (eVTOL) are progressing toward certification but unscaled: Joby completed 850+ flights in 2025 toward FAA certification flight testing, yet still face air-traffic integration, cost, noise and public acceptance.
- Shared micromobility is consolidating into profitable urban mix: Lime surpassed 1 billion lifetime rides in 2025, Seattle bike/scooter share hit 10 million rides in 2025 (+60% YoY), with commuting now a key trip purpose.
- Mobility hiring has pivoted from build to run: postings remain well below the 2022 peak and fell 2024–2025, steepest in electrical/software/systems/test engineering, while technicians (largest role) and operations managers held up; 37% of 2025 postings are operations, and AI (0.3×) and optimization (0.1×) talent supply falls short of demand.
- Adoption is regionally split (score 3—Piloting): China EVs >50% of 2025 car sales with 4.7M public chargers (>65% global, >75% of global growth); EU e-car sales +30% with ~1.3M chargers; US e-car sales −2% with ~235,000 chargers; India 2.3M EV sales and e-car sales +75%, SE Asia e-car sales more than doubled.

## 13. [[wiki/13-future-of-space-technologies|Future of space technologies]]
**In one sentence:** Satellite connectivity is scaling (LEO broadband mainstream, D2D nascent and risky), Earth observation is shifting from imagery to workflow-integrated decisions, defense demand anchors the space economy, orbital compute is only early experimentation, and talent is consolidating with shortages in networking and CI/CD while adoption stays at experimentation stage.
## Key points
- LEO broadband is moving from niche to mainstream for homes, businesses, maritime, aviation and remote sites via Starlink (thousands of satellites) and OneWeb, while Amazon Leo (ex-Project Kuiper) began full-scale deployment in 2025 and has launched 300+ satellites (375+ by July 2, 2026 update).
- Direct-to-device (D2D) connecting satellites directly to standard smartphones is nascent: AST SpaceMobile's BlueBird points from emergency messaging toward broader mobile use, but loss of BlueBird 7 after a New Glenn anomaly underscores deployment risk.
- Earth observation value is increasingly tied to integration into customer workflows via hyperspectral sensing, AI-enabled analytics, cloud platforms and data fusion for emissions, climate resilience, agriculture, insurance, energy and disaster response; e.g. GHGSat methane data via NASA CSDA and Carbon Mapper Tanager next-gen methane technology.
- Defense remains a major backbone: US Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture Tranche 1 launch shows commercial-style data-transport satellites giving integrated sensing and communications for national security.
- Orbital compute is nascent: Axiom Space + Spacebilt orbital data-center node on ISS and Starcloud (NVIDIA Inception, NVIDIA accelerated computing) are early proof points, but no evidence they compete with terrestrial cloud; near-term use is inference onboard spacecraft (CEO Philip Johnston).
- Talent market shifted from rapid expansion to consolidation: job postings well below 2022 peak and still falling across systems, test, technicians, design and software; R&D is 58% of 2025 postings; sharpest shortages are networking (0.4x availability) and CI/CD (0.1x), while C++ (1.0x cloud? actually C++ abundant) and traditional engineering are abundant.
- Adoption score 2 — Experimentation: US leads all four ecosystem layers, China advances state-backed launch/EO/stations/lunar, Europe focuses on resilience/sovereignty (Copernicus, Galileo, IRIS²) but struggles to scale private investment and launch access; India, Japan, Australia, Canada, NZ, South Korea, Middle East and Singapore expand in EO, satcom and defense uses.

## 14. [[wiki/14-future-of-life-sciences-and-bioengineering|Future of life sciences and bioengineering (Trend 13)]]
**In one sentence:** Life sciences and bioengineering are shifting from pure discovery to scale-up and market access, with precision diagnostics entering clinical workflows, outcome-led food/agriculture gaining traction, and biomanufacturing capacity becoming the strategic bottleneck — while talent supply is adequate and adoption (score 4, scaling in progress) varies widely by subfield and region.
## Key points
- Precision diagnostics are moving into workflows: liquid biopsy, in vitro companion diagnostics, and circulating-tumor DNA (ctDNA) testing are being embedded into clinical pathways, especially oncology, with NHS England's ctDNA expansion as proof that gains come from rapid molecular diagnosis, targeted treatment selection, and fewer repeat invasive procedures — though liquid-biopsy evidence remains mixed across indications.
- Companion diagnostics are already regulatory/clinical infrastructure for oncology drugs: the FDA says they provide information "essential for the safe and effective use of a corresponding therapeutic product," with oncology guidance supporting codevelopment and aligned labeling — the open question is reimbursement, interpretation, and tying tests to clinical decisions.
- Human-relevant preclinical models are advancing: CN Bio and Pharmaron announced a long-term strategic partnership (April 24, 2025) to validate CN Bio's PhysioMimix organ-on-chip technology and integrate it into Pharmaron's global R&D platform, alongside an FDA roadmap to reducing animal testing (April 2025).
- Food and agriculture are becoming outcome-led: gene-edited crops, biological inputs, and precision-fermented ingredients gain traction on measurable value (yield, nutrition, shelf life); the European Parliament passed a law (June 17, 2026) expediting approval of many gene-edited crops, while cultivated meat advances technically with case-by-case pathways (e.g., Singapore, US) but broad adoption stays longer-term on cost, scale, and consumer acceptance.
- Biomanufacturing capacity is the strategic control point: scaling cell culture, raw materials/fermentation capacity, quality/regulatory evidence, and treatment-center readiness constrain growth, pushing the industry toward pilot plants and shared infrastructure — e.g., BioMADE (US Department of Defense-backed) agreed to acquire/transition Lygos's pilot-scale facility into a nationally available multiuser site (April 30, 2025).
- Talent is shifting from discovery to scale-up: postings remain well below the 2022 peak and declined 2024→2025, but physicians, postdoctoral researchers, and account executives grew in 2025; operations (22%) + general/administrative (16%) exceed a third of 2025 postings while R&D sits below the cross-sector baseline; clinical-trials expertise (19% of postings) is most demanded with supply roughly in line (1.2×), data analysis/machine learning/Python supply exceeds demand, and only analytics shows a small shortfall (0.7×).
- Adoption score 4 — scaling in progress, regionally differentiated: US (mature clinical bioengineering, AI-discovery, venture capital, flexible pathways, but lab-to-viable-production gap), Europe (research + manufacturing + chemical base), China (manufacturing scale shaping global costs), India (agriculture + biomanufacturing hubs, gene-edited-crop regulatory changes), Singapore (novel-foods test bed), with emerging-market progress shaped by trade, food security, and regulation.

## 15. [[wiki/15-future-of-energy-and-sustainability|Future of Energy and Sustainability Technologies]]
**In one sentence:** Energy and sustainability tech is scaling past pilots toward deployment (adoption score 4) but in fragmented regional pathways, disciplined by costs, grid constraints, and policy rather than one uniform transition.
## Key points
- Hydrogen, ammonia, methanol and synthetic fuels remain relevant for hard-to-abate sectors (steel, chemicals, aviation, shipping) but face elevated costs, infrastructure gaps and uneven green premiums, progressing only where policy, low-cost power, infrastructure and offtake align.
- SWISS integrating Synhelion solar fuel from the DAWN facility into flight operations is a meaningful solar-fuels commercialization signal, but only a small-volume proof point, not scaled availability.
- Measurement, reporting and verification (MRV) is shifting from periodic manual audits to automated sensor- and satellite-enabled systems; Carbon Mapper's Tanager-1 published 5,300+ methane and 1,200+ carbon dioxide plume observations including facility-level detections.
- The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) places a carbon price on selected carbon-intensive imports and enters its definitive regime in 2026 after a transitional phase, shifting investment, supplier and competitiveness decisions.
- Energy job postings peaked in 2023 and fell sharply through 2024–2025, with most core build-out roles (technicians, electrical engineers, project managers, energy engineers) down roughly 40% or more since 2022 and sustainability managers down even further.
- Skills supply is uneven: renewables run above demand (3.6x availability-to-demand) and sustainability keeps pace (1.2x), while carbon (0.2x) and transmission/grid (0.2x) show the sharpest shortages.
- Adoption is regionally uneven and strongest where tech meets immediate system needs (reliable power for data centers, lower costs, grid flexibility); slower where it needs new infrastructure, uncertain policy, or large green premiums.

## The argument in five moves
1. The technology story has moved off the screen into the physical world: AI is no longer a screen workflow but an accelerant across 14 trends spanning software, compute, robots, mobility, space, life sciences, and energy.
2. Agentic systems promise large value — almost $1 trillion in software development, orchestrated enterprise workflows, closed-loop scientific discovery — yet measurable enterprise returns remain thin because gains require rewiring operating models, verification, and human–agent collaboration, not just more code or more agents.
3. Scaling AI has hit physical limits: industrial-scale deployment is constrained by power and time-to-power, inference cost per token, custom silicon and packaging, memory, supply chains, and geopolitics, shifting advantage to those who codesign hardware, software, and energy together.
4. Faster capability compresses risk windows everywhere: zero-day vulnerabilities dominate, autonomous chained attacks rise, machine identities outnumber humans 100-to-1, and quantum harvest-now threats loom — forcing zero-trust runtime security, quantum-safe migration, resilient sovereign connectivity, and continuous validation.
5. Deployment is therefore selective and uneven: connectivity, cybersecurity, mobility, life sciences, and energy scale where they solve a priced operational problem with infrastructure and offtake in place, while robotics, quantum, immersive reality, space, and frontier AI stay in experimentation — and the winners are decided by who secures energy, hardware, talent, and trust to operate in the real world.
