# Technology Trends

**Paper:** [Technology Trends Outlook 2026 (Michael Chui, Roger Roberts, Tanguy Catlin, 2026)](/Users/sergii/Downloads/mckinsey technology trends outlook 2026.pdf) — McKinsey & Company, September 2026

## Human Readable TL;DR

Think of 2026 as the year technology climbed off the computer screen and into the physical world, like a play moving from rehearsal into a real theater with real props. Artificial intelligence is the new electricity running through everything, from robots and drug labs to power grids and satellites, but it is so hungry for energy, chips, and skilled people that the plugs and the wiring now matter as much as the clever ideas. The report is a field guide to 14 such currents, showing which ones are ready to use today and which are still experiments in the lab.

## TL;DR

McKinsey's sixth-edition Technology Trends Outlook 2026 surveys 14 trends in three groups -- the AI revolution (agentic software development, agentic AI, AI for scientific discovery and engineering, AI infrastructure and model architectures), compute and connectivity frontiers (application-specific semiconductors, cybersecurity and trustworthy systems, advanced connectivity, immersive-reality technologies, quantum technologies), and cutting-edge engineering (robotics, mobility, space, life sciences and bioengineering, energy and sustainability) -- scored on six vectors (news, searches, research, patents, equity investment, talent demand) plus a 1-5 enterprise adoption scale. Its central argument is that AI has become an accelerant across all other trends while physical constraints decide who captures value: power availability and time-to-power for data centers, workload-specific silicon as inference overtakes training, a compressed zero-day cyber defense window, and R&D-heavy talent markets in AI versus deployment-weighted hiring elsewhere. Investment momentum is strongest in agentic software development, AI infrastructure, AI for science, space, and robotics, each on pace to at least double 2025 levels in 2026.

## Problem & Motivation

There is no shortage of technology hype, but leaders lack a grounded map of which advances are actually investable, deployable, and economically consequential over the next one to ten years. McKinsey's motivation is to give decision-makers that map: a repeatable, data-driven outlook that combines quantitative signals (search interest, news, patents, publications, equity investment, job postings) with practitioner interviews, so capital, talent, and operating-model choices rest on evidence rather than demos.

The 2026 edition is motivated by a regime change. The defining questions are no longer only what technology can do but who can build the hardware, secure the power, and assemble the workforce to deploy it in the real world. AI generates code, drug candidates, and materials faster than organizations can review, validate, manufacture, or regulate them; data centers can be built faster than the transmission lines and transformers that feed them; and autonomous agents compress cyberattack cycles from weeks to near-zero. The report therefore reframes technology strategy as an infrastructure, energy, talent, and trust agenda, not just a software agenda.

## Main Original Ideas

1. **Agentic software development as an operating-model rewiring** -- Large language model systems that autonomously plan, write, test, fix, and deploy code shift engineers into agent supervisors, but value comes only from rewiring the full product development life cycle with verification, knowledge graphs, and small human-agent pods rather than generating more code.

2. **Agentic AI as enterprise connective tissue** -- Systems that plan and execute multistep workflows across fragmented enterprise software form an orchestration layer (an agentic mesh) spanning ERP, CRM, and supply chain, with multiagent frameworks, Model Context Protocol integration, and new token and nonhuman-identity economics determining whether pilots become scaled operating models.

3. **AI for scientific discovery as closed-loop labs** -- The field moves from isolated prediction models (protein-structure pioneers like AlphaFold, generative-chemistry models like Boltz-2 and Evo 2) to orchestrated systems where language models coordinate simulations and robots run experiments, tightening the loop between hypothesis generation and physical validation in drugs, batteries, catalysts, and semiconductors.

4. **AI infrastructure as a physical-stack agenda** -- With capital expenditure for data centers projected near $7 trillion by 2030 and US data centers heading toward 14 percent of national power demand, scale is gated by chips, high-bandwidth memory, power delivery, cooling, and supply chains, while efficiency architectures (small language models, reasoning and multimodal models, world models, diffusion, neurosymbolic combinations) and orchestration decide cost per token.

5. **Inference-optimized application-specific silicon** -- As inference overtakes training as the dominant workload, heterogeneous data centers pair general processors with custom accelerators, chiplets, advanced packaging, and silicon photonics, codesigned by hyperscalers with chip firms so that performance per watt per dollar, not raw training throughput, becomes the competitive frontier.

6. **Zero-trust continuous runtime security** -- With more than three-quarters of vulnerabilities classified zero-day and agents able to chain exploits autonomously, defense shifts from perimeter prevention to continuous runtime inspection, granular data classification, automated containment, unified human-plus-machine identity governance, and quantum-safe cryptography migration ahead of Q-Day.

7. **Connectivity as AI-ready compute infrastructure** -- Fiber, 5G stand-alone with network slicing, software-defined automation, and complementary low-Earth-orbit satellite and direct-to-device coverage converge into a stack sold on reliability, resilience, and sovereignty, with AI workloads raising uplink, latency, and data-center-interconnect demands.

8. **Immersive reality pivoting from immersion to augmentation** -- Lightweight AI smart glasses that keep users anchored in the physical world displace heavy headsets as the growth center, acting as on-device context engines (vision, audio, spatial mapping, gesture) while enterprise headsets persist for training, surgery, and field service.

9. **Quantum as selective experimentation with near-term security relevance** -- Computing stays pre-advantage and experimental, sensing nears field deployment, and communication migrates toward post-quantum standards, so the actionable agenda is cryptographic inventory and migration plus codevelopment programs rather than production deployment.

10. **Physical AI in robotics and mobility reaching repeatable domains** -- Robots move from scripted demos toward dark factories, warehouse picking at billion-scale, and robotics-as-a-service economics, while mobility scales selectively: electric vehicles at 21 million sales in 2025, corridor-constrained driverless trucking, structured drone and sidewalk-robot delivery, and robotaxis as repeatable city services gated by charging, regulation, and trust.

11. **Space, life sciences, and energy as deployment and scale-up stories** -- Low-Earth-orbit broadband goes mainstream and Earth observation shifts from imagery to workflow-integrated decisions; precision diagnostics, gene-edited crops, and precision fermentation move into clinical and market pathways with biomanufacturing capacity as the bottleneck; and energy adoption scales unevenly by region, disciplined by grid queues, equipment lead times, and carbon pricing rather than a single global transition.

12. **A uniform measurement methodology** -- Every trend is scored on the same six vectors with enterprise adoption rated 1 (frontier innovation) to 5 (fully scaled), making cross-trend comparison of innovation, interest, investment, and talent possible within one framework.

## Key Findings

- Five trends are on pace to at least double 2025 equity investment in 2026: agentic software development (about 12.8x on a run-rate basis, dominated by the announced $60 billion Cursor acquisition), AI infrastructure and model architectures (about 3.2x, $145 billion in 2025 and nearly $384 billion through mid-2026), AI for scientific discovery (about 1.9x, $7.9 billion in 2025 to $12.5 billion in H1 2026), space (about 5.3x), and robotics (about 2.1x).
- Agentic coding delivers skewed returns: only about one-quarter of adopters achieve meaningful acceleration, the top 20 percent of engineers gain roughly 55 percent while the broad 80 percent gain about 3 percent, and in one study coding activity rose 180 percent but shipped releases only 30 percent.
- Agents are expensive to run: a single agentic workflow can consume five to 30 times the tokens of a chatbot query, 93 percent of surveyed organizations report exceeding AI budgets, and only 37 percent attribute any positive EBIT impact despite 89 percent regularly using AI.
- The grid is the binding constraint: US AI data centers alone could consume California-scale electricity by 2030, transformer lead times exceed two years in many markets, and more than 2,500 gigawatts of energy projects sit stalled in interconnection queues worldwide.
- Cyber timelines have collapsed: exploitation of public-facing applications surged 44 percent in one year with a 49 percent rise in active ransomware groups, machine identities outnumber humans roughly 100 to one, and frontier models demonstrate autonomous multi-step exploit chaining.
- Talent markets split cleanly: connectivity, cybersecurity, energy, life sciences, and mobility skew toward non-R&D deployment hiring, while all four AI trends plus application-specific semiconductors remain over 75 percent R&D, with the sharpest shortages in orchestration, CI/CD, optimization, GPU, carbon, transmission, and networking skills.
- Adoption scores cluster at piloting and experimenting: semiconductors, cybersecurity, connectivity, life sciences, and energy rate 4 (scaling in progress); agentic software, agentic AI, and mobility rate 3 (piloting); science AI, immersive reality, quantum, robotics, and space rate 2 (experimentation).

## Suggestions & Future Directions

1. Rewire the software development life cycle around asynchronous agents, enterprise context systems, and verification pipelines, and measure productivity net of token spend rather than by lines of code generated.
2. Redesign enterprise workflows around human-agent collaboration with orchestration layers, evaluation harnesses tied to business objectives, and governance for nonhuman identities before attempting broad agentic rollouts.
3. Build closed-loop discovery capacity by pairing generative models with automated labs, proprietary and federated data assets, and validation routines, while planning for wet-lab, trial, and scale-up bottlenecks downstream.
4. Treat AI scale as resilience planning: secure power early, codesign silicon, systems, and software for inference economics, and diversify memory, packaging, and supply-chain exposure.
5. Migrate security architectures to continuous runtime inspection, zero-trust identity for agents and devices, and post-quantum cryptography inventories, and consolidate tooling into AI-native platforms with automated containment.
6. Monetize connectivity, robotics, mobility, space data, diagnostics, and energy offerings only where they solve a specific operational problem better than incumbents, and coordinate charging, spectrum, launch-access, manufacturing-capacity, and regulatory pathways as preconditions for scale.
7. Use the report's open questions as a diligence checklist: which workflows suit orchestration, which chip architectures survive workload stabilization, which form factors reach scale first, where fault-tolerant quantum lands, and which low-emission technologies clear cost and offtake without large green premiums.
