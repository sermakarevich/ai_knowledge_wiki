# Evidence, numbers, and what organizations must do

**In one sentence:** Attributed results range from twice-as-fast tasks and 16–30 percent gains at strong organizations to 40–70 percent in a full agent factory and the bank's 10x-at-half-cost, and the article's consistent moral is that these go to firms that restructure work end to end rather than plug tools into old routines.

## Key points

- Developers using generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools complete some tasks up to twice as fast, with code documentation taking roughly half the time and refactoring significantly faster.
- The strongest development organizations show productivity gains of 16–30 percent, a ceiling-case figure rather than a median outcome.
- One financial services firm running a full agent factory reports 40–70 percent higher productivity, and LATAM Airlines reports 50 percent gains with smaller teams.
- The global systemically important bank (G-SIB) agent factory anchors the frontier at ten times the speed and half the cost, arithmetically consistent with the Level 4 claim of 20 times leverage.
- Value does not come from adoption alone: the organizations capturing the most rethink roles, processes, and operating models end to end instead of layering tools onto old habits.
- Serious risk controls are part of the prescription — security, privacy, intellectual property, and plausible-but-wrong outputs — implemented through human-in-the-loop review, continuous evaluation, and explicit governance.
- All figures in this page reach the article via secondary attribution, so exact in-article methodology and context are unconfirmed and flagged accordingly in the local copy `[unverified: methodology]`.

## The ladder of reported numbers

The figures form a ladder from task to organization to paradigm. At the task rung, AI-assisted developers finish some tasks up to twice as fast, with documentation near halved. At the organization rung, the strongest shops gain 16–30 percent. At the operating-model rung, a full agent factory delivers 40–70 percent higher productivity in financial services and LATAM Airlines takes 50 percent with smaller teams. At the frontier, the G-SIB bank hits 10x speed at half cost — a ~20x efficiency product that matches the Level 4 leverage framing. Each rung demands more restructuring than the last, which is why the numbers rise with operating-model change rather than with tool licenses.

## How to read them honestly

None of these figures arrives with its methodology in the available extracts: no sample sizes, no baselines, no distinction between pilot teams and scaled rollouts. Treat 2x as task-level best cases, 16–30 percent as strong-adopter ceilings, 40–70 percent and 50 percent as single-organization reports, and 10x-at-half-cost as the article's showcase case. Planning a roadmap on ceiling cases is the exact error practitioner write-ups warn against.

## The organizational moral

"McKinsey is consistent on one point: value does not come from adoption alone. Organizations that want real gains have to rethink workflows, team structures, and controls." The prescription has three strands: upgrade talent toward framing, review, and architecture; redesign workflows for AI-paced iteration with human-in-the-loop review and small batches; and put serious risk controls around security, privacy, intellectual property, and low-quality but plausible outputs. Tool rollout without this restructuring produces the shallow progress the article contrasts with its showcase cases.

**Covers:** the attributed evidence ladder — 2x tasks, 16–30 percent strong adopters, 40–70 percent agent factory, LATAM 50 percent, G-SIB 10x at half cost — honest-reading caveats, and the restructure-don't-bolt-on prescription with talent, workflow, and governance strands.
