# Four coordinated steps to prepare data for agentic AI

**In one sentence:** The article prescribes four coordinated steps linking strategy, technology, and people — identify a small number of high-value end-to-end workflows to agentify, modernize each architecture layer instead of rebuilding, shift data quality from periodic cleanup to continuous real-time management, and rebuild the operating and governance model around human supervision and orchestration — because data strategy and operating model matter as much as data quality and architecture.

## Key points

- Enabling a scaled transformation into an agentic organization starts with foundational data capabilities and demands a technology reboot plus an organizational one, since data strategy and operating model are as important as data quality and architecture.
- Step 1 tells leaders to identify a small number of high-value end-to-end workflows to agentify, prioritizing agentic use cases by value potential, feasibility, and strategic fit before scaling broadly, building on established data-product approaches.
- Step 2 tells leaders to modernize each layer of the data architecture for agents rather than rebuilding from scratch, pursuing interoperability and governance with modular evolutionary architectures whose components can be replaced as technologies emerge.
- Step 2 explicitly warns against leaning on AI advances to shortcut architecture best practices, arguing the strongest organizations keep modular discipline instead of letting models paper over structural debt.
- Step 3 moves organizations from periodic data cleanup to continuous real-time quality management, holding structured data, unstructured data, and agent-generated outputs to consistent standards for accuracy, lineage, and governance.
- Step 4 rebuilds the operating and governance model because human roles shift from execution to supervision and orchestration of agent-driven workflows, requiring clear governance for transparent safe operation in hybrid human-agent environments.
- The four steps are designed to run as one coordinated program across strategy, technology, and people rather than as four sequential projects owned by four separate teams.

---

## Step 1: agentify where autonomy unlocks impact

"Organizations can identify a small number of high-value, end-to-end workflows where increased autonomy could unlock impact." Scoping is deliberately narrow at first: a few workflows, chosen for value potential, feasibility, and strategic fit, reusing the data-product playbook of packaging data with ownership and quality standards. Breadth comes after proof.

## Step 2: evolve the architecture, don't rip and replace

"Rather than rebuilding everything from scratch, leaders can modernize existing platforms to support interoperability and governance across systems." The evolutionary constraint matters because the technology stack is still shifting; modular components that can be swapped out beat monoliths that must be rewritten. The shortcut warning — "some may be tempted to lean on advancements in AI to shortcut data architecture best practices" — reads as a direct rebuke of teams hoping retrieval tricks can substitute for architecture.

## Step 3: quality becomes continuous operations

The shift from periodic cleanup to continuous real-time quality management covers all three data classes the article cares about: structured records, unstructured content, and the outputs agents themselves generate. The standard is uniform — accuracy, lineage, governance — no matter who or what produced the data.

## Step 4: reorganize work around supervision

"Human roles are shifting from execution to supervision and orchestration of agent-driven workflows." The operating model must define who supervises which agents, with what authority to intervene, under governance that keeps hybrid work transparent and safe at scale. Technology and organization change in lockstep, or neither sticks.
