# Roles absorb, merge, and emerge

**In one sentence:** Generative Artificial Intelligence (gen AI) makes team-size debates messier rather than simpler — engineers absorb testing, product managers (PMs) and developers may merge into a single product developer who mocks up, specifies, and generates code, and entirely new roles appear around Artificial Intelligence (AI) safety and data responsibility, large language model (LLM) selection, agent training, third-party model liability, and LLM operations (LLMOps).

## Key points

- Smaller teams are tempting but not inevitable: higher productivity may argue for maintaining or enlarging teams to do more work, and in-versus-out role debates miss the messier reality.
- Roles absorb adjacent responsibilities first, with software engineers using gen AI tools to take on testing activities as the canonical example.
- The PM and developer roles could merge into a product developer, where one high-performing person creates mock-ups, develops requirements, and generates code from those requirements with gen AI tooling.
- Short-term unpredictability demands a new AI safety and data-responsibility role that also reviews and approves code.
- Further new-role territory includes LLM selection and management, gen AI agent training and management, third-party model liability, and LLMOps for overseeing model performance over time.
- Every one of these roles implies the skills in Pages 2 and 3: a product developer needs engineer-grade review judgment plus PM-grade trust and risk instincts.
- Leadership cannot delegate these choices to hiring managers alone, which is why this section shares its heading with the oversight agenda on Page 5.

## Emerging and merging roles

### Against simplistic head-count math

"With gen AI helping people be more productive, it's tempting to think that software teams will become smaller. That may prove true, but it may also make sense to maintain or enlarge teams to do more work. Too often, conversations focus on which roles are in or out, while the reality is likely to be more nuanced and messy." Absorption comes before elimination: "We can expect roles to absorb new responsibilities—such as software engineers using gen AI tools to take on testing activities—and for some roles to merge with others."

### The product-developer merger

"The product manager and developer roles, for example, could eventually merge into a product developer, in which one high-performing person can use an array of gen AI tools to create mock-ups, develop requirements, and generate code based on those requirements." Note the qualifier "high-performing": the merged role concentrates the review judgment of Page 2 and the trust stewardship of Page 3 in a single person, which raises the bar rather than lowering it.

### Genuinely new roles

"Given the unproven and unpredictable nature of gen AI over the short term, new roles will be needed, such as one that focuses on AI safety and data responsibility and that also reviews and approves code." The article then maps the adjacent white space: "Other areas of significant scope that could require new roles may include LLM selection and management, gen AI agent training and management, third-party model liability, and LLM operations (LLMOps) capabilities to oversee model performance over time." Third-party model liability is the sleeper item: as teams build on external models, someone must own the legal and performance exposure that choice creates.
