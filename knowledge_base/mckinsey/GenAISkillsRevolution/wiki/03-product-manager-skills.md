# Product managers: equal upskilling burden, plus trust

**In one sentence:** Product managers (PMs) need just as much Artificial Intelligence (AI) upskilling as any technical role — mastering low-code and no-code tools, iterative prompting, agentic large language model (LLM) frameworks, and inference-cost planning — while owning adoption and trust through empathy for user skepticism and risk checks at every product development life cycle (PDLC) stage.

## Key points

- One hardware and software organization assessed its tech workforce and found PMs needed just as much AI upskilling as any other role, exploding the myth that this is an engineers-only transition.
- As generative Artificial Intelligence (gen AI) gets better at prototyping, PMs must master low-code and no-code tools plus iterative prompting to refine model outputs.
- PMs must understand and develop agentic frameworks — LLMs that work together to complete a task — and plan for their economics, including inference running costs.
- Trust fails in both directions, with users either distrusting gen AI or trusting it too much, so standard tool training is explicitly insufficient.
- PMs need empathy skills to surface implicit and explicit barriers to trust, such as users not believing gen-AI-provided answers, and to design around them.
- Risk concerns require PMs to work with risk experts so the right checks and measures are built into every PDLC stage rather than bolted on.
- The PM skill shift previews role convergence: a PM who prototypes, specifies, and generates code is halfway to the merged product-developer role on Page 4.

## Gen AI technology use

### An equal upskilling burden

"* Gen AI technology use. Like software engineers, PMs will need to develop new skills to work effectively with gen AI technologies. One hardware/software organization, in fact, assessed the skills of its tech employees and found that PMs needed just as much upskilling on AI as any other role did." The toolkit is hands-on: "As gen AI becomes better at building prototypes, for example, PMs will need to be proficient with low-code and no-code tools and iterative prompts to work with models to refine outputs. PMs will also need to become proficient in understanding and developing "agentic" frameworks—large language models (LLMs) that work together to complete a task." And the economics are operational, not abstract: "This will require PMs to develop plans for working with these LLMs, taking into account unique considerations, such as the costs incurred when models run inferences."

## Adoption and trust

### Why basic training fails

"* Adoption and trust. Given significant concerns regarding trust—either not trusting gen AI or trusting it too much—standard adoption programs (for example, basic training on how to use a new tool) aren't sufficient." The PM's distinctive contribution is human, not technical: "PMs must develop strong empathy skills to identify implicit and explicit barriers to trust (such as not trusting the answers that gen AI solutions provide) and to address them. Significant concerns about risk mean PMs will need to work with risk experts to ensure the right checks and measures are incorporated into every stage of the PDLC."
