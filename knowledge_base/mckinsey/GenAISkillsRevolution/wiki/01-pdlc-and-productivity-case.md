# Why talent strategy must change: the productivity evidence and the life-cycle view

**In one sentence:** Generative Artificial Intelligence (gen AI) experiments show striking gains — 40 percent higher product manager (PM) productivity, halved documentation and coding time, 30 to 40 percent jumps at IBM Software — yet 65 percent of companies use gen AI regularly while only 13 percent use it systematically in software engineering, so the article argues leaders must rethink talent starting from how gen AI transforms every phase of the product development life cycle (PDLC).

## Key points

- Gen AI lifted PM productivity by 40 percent while halving documentation and coding time in the experiments the article cites, and IBM Software developers saw 30 to 40 percent productivity jumps.
- 65 percent of McKinsey Global Survey respondents use gen AI regularly, but only 13 percent use it systematically in software engineering — the gap between dabbling and rewiring that motivates the whole article.
- Buying hundreds of gen AI tool licenses without understanding the gains and without training developers produces predictably poor outcomes, the article warns.
- Every PDLC phase changes: gen AI turns self-created insights and feature ideas into proofs of concept and prototypes, cheapens testing, and raises verification confidence through multiple hypotheses and A/B testing.
- The expected payoff is PDLC times collapsing from months to weeks or even days, alongside better code quality and less technical debt.
- Over time gen AI should generate insights from automatically created tests, system logs, user feedback, and performance data, with applied Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems already feeding user-engagement patterns back into models.
- The article's thesis: because gen AI takes over tasks rather than whole roles, workforce planning must be rebuilt around skills, not job titles.

---

## The productivity evidence

### What the early experiments found

"Promising experiments that use gen AI to support coding tasks show impressive productivity improvements. Gen AI has improved product manager (PM) productivity by 40 percent, while halving the time it takes to document and code. At IBM Software, for example, developers using gen AI saw 30 to 40 percent jumps in productivity." The IBM figure is sourced to Business Standard reporting from July 2024. The standfirst frames the stakes for software-driven companies: "Developing the software talent companies need to grow means thinking in terms of skills rather than roles to navigate this period of uncertainty around talent."

### The adoption gap

"Despite its promise, gen AI has barely revealed its full potential. While some 65 percent of respondents to the recent McKinsey Global Survey on the state of AI report that they are regularly using gen AI, only 13 percent are systematically using gen AI in software engineering." The article's diagnosis of failed rollouts is blunt: "So the decisions that seem bold on paper—such as buying hundreds of gen AI tool licenses for developers—are made without a clear understanding of the potential gains and with insufficient training of developers. The result: predictably poor outcomes."

## Gen AI across the product development life cycle

### Start from the life cycle, not the org chart

"Any engineering talent rethink needs to begin with an understanding of how gen AI will affect the product development life cycle (PDLC). The changes are likely to be significant and affect every phase of the life cycle (exhibit)." The exhibit itself was not recovered in text form, but the article spells out the mechanisms: "Gen AI can use self-created insights and ideas for new features to create proofs of concept and prototypes, as well as to reduce the cost of testing and unlock higher verification confidence (for example, multiple hypotheses and A/B testing)." The quantified expectation is that "these developments are expected to significantly reduce PDLC times from months to weeks or even days, improve code quality, and reduce technical debt."

### The compounding data loop

The long-run mechanism is a feedback loop on engineering data: "Over time, gen AI should be able to generate insights from automatically created tests, system logs, user feedback, and performance data." A present-day signal is already visible: "Some organizations are already integrating gen AI with applied AI use cases, such as using applied AI systems to analyze the performance of gen-AI-created content by identifying patterns in user engagement, which are then fed back to the model."
